THE BOOK
- Layer 1: "Read the verse. Read the surrounding verses. Read the chapter. Ask: what is the speaker doing here? Who are they speaking to?"
- Layer 2: "what did the original word carry?"
- Layer 3: "what does the rest of the KJV say about this?" and "Where Layer 3 is the highest layer a conclusion reaches ... it is held as exactly that: consistent, not explicit."
- Layer 4: "the reader's own inference — the step taken beyond what the text states at L1, what the original word confirms at L2, and what scripture assembles at L3. ... They are not forbidden ... But an inference presented as text is the primary failure mode this method exists to prevent."
- "The text establishes. The text points. The reader infers."

A. MATTHEW 1:19
L1. "Then Joseph her husband, being a just [man], and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." The narrator speaks. Before it: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (1:18). After it: "while he thought on these things ... fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" (1:20); he "took unto him his wife" (1:24).
 The verse establishes four things: he is "her husband"; he is "just"; he was "not willing to make her a publick example"; he "was minded to put her away privily".
 The verse does not state why putting her away came into his mind. It names no sin, no law and no tradition, and it says nothing of what he thought about the child. The angel's word names one thing in him: a fear about taking her ("fear not to take").
L2. "husband" aner (G435). "just" dikaios (G1342): "equitable (in character or act); by implication, innocent, holy". "make ... a publick example" paradeigmatizo (G3856): "to show alongside (the public), i.e. expose to infamy:--make a public example, put to an open shame". "minded" boulomai (G1014). "put away" apolyo (G630): "relieve, release, dismiss ... or (specially) divorce". "privily" lathra (G2977): "privately:--privily, secretly".
 The word establishes that what he would not do to her is expose her to infamy before the public. The word is of shame before men. It is not a word of sentence or judgment.
 ERROR IN THE RECORD (W1 found it, Wall 12736; I copied it into my draft): Sight 266 gives this word as deigmatizo G1165. The word map gives G3856. G1165 is the word of Colossians 2:15.
L3. The same word has one other row: "put [him] to an open shame" (Hebrews 6:6). The rows call them husband and wife before the taking (1:19, 1:20, 1:24). Scripture sets the tradition of men against the commandment of God (Mark 7:7-13; Colossians 2:8). None of these rows speaks of Joseph or of an espousal custom. So this layer is "consistent, not explicit": the text points.
L4. Named as inference: the shame he would keep from her is the scorn of men's tradition toward a wife found with child before the taking, and he knows the child is his. Under it: L1 (her husband; thy wife; a fear about taking her), L2 (the word is infamy before the public), L3 (Hebrews 6:6; the tradition rows). No row names that tradition.
 The other reading, that Joseph suspected adultery, is also Layer 4. The verse does not state it either. It has no row that says Joseph thought so.
RESULT. The sentence in Sight 32 that I reported as a flaw, "no row states the ground of the intent in its own words", is what Layer 1 gives. It is the honest flag the book requires, not a defect.

B. LEVITICUS 19:19
L1. "Ye shall keep my statutes. Thou shalt not let thy cattle gender with a diverse kind: thou shalt not sow thy field with mingled seed: neither shall a garment mingled of linen and woollen come upon thee." The LORD's statute, spoken to "Ye" and "Thou". Before it: "thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself" (19:18). After it: the case of a bondmaid betrothed to an husband (19:20). The chapter closes: "observe all my statutes" (19:37).
 The verse establishes three prohibitions on what a man does with his cattle, his field and a garment. It does not speak of Spirit and flesh, and it does not speak of what God himself does.
L2. "statutes" chuqqah (H2708). "gender" raba (H7250): "to squat or lie out flat, i.e. (specifically) in copulation". "diverse kind", "mingled seed" and "mingled" are one word, kil'ayim (H3610): "two heterogeneities; divers seeds (-e kinds), mingled (seed)". It is not the word of Genesis 1:11, "after his kind" (W1, Wall 12736: miyn, H4327).
 The word establishes that the thing forbidden is the joining of two unlike things, and that the first clause is about copulation.
L3. kil'ayim has one other row: "Thou shalt not sow thy vineyard with divers seeds: lest the fruit ... be defiled" (Deuteronomy 22:9). raba has two other rows, both of a woman and a beast: "it [is] confusion" (Leviticus 18:23; 20:16). "That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit" (John 3:6). "to every seed his own body" (1 Corinthians 15:38). Scripture carries a statute about a beast to men: "Doth God take care for oxen? ... For our sakes, no doubt, [this] is written" (1 Corinthians 9:9-10; 1 Timothy 5:18), which is Deuteronomy 25:4, a different statute. No row applies Leviticus 19:19 to Spirit and flesh. Consistent, not explicit: the text points.
L4. Named as inference: the claim that the Holy Ghost begat the flesh has God doing what his own statute forbids. Under it: L1 (the statute), L2 (kil'ayim; raba), L3 (Deuteronomy 22:9; John 3:6; the ox statute carried to men). Sight 1822 already marks this step [L4].
RESULT. This step is an inference, and the sight names it as one. That is the method, not a defect. What qwen4max wrote, that the statute "speaks of cattle, fields and garments", is true at Layer 1; the answer to it is the layers under the [L4], laid out.
