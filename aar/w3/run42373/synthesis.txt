## 1. Uncontested L1–L2–L3 ground

These points were expressly granted and were not themselves charged in either premise list. Their disputed implications belong under section 2.

- **Joseph is Mary’s husband.** The challenger expressly granted Matthew 1:16: “Joseph the husband of Mary.”
- **Joseph is of David’s house.** Both sides accepted Luke 2:4: “he was of the house and lineage of David.”
- **Christ is David’s seed according to the flesh.** The challenger granted Romans 1:3: “made of the seed of David according to the flesh.” The route of that descent remained disputed.
- **Christ shares humanity with the children.** Both sides accepted Hebrews 2:14: “he also himself likewise took part of the same.” Whether this establishes a particular manner of conception remained disputed.

Agreement on these propositions does not establish either side’s disputed connecting inferences.

## 3. Defender-only instruction violations and limits

### Demonstrable omission concerning John 1:14

The governing instruction required John 1:14’s only-begotten language to be accompanied by Hebrews 11:17’s use concerning Isaac.

The defender quoted John 1:14 in its opening:

> “the glory as of the only begotten of the Father”

It did not accompany that quotation there with Hebrews 11:17. It supplied the Isaac comparison in round 2. That later supplementation does not erase the opening omission.

### Self-application and lexical reasoning

The defender objected when the challenger selected the conjugal sense of *synerchomai*, but defended its own selections by dictionary ordering:

> “This is the literal sense, near, the first the gloss lists.”

And concerning *nomizō*:

> “the answer rests on its first sense”

These are observable instances where the requirement to test one’s own lexical inference equally needed application. A gloss’s first-listed sense does not, by its position, establish the contextual sense. The defender did supply contextual citations; the defect is treating dictionary order as additional determinative support.

### What cannot be charged from this transcript

- No defender grant or concession expressly accepts the opposing biological-paternity conclusion.
- The transcript does not establish whether full chapters were privately consulted. A failure to perform that consultation cannot be reported as a verified event.
- The defender did not supply a narrated reconciliation of the divergent genealogies. Its rejection of a maternal reassignment is not itself such a reconciliation.

## 4. Engagement and defense method

The challenger **engaged chains as chains**: it challenged transitions between Davidic descent and immediate paternity, ordinary conception accounts and this conception, household standing and biological descent, and divine presence and conception.

Both sides also made lexical transfers requiring contextual justification:

- The challenger moved from the conjugal use in 1 Corinthians 7:5 to Matthew 1:18.
- The defender moved from empowerment and overshadowing elsewhere to their function in Luke 1:35.
- Both used conception or formation accounts involving other persons to interpret this account.

The defender supplied substantive responses rather than a review placeholder. It maintained its declared position throughout. That describes its conduct; it does not adjudicate whether its inferences were established.

## 5. Most vulnerable connecting inferences

**Defender:**
- Moving from abstinence narrated after the taking to an affirmative earlier union.
- Moving from Davidic ancestry and Joseph’s named house to the particular immediate begetter.
- Applying the heterogeneous-kinds statute to divine action in conception.
- Identifying a particular tradition behind Joseph’s fear.
- Treating restored signet imagery as establishing removal of the throne restriction.

**Challenger:**
- Assigning Luke’s genealogy to Mary without an exhibited textual connection naming that assignment.
- Deriving the proposed tribal-house mechanism from taking and naming.
- Making the Spirit-origin language exclude human participation through the exhibited cross-references.
- Treating Joseph’s contemplated dismissal as establishing his knowledge of non-paternity.
- Making the Coniah argument depend on the disputed genealogy and succession construction.

These are the disputed links requiring assessment—not doctrinal conclusions supplied by this synthesis.
