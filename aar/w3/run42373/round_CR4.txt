# DEFENDER, CLOSE ROUND

The question is whether Christ had a biological father. The chain stands as declared, D1 to D6, and every link is kept. Each witness the challenger brought in the link round (C1 to C6) is answered below by book, chapter and verse, at the word the challenger pressed.

---

## THE CHAIN, KEPT

**D1: KEPT.** The oath is fulfilled in seed out of a man's loins, according to the flesh.
- [L1] 2 Samuel 7:12: "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom."
- [L2] mêʻeh (H4578): "the uterus (or of men, the seat of generation)". zeraʻ (H2233): "seed; figuratively, fruit, plant, sowing-time, posterity". osphys (G3751), in Acts 2:30: "internally (by extension) procreative power". sperma (G4690), in Romans 1:3: "something sown, i.e. seed (including the male 'sperm'); by implication, offspring".
- [L3] Acts 2:30: "that of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne".
- [L3] Romans 1:3: "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh".
- [L4] The oath's seed comes out of David's bowels (mêʻeh), "of men, the seat of generation" (2 Samuel 7:12). Acts 2:30 names the same source as "his loins" (osphys, "procreative power"), "according to the flesh". Romans 1:3 calls what reached Christ sperma, "seed (including the male 'sperm')", again "according to the flesh". Three rows name one source, and it is a man's seat of generation. The challenger granted at C3: "I hold that the flesh is of David's seed."

**D2: KEPT.** A wife conceives by her husband knowing her, and where the LORD gives the conception, the husband stands in the row.
- [L1] Genesis 4:1: "And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain".
- [L2] yâdaʻ (H3045): "to know ... [phrase] lie by man". hârâh (H2029): "to be (or become) pregnant, conceive". hêrôwn (H2032), in Ruth 4:13: "pregnancy; conception".
- [L3] Ruth 4:13: "So Boaz took Ruth, and she was his wife: and when he went in unto her, the LORD gave her conception, and she bare a son."
- [L3] 1 Samuel 1:19: "and Elkanah knew Hannah his wife; and the LORD remembered her."
- [L4] Genesis 4:1 joins the knowing (yâdaʻ, "lie by man") to the conceiving (hârâh) in one row: "knew Eve his wife; and she conceived". Ruth 4:13 sets the LORD's gift of conception (hêrôwn) in the same row as "when he went in unto her". 1 Samuel 1:19 sets "the LORD remembered her" beside "Elkanah knew Hannah his wife". So the LORD's part at a conception stands together with the husband's knowing.

**D3: KEPT.** Joseph and Mary were husband and wife in the espousal. The finding with child comes before the household coming together and the taking. The not-knowing runs from the taking to the birth.
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] synerchomai (G4905): "to convene ... associate with ... come (together)". paralambanō (G3880): "to receive near, i.e. associate with oneself ...:--receive, take (unto, with)". ginōskō (G1097): "to 'know'".
- [L3] Matthew 1:19: "Then Joseph her husband".
- [L3] Matthew 1:24: "and took unto him his wife".
- [L3] Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son".
- [L4] The rows name them husband and wife before the coming together: "Joseph her husband" (Matthew 1:19). Matthew uses three words for three steps. The first is the coming together (synerchomai). The second is the taking (paralambanō), which Joseph does at Matthew 1:24 by the angel's word. The third is the knowing (ginōskō) of Matthew 1:25, the word of "Adam knew Eve his wife" (Genesis 4:1, quoted above). The finding is told before the taking. The "knew her not till" is told after the taking and bounded by the birth.

**D4: KEPT.** "Of the Holy Ghost" marks the child's holy origin and anointing, in a row that names "Joseph, thou son of David" and "Mary thy wife". Spirit brings forth spirit, and flesh brings forth flesh.
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost. [conceived: Gr. begotten]"
- [L2] ek (G1537): "a primary preposition denoting origin (the point whence action or motion proceeds)". Christos (G5547), in Matthew 1:18: "anointed, i.e. the Messiah".
- [L3] Matthew 1:18: "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise".
- [L3] John 3:6: "That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit."
- [L4] The ek of Matthew 1:20 marks the point whence. Matthew 1:18 names the child in that same account "Jesus Christ", Christos, "anointed". John 3:6 gives what is born of the Spirit as spirit and what is born of the flesh as flesh. So "of the Holy Ghost" marks the holy, anointed origin of the child. The same row names the husband, "Joseph, thou son of David", and the wife, "Mary thy wife".

**D5: KEPT.** The father is named in Mary's mouth, the narrator's, the town's and a disciple's, and he is of the house of David, by whose father's house the tribe is reckoned.
- [L1] Luke 2:48: "and his mother said unto him, Son, why hast thou thus dealt with us? behold, thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing."
- [L2] patēr (G3962): "a 'father' (literally or figuratively, near or more remote)". patria (G3965), in Luke 2:4: "paternal descent ... family, kindred, lineage".
- [L3] John 6:42: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?"
- [L3] Luke 2:4: "(because he was of the house and lineage of David:)"
- [L3] Numbers 1:18: "they declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers".
- [L4] Mary calls Joseph "thy father" (patēr) to the son (Luke 2:48). The town names "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know" (John 6:42). Joseph's own lineage, patria, "paternal descent", is David's (Luke 2:4). The pedigree is declared "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:18). So the named father carries David's house to the child.

**D6: KEPT.** Yes. Christ had a biological father, Joseph, the son of David, who knew Mary his wife within the espousal. The child is of his loins, "made of the seed of David according to the flesh".
- [L1] Romans 1:3, quoted above.
- [L2] sperma (G4690): "seed (including the male 'sperm')". gennaō (G1080), in Matthew 1:20's margin: "to procreate (properly, of the father, but by extension of the mother)".
- [L3] Genesis 4:1, quoted above.
- [L3] Matthew 1:20, quoted above.
- [L3] Matthew 1:25, quoted above.
- [L4] D1 gives seed (sperma) out of a man's loins, according to the flesh (Romans 1:3). D2 gives the canon's way of begetting as the husband knowing his wife (Genesis 4:1). D3 gives the order, in which the not-knowing runs from the taking to the birth (Matthew 1:25). D4 gives "of the Holy Ghost" as the child's holy origin, in the row that names "Joseph, thou son of David" and "Mary thy wife" (Matthew 1:20). D5 gives the named father of David's house. The margin's gennaō is "properly, of the father". Read together, these rows show that Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal, and that the child is of his loins.
