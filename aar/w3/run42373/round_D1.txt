CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

# CHALLENGER'S ANSWER (full round, layers completed)

**Position:** No. Christ had no biological father. Joseph was the husband of Mary and the man who took the child and named him. The child was begotten in Mary by the Holy Ghost.

I keep Isaiah 28:10: "precept [must be] upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line". Each line below carries its layers, and every summary line quotes its verses.

---

## 0. WHAT I GRANT

- [L1] Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary". Joseph is Mary's husband.
- [L1] Luke 2:4: "he was of the house and lineage of David". Joseph is of David's house.
- [L1] Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh". Christ's flesh is of David's seed.
- [L3] Hebrews 2:14: "he also himself likewise took part of the same". Christ was fully man.

The dispute is the route by which David's seed came to Christ. The opening asserts a route through Joseph's loins. I press the rows that state the source of the child.

---

## I. MY CASE

**C1. Matthew's genealogy closes with a different verb and a different source.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
- [L2] "begat" is the active gennaō (G1080), "to procreate". "was born" is the same verb in the passive, egennēthē. "Of whom" is hēs, the feminine singular of hos (G3739), "who, which".
- [L3] Matthew 1:1: "The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham."
- [L4] The line is a run of "begat": the father is the one who begat. At the end the verb turns passive and its source is the feminine "of whom", Mary. Joseph is given as "the husband of Mary". Matthew 1:1 puts "the son of David" over the whole generation, so it reaches the child across the generations and is not tied to Joseph as immediate begetter.

**C2. The origin is stated twice, by the narrator and by the angel, and both name the Holy Ghost.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] "conceived" is gennaō (G1080), margin "begotten". The opening's own lexicon gives it as "to procreate (properly, of the father...)". "Of" is ek (G1537), "origin (the point whence action or motion proceeds)".
- [L3] John 1:13: "Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God."
- [L4] Matthew 1:20 carries the verb the opening defines as "properly, of the father", and ek names the point of origin: the Holy Ghost. John 1:13 uses the same verb and preposition for birth "of God", and there "the will of man" is set outside the birth. The opening makes John 3:5-6 its parallel. John 1:13 shows what that parallel carries.

**C3. "Before they came together" is before a married coming together, and Joseph's fear and purpose fit a man who knew himself not the father.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] "came together" is synerchomai (G4905).
- [L3] 1 Corinthians 7:5: "and come together again, that Satan tempt you not for your incontinency." Husband and wife "come together" in marriage.
- [L1] Matthew 1:19: "Joseph her husband, being a just [man], and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily."
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] "for" is gar (G1063), "assigning a reason".
- [L3] Deuteronomy 24:1: "then let him write her a bill of divorcement".
- [L4] Synerchomai is used of a married pair at 1 Corinthians 7:5, so Matthew 1:18 dates the finding before that coming together. The angel gives a reason (gar) for removing Joseph's fear: the child is "of the Holy Ghost". That reason meets a fear that, in Matthew 1:19, had him minded to put her away, as Deuteronomy 24:1 provides. A husband who knew himself the begetter has no ground for that fear. The angel's reason is a reason because the child was not Joseph's.

**C4. The angel's answer to Mary's question states the means.**
- [L1] Luke 1:27: "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph".
- [L1] Luke 1:31: "thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son".
- [L1] Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- [L2] "therefore" is dio (G1352), "through which thing, consequently". "Overshadow" is episkiazō (G1982), "to cast a shade upon, to invest with preternatural influence". "I know not" is ginōskō (G1097), the opening's own word for the union.
- [L3] Luke 1:13: "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son". Luke 1:37: "For with God nothing shall be impossible."
- [L4] Mary asks "How?", and the stated means is the Holy Ghost coming upon her. Dio ties the holy thing's title to that coming and overshadowing, the clause directly before it. In Luke 1:13 the angel says to a husband "shall bear thee a son". To Mary he names the Holy Ghost as the means. Luke 1:37 gives the ground of what is told.

**C5. The sign was a virgin conceiving, and "all this" includes the conception.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:22-23: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel".
- [L1] Isaiah 7:14: "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel."
- [L2] "virgin" is parthenos (G3933), "a maiden, an unmarried daughter". In Isaiah 7:14 it is 'almah (H5959), the KJV's "virgin".
- [L3] Genesis 24:43: "when the virgin cometh forth to draw [water]". Genesis 24:16: "a virgin, neither had any man known her". The same 'almah is used of Rebekah.
- [L4] "All this" (Matthew 1:22) covers the verses before it, including the child "of the Holy Ghost" (1:18, 1:20). Isaiah 7:14 makes the virgin's conceiving the sign. Genesis 24:43 read with 24:16 shows 'almah applied to one "neither had any man known her".

**C6. Adam shows that full manhood does not depend on a human father.**
- [L1] Genesis 2:7: "the LORD God formed man [of] the dust of the ground".
- [L1] Luke 3:38: "which was [the son] of Adam, which was [the son] of God."
- [L3] 1 Corinthians 15:47: "The first man [is] of the earth, earthy: the second man [is] the Lord from heaven."
- [L3] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me".
- [L4] Adam was a man, and Luke 3:38 gives his source as God. Christ is "the second man" (1 Corinthians 15:47), and his body is one "prepared" (Hebrews 10:5). So the full humanity of Hebrews 2:14 stands where no human father is stated.

**C6b. Seed of David through the woman.**
- [L1] Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh".
- [L3] Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law".
- [L3] Luke 1:42: "blessed [is] the fruit of thy womb".
- [L2] "made" in Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 is ginomai (G1096), "to become", not gennaō (G1080), "to procreate".
- [L4] Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 use the same verb, ginomai, for his coming to be. Galatians 4:4 gives "of a woman", and Luke 1:42 names her womb's fruit. So "seed of David according to the flesh" is carried by Mary, of whom he was born (Matthew 1:16). It is not carried by seed from Joseph.

## II. PRESSES ON THE DEFENDER'S CHAIN

**PRESS ON D1** (the oath is fulfilled in seed out of a man's loins).
PRESSED WITH:
- [L1] Acts 2:30: "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh". The loins named are David's.
- [L3] Hebrews 7:10: "For he was yet in the loins of his father, when Melchisedec met him." Hebrews 7:9: "Levi also... payed tithes in Abraham." Levi was in Abraham's loins through intervening fathers.
- [L3] Luke 1:42: "blessed [is] the fruit of thy womb."
- [L4] The opening's own row, Hebrews 7:10, shows "in the loins" reaching from a remote ancestor through generations. So Acts 2:30 and Romans 1:3 carry Christ's flesh to David's loins and do not place it in Joseph's loins. Luke 1:35 gives the immediate source as "born of thee", and Luke 1:42 names "the fruit of thy womb".
- [L2] "made" in Romans 1:3 is ginomai (G1096), "to become". It is not gennaō (G1080), "to procreate".

**PRESS ON D1, the Jeconiah row** (the seed of Joseph and the throne).
- [L1] Matthew 1:11: "Josias begat Jechonias". Matthew 1:12: "Jechonias begat Salathiel".
- [L1] Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph". The "begat" line runs to Joseph.
- [L3] 1 Chronicles 3:16: "the sons of Jehoiakim: Jeconiah his son". Jeremiah 22:24: "Coniah the son of Jehoiakim king of Judah".
- [L1] Jeremiah 22:30: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah."
- [L1] Luke 1:32: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David".
- [L4] Jeremiah 22:30 bars the seed of Coniah, the same man as Jeconiah, from the throne of David. Matthew 1:11-16 runs Joseph's begetting line through Jechonias. Luke 1:32 gives Christ that throne. If Christ's flesh were of Joseph's seed, Luke 1:32 and Jeremiah 22:30 would collide. A child begotten in Mary by the Holy Ghost (Matthew 1:20) and named by Joseph (Matthew 1:21, 1:25) is not of Jechonias's seed, and the two rows then stand together.

**PRESS ON D2** (the husband knowing the wife is the way of conception).
PRESSED WITH:
- [L1] Luke 1:34: "seeing I know not a man".
- [L1] Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son".
- [L2] Both use ginōskō (G1097), the opening's own word for the union.
- [L3] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee".
- [L4] Genesis 4:1 and 1 Samuel 1:19 state the husband's knowing for their couples. For Mary, Luke 1:34 states her not knowing a man, and Luke 1:35 states the Holy Ghost coming upon her as the means. Matthew 1:25 states the not-knowing through the birth. The rows about this couple state their own means, and it is not the one carried over from other couples.

**PRESS ON D3** (the finding came before the household union, so the begetting was within the espousal).
PRESSED WITH:
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost".
- [L3] 1 Corinthians 7:5: "come together again".
- [L2] synerchomai (G4905) as in C3.
- [L4] The opening splits "come together" from "knew". 1 Corinthians 7:5 uses "come together" of a married pair, and Matthew 1:18 gives the source of what was found in the same sentence: "of the Holy Ghost". The finding is dated before they came together, and its source is the Holy Ghost.
- [L1] Mark 7:7: "teaching [for] doctrines the commandments of men." Mark 7:8: "the washing of pots and cups".
- [L2] "fear" in Matthew 1:20 is phobeō (G5399), "to be afraid"; "for" is gar (G1063), "assigning a reason".
- [L4] The opening reads Matthew 1:19 as sparing a wife the scorn of men's tradition (Mark 7:7-8). Mark 7:7-8 speak of the washing of pots and cups and share no word with Matthew 1:19. Matthew 1:20 has a husband afraid to take his wife and an angel giving a reason (gar) for the fear. The reason given is the origin of the child.

**PRESS ON D4a** (John 3:5-6 makes "of the Holy Ghost" a holy marking).
PRESSED WITH:
- [L1] John 3:6: "That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit."
- [L3] John 1:13: "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God".
- [L2] gennaō with ek (G1080 + G1537) in all three verses.
- [L4] The opening takes gennaō + ek + Spirit in John 3:5-6 as the new birth. John 1:13 shows what that birth is: the source is God, and "the will of man" is set outside it. So the parallel gives Matthew 1:20 a source in the Holy Ghost, with the will of man set outside.

**PRESS ON D4b** (Leviticus 19:19: the Holy Ghost as begetter of flesh would make God break his own statute).
PRESSED WITH:
- [L1] Leviticus 19:19: "Ye shall keep my statutes. Thou shalt not let thy cattle gender with a diverse kind".
- [L1] Genesis 2:7: "the LORD God formed man [of] the dust of the ground".
- [L1] Genesis 2:22: "the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman".
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "that holy thing which shall be born of thee".
- [L3] 1 Corinthians 15:39: "there [is] one [kind of] flesh of men".
- [L4] The statute is addressed to "ye", Israel, about cattle, field and garment. Genesis 2:7 and 2:22 show God forming flesh of man by his own act. Luke 1:35 says the holy thing is born "of thee": the flesh is of Mary, a woman of the one kind of flesh of men (1 Corinthians 15:39). So the kind is kept.

**PRESS ON D4c** (Luke 1:35 read with 1 Chronicles 28:6).
PRESSED WITH:
- [L1] 1 Chronicles 28:6: "I have chosen him [to be] my son, and I will be his father."
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee... therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- [L2] dio (G1352), "consequently".
- [L3] John 1:14: "the glory as of the only begotten of the Father".
- [L4] The stated ground of Solomon's sonship is "I have chosen him". The stated ground at Luke 1:35 is "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore". The grounds differ. Acts 1:8, which the opening sets beside Luke 1:35, is power on those already born. Luke 1:35 is a coming upon Mary before a birth "of thee". John 1:14 names the one begotten as "of the Father".

**PRESS ON D5** (the father is named by Mary, the narrator, the town and a disciple).
PRESSED WITH:
- [L1] Luke 2:48: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing."
- [L1] Luke 2:49: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"
- [L2] patēr (G3962), the opening's own gloss: "a 'father' (literally or figuratively, near or more remote)".
- [L1] Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph".
- [L2] "supposed" is nomizō (G3543), "to deem, to think, to suppose".
- [L3] John 6:41: "The Jews then murmured at him, because he said, I am the bread which came down from heaven." John 6:42: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? how is it then that he saith, I came down from heaven?" Matthew 13:55: "Is not this the carpenter's son?"
- [L3] Luke 2:33: "Joseph and his mother marvelled at those things which were spoken of him."
- [L4] In Luke 2:48-49, "thy father" and "my Father's" stand in adjacent verses, and the child answers Mary's "father" with his own "Father's business". The opening's own lexicon allows "figuratively". Luke 3:23 gives "as was supposed" (nomizō) for "son of Joseph", so that phrase is what was deemed. The speakers of John 6:42 are the murmurers of 6:41, and they set "the son of Joseph" against "I came down from heaven". Luke 2:33 gives the narrator's word as "Joseph and his mother". Each voice the opening gathers is household speech or supposition.

## III. THE ANSWER TO THE QUESTION

**The question:** "According to Hebrews 7:14, how is it evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda, given the law of Numbers 1:2 and 1:18?"

- [L1] Hebrews 7:14: "For [it is] evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda".
- [L2] "sprang" is anatellō (G393), "to arise, spring up (as a plant or dayspring)". It names an arising out of a stock and is not gennaō (G1080), "to procreate".
- [L3] Isaiah 11:1: "there shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse".
- [L1] Luke 1:32: "the throne of his father David".
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "that holy thing which shall be born of thee".
- [L1] Matthew 1:20-21: "Joseph, thou son of David... thou shalt call his name JESUS".
- [L3] Numbers 1:18: "by the house of their fathers". Matthew 1:24: "took unto him his wife". Matthew 1:25: "he called his name JESUS".
- [L3] Hebrews 7:10: "yet in the loins of his father". The opening's own row shows the loins-descent reaching through earlier fathers.
- [L4] Hebrews 7:14 uses "sprang", a verb of arising from a stock, and Isaiah 11:1 gives the stock-and-branch pattern. The stated source of the flesh is "of thee", Mary (Luke 1:35), and her "womb" (Luke 1:42). Joseph, "son of David", took his wife (Matthew 1:24) and named the child (Matthew 1:21, 1:25), and the child is reckoned in his house (Luke 2:4). Jeremiah 22:30, quoted above, shows that a line of begetting through Jechonias could not be the line of the throne.

## DECLARED CHAIN (each line carries its quoted verses)

1. **C1.** Matthew's genealogy ends with "of whom was born Jesus", passive and feminine, after a line of "begat".
   [L1] Matthew 1:16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
   [L3] Matthew 1:1: "the son of David, the son of Abraham."
   [L2] gennaō (G1080) active and passive; hēs, feminine "of whom".

2. **C2.** The origin of the child is "of the Holy Ghost", twice stated, and the will of man is set outside such a birth.
   [L1] Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
   [L3] Matthew 1:18: "she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." John 1:13: "nor of the will of man, but of God."
   [L2] gennaō (G1080), ek (G1537) "origin".

3. **C3.** The finding was before a married coming together, and the angel's reason answers Joseph's fear and purpose.
   [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child".
   [L3] 1 Corinthians 7:5: "come together again". Matthew 1:19: "was minded to put her away privily."
   [L2] synerchomai (G4905); gar (G1063) "assigning a reason".

4. **C4.** To Mary's "How?" the means is the Holy Ghost coming upon her, and the holy thing is born "of thee".
   [L1] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee".
   [L3] Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" Luke 1:37: "For with God nothing shall be impossible."
   [L2] dio (G1352), episkiazō (G1982), ginōskō (G1097).

5. **C5.** The virgin conceiving is the fulfilled sign.
   [L1] Matthew 1:23: "Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son".
   [L3] Isaiah 7:14: "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son". Genesis 24:16: "a virgin, neither had any man known her".
   [L2] parthenos (G3933); 'almah (H5959).

6. **C6.** Full manhood stands where no human father is stated, and the seed of David is carried through the woman.
   [L1] Luke 3:38: "which was [the son] of Adam, which was [the son] of God."
   [L3] 1 Corinthians 15:47: "the second man [is] the Lord from heaven." Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me". Galatians 4:4: "made of a woman".
   [L2] ginomai (G1096), "to become".

7. **C7 (conclusion).** No. Christ had no biological father. His flesh is of David's seed by Mary, he was reckoned into David's house by Joseph taking and naming him, and he was begotten in Mary by the Holy Ghost.
   [L1] Matthew 1:20, quoted above: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." Luke 1:35, quoted above: "that holy thing which shall be born of thee".
   [L3] Matthew 1:25, quoted above: "he called his name JESUS." Jeremiah 22:30, quoted above: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David".
   [L4] on C1 and C2 (the verb and its source) + C4 (the stated means) + C5 (the sign) + the Jeconiah press (Matthew 1:11-16 with Jeremiah 22:30 and Luke 1:32).
