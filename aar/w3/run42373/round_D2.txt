## B. MATTHEW'S ORDER TESTED (against D2 and D3)

**1. The chain needs an act that the three words of Matthew do not list.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost".
- [L1] Matthew 1:24-25: "and took unto him his wife: And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son".
- [L2] The defender lists three acts: synerchomai (G4905, come together), paralambanō (G3880, receive near, take unto) and ginōskō (G1097, know).
- [L3] Luke 1:27: "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph". The woman espoused to Joseph is called parthenos (G3933, a maiden, an unmarried daughter).
- [L4] Matthew's order is: finding, taking, not-knowing until the birth. The chain's "knew Mary his wife within the espousal" would be a fourth act. It would stand between the espousal of Luke 1:27 and the finding of Matthew 1:18. Genesis 4:1 and 1 Samuel 1:19 give that act to other couples.

**2. The defender's own rule for 1 Corinthians 7:5 makes "came together" conjugal.**
- [L1] 1 Corinthians 7:5: "Defraud ye not one the other, except [it be] with consent for a time, that ye may give yourselves to fasting and prayer; and come together again, that Satan tempt you not for your incontinency."
- [L2] "Defraud" is aposteréō (G650, to despoil). "Incontinency" is akrasia (G192, want of self-restraint). Both stand in the verse, and the verb "come together" is the verb of Matthew 1:18.
- [L3] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together".
- [L4] The defender says 1 Corinthians 7:5 is "set by its own context". Its context is a defrauding and a want of self-restraint, which is the married union. So the verb carries that sense, and Matthew 1:18 dates the finding before that union.

**3. The finding is dated before the union, and its source is stated in the same clause.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "she was found with child of the Holy Ghost".
- [L2] The source is stated with ek (G1537, origin, the point whence action proceeds).
- [L3] Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost".
- [L4] The narrator and the angel state the same source with the same preposition. The clause "before they came together" sets that source ahead of any union.

## C. JOSEPH'S PURPOSE AND THE ANGEL'S REASON (against D3)

- [L1] Matthew 1:19: "Joseph her husband, being a just [man], and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily."
- [L2] "just" is dikaios (G1342, equitable, just). "Put away" belongs with apolyō (G630, to release, to divorce). "Privily" is lathra (G2977, secretly).
- [L3] Deuteronomy 24:1: "then let him write her a bill of divorcement".
- [L4] The verse gives two stated reasons. Justice leads to the putting away, and unwillingness to expose leads to doing it privily. The defender's "infamy" explains the second. The justice that leads to putting away is stated in the verse, and a husband who knew himself the begetter stands apart from it.

**The angel's reason.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost".
- [L2] "for" is gar (G1063, assigning a reason). "Holy" is hagios (G40, sacred) in the phrase "Holy Ghost", and it describes the Spirit.
- [L3] Luke 1:35: "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God".
- [L4] The defender says "the child in her is hagios" is the reason given. The reason Matthew 1:20 gives is the origin of what is conceived: "of the Holy Ghost". The child's holiness is stated in Luke 1:35 as a consequence (dio, G1352, consequently) of the Holy Ghost coming. It is stated there as that consequence and is not stated as a comfort to a father.

**Mark 7.**
- [L1] Mark 7:8: "the washing of pots and cups".
- [L2] "washing" is baptismos (G909, ablution).
- [L3] Mark 7:9: "that ye may keep your own tradition".
- [L4] The defender calls this "an [L3] application". The matter of Mark 7:8-9 is washings and the keeping of tradition, and that is the matter it states.

## E. SEED, WOMB AND THE LINE OF DAVID (against D1)

**1. Seed is said of a woman.**
- [L1] Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh".
- [L2] sperma (G4690) is "seed (including the male 'sperm'); by implication, offspring". The word "including" is the lexicon's own. [L2] zera' (H2233, seed, posterity) stands in 2 Samuel 7:12.
- [L3] Genesis 3:15: "between thy seed and her seed".
- [L4] The defender reads sperma as male sperm and joins it to ginomai (G1096, to become). Genesis 3:15 gives seed to a woman, and Romans 1:3 uses the verb of becoming, not gennaō.
- [L3] Genesis 1:11: "whose seed [is] in itself". The defender's tiktō gloss reads "from seed, as a mother, a plant, the earth". The seed there is in the one that brings forth.

**2. The chain to David runs through the stated source of the flesh.**
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "that holy thing which shall be born of thee".
- [L1] Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh".
- [L3] Galatians 4:4: "made of a woman, made under the law". The same verb ginomai stands in Galatians 4:4 and Romans 1:3.
- [L3] Luke 1:42: "blessed [is] the fruit of thy womb".
- [L2] koilia (G2836, womb) stands in Luke 1:42.
- [L3] Psalm 132:11: "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne."
- [L2] beten (H990, the belly, especially the womb) is the Hebrew behind "body" in Psalm 132:11.
- [L4] The defender charges that I assume Mary is of David's house. Luke 1:35 and Luke 1:42 state Mary as the source of the flesh. Romans 1:3 states that flesh as of David's seed. So the seed of David is stated at Mary's end of the line by these rows. Luke 3:23 adds "which was [the son] of Heli". The KJV prints "[the son]" in brackets there, while Matthew's list says "begat" at every step. This is a further [L3] line.
- [L4] In the oath's own wording (Psalm 132:11) the fruit is joined to the belly. The same pair, fruit and womb, stands in Luke 1:42.

**3. Jeremiah 22:30 and the defender's answer.**
- [L1] Jeremiah 22:30: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah".
- [L1] Matthew 1:12: "Jechonias begat Salathiel". Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph".
- [L3] Haggai 2:21: "Speak to Zerubbabel, governor of Judah". Haggai 2:23: "will I take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant, the son of Shealtiel ... and will make thee as a signet".
- [L2] zera' (H2233, seed) stands in Jeremiah 22:30, and Matthew 1:12 shows Salathiel as Jechonias's begotten son.
- [L4] The defender answers with the signet and Jeremiah 18:8. Haggai gives the signet to Zerubbabel, called "governor of Judah", and that is the office Haggai names. Jeremiah 22:30 speaks of seed "sitting upon the throne of David". Matthew 1:16 gives Joseph by the "begat" line from Jechonias. So a Christ of Joseph's seed meets that bar. Luke 1:32 gives the throne to the child: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David".

## F. SPIRIT, FLESH AND THE STATUTE (against D4)

- [L1] Leviticus 19:19: "Thou shalt not let thy cattle gender with a diverse kind: thou shalt not sow thy field with mingled seed".
- [L2] kil'ayim (H3610, two heterogeneities, mingled) stands here. The verse is addressed to "Ye", about cattle, a field and a garment.
- [L1] Genesis 2:7: "the LORD God formed man [of] the dust of the ground". [L3] Genesis 2:22: "And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman".
- [L3] 1 Corinthians 15:39: "[there is] one [kind of] flesh of men". [L3] Hebrews 2:14: "he also himself likewise took part of the same".
- [L4] The defender says a Spirit-begotten child joins two kinds. Luke 1:35 states the flesh as "born of thee", the flesh of a woman, and 1 Corinthians 15:39 gives that flesh as one kind of flesh of men. So the kind stays whole. Genesis 2:7 and Genesis 2:22 show God forming flesh of that kind by his own act. The statute is addressed to Israel about cattle, seed and a garment, and it stands apart from God's forming of flesh.

**John 1:13 and the Spirit-filled child of Luke 1:15.**
- [L1] John 1:13: "Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God."
- [L2] gennaō (G1080) with ek (G1537, origin) stands here.
- [L3] Luke 1:15: "he shall be filled with the Holy Ghost, even from his mother's womb".
- [L4] The defender says "the father stands in place" in this idiom. John 1:13 sets "the will of man" outside that birth. Luke 1:15 says "filled", of a child already carried. Matthew 1:20 uses gennaō with ek of "that which is conceived", at the conceiving. The father standing in place comes from the row about John, and that row differs in its verb and its occasion.

**Luke 1:35 and the "also".**
- [L1] Luke 1:32: "shall be called the Son of the Highest". [L1] Luke 1:35: "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God".
- [L2] dio (G1352, consequently) ties the title to the coming upon and the overshadowing.
- [L3] Luke 1:31: "thou shalt conceive in thy womb".
- [L4] The title at 1:32 is the Highest's. The title at 1:35 is the Son of God's, and it follows (dio) the Holy Ghost's coming. That coming is stated before the conceiving. Acts 1:8 and Luke 9:34 are comings upon persons already born, so they do not carry this occasion.

## G. THE FATHER NAMED, AND HEBREWS 7:14 (against D5)

- [L1] Luke 2:48-49: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing", then "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"
- [L2] patēr (G3962) is "father (literally or figuratively, near or more remote)". The same word stands in both verses.
- [L3] Luke 2:48: "thy father and I".
- [L4] The child's answer to Mary's "thy father" names the Father whose business he must be about. The defender says both stand, as "thy son" and "my son" do in 1 Chronicles 28:6. In Luke 2:49 the second is the child's answer to the first.
- [L1] John 6:42: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? how is it then that he saith, I came down from heaven?"
- [L3] John 6:41: "The Jews then murmured at him".
- [L4] The speakers of "we know" are the murmurers, and they set that sonship against "I came down from heaven".

**Hebrews 7:14 and Numbers 1:18.**
- [L1] Numbers 1:18: "by the house of their fathers". [L2] bayith (H1004, house, family) and 'ab (H1, father).
- [L3] Genesis 48:5: "as Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine". [L3] Matthew 1:24-25: "took unto him his wife ... he called his name JESUS". [L1] Luke 2:4: "of the house and lineage of David".
- [L4] I grant Joseph's house (Luke 2:4). A father's house is reckoned by the father's taking and naming, in a row where the sons are "mine" by his word (Genesis 48:5), and Joseph's taking and naming are in Matthew 1:24-25. So the evidence of Juda rests on the house Joseph gave by taking and naming, together with the stock and branch of Hebrews 7:14 and Isaiah 11:1 and with Luke 1:35 and Luke 1:42.
