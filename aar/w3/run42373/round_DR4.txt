CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 4, CLOSE ROUND

# ROUND 4, MY CLOSE

I hold the standard reading: Christ had no biological father. I grant what my record grants: Joseph's house is David's (Luke 2:4), and Joseph's taking and naming give the child that house. House and begetting are different questions. Everything below keeps C1 to C6, and each is kept as the footing shows.

---

**C1: KEPT. Matthew 1:16 turns the verb of begetting at the last link.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
- [L2] gennaō (G1080) means "to procreate (properly, of the father, but by extension of the mother)". hós (G3739) is the relative pronoun "who, which". anēr (G435) is "a man, husband".
- [L3] Matthew 1:15: "And Eleazar begat Matthan; and Matthan begat Jacob". The father-to-son verb is the same one used through the line.
- [L4] Matthew 1:15 shows the line moving by "begat" from father to son. Matthew 1:16 carries it as far as Joseph, "the husband of Mary", and then moves to "was born", and that birth is "of whom". The defender's own answer grants: "The feminine "of whom" (hós) gives the mother's bearing." So the last link names Joseph by his office, husband, and gives the birth to the mother.
- [L1] Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph".
- [L4] The same pair, Joseph as husband and the birth through the mother, stands in Luke 3:23, where the sonship to Joseph is "as was supposed".

**C2: KEPT. Jeremiah 22:30 and the throne.**
- [L1] Jeremiah 22:30: "for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David".
- [L2] ʻărîyrîy (H6185) means "childless", and the verse is set by kîy (H3588), "for", as a reason of "all kinds".
- [L3] Matthew 1:12: "Jechonias begat Salathiel". Luke 1:32: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David".
- [L4] Matthew 1:11-16 runs Joseph's line through Jechonias, and Jeremiah 22:30 bars the seed of Coniah from David's throne. Luke 1:32 gives the child that throne by the Lord God's own gift. A child begotten of Joseph would be a man of Jechonias's seed, so the sentence and the gift meet only if the child's begetting is not of Joseph.

**C3: KEPT. God prepares the body.**
- [L1] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me". Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman".
- [L2] katartizō (G2675) means "to complete thoroughly, i.e. repair or adjust: fit, frame, prepare". sōma (G4983) is "the body". ginomai (G1096) is "to become (come into being)".
- [L3] Genesis 2:22: "And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman". Genesis 2:7: "the LORD God formed man [of] the dust of the ground". 1 Corinthians 15:47: "The first man [is] of the earth, earthy: the second man [is] the Lord from heaven."
- [L4] Hebrews 10:5 gives the one who speaks a body "prepared" by God. Galatians 4:4 gives the sender as God and the source of the "made" as "a woman". Genesis 2:7 and 2:22 show God framing bodies without the way of Genesis 4:1. 1 Corinthians 15:47 sets the first man "of the earth" against the second "from heaven". So the canon puts the body of Christ in the line of God's own framing.

**C4: KEPT. Joseph's mind, and the angel's reason.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:19: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just [man], and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily."
- [L2] apolyō (G630) means "to free fully ... (specially) divorce". paradeigmatizō (G3856) is "to expose to infamy".
- [L3] Deuteronomy 24:1: "then let him write her a bill of divorcement". Matthew 1:20: "while he thought on these things ... fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] gar (G1063) is "assigning a reason". ek (G1537) is "origin (the point whence action or motion proceeds)".
- [L4] Joseph, found "her husband" at Matthew 1:19, is minded to put her away. The reason (gar) the angel gives for taking her is the point whence (ek) of what is conceived: "of the Holy Ghost". The margin reads "Gr. begotten". The angel answers Joseph's thought by naming who begat.

**C5: KEPT. The father named, and the Father named.**
- [L1] Luke 2:48: "behold, thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." Luke 2:49: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"
- [L2] patēr (G3962) is "a 'father' (literally or figuratively, near or more remote)". It stands in both verses.
- [L3] Matthew 1:21: "thou shalt call his name JESUS". Matthew 1:25: "and he called his name JESUS". Numbers 1:18: "by the house of their fathers". John 6:42: "whose father and mother we know?" John 6:41: "I am the bread which came down from heaven."
- [L4] The household calls Joseph "father" (Luke 2:48), and Joseph names the child at the angel's word (Matthew 1:21, 1:25). By the law of Numbers 1:18, that gives Joseph's house, which I grant. In the same exchange the child answers with "my Father's business" (Luke 2:49). John 6:41-42 sets the town's "whose father and mother we know" over against "I came down from heaven". So the named father of the household and the Father named by the child are two, and Luke 3:23 writes the first as "supposed".

**C6: KEPT. Luke 1:34-35 and Matthew 1:18-25.**
- [L1] Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- [L2] pōs (G4459) is "in what way? ... how?" ginōskō (G1097) is "to 'know'". epercomai (G1904) is "to supervene, i.e. arrive ... (figuratively) influence". episkiazō (G1982) is "to envelop in a haze of brilliancy". dio (G1352) is "consequently".
- [L3] Luke 1:37: "For with God nothing shall be impossible." Isaiah 7:14: "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son". Matthew 1:23: "Behold, a virgin shall be with child". Genesis 24:16: "a virgin, neither had any man known her". Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son".
- [L2] synerchomai (G4905) is "to ... cohabit (conjugally)". parthenos (G3933) is "a maiden; by implication, an unmarried daughter".
- [L4] Mary asks the manner (pōs), and the angel answers the manner: the Holy Ghost comes upon her and the power of the Highest overshadows her. My record grants the same verb and power as Acts 1:8 and the overshadowing as presence. So the answer names God's presence as the way of the conceiving. Genesis 24:16 defines "a virgin" as one no man had known, and Isaiah 7:14 and Matthew 1:23 set the conceiving upon "a virgin". Matthew 1:18 puts the finding with child before the coming together (synerchomai, conjugal cohabiting). Matthew 1:25 runs the not-knowing to the birth. So no knowing stands before the finding or between the taking and the birth. The child is "of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20) and "the Son of the Highest" (Luke 1:32).

---

**Conclusion.** The rows I rest on are these:
- Matthew 1:16 gives the birth "of whom".
- Matthew 1:20 gives the begetting as "of the Holy Ghost".
- Luke 1:35 gives the Holy Ghost coming upon her and the power of the Highest overshadowing her.
- Luke 2:49 gives "my Father's business".

Joseph is husband, namer, and keeper of David's house. Christ had no biological father.
