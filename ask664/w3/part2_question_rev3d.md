THE QUESTION:
You do not judge the debate and you do not say which side is right.
Do the TAKES words take up this witness's own words and say how the position stands with them? Stating what the witness means is not enough by itself; the TAKES must say how the position agrees with that meaning. Whether it is right is not your question.
The TAKES words themselves must say it. A word such as "that" or "it" pointing to a sentence you are not shown does not say it.
A TAKES that reads this witness together with other verses and says how the position agrees with them all accounts for it; the witness is not required to state the point by itself.
TAKES words do not account for the witness when they only name it, when they speak only of another verse or another matter without joining it to this witness, or when they state the position's conclusion without taking up the witness's own words.
TAKES words that only say who spoke the witness, to whom it was addressed, or what it is about, without saying how the position agrees with its words, do not account for it.
TAKES words that rest only on what the witness does not state do not account for it; TAKES words that say scripture, read together, does not give a fact, and name the fact and the verses that do speak, account for it.
The position sentence and the layer lines are there to inform your reading of the TAKES words. They do not decide the answer, and a TAKES is not failed for citing no layer; a witness may be taken up by reading it together with other verses.
Answer on one line, in one of two forms: ACCOUNTS -- TAKES: "<words of the TAKES sentence that take up the witness>" -- WITNESS WORDS: "<the witness words that they take up>" or DOES NOT ACCOUNT -- "<the TAKES words that decide it>".
