THE FRAMEWORK IS NOT A COMBATANT. The framework is the Rules of Engagement and Rules of Interaction -- Isaiah 28:10 method, the L1-L4 layer rules, the burden rule -- and it binds BOTH combatants equally. The debate is about the TOPIC. The DEFENDER holds a chain of interlocking citations on the topic; the CHALLENGER presses an opposing view and settles it only by breaking that chain. A claim by either side that restates resolved ground with its stating verses cited is not an overpress.
This debate was CHAIN-SCOPED (Isaiah 28:10 declared): judge whether the DEFENDER'S CHAIN on the topic survived as a chain -- isolated-link objections that never engage the chain do not break it. The rule cuts both ways: an objection that is itself a floating [L4] (no exhibited layer footing) cannot break a link that exhibits its footing. Name every floating [L4] on either side before weighing it; an [L4] floats only when the block that states it exhibits fewer than two prior layers ([L1]-[L3]) for it; that one test applies to the defender and the challenger alike. FOOTING LAYERS (judge_fix8; the definition the system link-round check uses; md_notes_rulings 400: L3 is supporting verse(s) for L1): a quoted KJV verse given with its book, chapter and verse is [L1], whether or not it is tagged; a named Hebrew or Greek word given with its meaning is [L2]; any other verse quoted with its book, chapter and verse after that [L1] verse, as its support, is [L3], whether or not it is tagged, the same chapter included. A reference with no quotation adds no layer. A tag alone makes no layer, and relabelling a verse in a judgment does not change what the block shows. That no quoted row states an [L4] is never a reason to call it floating: an [L4] is an inference by definition, and that demand is the retired stating-verse test, at a collision as everywhere else. The same holds against the challenger: a challenger premise or answer whose support is that no verse states the defender's link, or an exclusion, in its own words is that retired test and is not SUPPORTED on that ground. A reading cited from the Septuagint (LXX) is a citation error for either side: name it at the citation check and give it no weight as footing. A judge that names an [L4] floating names, in the same sentence, the layers its block exhibits and the layer it lacks. An [L4] whose block exhibits two or more prior layers is footed for either side and is weighed at its collision on the rows it rests on; the witnesses held to contradict it are quoted, and they contradict it read together, line upon line; an inference admitted through the prior layers (Isaiah 28:10) is EVIDENCE, an inference that floats free of the text is not.

COUNTING RULE -- BINDING, read before grading: grade and COUNT from the two LINK ROUND blocks above, [CR3] (defender) and [DR3] (challenger), and from the close rounds as ruling 412 binds (a close-round concession of a declared link or a listed premise ENTERS the counts); an ANSWER to a pressed witness is read from any later round of the side that holds the link, its round 2, its link round or, for the defender, its CLOSE ROUND [CR4]. CLOSE ROUNDS (operator ruling): [CR4] is the defender's final defense: it answers witnesses still pressed against the defender, and an answer there counts as an answer in a later defender round; it presses nothing against the challenger, and a verse it names against the challenger is not a pressed witness. Material in [CR4] counts only as an answer to a witness already pressed against the defender: a verse [CR4] names that no earlier round of either side names is NEW IN CLOSE and NOT COUNTED (the system lists any under [CR4]), and a sentence in [CR4] that grades or presses against a challenger link is disregarded. Both close rounds keep the D numbers of the DECLARED CHAIN in [CR1] and the C numbers of the [DR3] LINK blocks; a numbering made in a close round does not govern (the system flags one). [DR4] is the challenger's final words on its own position: it answers nothing new and may not press: a verse it names that no earlier challenger round names is a new press and is NOT COUNTED (the system lists any under [DR4]); [DR4] never answers a witness pressed against the challenger. The POST-DEBATE ANALYSIS block is CONTEXT ONLY and is NOT evidence of concession -- never take a count from it, and ignore the word "unanswered" wherever it appears there. In this format the challenger close round [DR4] is structurally LAST: no defender round follows it, so the defender being silent after [DR4] is procedural and is NEVER a challenger concession. A premise counts as CONCEDED when the link round block or its own side close round states it as conceded (ruling 412), or the link round block exhibits no layer footing for it, or it is UNEXHIBITED under the rule given with CONCEDED LINKS below, whatever label its own side gave it. UNANSWERED WITNESS: a pressed witness is a verse the other side carries under PRESSED WITH: beside a premise of its listed premises (against the defender: a premise of the list that closes [DR2], the verse quoted in [DR1] or [DR2]; against the challenger: a premise of the CHALLENGER'S UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES list that closes [CR3], the verse quoted in [CR1], [CR2] or [CR3]); it is ANSWERED when any later round of the side that holds the link (for the defender: [CR2], [CR3] or [CR4]; for the challenger: [DR1], [DR2] or [DR3]) quotes it or names it by reference and shows from the text why it does not overturn the link. Re-citing the link's own footing while passing over the witness pressed against it is not an answer. ANSWER CHECK (ruling 412): an answer counts only when it meets the meaning pressed. For EVERY verse the PRESSED-WITNESS LEDGER lists under a premise of either list, write one line in reasoning after the COLLISION POINTS lines: ANSWER CHECK: <book chapter:verse> -- [<the round that holds the quoted ANSWER; with ANSWER: NONE, the answering side's round for that press>] -- MEANS: "<the meaning the pressing side claims for the verse, quoted>" -- ANSWER: "<the answer, quoted>" -- MEETS | MISSES | FAILS -- "<the row's own words that decide it>". MEETS: the answer's layers carry a meaning the link can agree with. An answer whose fit rests on a fact that the quoted 1611 rows, read together line upon line (Isaiah 28:10), do not give does not meet the press. One verse read alone does not give a contested fact. Grade it MISSES and name the fact. Quoting the rows that together give the fact, each step in a row's own words, meets it. An answer whose operative words are an absence (the verse, or the other side, names no, states no, does not name, gives no, or is silent on a thing) and that shows no fit of its own MISSES: the absence form is barred to both sides (ruling 421). MISSES: the answer quotes or names the verse but does not engage the meaning pressed. FAILS: the answer's own layers do not carry the meaning it claims, or its quotation is marked NOT COUNTED. A witness the holding side never answered is written ANSWER: NONE -- MISSES. An answer that MISSES or FAILS leaves the witness unanswered (the UNANSWERED WITNESS test; md_notes_rulings 372). VERSE CITATION (operator ruling 3): a verse counts as evidence, as footing, as a witness or as an answer only when its book, chapter and verse are stated and the words quoted are exactly the words of the KJV; a quotation may be part of a verse. A verse woven into an argument without its book, chapter and verse named, or with its words paraphrased or misquoted, is not counted; an answer given in substance without the reference named does not answer a pressed witness. A quotation the CITATION CHECK marks NOT COUNTED is not counted. A premise whose pressed witness goes unanswered is UNEXHIBITED: it counts toward CONCEDED LINKS and the standings. In a debate a non-response is never neutral. A PRESS NEEDS A PREMISE (md_notes_rulings 392): a press must have a premise behind it; a verse pressed without one is not a witness. Only a verse carried under PRESSED WITH: beside a premise of the list against a side is a pressed witness, and the UNANSWERED WITNESS rule and ruling 372 apply to it and to no other verse. A verse cited elsewhere, on a "cited in" line of the PRESSED-WITNESS LEDGER or pressed in prose against a link but carried under no listed premise's PRESSED WITH:, is not a witness: leaving it unanswered never counts, and no premise is counted UNEXHIBITED for it. The "cited in" lines are informational and are not a list of witnesses. A PRESS HAS A CAP (md_notes_rulings 393): a premise carries at most eight pressed witnesses; a verse after the eighth under the PRESSED WITH: of one premise is not a witness (ruling 392 applies to it), and the ledger lists it as over the cap. CHAIN SCOPE (Isaiah 28:10): no single verse is required to state a link or its contrary. The witnesses a side exhibits, read together line upon line, are the text's own statement, and "no single verse states it" is never a ground for STANDS or UNDERDETERMINED. A link marked STANDS, or a collision graded UNDERDETERMINED, against a pressed witness the other side exhibited (a verse a listed premise carries under PRESSED WITH:, md_notes_rulings 392) names in the same line the round that answered that witness. A pressed witness (a verse a listed premise carries under PRESSED WITH:, md_notes_rulings 392) left unanswered is a BROKEN ground; a verse under no PRESSED WITH: line, left unanswered, is no BROKEN ground. That a single verse does not state a link or its contrary is never a ground for STANDS or UNDERDETERMINED, for either side. Scripture does not contradict itself: if a link cannot agree with the meaning of a pressed witness, as the debaters show that meaning with its layers, the link fails; the verse stands, and the link is the error. You weigh only the meaning the debaters show and add none of your own. The wording of one verse alone never decides a link, and no side may demand that one verse state the point: "no verse states it", "the text nowhere narrates it" and "no witness's own words state it" are never a ground, for either side. Scripture is read precept upon precept (Isaiah 28:10): "scripture, read precept upon precept, does not give [the fact]" is a ground when it names the fact and exhibits the scripture that does speak to it, one verse or many read together, precept upon precept; no count of verses decides it.
