\#\# How to read this debate  
  
\*\*The method.\*\* Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept \*must be\* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, \*and\* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.  
  
\- \*\*\[L1\] The KJV verse:\*\* the verse itself, quoted exactly.  
\- \*\*\[L2\] Source language:\*\* the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.  
\- \*\*\[L3\] Cross-reference:\*\* what the rest of scripture says.  
\- \*\*\[L4\] Inference:\*\* a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.  
  
\*\*How the case is judged.\*\* To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.  
  
\*\*The link round.\*\* The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.  
  
\*\*Labels in the judges' texts.\*\* \[CR1\], \[CR2\] and \[CR3\] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; \[DR1\], \[DR2\] and \[DR3\] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in \[DR3\].  
  
\*\*How each judge reaches a verdict.\*\* The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.  
  
\- \*\*BROKEN:\*\* the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).  
\- \*\*PARTIAL:\*\* the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.  
\- \*\*HELD:\*\* none of the above.  
  
\*\*One rulebook.\*\* All four judgments (the three panel judges and the single judge) apply these same rules.  
  
\*\*What else decides a link.\*\* The judges' rulebook adds four rules, quoted here in its own words.  
  
\- \*\*Who-belongs verdicts.\*\* A "WHO-BELONGS VERDICT -- a claim that a person or a people-group is, or is not, of Israel, in any vocabulary", and "an identity closed from circumcision status", is "graded UNEXHIBITED whatever its footing, on either side" ("Gentiles in Paul" (DG-1) and "Seed, not blood" (DG-60)). Not in that class: "a cross-reference identification of a text's referent or structure held at \[L3\] and stated as L3 with the census disclaimed".  
\- \*\*UNEXHIBITED.\*\* A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.  
\- \*\*VOID.\*\* A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession".  
\- \*\*KEYSTONE.\*\* The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.  
  
\*\*What the standings mean.\*\* Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.  
  
\- \*\*SELF-SUPPORTED:\*\* every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).  
\- \*\*PROP-DEPENDENT:\*\* some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.  
\- \*\*COLLAPSED:\*\* more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

\#\# Round 1 - Defender (MiMo)  
  
DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING  
  
\*\*OPENING — THE DEFENDER\*\*  
  
The question is whether Christ had a biological father. The record answers yes, and it answers it from the named witnesses of the text: a man was involved, the man is named, and the promised seed is honored through that named line. What I affirm is the WHO — Joseph, the named father of the house of David. What I do not do is narrate the HOW: the text does not narrate it, and neither will I. Two things are refused together — "no man was involved," and any account of the conception's mechanics. The text gives a father, not a procedure.  
  
\*\*I. THE PROMISED SEED IS A BODILY SEED — IT PROCEEDS OUT OF THE FATHER'S BODY. \[L1\]\*\*  
  
"And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, \*\*which shall proceed out of thy bowels\*\*, and I will establish his kingdom" (2 Samuel 7:12) \[L1\] — the covenant seed is defined as coming out of the father's own body.  
  
"The LORD hath sworn in truth unto David; he will not turn from it; \*\*Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne\*\*" (Psalm 132:11) \[L1\] \[L3\] — the throne is promised to the fruit of David's body.  
  
"Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, \*\*which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh\*\*" (Romans 1:3) \[L1\] — Paul names Jesus' descent and marks it fleshward with kata sarka (G2596 + G4561) \[L2\].  
  
"Of the fruit of his loins, \*\*according to the flesh\*\*, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30) \[L1\] — Peter preaches the same anatomical seed: "fruit of his loins" is osphys, the loins-word (G3751) \[L2\].  
  
"\*\*For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda\*\*" (Hebrews 7:14) \[L1\] — the writer says the descent is \*evident\*, visible on the face of the record.  
  
"Jesus Christ \*\*of the seed of David\*\*" (2 Timothy 2:8) \[L1\], with "The sceptre shall not depart from Judah" (Genesis 49:10) \[L3\]. A seed that proceeds out of bowels, of the fruit of the body, of the fruit of the loins, "evident," "sprang out" — this is a real descent through a real father. Legal appointment produces no fruit of the body (Sight 2 \[L2\]).  
  
\*\*II. THE FATHER IS NAMED — FOUR REGISTERS OF SPEAKERS SAY IT. \[L1\]\*\*  
  
Mary herself: "Son, why hast thou thus dealt with us? behold, \*\*thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing\*\*" (Luke 2:48) \[L1\].  
  
The narrator: "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33) \[L1\]; "his parents went down to Nazareth" (Luke 2:41 — goneis, G1103, parents) \[L2\]; "(as was supposed) the son of Joseph" (Luke 3:23) \[L1\].  
  
The town: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22) \[L1\]; "Is not this Jesus, \*\*the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know\*\*?" (John 6:42) \[L1\] — the crowd names the father and claims personal knowledge of both parents.  
  
A disciple: "Jesus of Nazareth, \*\*the son of Joseph\*\*" (John 1:45) \[L1\].  
  
The angel: "\*\*Joseph, thou son of David\*\*, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" (Matthew 1:20) \[L1\] — the heavenly register names Joseph as the son of David, and Matthew 1:16 has already married him into the line: "Jacob begat \*\*Joseph the husband of Mary\*\*, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" \[L1\].  
  
\*\*III. THE RECKONING RUNS THROUGH THE FATHER'S HOUSE. \[L1\] \[L2\]\*\*  
  
"Take ye the sum of all the congregation of the children of Israel, \*\*after their families, by the house of their fathers\*\*... every male by their polls" (Numbers 1:2), and "they declared their pedigrees \*\*by the house of their fathers\*\*" (Numbers 1:18) \[L1\] — the census runs by the father's house. The inverse is exclusion: they "could not shew their father's house" were "put... as polluted" (Ezra 2:59, 61-62) \[L3\]. No wife's register is ever the reckoning.  
  
Joseph's placement is exactly that reckoning: "a man whose name was Joseph, \*\*of the house of David\*\*" (Luke 1:27 — ex oikou David, attached to the man) \[L2\]; "because \*\*he was of the house and lineage of David\*\*" (Luke 2:4) \[L1\]; "the throne of \*\*his father David\*\*" (Luke 1:32) \[L1\].  
  
Luke 3:23 "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" \[L1\]: "as was supposed" is enomizeto (G3543, V-IPI-3S) \[L2\] — the same verb form Luke uses of prayer "wont to be made" (Acts 16:13) \[L3\]. The word marks custom and report; it carries no verdict of its own. Read at the floor it stands beside Mary's "thy father" and the reckoning "by the house of their fathers."  
  
\*\*IV. "OF THE HOLY GHOST" IS COVENANT-ORIGIN LANGUAGE; "SON OF GOD" IS A COVENANT TITLE — NEITHER IS A BEGETTING STATEMENT. \[L1\] \[L2\]\*\*  
  
"That which is conceived in her is \*\*of\*\* the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20) \[L1\] is ek + Spirit (G1537 + G40) \[L2\]. The New Testament's ek + God/Spirit construction speaks of origin in the covenant sense every time — "born of God" (John 1:13) \[L1\], "born of God" (1 John 5:1) \[L1\], "is of God" (1 John 4:7) \[L3\] — spiritual origin, never biological paternity. When Paul means flesh he says so and adds kata sarka (Romans 1:3) \[L2\]; Matthew 1:20 has no such qualifier \[L2\].  
  
And "Son of God" is a title God gives to a man who has a living father: "Solomon \*\*thy son\*\*... \*\*I have chosen him to be my son\*\*, and I will be his father" (1 Chronicles 28:6) \[L1\]; "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14) \[L1\]. The begetting the apostles preach is the resurrection: "he hath raised up Jesus again; as it is also written... \*\*this day have I begotten thee\*\*" (Acts 13:33) \[L1\] \[L3\], "declared to be the Son of God with power... \*\*by the resurrection from the dead\*\*" (Romans 1:4) \[L1\]. A title that Solomon bore while David lived cannot be a paternity claim. The Son's own words settle it: he is "of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3) \[L1\] — the flesh runs to David, not to God.  
  
\*\*V. THE THREE SILENCE ROWS DO NOT SAY "NO MAN." \[L1\] \[L2\]\*\*  
  
Matthew 1:16 "of whom was born Jesus" (ex hēs egennēthē, passive) \[L2\] after thirty-nine active "begat" rows — it does not narrate the begetting; it does not deny a father. \[L1\]  
  
Matthew 1:19: Joseph "was a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily" \[L1\] — dikaios ōn, eboulēthē lathra apolysai (G1014, G2989) \[L2\]: a record of \*intent\*, with no verb of knowledge.  
  
Matthew 1:25 "knew her not \*\*till\*\* she had brought forth her firstborn son: \*\*and he called his name JESUS\*\*" \[L1\] — the naming is the father's act (cf. Luke 1:13; 1:62-63, "signs to his father, how he would have him called") \[L3\], and the verse itself marks a till. Luke 1:34 "seeing I know not a man" is andra ou ginōskō, present active indicative \[L2\] — her state at the moment of speaking, to a betrothed woman (Luke 1:27) \[L1\], not a vow of permanent virginity. Neither row denies a father. Silence is not denial.  
  
\*\*VI. WHAT THE RECORD FORBIDS, BOTH WAYS.\*\*  
  
The text forbids "no man": the seed rows name bowels, body, loins, "sprang out," "evident," and the father is named in four registers. The text also forbids my narrating the how: no verse states the mechanism, and I assert none. I affirm the WHO — a man, Joseph, the named Davidic father — and stop where the text stops. The three rows a no-father case rests on measure as: one passive verb (Matthew 1:16) read against a chorus of naming, one intent-record (Matthew 1:19), and one present-state question (Luke 1:34) — while "of the Holy Ghost" carries no kata sarka and "Son of God" is the title Solomon bore with David as his father.  
  
\*\*DECLARED CHAIN\*\*  
  
D1 — The covenant seed is a bodily seed proceeding out of the father's own body: \[L1\] 2 Samuel 7:12 "out of thy bowels"; \[L1\] Psalm 132:11 "fruit of thy body"; \[L2\] Acts 2:30 osphys "fruit of his loins, according to the flesh."  
  
D2 — Jesus is that Davidic seed, and the text calls the descent evident: \[L1\] Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" + \[L2\] ek spermatos Dauid kata sarka; \[L1\] Hebrews 7:14 "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda"; \[L3\] 2 Timothy 2:8; Genesis 49:10.  
  
D3 — The father is named as Joseph by four registers of speakers: \[L1\] Luke 2:48 "thy father"; John 6:42 "son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know"; Luke 4:22; John 1:45; Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David"; \[L3\] Matthew 1:16 "Joseph the husband of Mary."  
  
D4 — The reckoning runs by the father's house and places Joseph in David's line: \[L1\] Numbers 1:2, 1:18 "by the house of their fathers"; Luke 1:27; Luke 2:4; \[L2\] Luke 3:23 enomizeto "as was supposed" carries no verdict; \[L3\] Ezra 2:59, 61-62 (no father's house = excluded).  
  
D5 — "Of the Holy Ghost" is covenant-origin (ek + Spirit with no kata sarka), and "Son of God" is a covenant and resurrection title borne by a son with a living father: \[L1\] Matthew 1:20; 1 Chronicles 28:6; 2 Samuel 7:14; Acts 13:33; Romans 1:4; \[L2\] John 1:13 / 1 John 5:1 ek + God pattern; \[L3\] Romans 1:3's kata sarka as the flesh-marker.  
  
D6 — Therefore YES: Christ had a biological father — Joseph, the named father of the house of David; a man was involved and the Davidic seed is honored through the named line (the how being nowhere narrated by the text).

\#\# Round 1 - Challenger (Claude)  
  
CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE  
  
\*\*CHALLENGER: THE STANDARD READING\*\*  
  
\*\*Thesis.\*\* Christ had no biological human father. Mary conceived by the Holy Ghost without a man. Joseph was his legal father by betrothal, marriage and naming. The defender's chain proves a \*legal\* house, not a seed. At each link it reads past what the text says about the conception itself.  
  
\---  
  
\*\*I. THE TEXT NARRATES THE CONCEPTION, AND IT EXCLUDES JOSEPH\*\*  
  
\*\*1. Matthew 1:18 puts the conception before any union.\*\* "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, \*\*before they came together\*\*, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" \[L1\]. \*Prin ē synelthein autous\* (G4390, G4905) fixes the pregnancy before cohabitation \[L2\]. The defender calls "of the Holy Ghost" covenant-origin language. But the verse says \*heurethē en gastri echousa ek pneumatos hagiou\*, "found \*\*having in womb\*\* of the Holy Ghost" \[L2\]. The ek there names the source of a pregnancy, and "womb" is not covenant language \[L4: footing is Matt 1:18 \*en gastri echousa\* \[L2\] plus the \*prin ē synelthein\* clause \[L1\], which together mark a physical pregnancy and exclude union with Joseph\].  
  
\*\*2. Matthew 1:20 uses the begetting verb.\*\* "That which is \*\*conceived\*\* in her" is \*to … gennēthen\* (G1080) \[L2\]. This is the verb of the thirty-nine "begat" rows (Matt 1:2-16), and here its source is \*ek pneumatos hagiou\* \[L1\] \[L2\]. In Matthew the same verb that carries "Abraham begat Isaac" has, for this child, the Spirit as its source. The defender cannot say "ek never means begetting" when ek marks the source of a \*gennēthen\* \[L4: footing is the \*gennaō\* parallel across Matt 1:2-20 \[L3\] plus the ek phrase \[L2\]\].  
  
\*\*3. Matthew 1:16 breaks the pattern deliberately.\*\* The rows run "A begat B." Then: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, \*\*of whom\*\* was born Jesus" \[L1\]. The relative \*hēs\* is \*\*feminine singular\*\* \[L2\], so it can only refer to Mary. Had Joseph been the begetter, the text would read \*hou\* (masculine) or "Joseph begat Jesus." The defender says the passive "does not deny a father." But the verb switches from active to passive, the relative is feminine, and Joseph is titled "husband," not "father." Each of those changes works against Joseph's begetting \[L4: footing is the feminine \*hēs\* \[L2\] plus the contrast with the active "begat" rows \[L1\]\].  
  
\*\*4. Luke 1:34-35 gives the answer, and Joseph is absent from it.\*\* Mary asks "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" \[L1\]. The defender reads \*ginōskō\* as a mere present state. But the question only has force because the ordinary route, a man, is what she is asking about. The angel does not say "Joseph shall." He says: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: \*\*therefore\*\* also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35) \[L1\]. \*Dio kai\* (G1352) makes the Sonship the \*\*consequence of the conception\*\* \[L2\]. The defender's account has Sonship resting on the resurrection. Luke has it resting on the overshadowing \[L4: footing is Luke 1:35 "therefore" \[L1\] plus \*dio kai\* \[L2\]\].  
  
\*\*5. The sign requires it.\*\* "Behold, a virgin shall conceive" (Matt 1:23, citing Isaiah 7:14) \[L1\]. Isaiah introduces it with "The Lord himself shall give you a \*\*sign\*\*" \[L3\]. A woman bearing a child to her husband is no sign. Matthew's \*parthenos\* (G3933) is the word he applies to Mary \[L2\].

\*\*II. THE DEFENDER'S "NAMED FATHER" WITNESSES ARE ANSWERED IN THE SAME TEXTS\*\*  
  
\*\*Luke 2:48-49.\*\* The defender cites Mary's "thy father and I have sought thee." The very next verse is Jesus' answer: "How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about \*\*my Father's\*\* business?" (Luke 2:49) \[L1\]. The correction of "thy father" comes in the same scene. The defender quoted the first half and left out the answer \[L4: footing is Luke 2:48-49 as one exchange \[L1\] plus Luke 1:35 \[L3\], where Luke has already told us whose Son he is\].  
  
\*\*John 6:42.\*\* "Whose father and mother we know" is the \*\*unbelieving crowd\*\* murmuring against "I am the bread which came down from heaven" (John 6:41) \[L1\]. Their ignorance is the objection the text records. The same Gospel says "no man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son… hath declared him" (John 1:18) \[L1\] and gives Jesus "his own Father," \*patera idion\* (John 5:18) \[L2\]. Solomon was \*chosen\* (1 Chr 28:6, \*bachar\*, H977) \[L2\]. Christ's Sonship is \*idios\* \[L2\] and "only begotten," \*monogenēs\* (John 1:14; 3:16) \[L2\]. A title Solomon received by choice is not the same as that.  
  
\*\*Luke 3:23 "as was supposed."\*\* The defender says \*enomizeto\* "carries no verdict." Luke's own usage of the verb (G3543) shows otherwise \[L3\]:  
\- "Supposing him to have been in the company" (Luke 2:44). He was not.  
\- "Supposing he had been dead" (Acts 14:19). He was not.  
\- "Supposing that the prisoners had been fled" (Acts 16:27). They had not.

\*\*4. Genealogy and reckoning.\*\*  
  
The defender says both genealogies "run through Joseph... both reckon him as the channel of the line," and concedes that Joseph has two fathers: Jacob (Matt 1:16) and Heli (Luke 3:23). Two fathers cannot both be biological \[L1\]. So the text itself shows a reckoning that does not coincide with begetting.  
  
His claim that "No wife's register is ever the reckoning" fails on Ezra 2:61 ("took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai... and was called after their name") \[L1\]. He tries to rescue it by noting that the man was expelled. That shows the exception was costly. It does not show there was no exception.  
  
Jeremiah 22:30 also remains. "No man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David" \[L1\], and Joseph's line runs through Jechonias (Matt 1:11-12) \[L1\]. His answer is Haggai 2:23, but Zerubbabel was a governor and never sat on David's throne (Hag 1:1) \[L3\]. Jeremiah 18:7-8 is a general principle. No verse applies it to Jeconiah. His answer to the curse rests on that extra step \[L4\].  
  
\*\*5. Gal 4:4 and Hebrews 7:14.\*\*  
  
Galatians 4:4 says "made of a woman" (\*genomenon ek gynaikos\*) \[L2\]. Paul names the woman and the Law and omits any man. The defender cites Job 14:1 and Matthew 11:11 to show "born of a woman" is an idiom for mortality. But those verses say "born of a woman" (\*gennētos gynaikōn\*), not \*genomenon\*. Paul writes \*genomenon\*, the same verb he uses in Romans 1:3 (\*genomenou ek spermatos Dauid\*) \[L2\]. In Romans 1:3 the \*kata sarka\* is paired with \*kata pneuma hagiōsynēs\* (Rom 1:4), so the flesh comes from the human side and the Spirit from God \[L1\] \[L2\].  
  
Hebrews 7:14 says "sprang out of Juda." The word is \*anatetalken\* ("arisen," as of a star) \[L2\], and its context (7:13-14) is about \*tribe\*. It says nothing of paternity \[L1\].

The defender's reply draws on \*almah\* and the margin. Matthew, writing under inspiration, renders it \*parthenos\* (G3933) in Matthew 1:23 \[L1\] \[L2\]. He cites the passage as fulfilled \*in this conception\*: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled" (Matt 1:22) \[L1\]. The sign is "the LORD himself shall give you a sign" \[L1\].  
  
A pregnancy by the betrothed husband would fulfil nothing. The defender never addresses Matthew's own use of \*parthenos\*.  
  
\*\*7. "WHO not HOW" fails.\*\*  
  
"Biological father" means the begetter. The defender affirms that the seed "proceeds out of the father's own body" (D1) and also declines to say how. But that D1 claim is the mechanism claim. A man's bodily seed is exactly what the defender says Joseph supplied.  
  
The text does speak to the matter: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee... therefore" (Luke 1:35) \[L1\]. The defender says it only states "a pregnancy, a plan, a state, and a sequence." But Matthew 1:18 states pregnancy before union ("before they came together"), 1:20 states the Spirit as source of the begotten one, and 1:25 states that no union preceded the birth \[L1\]. Together these amount to "no man."  
  
\*\*CLOSING STATEMENT\*\*  
  
The defender needs six steps: that \*ek pneumatos\* is not biological, that "supposed" is custom, that David's loins require Joseph's, that Luke 3:23 and Matthew 1:16 are both biological, that Jeremiah 22:30 does not apply, and that \*parthenos\* is not a virgin. Each fails on the cited text. On the text, Christ had no biological human father. Joseph was legal father, and the Holy Ghost was the author of the conception.
