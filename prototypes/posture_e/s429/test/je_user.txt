RULE E-ANS (an answer to a pressed verse). Decide FIRST what the right grade is: MEETS only when the answer shows, in its own words, how the verse fits the answering side's case, or why the stated meaning does not follow from what the verse says. MISSES when the answer rests on an absence (it says only that the verse, or the other side, names no, states no, does not name, gives no, or is silent on something) without a fit of its own, or when it does not address the stated meaning. Then for each judge's grade: AGREE when it matches the right grade, DISAGREE when it does not.
RULE E-VOID (a listed premise ruled VOID). A premise is VOID only when its charge says the opponent's quoted words state something they do not state (a negation, a universal, or another claim beyond the quote). The charge is the premise's own words before its quote (for example "The inference that ..."), read against the words of that quote. Set aside only a clause saying what the premise props ("which props ...") or a sentence naming the objection it serves ("The objection it props is ..."); those are not part of the charge. AGREE with the VOID ruling when the charge goes beyond its quote; DISAGREE when it does not.
RULE E-COL (a collision line: FORCED, or LINK BROKEN). A link is broken only when the answer's own quoted words cannot agree with the meaning pressed, as the line quotes them; it is not broken because no verse states it, or because of an absence. AGREE when the quoted words show the answer cannot agree with the meaning; DISAGREE when they do carry a meaning the link can agree with, or the line rests on an absence or on a demand for a stating verse.
RULE E-SYN (a sentence of the synthesis saying a side withdrew, conceded, granted, dropped or narrowed something). AGREE when that side's own sentences given you give up that same thing at the same scope; DISAGREE when they do not, or give up less (for example, "no row I cite" is not "no biblical text").

RULE E-ANS (an answer to a pressed verse). Decide FIRST what the right grade is: MEETS only when the answer shows, in its own words, how the verse fits the answering side's case, or why the stated meaning does not follow from what the verse says. MISSES when the answer rests on an absence (it says only that the verse, or the other side, names no, states no, does not name, gives no, or is silent on something) without a fit of its own, or when it does not address the stated meaning. Then for each judge's grade: AGREE when it matches the right grade, DISAGREE when it does not.
RULE E-VOID (a listed premise ruled VOID). A premise is VOID only when its charge says the opponent's quoted words state something they do not state (a negation, a universal, or another claim beyond the quote). The charge is the premise's own words before its quote (for example "The inference that ..."), read against the words of that quote. Set aside only a clause saying what the premise props ("which props ...") or a sentence naming the objection it serves ("The objection it props is ..."); those are not part of the charge. AGREE with the VOID ruling when the charge goes beyond its quote; DISAGREE when it does not.
RULE E-COL (a collision line: FORCED, or LINK BROKEN). A link is broken only when the answer's own quoted words cannot agree with the meaning pressed, as the line quotes them; it is not broken because no verse states it, or because of an absence. AGREE when the quoted words show the answer cannot agree with the meaning; DISAGREE when they do carry a meaning the link can agree with, or the line rests on an absence or on a demand for a stating verse.
RULE E-SYN (a sentence of the synthesis saying a side withdrew, conceded, granted, dropped or narrowed something). AGREE when that side's own sentences given you give up that same thing at the same scope; DISAGREE when they do not, or give up less (for example, "no row I cite" is not "no biblical text").
