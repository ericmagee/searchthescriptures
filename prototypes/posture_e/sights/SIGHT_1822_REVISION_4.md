Luke 1:13 has the same shape as Matthew 1:20. An angel speaks to the husband ("Fear not, Zacharias"; "Joseph ... fear not"), names "thy wife", and names the son. The conception follows in the house of the husband.

L2. The words for "conceive":
- harah (H2029): "to be (or become) pregnant, conceive (literally or figuratively)".
- syllambanō (G4815, Luke 1:24, 31, 36; 2:21): "to clasp, i.e. seize ... specially, to conceive (literally or figuratively)".
- At Matthew 1:20 the KJV "conceived" is gennaō (G1080, margin "Gr. begotten"): "to procreate (properly, of the father, but by extension of the mother)".
- At Romans 9:10, "conceived by one, [even] by our father Isaac" is koitē (G2845): "a couch; by extension, cohabitation; by implication, the male sperm". The text names the conception as the father's.

Romans 9:10: "And not only [this]; but when Rebecca also had conceived by one, [even] by our father Isaac;"

L1-L2. ROMANS 9:10 AND MATTHEW 1:20 ARE TWO FORMS, NOT ONE.

Romans 9:10's "by one" is ek (G1537) joined to koite, and it names a man: "our father Isaac".

Matthew 1:20's form is gennao (G1080) + ek (G1537) + pneuma (G4151). Scripture uses that same form for every one born of the Spirit, and it gives spirit, not flesh:
- John 3:5: "Jesus answered, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and [of] the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God."
- John 3:6: "That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit."
- John 3:8: "The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit."
- John 1:13 has gennao + ek with God: "Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God."

So "of the Holy Ghost" at Matthew 1:20 is scripture's own form of spiritual origin (Sight 10). Romans 9:10's ek, joined to koite, names the man. Neither row cancels the other (DG-59).

THE WHOLE COUNT. The canon (66 books) has 60 rows with "conceive", "conceived" or "conceiving". The 4 Apocrypha rows (2 Esdras 13:16; Sirach 16:20; Wisdom 7:15; Judith 9:9) are all of the mind.
1. 33 rows narrate a woman's conception:
   - Genesis 4:1, 4:17, 16:4, 16:5, 21:2, 25:21, 29:32-35, 30:5, 30:7, 30:17, 30:19, 30:23, 38:3-5, 38:18;

   In every one, the man is named in the row or its chain. The act itself ("knew", "went in unto", "came in unto", "lay with") is stated in 21 of them. In two of them the woman was not his wife:
   - Genesis 38:18, Judah and Tamar: "came in unto her, and she conceived by him";
   - 2 Samuel 11:5, David and Bathsheba ("he lay with her", 11:4).
   At Isaiah 8:3 the text says "I went unto the prophetess [went: Heb. approached]".
2. 7 rows announce a conception and do not tell the means: Isaiah 7:14; Judges 13:3, 5, 7; Luke 1:31; Luke 2:21; Matthew 1:20. In Judges 13 the woman is "his wife" (13:2, Manoah). In Matthew 1:20 she is "Mary thy wife".
3. 4 rows are of cattle (Genesis 30:38, 39, 41; 31:10). At 31:10, "the rams which leaped upon the cattle".
4. 2 rows are laws for a man's wife or a woman (Leviticus 12:2; Numbers 5:28).
5. 4 rows name a mother who conceived (Job 3:3; Psalm 51:5; Song of Solomon 3:4; Hosea 2:5).
6. 10 rows are of the mind (Numbers 11:12; Job 15:35; Psalm 7:14; Isaiah 33:11; 59:4; 59:13; Jeremiah 49:30; Ezekiel 38:10 margin; Acts 5:4; James 1:15).

No row in the canon gives a woman's conception without a man.

## Change C: new [L4] point (5)

(5) In the 21 rows where scripture states how a woman conceived, a man knew her, went in unto her, came in unto her, or lay with her. In all 33 rows that narrate a conception, the man is named in the row or its chain. Where it says the LORD gave the conception, the husband stands in the same row or its chain:
- "when he went in unto her, the LORD gave her conception" (Ruth 4:13);
- "Elkanah knew Hannah his wife; and the LORD remembered her" (1 Samuel 1:19).

The LORD's hand named at a conception never removes the husband. Matthew 1:20 carries both in one row: God's part ("of the Holy Ghost") and the husband ("Joseph, thou son of David ... Mary thy wife"). Read precept upon precept (Isaiah 28:10), "conceived ... of the Holy Ghost" says the child was conceived. It does not say the Holy Ghost impregnated her. That reading has no row in scripture. Luke 1:34, "seeing I know not a man", is a present state, not a vow (Sight 32). Luke 1:35: "And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- Its "come upon" (eperchomai, G1904) is the word of Acts 1:8: "But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth. [power...: or, the power of the Holy Ghost coming upon you]". That is power for witness, not a begetting (Sight 8).
- Its "overshadow" (episkiazo, G1982) is the word of Matthew 17:5: "While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him." That is the presence cloud (Sight 9).
- Its "born of thee" is gennao (G1080) + ek (G1537) (kjv_word_map), and it names the mother as the one the child is born of.
Luke 1:35 is therefore no row of a conception without a man.
