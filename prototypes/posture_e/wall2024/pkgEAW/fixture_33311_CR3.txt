Answers to the pressed verses.

Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." The press: it addresses a son of David as husband, and grounds the command to take her.
- I grant the address and the ground. The ground is the clause "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] gennaō is the verb of that clause, and the clause gives its source as the Holy Ghost.
- [L3] Matthew 1:18 gives the same source at the finding, "before they came together."
- [L4] The "for" supplies a reason, and the reason it supplies is a source named for the begetting. That fits the link.

1 John 5:1 "Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is born of God," and 1 John 4:7 "every one that loveth is born of God." The press: "born of God" is said of men whose fathers begat them.
- I grant it. I do not rest the link on the phrase "born of God" alone.
- [L2] gennaō in 1 John is the figurative sense, "to regenerate". It is said of a believer already living.
- [L1] Matthew 1:20 says "that which is conceived in her", and the object is a thing conceived in the womb, not a living believer.
- [L3] John 3:6: "That which is born of the Spirit is spirit." Regeneration of a living man is a second birth. Matthew 1:20 and 1:18 speak of the conceiving.

Luke 1:34 "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" The press: the present indicative states her condition at that hour.
- I grant it. [L2] ginōskō (G1097): "to know".
- [L1] Luke 1:31: "thou shalt conceive in thy womb", which is the thing she asks about. She asks "How shall this be" about that conceiving, in the condition she names.
- [L4] The reply of Luke 1:35 begins with the Holy Ghost coming upon her. The reply names the means of that conceiving as divine.

Luke 1:35 "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." The press: it ends in a naming that adds "also" to the title of verse 32, with kaleō (G2564, to call, to name).
- I grant that kaleō is in both verses.
- [L4] The "therefore" stands in verse 35, after the two clauses of verse 35 about the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest. The nearest antecedent is those two clauses. "Also" adds the ground to the title already given.

Luke 1:32 "He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David." The press: the title and David's throne are given before verse 34.
- I grant the order.
- [L2] patēr (G3962) means "father (near or more remote)". The father-word this verse states is "his father David", and David is the remote father the word covers.
- [L2] didōmi (G1325): "to give". The Lord God is the giver of the throne in this verse.
- [L3] Hebrews 7:10: "For he was yet in the loins of his father, when Melchisedec met him." The same word patēr is used there of an ancestor generations back.
- [L4] Luke 1:32 with patēr and Hebrews 7:10: the verse names David as "his father", the throne comes by God's gift, and the word "father" is used in Scripture of a remote ancestor. So the verse's father-word is met by David as ancestor and God as giver, and it gives no ground for a human begetter at the last step.

Acts 1:8 "ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you." The press: the coming upon is empowerment of men already born.
- I grant it.
- [L2] epercomai is the same verb, and dynamis (G1411, "force... miraculous power") is in both verses: "the power of the Highest" in Luke 1:35 and "power" in Acts 1:8.
- [L4] In Acts 1:8 the coming upon of the Holy Ghost produces a result in the person: power received. In Luke 1:35 the result named after the same coming is "that holy thing which shall be born of thee". The verb is the same and the result is named in the verse.
