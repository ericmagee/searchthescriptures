DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

# Opening: Christ had no biological father

**Definition.** By "biological father" I mean a man whose seed brought about the child's conception. I defend the standard reading: Jesus Christ was conceived in Mary without any man, by the Holy Ghost. Joseph was his supposed father and legal guardian, not his begetter.

## 1. Mary was a virgin who had not come together with any man

- [L1] Luke 1:27: "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin's name was Mary."
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] The Greek behind "came together" is *sunerchomai* (G4905, to come together, here the union of husband and wife). The conception is placed before that union. The Greek behind "virgin" is *parthenos* (G3933, a maiden who has not known a man).
- [L1] Luke 1:34: "Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"
- [L2] *Ginosko* (G1097, to know) is used here for sexual knowledge. *Aner* (G435, a man, a male person) shows that Mary points to the absence of any male.
- [L3] Isaiah 7:14: "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." The Hebrew is *almah* (H5959), which the KJV renders "virgin". Matthew 1:23 quotes it: "Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son."
- [L4] Mary says she knows no man, and the text places her pregnancy before any coming together with Joseph. Her pregnancy therefore has no male source.

## 2. The stated source of the conception is the Holy Ghost

- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] The preposition is *ek* (G1537, out of, from, denoting origin or source). The participle is *gennao* (G1080, to beget or conceive), in the passive. The thing begotten in her has its origin *out of* the Holy Ghost.
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- [L2] *Episkiazo* (G1982, to overshadow) is the word for the working of divine power. *Dia touto* ("therefore", G1223 with G3778) makes the title follow from the manner of conception.
- [L4] Luke 1:34 asks how this can be without a man. Luke 1:35 answers with the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest, and names no man. The source of the child is stated and it is not a man.

## 3. Joseph's place is legal and supposed, not generative

- [L1] Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph."
- [L2] *Enomizeto* (G3543, was supposed, was reckoned by custom or opinion) marks "son of Joseph" as the common opinion and not the fact.
- [L1] Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
- [L2] The chain of "begat" ends at Joseph. The birth is "of whom" (*ex hes*, G1537 with a feminine singular relative), which refers to Mary alone and not to Joseph.
- [L1] Matthew 1:19: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily."
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife."
- [L1] Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son."
- [L2] *Ginosko* (G1097, to know) is again the idiom of marital union. It is negated up to the birth.
- [L4] Joseph is minded to put away a wife found with child, and the angel must tell him to take her. He is told the source (Matthew 1:20) and does not know it by himself. Matthew 1:25 says he had no union with her until the birth. Joseph therefore stands as husband and guardian, not as begetter.

## 4. The pattern of Scripture agrees

- [L1] Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his son, made of a woman, made under the law."
- [L2] *Ginomai* (G1096, to become, to be made) is used of the child, with the woman named as the one he came of. No man is named in the generation.
- [L3] Genesis 3:15: "between thy seed and her seed." The Hebrew *zera* (H2233, seed) is credited to the woman.
- [L3] Jeremiah 31:22: "the LORD hath created a new thing in the earth, A woman shall compass a man." The Hebrew *bara* (H1254, to create) marks the thing as a new work of the LORD and not an ordinary generation.
- [L3] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me."
- [L1] Luke 2:48: "Behold, thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing."
- [L1] Luke 2:49: "wist ye not that I must be about my father's business?"
- [L4] When Mary says "thy father" of Joseph, the child answers by pointing to "my father's business" (Luke 2:49), which is not Joseph's. The child corrects the phrase used for Joseph.

## 5. Anticipated objections

- [L1] John 6:42: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" The speakers are the crowd, "they said". [L3] Luke 3:23, "as was supposed", shows that this was common opinion.
- [L1] Luke 2:48 "thy father" is a form of address within the household. [L3] The reply at Luke 2:49 names another father.
- [L1] Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh." [L2] *Sperma* (G4690, seed) is here descent, a line. [L3] Matthew 1:16 has the birth "of whom", Mary. [L3] Luke 1:32 gives the throne of "his father David", which is the line of descent and not a claim of a begetting by Joseph.

DECLARED CHAIN

1. Mary was a virgin, had not come together with Joseph, and knew no man: [L1] Luke 1:27, Matthew 1:18, Luke 1:34; [L2] *sunerchomai* (to come together), *ginosko* (to know); [L3] Isaiah 7:14 with Matthew 1:23, *almah* (virgin).
2. The conception is stated to be of the Holy Ghost: [L1] Matthew 1:20, Luke 1:35; [L2] *ek* (out of, source) and *episkiazo* (to overshadow); [L3] Luke 1:34 asks how without a man, and Luke 1:35 answers with divine power.
3. Joseph is the supposed father and husband, not the begetter: [L1] Luke 3:23, Matthew 1:16, Matthew 1:25; [L2] *enomizeto* (was supposed), *ex hes* (feminine "of whom"); [L3] Matthew 1:19-20 on Joseph's decision to put her away and the angel's explanation.
4. The wider Scripture pattern puts the woman and the new creation at the source: [L1] Galatians 4:4; [L2] *ginomai* (to be made), *bara* (to create); [L3] Genesis 3:15, Jeremiah 31:22, Hebrews 10:5, Luke 2:48-49.
5. Christ had no biological father: no man's seed was the source of his conception; the Holy Ghost is stated as the source, and the child was born of Mary.
