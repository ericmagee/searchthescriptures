# Challenge: the seed is David's "according to the flesh," out of the loins, and the man the rows name is Joseph

My reading, footed: [L1] Romans 1:3, "which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh." [L2] The Greek behind "seed" is *spermatos* (G4690, seed), and "according to the flesh" is *kata sarka* (by the flesh). [L3] Acts 2:30, "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne." [L4] The rows place the child's flesh in David's loins and carry it by the flesh. They set it down the house of the fathers, and the man that line reaches is "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20). I press each of the defender's links from these rows and aim at the keystone, D5.

---

## Against LINK D5 (keystone): the seed rows require loins

**PRESS.** [L1] 2 Samuel 7:12: "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels." [L2] The Hebrew behind "seed" is *zera'* (H2233, seed, posterity), and behind "bowels" is *me'eh* (H4578, inward parts, bowels). [L3] Psalm 132:11: "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne. [body: Heb. belly]". [L3] Hebrews 7:10: "For he was yet in the loins of his father." [L2] There "loins" is *osphys* (G3751, the loins, procreative power) and "father" is *patēr* (G3962, father). The same *osphys* stands in Acts 2:30, "the fruit of his loins."

[L4] The promise is seed that proceeds out of a man's body (2 Samuel 7:12) and is carried in the loins of the father (Hebrews 7:10). Acts 2:30 says Christ is that fruit "according to the flesh." So a seed of David "according to the flesh" runs through loins. D5's "no man's seed was the source of his conception" sets itself against the very words "seed" and "loins" that Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30 apply to Christ.

**PRESS (the tribe).** [L1] Hebrews 7:14: "For [it is] evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood." [L3] Numbers 1:18: "they declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers." [L2] "House of their fathers" is *bayith* (H1004, house, family) with *'ab* (H1, father). [L3] Ezra 2:62 shows the reverse case. Priests "called after the name" of a wife's house (Ezra 2:61) "sought their register... but they were not found: therefore were they, as polluted, put from the priesthood." [L4] The law declares a tribe by the house of the fathers. Ezra 2:61-62 struck those reckoned through a wife's house. So Hebrews 7:14's "evident" stands on a father's house of Judah, and the rows give that house to Joseph: "he was of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4).

**The defender's Romans 1:3 objection, met at its means.** The defender says *sperma* "is here descent, a line." The verse fixes the line itself: [L1] Romans 1:3, "according to the flesh." [L2] *kata sarka* (by the flesh). [L3] Hebrews 7:5 gives the same descent its organ: "though they come out of the loins of Abraham." [L4] Descent in these rows is a line carried in loins, by the flesh, so "a line" leads back to the loins of Acts 2:30.

## Against LINK D3: the father is named by every voice

**PRESS (Luke 3:23 at the word).** [L1] Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph." [L2] The Greek is *enomizeto* (G3543, to do by custom, to deem, "be wont"). [L3] Acts 16:13 has the same verb form: "where prayer was wont to be made." [L4] At Acts 16:13 the word names what was customary and true, a place of prayer. The word itself gives the custom and leaves the verdict to the rows around it. Luke's rows around it give Joseph as father: [L1] Luke 2:48, "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Mary). [L3] Luke 2:27, "the parents brought in the child Jesus" (the narrator). [L3] Luke 2:41, "his parents went to Jerusalem every year." [L3] John 1:45, "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (a disciple). [L3] Luke 4:22, "Is not this Joseph's son?" (the town).

**PRESS (Matthew 1:16).** [L1] Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus." [L2] *ex hēs* (out of whom, feminine) gives the mother of the birth. [L3] Genesis 4:25 shows the same pattern: Eve says "God... hath appointed me another seed," in the verse that opens "Adam knew his wife again." [L4] A birth stated of the mother sits in Genesis 4:25 beside the husband's knowing. In Matthew 1:16 the same verse names "Joseph the husband of Mary." It fits the link.

**PRESS (Matthew 1:19, the defender's means: "He is told the source and does not know it by himself").** [L1] Matthew 1:19: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just [man], and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." [L2] *dikaios ōn* (being just) and *mē thelōn paradeigmatisai* (not willing to make a public example) are the two grounds Matthew states. *eboulēthē* (he was minded) states intent. [L3] The angel then answers that intent: Matthew 1:20, "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost. [conceived: Gr. begotten]." [L2] "For" is *gar* (for, because). It grounds the command "fear not to take." [L4] The angel addresses Joseph by his Davidic house and tells him to keep his wife. The text's verb for Joseph is a decision ("minded"), and its two stated grounds are justice and mercy. The angel's word joins the child to David's house through the man he names "son of David."

**PRESS (the father's act).** [L1] Matthew 1:21: "thou shalt call his name JESUS". This is done at Matthew 1:25: "he called his name JESUS." [L3] Luke 1:13: "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son, and thou shalt call his name John." [L3] Luke 1:62: "they made signs to his father, how he would have him called." [L4] In Luke 1:13 and 1:62 the angel gives the naming to the father. Matthew 1:21 gives the naming to Joseph in the same words.

**The timing rows, read as written.** [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child." [L3] Matthew 1:24: "took unto him his wife." [L3] Deuteronomy 20:7: "hath betrothed a wife, and hath not taken her." [L1] Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." [L4] Matthew dates the finding before the taking (1:18), then the taking (1:24), then a bounded abstinence "till" the birth (1:25). The coming together of 1:18 is the taking of 1:24, the step Deuteronomy 20:7 names.

The union, stated as the rows carry it:
- [L1] Joseph is "Joseph her husband" (Matthew 1:19), and Mary is "Mary thy wife" (Matthew 1:20).
- [L3] The law calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:24).
- [L3] The canon's stated means of begetting is "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1).
- [L2] *zera'* and *spermatos* (seed) require loins, as shown above.
- [L4] Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal, and the child is of his loins, "the seed of David according to the flesh."

## Against LINK D2: "of the Holy Ghost" and Luke 1:35 at the word

**PRESS (Matthew 1:20 whole).** [L1] Matthew 1:20: "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost. [conceived: Gr. begotten]." [L2] "Of" is *ek* (out of, from). [L3] John 1:13 uses the same *ek*: "Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." [L3] 1 John 5:1: "Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is born of God. [is born: Gr. has been born]." [L4] Believers are "born of God" by the same *ek* and the same begetting verb (*gennaō*, to beget). Each of them has a fleshly father. So *ek* with God or the Spirit marks a covenant origin, and the verse that carries it names "Joseph, thou son of David" and "Mary thy wife." [L3] John 3:6 keeps the two kinds apart: "That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit."

**PRESS (Luke 1:35 at the word).** The defender's [L2] names *dia touto*. The Greek of Luke 1:35 is *dio kai* (G1352, "wherefore," with *kai*, "also"). The *dio kai* ("therefore") in v.35 connects back to v.32, not forward to biological divine paternity. [L1] Luke 1:32: "He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David." That title and "the throne of his father David" are given before Mary asks "How shall this be" in Luke 1:34. [L3] 1 Chronicles 28:6 joins the two in one verse: "Solomon thy son... for I have chosen him [to be] my son, and I will be his father." [L3] 2 Samuel 7:14 says of the seed "out of thy bowels" (7:12): "I will be his father, and he shall be my son." [L4] In 1 Chronicles 28:6 and 2 Samuel 7:12-14, God's "my son" stands beside a seed from David's own body. Luke 1:35's "shall be called the Son of God" carries that same covenant title.

**PRESS (the angel's verbs).** [L1] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee."
- [L2] "Come upon" is *eperchomai* (G1904, to come upon). [L3] Acts 1:8 uses it of the apostles: "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me... [power...: or, the power of the Holy Ghost coming upon you]."
- [L2] "Overshadow" is *episkiazō* (G1982, to overshadow). [L3] Luke 9:34: "there came a cloud, and overshadowed them." [L3] Acts 5:15: "the shadow of Peter passing by might overshadow some of them."

[L4] At Acts 1:8, Luke 9:34 and Acts 5:15 these two verbs carry empowerment and presence. The same verbs in Luke 1:35 carry the same meaning, joined to the covenant title of Luke 1:32.

## Against LINK D1: the virgin, the sign, and "I know not"

**PRESS (Luke 1:34's tense).** [L1] Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" [L2] *andra ou ginōskō* (I know not a man) is present indicative and states her state when the angel spoke. [L3] Luke 1:31 sets the conception ahead of her: "thou shalt conceive in thy womb." [L3] Luke 1:27 gives her as "a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David." [L4] A present "know not," a future "shalt conceive," and an espousal to a man of David's house fit together. Her words give her state that day, and Matthew 1:24-25 gives the order that follows.

**PRESS (Isaiah 7:14 in its own frame).** [L1] Isaiah 7:14: "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel. [shall call: or, thou, O virgin, shalt call]." [L2] The Hebrew is *almah* (H5959, maid, damsel). The KJV itself prints that word "the maid" at Exodus 2:8, "the damsels" at Psalm 68:25 and "a man with a maid" at Proverbs 30:19. [L3] Isaiah 7:16 sets the sign's clock: "before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken." [L3] Isaiah 8:3-4: "And I went unto the prophetess; and she conceived, and bare a son... [went: Heb. approached]... For before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother." [L3] Isaiah 8:8: "O Immanuel." [L4] Inside Isaiah, the sign's conception follows the prophet's approach to the prophetess. Its child cries "My father, and my mother" (8:4). So the verse the defender lays under D1 carries a father within its own frame. Matthew 1:22-23 applies that sign as "fulfilled," and the fulfilment fits the pattern of its source.
