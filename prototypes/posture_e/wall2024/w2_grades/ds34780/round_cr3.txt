# LINK ROUND

## LINK 5 [premise 1]: SUPPORTED

**Footing for the link.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost. [conceived: Gr. begotten]". [L2] *gennaō* (to beget) is the word the margin gives, so the begetting is "of the Holy Ghost".
- [L3] Matthew 1:18: "she was found with child of the Holy Ghost".
- [L3] Luke 1:34-35: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" is answered by "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- [L4] Mary names the knowing of a man as the means in question (Luke 1:34). The angel gives the Holy Ghost's coming upon her instead (Luke 1:35). Matthew 1:20 puts the begetting verb on the Holy Ghost. So the stated source is the Holy Ghost and the bearer is Mary: "born of thee".

**Answers to the pressed verses.**

1. 2 Samuel 7:12: "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels".
   - I grant that the promise runs by descent from David's body. [L2] *zera'* (H2233, seed, posterity) is the word, and *yatsa'* (H3318, to go or proceed out).
   - The promise is "after thee", and the next verse says "I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever" (2 Samuel 7:13). The seed is a line over generations, not only the next step.
   - [L3] Hebrews 7:10: "For he was yet in the loins of his father". [L2] *patēr* (G3962, father, near or more remote) shows the loins-word reaches a remote ancestor. [L3] Matthew 3:9: "We have Abraham to [our] father".
   - [L3] Genesis 3:15: "between thy seed and her seed". [L3] Genesis 4:25: "hath appointed me another seed instead of Abel". [L2] *zera'* is the word also for the woman's seed.
   - [L4] The seed-word is spoken of a woman's offspring in Genesis 3:15 and 4:25. Remote descent is carried by loins-language in Hebrews 7:10. So 2 Samuel 7:12 does not make a man the last step.

2. Psalms 132:11: "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne".
   - [L2] *karpos* (G2590, fruit) is the word in Acts 2:30 and in Luke 1:42: "blessed [is] the fruit of thy womb".
   - [L4] On the same word, the fruit sworn to David is called the fruit of Mary's womb.

3. Acts 2:30: "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne".
   - [L2] *osphys* (G3751, loin, internally procreative power) is the word. [L2] *anistēmi* (G450, to raise up) has God as the one who raises up, since "God had sworn with an oath" stands in the same verse.
   - [L3] Hebrews 7:5: "though they come out of the loins of Abraham", said of Levi's sons.
   - [L4] The loins-word names David's line, and the raising-up is God's act.

4. Romans 1:3: "which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh".
   - [L2] *sperma* (G4690, seed, "including the male sperm; by implication, offspring") covers offspring.
   - [L2] *ginomai* (G1096, to become, be made) is the verb. It is not the verb "begat" of Matthew 1:2.
   - [L3] Matthew 1:16.

5. Matthew 1:2: "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob".
   - I grant the begetting chain runs to Joseph.
   - [L3] Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus". The verb "begat" has Joseph as its object. The clause about Jesus uses "was born" and names Mary.
   - [L4] On Matthew 1:2 and 1:16, the chain of "begat" ends at Joseph and the birth is placed in Mary.

6. Hebrews 7:5 and 7:10 (quoted above) give descent by loins to a remote forefather. I grant that. [L4] They name the ancestral line, and with [L2] *patēr* (near or more remote) they do not make a man the final parent.

7. Genesis 4:1: "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived".
   - I grant that this is the stated means for Adam.
   - Luke 1:34 uses the same knowing: "seeing I know not a man". Luke 1:35 answers with "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee".
   - [L4] In Mary's case the means asked about is set aside, and the Holy Ghost's coming is stated in its place.

## LINK 5 [premise 2]: SUPPORTED

**Footing.**
- [L1] Romans 9:5: "Whose [are] the fathers, and of whom as concerning the flesh Christ [came]". [L2] *sarx* (G4561, flesh, "the means of kindred").
- [L3] Romans 9:3: "my kinsmen according to the flesh".
- [L4] In Romans 9:3 "according to the flesh" names kinship of many people. In Romans 9:5 "of whom" refers to the people who own "the fathers". So "according to the flesh" marks descent-kinship with that people, and I take it as real kinship with David. The clause names no begetter.

**Answers to the pressed verses.**

1. Romans 9:5. The clause has two parts joined by "and": "Whose are the fathers" and "of whom ... Christ came". The first says the fathers belong to them. The second says Christ came of them. The flesh-kinship is with the people.
2. Galatians 3:29: "then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise". I grant the two qualifiers mark two senses. I hold the flesh-sense in Romans 1:3 as David-kinship, with [L2] *ginomai* (to be made) as its verb.
3. Luke 1:42: "blessed [is] the fruit of thy womb". [L2] *karpos* (fruit) is the word of Acts 2:30. I grant the mother's fruit stands beside the loins. [L3] Luke 1:34-35 gives the Holy Ghost, in place of the knowing of Genesis 4:1, as the means.
4. Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus". The sentence names Joseph as "the husband of Mary". "Begat" has Joseph as its object, and "was born" goes with Mary.
5. Hebrews 2:14: "he also himself likewise took part of the same". "The same" is "flesh and blood". The likeness stated is flesh and blood.
6. Hebrews 2:17: "in all things it behoved him to be made like unto [his] brethren, that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest". The purpose clause that follows is priesthood and mercy.
7. Hebrews 4:15: "was in all points tempted like as [we are, yet] without sin". "In all points" is stated with its limit, "without sin". [L3] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me". [L4] Hebrews 2:17 has its purpose clause, Hebrews 4:15 has "without sin", and Hebrews 10:5 has the body "prepared". So the likeness is of nature and office, with sin as the stated limit, and not an identical manner of origin.
8. Hebrews 2:16: "he took on [him] the seed of Abraham [margin: taketh hold of ... the seed of Abraham]". [L2] *epilambanomai* (G1949, to take hold of). I hold he is of Abraham's seed, as in Romans 1:3. The verse names whom he takes hold of, set over against angels.

## LINK 3 [premise 3]: SUPPORTED

**Footing.**
- [L1] Hebrews 7:14: "For [it is] evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda". [L2] *prodēlos* (G4271, plain before all men) is "evident". [L2] *anatellō* (G393, to arise) is "sprang".
- [L3] Luke 2:4-5: "because he was of the house and lineage of David" and "To be taxed with Mary his espoused wife, being great with child [taxed: or, enrolled]".
- [L3] Matthew 1:20, 1:24: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" and "took unto him his wife".
- [L4] The enrolment recorded Mary and the child under Joseph's house (Luke 2:4-5). He came into that house by the taking (Matthew 1:24). The tribe is "evident" (*prodēlos*) on that record.

**Answers to the pressed verses.**

1. Hebrews 7:14 (quoted above). [L2] *anatellō* glosses as "rise, spring (up)". It is a verb of arising, and the tribe named is Judah.
2. Numbers 1:18: "they declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers". The subject of "declared" is the congregation. I grant that [L2] *yalad* (H3205, to beget, show lineage, declare pedigrees) is the verb. The rule of reckoning is "by the house of their fathers".
3. Numbers 1:2: "by the house of their fathers". The reckoning runs by house. [L3] Luke 2:4 gives Joseph's house.
4. Ezra 2:59: "they could not shew their father's house, and their seed, whether they [were] of Israel". I grant the margin gives "pedigree" for "seed". The thing to show is the father's house and the pedigree. Luke 2:4 gives the house.
5. Ezra 2:62: "These sought their register [among] those that were reckoned by genealogy, but they were not found". The register is an enrolment by pedigree, and Luke 2:5 records the enrolment of Mary and the child with Joseph.
6. Ezra 2:61: "which took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai the Gileadite, and was called after their name". [L2] *laqach* (H3947, to take). A man who took a wife was "called after their name". [L3] Matthew 1:24: "and took unto him his wife". [L4] Ezra 2:61 shows that a name in the register came by taking, and Matthew 1:24 is Joseph's taking.
7. Luke 2:4 (quoted above). I grant the Davidic house the register reaches is Joseph's, and Matthew 1:21 gives the child his place in it. [L3] Matthew 1:21: "thou shalt call his name JESUS".

## LINK 1: SUPPORTED

**Footing.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost".
- [L1] Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son".
- [L3] Luke 1:27 and 1:34. At the angel's word she is "a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph" and says "I know not a man". [L3] Luke 1:35 gives the Holy Ghost's coming.
- [L4] Before conception she is virgin and not knowing (Luke 1:27, 1:34). The conception is the Holy Ghost's (Luke 1:35, Matthew 1:18). From the taking to the birth there was no knowing (Matthew 1:25). So no window holds Joseph's seed.

**Answers to the pressed verses.**

1. Matthew 1:19: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just [man], and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily".
   - I grant "her husband" names the espoused man.
   - [L3] Deuteronomy 22:23: "If a damsel [that is] a virgin be betrothed unto an husband". The law's own words call the betrothed damsel "a virgin" and name "an husband" in the same verse.
   - The press means a husband who knew himself the father would have no cause to put her away. Joseph was "minded to put her away privily" on finding her with child. Matthew 1:20 meets that with "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost". The "for" gives the Holy Ghost as the ground of the command.
   - [L4] If Joseph's seed were the source, the angel's reason, that the conceived is "of the Holy Ghost", could not be given as the ground for taking her.
2. Matthew 1:20 is quoted in the footing and the point above.
3. Deuteronomy 22:24: "because he hath humbled his neighbour's wife". I grant the law calls a betrothed damsel a wife. The same law calls her "a virgin" (22:23).
4. Deuteronomy 20:7: "that hath betrothed a wife, and hath not taken her? let him go and return unto his house". I granted the taking is the step into the house, and [L3] Matthew 1:24 "took unto him his wife" is that step. [L3] Matthew 1:18's "before they came together" puts the finding with child before the step.
5. Matthew 1:24 is as above.
6. Matthew 1:25: "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son". I grant it bounds the abstinence after the taking. With Matthew 1:18 it covers the time from the finding to the birth, so that no knowing is placed before the birth.
7. Genesis 4:1: "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived". [L3] Luke 1:34-35 states for Mary the means other than knowing.
8. Luke 1:31: "thou shalt conceive in thy womb". I grant it is future to the angel's word. [L3] Luke 1:27 and 1:34 give her as virgin and "I know not a man" at that word, and Luke 1:35 gives the Holy Ghost as the means.

## LINK 2: NARROWED TO: "The conception is the Holy Ghost's begetting: Matthew 1:20 names the conceived thing 'begotten' of the Holy Ghost, and Luke 1:35 ties 'the Son of God' to the Holy Ghost's coming upon Mary."

I drop any claim that Psalms 2:7's "this day" is the conception.

**Footing.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost. [conceived: Gr. begotten]". [L2] *gennaō* (to beget) is the margin's word.
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God". [L2] *dio kai* (wherefore, with also) joins the title to the Holy Ghost's coming.
- [L3] John 1:14: "the glory as of the only begotten of the Father". It is said of the one who "dwelt among us".
- [L4] Matthew 1:20 and Luke 1:35 state a begetting of the Holy Ghost at the conception and tie the Sonship title to it. The other begetting-verses do not remove them.

**Answers to the pressed verses.**

1. Psalms 2:7: "Thou [art] my Son; this day have I begotten thee". I grant it is applied at the occasions below. Matthew 1:20 is a further statement of begetting. The two stand together.
2. Acts 13:33: "in that he hath raised up Jesus again". I grant "begotten" is set there at the raising. It does not remove Matthew 1:20.
3. Hebrews 5:5: "Thou art my Son, to day have I begotten thee". I grant the priesthood occasion.
4. Hebrews 1:5: "And again, I will be to him a Father, and he shall be to me a Son?" I grant it pairs with [L3] 2 Samuel 7:14: "I will be his father, and he shall be my son". The words "I will be to him a Father" name God as father of the son.
5. Romans 1:4: "declared [to be] the Son of God with power ... by the resurrection from the dead [declared: Gr. determined]". The verb is "declared" or "determined". [L3] Romans 1:3 gives "made" (*ginomai*) of the flesh. The Sonship is called Son of the Highest before the resurrection in Luke 1:32.
6. Luke 1:32: "shall be called the Son of the Highest". [L4] Luke 1:35 joins back to it with "therefore also", and its cause is the Holy Ghost's coming.
7. Acts 1:8: "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you". [L2] *eperchomai* (G1904, to come upon) is the verb in both Acts 1:8 and Luke 1:35. In Luke 1:35 what follows the coming is "that holy thing which shall be born of thee". [L4] In Acts 1:8 the coming is on the disciples, who receive power. In Luke 1:35 it is on Mary and results in the thing born.
8. 1 Chronicles 28:6: "I have chosen him [to be] my son, and I will be his father". Of Solomon, the verb is "chosen". In Luke 1:35 the Holy Ghost's coming upon is given as the cause.

## LINK 3 [premise 6]: SUPPORTED

**Footing.**
- [L1] Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph". The verse itself carries "as was supposed".
- [L3] Luke 2:48-50: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing", then "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?", then "And they understood not the saying which he spake unto them".
- [L4] Luke 3:23 marks Joseph's sonship as a supposition. The child's reply in Luke 2:49 sets "my Father's" against Mary's "thy father". The source is given in Luke 1:35 and Matthew 1:20.

**Answers to the pressed verses.**

1. Acts 16:13: "where prayer was wont to be made". I grant the same verb form names a true custom. [L2] *nomizō* (G3543, to do by usage, be wont). [L4] The verb names what usage holds. Luke 3:23 sets usage in "as was supposed", and usage is the household reckoning of Luke 2:4-5.
2. Luke 2:48 (quoted above) is answered by Luke 2:49.
3. Luke 2:27: "when the parents brought in the child Jesus". [L2] *goneus* (G1118, a parent). I grant the narrator's word. [L3] The same narrator writes "Joseph and his mother" in Luke 2:33 and in Luke 2:43. [L3] The angel says "take the young child and his mother" in Matthew 2:13.
4. Luke 2:41: "Now his parents went to Jerusalem every year". As above.
5. John 1:45: "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph". This is Philip's voice. [L3] John 6:42: "how is it then that he saith, I came down from heaven?"
6. Luke 4:22: "Is not this Joseph's son?" This is the town's question. [L3] John 6:42 answers with the claim "I came down from heaven".
7. Matthew 1:21: "thou shalt call his name JESUS". I grant it gives Joseph the naming act. [L3] Genesis 4:25 states: "she bare a son, and called his name Seth". [L3] Luke 1:60 states: "his mother answered and said, Not [so]; but he shall be called John". [L4] Eve in Genesis 4:25 and Elisabeth in Luke 1:60 each perform the naming act. So in Matthew 1:21 the naming act marks Joseph's place as the one who gives the child his name in the household, as Luke 1:60 shows a mother also giving a name. The act is a household role, shared by both parents in these rows.
8. Luke 1:62: "And they made signs to his father, how he would have him called". Luke 1:60 states the mother naming in the same account: "Not [so]; but he shall be called John". The naming is put to both the father and the mother in Luke 1, and so it is a household act.
