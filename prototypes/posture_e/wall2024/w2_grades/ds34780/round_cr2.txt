DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

# Round 2: the defender answers

I answer the challenger's presses in order, beginning with the question put to me. Where a press lands, I say so.

## The question: how is it evident (Hebrews 7:14) that our Lord sprang out of Juda?

- [L1] Hebrews 7:14: "For [it is] evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda."
- [L2] *prodēlos* (G4271, plain before all men) is the word for "evident". *anatetalken* (G393 *anatellō*, to arise, spring up) is the verb for "sprang". It is the verb of a rising, and it is not *gennaō* (G1080, to beget).
- [L1] Luke 2:4-5: Joseph went up to Bethlehem "because he was of the house and lineage of David", "To be taxed with Mary his espoused wife".
- [L3] Matthew 1:20-21 and 1:24-25: Joseph is told "take unto thee Mary thy wife", he "took unto him his wife", and "he called his name JESUS".
- [L3] Numbers 1:18: "they declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers."
- [L4] The evidence is the public register. The law enrolled by the house of the father (Numbers 1:18). Joseph, of David's house (Luke 2:4), took the mother and named the child (Matthew 1:24-25). The child was therefore enrolled in Joseph's house, and "supposed" (Luke 3:23) is the word for that reckoning. The register records the house and does not report who begat. This route is evident to all (G4271) and requires no seed from Joseph.
- [L3] Ezra 2:61 shows a man "called after their name" by a house he entered through a wife, which is a naming by house and not a begetting. Ezra 2:62 names the failure: "they were not found". The failure is a register not found (*matsa'*, H4672, to find), not a rule excluding a wife's line.
- [L3] Luke 1:42, to Mary: "blessed is the fruit of thy womb." The word *karpos* (G2590, fruit) is the word of Acts 2:30, "the fruit of his loins". The promise-word "fruit" is used of Mary's womb in Luke's own text.
- [L1] Hebrews 7:16: "Who is made, not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life." The same chapter that says "evident" sets the priesthood apart from a carnal rule of descent.

## Link D5: Romans 1:3, Acts 2:30, 2 Samuel 7:12, Hebrews 7:10

- [L1] Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh." [L2] The verb is *ginomai* (G1096, to become, be made). The noun is *sperma* (G4690, something sown, "seed (including the male sperm); by implication, offspring"). *Sarx* (G4561, flesh) carries "the means of kindred".
- [L3] Romans 9:5: "of whom as concerning the flesh Christ came" is said of Israel as a people. [L4] "According to the flesh" in these rows marks kinship by descent, and it does not prove which parent carries it.
- [L3] Genesis 3:15 gives "her seed" to the woman (H2233, seed, posterity). Galatians 3:29: "then are ye Abraham's seed". [L4] "Seed" is used where no loins are in view, so the word alone does not set a man's act as the source.
- [L1] Hebrews 7:10: "For he was yet in the loins of his father." The "father" is Abraham, the forefather of Levi, as the verse's own context shows (Hebrews 7:5, "the loins of Abraham"). [L3] *patēr* (G3962) means a remote forefather in Matthew 3:9, "We have Abraham to our father", and Luke 1:73, "our father Abraham". [L4] Loins in Hebrews 7:10 are an ancestor's across generations. They are not a claim that the last man in a line begat. The press makes loins an immediate father's act, and the verse does not use them that way.
- I withdraw the term "legal guardian" as an office word. What the rows give is this: Joseph took his wife (Matthew 1:24), named the child (Matthew 1:25), and the child "was subject unto them" (Luke 2:51). I keep "supposed father" ([L1] Luke 3:23).
- Luke 1:32, "his father David": [L3] the forefather sense of *patēr* above means the phrase names David as ancestor. I do not rest it on Mary's house.

## Link D3

**Luke 3:23.** [L2] *enomizeto* (G3543, was reckoned, deemed by custom) is the same form as Acts 16:13, "was wont". I grant that it states what was customary. [L4] Luke writes it in his own narrator's voice inside a genealogy and attaches it to "son of Joseph". That is a reckoning marked as a reckoning, and the same Luke gave the source at Luke 1:35.

**The voices.** [L1] Luke 2:48 (Mary), [L3] Luke 4:22 and John 1:45 (the town, Philip), and John 6:42 (the crowd) are speakers' reckonings. [L3] John 6:42 sits beside the same crowd's "whose father and mother we know", the very supposition Luke 3:23 marks. Luke's own narration names the two of them differently. [L3] Luke 2:33: "Joseph and his mother marvelled". [L3] Luke 2:43: "Joseph and his mother knew not of it." [L3] The angel's phrase is "the young child and his mother" (Matthew 2:13). [L4] The narrator and the angel never say "his father and mother" in these verses. The speakers do. The "parents" of Luke 2:27 and 2:41 name the household that carried him.

**Matthew 1:16 against Genesis 4:25.** I grant Eve's "another seed" sits beside "Adam knew his wife". [L1] Matthew 1:25 puts the opposite beside the mother: "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son". [L3] Matthew 1:2-16 repeats "begat" at every link, and at the last the verb changes to "of whom was born Jesus" (the feminine *ex hēs*, G1537 with a feminine relative). [L4] The sentence shifts the verb at the one place where the husband is named "the husband of Mary".

**Matthew 1:19-20 at its means.** The press says the angel joins the child to David's house through "Joseph, thou son of David". [L1] Matthew 1:20: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." [L2] *gar* (G1063, for) gives the ground of "fear not to take". The ground stated is the source, "of the Holy Ghost". It is not the child's being Joseph's. [L2] *gennēthen* (G1080, begotten, as the KJV margin has it) is the verb: the thing begotten in her is of the Holy Ghost. [L4] The address "son of David" and the command to take her fit the legal route above (Matthew 1:24-25), and the reason that Matthew gives for the command is the Holy Ghost.

**The naming.** [L3] Luke 1:13 and 1:62 give the naming to the father. [L1] Luke 1:31 gives it to Mary: "shalt call his name JESUS". [L3] Luke 1:60: "his mother answered and said, Not so; but he shall be called John". [L3] Genesis 4:25: Eve "called his name Seth". [L4] The naming is done by either parent, so it does not mark Joseph as the begetter. It marks him, in Matthew 1:21-25, as the man who received the child.

**The timing.** [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child". I grant the challenger's reading that this "coming together" is the taking of Matthew 1:24. [L1] Matthew 1:25 then sets "knew her not till she had brought forth". [L4] On the challenger's own order, before the taking there was no coming together (1:18), and from the taking to the birth there was no knowing (1:25). That covers the whole pregnancy, so Joseph's seed has no place in the order. The "wife" title (Matthew 1:20; Deuteronomy 22:24) is a betrothal title and gives no knowing.

## Link D2

**Luke 1:35 at the word.** I correct my earlier Greek. The word is *dio kai* (G1352, wherefore, with *kai*, also), not *dia touto*. [L1] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." [L4] The ground sits in the same verse, in the clause just before the "therefore". The "also" adds this ground to the title already given in Luke 1:32. It does not remove the ground.

**The answer to the "how".** [L1] Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" [L4] The angel answers with the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest, acting on her person. [L3] 1 Chronicles 28:6 grounds the sonship in "I have chosen him", and Luke 1:35 grounds it in what comes "upon thee". The grounds differ, so the covenant-title parallel does not cover Luke 1:35.

**The verbs.** [L2] *eperchomai* (G1904, to come upon) and *episkiazō* (G1982, to overshadow) mean presence and power at Acts 1:8, Luke 9:34 and Acts 5:15. I grant that. [L4] In Luke 1:35 the angel answers the "how" of a conception with these verbs, and the product is "that holy thing which shall be born of thee". The power acts upon Mary and produces the child, which Acts 1:8 does not do.

**John 1:13, 1 John 5:1, John 3:6.** [L1] John 1:13 itself rules out three human sources: "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." [L3] John 3:4 shows a believer's "born of God" is a second birth, after a first from the womb. [L1] Matthew 1:20 names the first begetting: "that which is conceived in her". [L4] A believer's divine begetting follows a natural one. Christ's divine begetting is the conception itself. I grant that *ek* (G1537, out of) is the same preposition in both places.

## Link D1

**Luke 1:34 tense.** [L4] The "how" is Mary's response to Luke 1:31, "thou shalt conceive in thy womb". She is already espoused (Luke 1:27) and her marriage is expected, yet she asks "how". Her question treats the conception as one to come while she knows no man. The angel does not reply with "after the taking". He names the Holy Ghost. [L1] Matthew 1:18 confirms the order: found with child "before they came together".

**Isaiah 7:14.** The KJV renders *almah* (H5959) "virgin" at Isaiah 7:14 and, in the quotation, *parthenos* (G3933) "virgin" at Matthew 1:23. [L3] Genesis 24:43, "when the virgin cometh forth", uses the same Hebrew word of Rebekah, whom Genesis 24:16 calls "a virgin, neither had any man known her". [L4] The KJV's "maid" and "damsels" at Exodus 2:8 and Psalm 68:25 are also unmarried girls. The press against "virgin" from the KJV's own range does not displace the KJV's own word at Isaiah 7:14.

**Isaiah 8:3-4.** [L1] Isaiah 8:3 gives "Call his name Mahershalalhashbaz". The Immanuel child of Isaiah 7:14 has a different name, and the prophetess is a different woman. [L4] The text names two children by two names. Matthew 1:22-23 applies the "virgin" of 7:14, and Matthew 1:18 places Mary "before they came together".

## Link D4

I narrow D4. It supports the chain and does not carry it alone.

- **Galatians 4:4.** I grant that "born of women" (Matthew 11:11) is said of John, who had a father (Luke 1:13). [L1] Galatians 4:4 reads "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman". [L2] *ginomai* (G1096) here is "be made", and the Son is "sent forth" before he is "made of a woman". [L3] John 1:14: "the Word was made flesh". D4 fits the exclusion and does not state it. D1 and D2 carry the exclusion.
- **Genesis 3:15.** I grant that Genesis 4:25 sets "another seed" beside the knowing. The "her seed" of Genesis 3:15 fits and is not an independent proof.
- **Jeremiah 31:22.** I withdraw it from the chain. Its context (Jeremiah 31:21, "O virgin of Israel") is the nation's return, and I do not rest a premise on it.
- **Hebrews 10:5.** [L2] *katērtisō* (G2675, to fit, prepare) is God's act on the body. [L3] Hebrews 2:17, "in all things... like unto [his] brethren", is limited by Hebrews 4:15, "yet without sin". I take it as support only.
- **Luke 2:48-49.** I narrow "corrects" to "contrasts". [L1] Mary says "thy father", and the child answers "my Father's business". [L1] Luke 2:50 notes "they understood not the saying which he spake unto them". [L3] 1 Chronicles 28:6 shows a covenant father beside a fleshly one, and I grant that this reading fits Luke 2:49. [L4] The reversal of the possessive shows whose Father the child claims. It does not alone exclude Joseph, and I do not claim that it does.

## Where the case stands

- Matthew 1:18, 1:20 and 1:25 with Luke 1:34-35 carry the exclusion (D1, D2). The press reading "know not a man" as a present state fails to explain why the angel answers with the Holy Ghost.
- D3 and Hebrews 7:14 run on the register route, from Numbers 1:18, Luke 2:4-5 and Matthew 1:24-25.
- The Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30 presses fail on the word "seed" (G4690, offspring) and on "fruit" at Luke 1:42.
- I withdraw "legal guardian" as an office term and withdraw Jeremiah 31:22. D4 is narrowed to a supporting link.
