CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, CLOSING AND PREMISE LIST

# Close of the challenge: the seed is David's "according to the flesh," and the begetting line the rows draw reaches Joseph

The defender has made real repairs. He corrected *dia touto* to *dio kai*, withdrew "legal guardian," withdrew Jeremiah 31:22, and narrowed Luke 2:49 to "contrasts." What remains is the keystone (D5), the register route (D3), the timing (D1) and the conception-as-divine-begetting (D2). I answer each at its stated meaning.

---

## 1. Hebrews 7:14: the register the defender appeals to is a register of begetting

The defender's means: "The register records the house and does not report who begat."

- [L1] Numbers 1:18: "they declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers."
- [L2] The verb behind "declared their pedigrees" is *yalad* (H3205). Its gloss is "to bear young; causatively, to beget; ... specifically, to show lineage; ... declare pedigrees."
- [L3] Ezra 2:59: "they could not shew their father's house, and their seed, whether they [were] of Israel." The 1611 margin at "seed" reads "or, pedigree."
- [L3] Ezra 2:62: "These sought their register [among] those that were reckoned by genealogy, but they were not found." [L2] "Reckoned by genealogy" is *yachas* (H3187, "to sprout; ... to enroll by pedigree").
- [L4] The law's own word for the register is the begetting verb (*yalad*). Its margin sets "seed" equal to "pedigree" (Ezra 2:59). Its word for enrollment is "to sprout" (*yachas*). The register the defender calls "evident" is a register of seed by the father's house.

Hebrews 7:14's verb fits this.
- [L2] *anatellō* (G393, "to (cause to) arise ... spring (up)") is the verb of a sprout, the same picture as *yachas*.
- [L3] The same chapter measures descent by loins: Hebrews 7:5, "though they come out of the loins of Abraham."
- [L4] So "sprang out of Juda" stands on the chapter's loins-descent. The tribe's register reached the child through the house of Joseph, "because he was of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4).

Hebrews 7:16's "law of a carnal commandment" names the priesthood's rule, as its verse says: "Who is made, not after the law of a carnal commandment" (Hebrews 7:16), set against Hebrews 7:14's tribe "of which ... Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood." That verse governs the priesthood, and the tribe stays "evident."

## 2. Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30: "according to the flesh" travels with the fathers

The defender's means: "According to the flesh" marks kinship "and it does not prove which parent carries it."

- [L1] Romans 9:5, which he cites: "Whose [are] the fathers, and of whom as concerning the flesh Christ [came]." [L2] *sarx* (G4561, flesh, "the means of kindred").
- [L4] In the defender's own witness, "as concerning the flesh" is joined to "the fathers" in the same clause.
- [L3] Acts 2:30 names the carrier organ: "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh." [L2] *osphys* (G3751, "procreative power").
- [L3] Matthew 1:2: "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob." [L4] Hebrews 7:10's remote "loins of his father" reach a descendant through a chain of begetters, and Matthew 1:2-16 is that chain.

The defender wrote in his first round: "The chain of 'begat' ends at Joseph." The loins of David therefore reach Joseph by the rows the defender himself reads.

On his other witnesses:
- Galatians 3:29, "if ye [be] Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed ... according to the promise," carries the promise qualifier. Romans 1:3 carries the flesh qualifier, *kata sarka*. The two qualifiers mark two senses.
- Luke 1:42, "blessed [is] the fruit of thy womb," names the mother's fruit. It stands beside the father's loins as Genesis 4:1 stands: "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived." Womb and loins are both in that one verse.

## 3. The timing: the defender granted that "came together" is the taking into the house

The defender wrote: "before the taking there was no coming together (1:18), and from the taking to the birth there was no knowing (1:25)."

- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child."
- [L3] Deuteronomy 20:7: "that hath betrothed a wife, and hath not taken her? let him go and return unto his house." [L2] *synerchomai* (to come together). Granted as the taking, it is the step into the house.
- [L3] Deuteronomy 22:24 counts the betrothed damsel "his neighbour's wife" before any taking.
- [L1] Matthew 1:19-20: "Joseph her husband," "Mary thy wife."
- [L4] Matthew 1:18 places the conception in the espousal, "espoused to Joseph," before the house step. Matthew 1:25 covers the time "till she had brought forth," after the taking. The espousal window is the one in which the law already calls them husband and wife. Genesis 4:1 gives the begetting of a wife by knowing.

Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal, footed on Matthew 1:19, Matthew 1:20, Deuteronomy 22:24 and Matthew 1:25's "till." The defender's "covers the whole pregnancy" reads 1:18's house step as the knowing he had just granted it was not.

## 4. Luke 1:35 and the begetting the New Testament gives to God

The defender's means: "Christ's divine begetting is the conception itself."

- [L1] Psalm 2:7: "Thou [art] my Son; this day have I begotten thee." The New Testament gives this at three occasions:
  - Acts 13:33: "in that he hath raised up Jesus again; as it is also written in the second psalm, Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee."
  - Hebrews 5:5: "Christ glorified not himself to be made an high priest; but he that said unto him, Thou art my Son, to day have I begotten thee."
  - Hebrews 1:5: "Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee? And again, I will be to him a Father, and he shall be to me a Son?"
- [L3] Romans 1:4: "declared [to be] the Son of God with power ... by the resurrection from the dead: [declared: Gr. determined]."
- [L4] God's "begotten" of the Son is placed at the resurrection, at the priesthood, and beside 2 Samuel 7:14's covenant "Father ... Son." Romans 1:3-4 sets the flesh "of the seed of David" and the Sonship "by the resurrection" side by side.

On "the power acts upon Mary and produces the child":
- [L1] Luke 1:35's clause after "therefore also" is "shall be called the Son of God." [L2] "Called" is *klēthēsetai* (shall be called), the naming verb of Luke 1:32, "shall be called the Son of the Highest."
- [L3] Acts 1:8: "ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses." [L3] 1 Samuel 16:13: "the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward."
- [L4] In those rows the coming-upon yields an office: witnesses, and a king. In Luke 1:35 it yields a title, "called," joined by *dio kai* back to "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32).

On John 1:13, which the defender cites ("not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God"): it is said of believers, who have fathers. So "of God" birth language stands beside a fleshly father in the very verse the defender offers.

## 5. Luke 3:23 and the voices

- [L2] *enomizeto* (G3543), which the defender grants is the "was wont" of Acts 16:13, names a custom.
- [L3] The narrator's own word at Luke 2:27 and 2:41 is "parents." [L2] *goneus* (G1118, a parent).
- [L3] Matthew 1:21 gives Joseph the father's act: "thou shalt call his name JESUS." Luke 1:62 shows the same act: "they made signs to his father, how he would have him called."
- [L4] Luke's narrator writes "parents," and Mary writes "thy father" (Luke 2:48). Luke 3:23's custom-word stands inside that voice.
