# CLOSE ROUND: DEFENDER

**Status of the chain.** D1 kept. D2 kept. D3 kept. D4 NARROWED TO: Galatians 4:4, Genesis 3:15 and Hebrews 10:5. D5 kept.

## The hinge: the order of the finding and the knowing (D1, D3)

The challenger's whole chain at C3 rests on one placement: "Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal", between the angel's word and the finding. The challenger's own grants exclude that placement.

- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] *synerchomai* (G4905, to come together) is the verb that sits after the finding.
- [L3] Matthew 1:24-25: "and took unto him his wife: And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son". [L2] *ginōskō* (G1097, to know) is "knew".
- [L4] The challenger granted in C3 that "the finding comes before the coming together." Matthew 1:18 puts the finding before any coming together, so no knowing lies before the finding. Matthew 1:24-25 covers the next period: after the taking, "knew her not till she had brought forth". The espousal is covered by 1:18 and the household by 1:25. The knowing the challenger needs fits in neither.

## Answers to the witnesses pressed in the link round

ANSWER: Matthew 1:25 -- D3 -- "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS."
- [L2] *ginōskō* (to know), in the past tense "knew her not", states the not-knowing up to the birth.
- [L3] Matthew 1:18 puts the finding before the coming together.
- [L4] The challenger reads "till" as limiting abstinence to the pregnancy after the taking. "Till" marks the end of the not-knowing at the birth. It cannot move a knowing back before the finding, because Matthew 1:18 says "before they came together, she was found with child". Verses 18 and 25 together hold that no knowing preceded the birth.

If Joseph had known himself the father, that clause would not answer the fear. It answers it only as a source other than Joseph. The challenger's C3 grants the "for" grounds the command to take her, and that ground is the Holy Ghost as source.

## D5

Christ had no biological father. No man's seed was the source of his conception. The Holy Ghost is stated as the source (Matthew 1:18, 1:20, Luke 1:35), Mary was, before the finding and until the birth, not known (Matthew 1:18, 1:25), and the child was born of Mary (Matthew 1:16, Luke 1:35, Galatians 4:4). The challenger's reading needs a knowing before the finding, and Matthew 1:18 says the finding came "before they came together".
