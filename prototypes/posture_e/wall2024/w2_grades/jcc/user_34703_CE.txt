# OPENING: Christ had no biological father

**Definition.** By "biological father" I mean a man whose seed begot the child. I affirm that Jesus had a mother, Mary, and no such father. [L4] The case rests on what the KJV states about the conception.

---

## I. Matthew 1 gives the source of the conception, and it is not Joseph

**1. Before they came together.**
[L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
[L2] "Came together" is *synelthein* (G4905, to come together), and "of" is *ek* (G1537, out of, as source). The pregnancy is dated before the pair came together, and its source is stated as the Holy Ghost.
[L3] Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." "Knew" is *ginōskō* (G1097), here used of marital knowledge. The text states that Joseph did not know her until the birth.
[L4] The verses give two footings. Matthew 1:18 puts the pregnancy before union with Joseph, and Matthew 1:25 says he did not know her until she had brought forth. Joseph's seed therefore cannot be the source of the child, so he was not the biological father.

**2. The angel names the source.**
[L1] Matthew 1:20: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
[L2] "Conceived" is *gennēthen* (G1080, passive of *gennaō*, to beget or bring forth). It stands with *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of the Holy Ghost). The thing begotten in her is out of the Holy Ghost.
[L3] Matthew 1:19: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily."
[L4] A husband who knew himself to be the father would have had no reason to put her away. Joseph's resolve (Matthew 1:19) is explained by the angel's correction (Matthew 1:20), which assigns the source to the Holy Ghost and not to him.

**3. The genealogy breaks its own pattern at the end.**
[L1] Matthew 1:2: "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob." Matthew 1:16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
[L2] Every father in the list is *egennēsen* (G1080, active, "begat"). In Matthew 1:16 the verb becomes *egennēthē* (G1080, passive, "was born"), with *ex hēs* (out of whom), a feminine singular relative. Joseph is called "the husband of Mary" and is not said to have begotten Jesus.
[L4] The chain is a repeated active "begat" down the line, then a switch to the passive "was born" with a feminine "of whom". This puts the birth out of Mary alone and not out of Joseph.

## Conclusion of the close

- **D1, D2 and D4** are footed on rows that state the source and the means: Matthew 1:18, 1:20, Luke 1:34-35.
- **D3** stands on Matthew 1:3 ("Judas begat Phares and Zara of Thamar"), where the father is subject of the active "begat" though the mother is named. In Matthew 1:16 the line turns: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus".

- **D6** stands on Luke 1:35 ("born of thee") and Matthew 1:20, with the father-texts a speaker's address (Luke 2:48), a supposition (Luke 3:23) or the crowd's question (John 6:42).
