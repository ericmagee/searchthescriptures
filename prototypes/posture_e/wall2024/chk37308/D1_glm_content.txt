# DEFENDER'S OPENING — "Did Christ have a biological father?"

**The reading I defend:** No. The record states the source of the child found in Mary — the Holy Ghost; it states that Joseph's coming together with her and his knowing of her stand wholly after the birth; it states the means of the conception — the Holy Ghost coming upon her and the power of the Highest overshadowing her; and it carries Joseph's tie to the child in the register of "the husband of Mary" and "as was supposed." I argue from rows that affirm these things. I require no verse to state my conclusion in its own words, and I charge no one with lacking such a verse (DG-61).

**PRELIMINARY — the register of "father" in this record.**
[L1] Matthew 1:2: "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob; and Jacob begat Judas and his brethren;" — [L2] ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, aorist active of γεννάω, gennao, G1080, "to beget") stands in Matthew 1:2 behind "begat." [L3] Matthew 1:16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
[L4] FOOTING: [L1] Matthew 1:2 + [L2] ἐγέννησεν (G1080); [L3] Matthew 1:16. CHAIN: from Abraham to Joseph the record carries fatherhood in one stated register — a man begets a son; a father in this record is a man whose begetting stands behind the child. The question before us is whose begetting the record states as standing behind this child. The links below answer from stated rows.

---

## LINK I — The stated source of the child in Mary: "of the Holy Ghost"

[L1] Matthew 1:18: "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."

[L2] ἐκ (ek, G1537, "out of, from") stands in Matthew 1:18: "with child of the Holy Ghost" carries ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (ek pneumatos hagiou), "having [it] out of the Holy Ghost" — ἐκ with the genitive is the Greek marker of source and origin.

[L3] Matthew 1:20: "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." [L2] γεννηθέν (to gennēthen, neuter passive participle of γεννάω, gennao, G1080, "to beget, bring forth") stands in Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her" is τὸ ἐν αὐτῇ γεννηθέν, "the thing begotten in her." [L2] γάρ (gar, G1063, "for, because") stands in Matthew 1:20: the stated reason Joseph may take her is that the thing begotten in her is out of the Holy Ghost.

[L4] FOOTING: [L1] Matthew 1:18 + [L2] ἐκ (G1537); [L3] Matthew 1:20 + [L2] γεννηθέν (G1080). CHAIN: Matthew 1:18 states the child in the womb as ἐκ the Holy Ghost; Matthew 1:20 states the thing begotten in her as ἐκ the Holy Ghost, and makes that the stated ground of the angel's charge to Joseph. Precept upon precept: the record states the source of the begetting twice, and both times the source it states is the Holy Ghost. This link rests on what the two verses say.

## LINK II — The stated order: the child before the union; no knowing until the birth

[L1] Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS."

[L2] ἐγίνωσκεν (eginōsken, imperfect of γινώσκω, ginōskō, G1097, "to know," the knowing of man and wife) stands in Matthew 1:25 behind "knew"; with οὐκ (ouk, "not") it negates the knowing continuously, and ἕως οὗ (heōs hou, "until," ἕως G2193) bounds it at the bringing forth.

[L3] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." [L2] συνελθεῖν (synelthein, aorist infinitive of συνέρχομαι, synerchomai, G4905, "to come together," the union of man and wife) stands in Matthew 1:18 behind "before they came together"; [L2] μνηστευθείσης (mnēsteutheisēs, from μνηστεύω, mnēsteuō, G3423, "to betroth, espouse") stands in Matthew 1:18 behind "espoused."

[L3] Luke 2:5: "To be taxed with Mary his espoused wife, being great with child." At the taxing, at the birth, she is still the espoused wife.

[L4] FOOTING: [L1] Matthew 1:25 + [L2] ἐγίνωσκεν (G1097) with ἕως οὗ; [L3] Matthew 1:18 + [L2] συνελθεῖν (G4905); [L3] Luke 2:5. CHAIN: Matthew 1:18 states the child found in her before the coming together; Matthew 1:25 states the knowing negated until she brought forth; Luke 2:5 holds the espoused state to the birth. Precept upon precept: every stated row of union between Joseph and Mary stands after the birth of this child; the verses themselves carry the negations, and I read the till-clause for what it states — knowing withheld to the bringing forth — and no further.

## LINK III — The stated means of the conception: the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest

[L1] Luke 1:34: "Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"

[L2] γινώσκω (ginōskō, G1097, "to know," the conjugal idiom) and ἄνδρα (andra, accusative of ἀνήρ, anēr, G435, "man, husband") stand in Luke 1:34: her stated ground for asking "how" is that she has not known a man.

[L3] Luke 1:35: "And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." [L2] ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, future of ἐπισκιάζω, episkiazō, G1982, "to overshadow, to cast a shadow upon") stands in Luke 1:35 behind "shall overshadow thee"; [L2] γεννώμενον (to gennōmenon, present passive participle of γεννάω, gennao, G1080, "to beget") stands in Luke 1:35 behind "that holy thing which shall be born" — the Greek is τὸ γεννώμενον, "the thing being begotten," called ἅγιον, "holy"; [L2] διό (dio, G1352, "therefore, on which account") stands in Luke 1:35: the naming as "the Son of God" is tied by the angel's own "therefore" to the means just stated.

[L3] Luke 1:31: "And, behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name JESUS." [L3] Luke 1:37: "For with God nothing shall be impossible."

[L4] FOOTING: [L1] Luke 1:34 + [L2] γινώσκω (G1097) and ἀνήρ (G435); [L3] Luke 1:35 + [L2] ἐπισκιάζω (G1982), γεννώμενον (G1080), διό (G1352); [L3] Luke 1:31, Luke 1:37. CHAIN: the record states the promise — she shall conceive in her womb (Luke 1:31); the stated ground of her question is that she knows not a man (Luke 1:34); the answer the angel gives states the means — the Holy Ghost coming upon her, the power of the Highest overshadowing her — and ties the child's being called the Son of God to that stated means by διό. I ask Luke 1:35 for what it states, and it states the means.

## LINK IV — The stated register of Joseph's tie: husband and supposal; the begat-series ends at Joseph

[L1] Matthew 1:16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."

[L2] The series-verb is ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, aorist active of γεννάω, gennao, G1080, "begat"): "begat" — 39 tokens in Matthew 1:2–16 (15 verses), from Matthew 1:2 ("Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob; and Jacob begat Judas and his brethren;") to Matthew 1:16 ("Jacob begat Joseph"). The Jesus-link changes voice and reference: [L2] ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē, aorist passive of the same γεννάω, G1080) stands in Matthew 1:16 behind "was born"; [L2] ἧς (hēs, feminine relative pronoun, from ὅς, hos, G3739, "of whom — her") stands in Matthew 1:16 behind "of whom": ἐξ ἧς ἐγεννήθη Ἰησοῦς, "out of whom (feminine) was begotten Jesus" — the relative points to Mary, while Joseph stands in the same verse as "the husband of Mary."

[L3] Luke 3:23: "And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli," — [L2] ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, imperfect passive of νομίζω, nomizō, G3543, "to suppose, to reckon by custom or opinion") stands in Luke 3:23 behind "was supposed": the filial tie to Joseph is carried under the verb of supposal.

[L3] Matthew 1:24: "Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife:" — with Matthew 1:25 (LINK II), Joseph's stated acts toward this child are the taking of his wife and the calling of his name JESUS.

[L4] FOOTING: [L1] Matthew 1:16 + [L2] ἐγεννήθη (G1080, passive) and ἧς (G3739, feminine); [L3] Luke 3:23 + [L2] ἐνομίζετο (G3543); [L3] Matthew 1:24–25. CHAIN: Matthew's list runs 39 active "begat" tokens and ends the series with Joseph as the one begotten; the link from that generation to Jesus is stated in the passive — "was born" — out of the feminine "whom," Mary — with Joseph named "the husband of Mary." Luke's genealogical row opens with "as was supposed." Precept upon precept: both genealogies, each by its own stated words, carry Joseph's relation to Jesus as husband and supposed sonship, and the begetting-word the record gives for Jesus in Matthew 1:16 is passive, out of her.

## LINK V — The stated frame of the conception: a virgin conceives and bears a son

[L1] Luke 1:27: "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin's name was Mary." [L2] παρθένος (parthenos, G3933, "virgin") stands in Luke 1:27 behind both "a virgin" and "the virgin's" — παρθένος: 2 tokens in Luke 1:27.

[L3] Isaiah 7:14: "Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." [L2] הָעַלְמָה (ha'almah, from עַלְמָה, almah, H5959, "virgin, young woman — a maiden of marriageable age") stands in Isaiah 7:14 behind the KJV's "a virgin"; [L2] הָרָה (harah, H2030, "pregnant, conceiving") stands behind "shall conceive."

[L3] Matthew 1:22–23: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us." [L2] παρθένος (parthenos, G3933, "virgin") stands in Matthew 1:23 behind "a virgin."

[L3] Luke 1:31: "thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son" — and Luke 1:34: "I know not a man" (both quoted under LINK III).

[L4] FOOTING: [L1] Luke 1:27 + [L2] παρθένος (G3933); [L3] Isaiah 7:14 + [L2] עַלְמָה (H5959) and הָרָה (H2030); [L3] Matthew 1:23 + [L2] παρθένος (G3933); [L3] Luke 1:31, Luke 1:34. CHAIN: the record states who conceives and bears — a virgin, Mary, espoused to Joseph (Luke 1:27); the conceiving is in her womb (Luke 1:31); her own stated state is "I know not a man" (Luke 1:34); the prophet's sign is the עַלְמָה conceiving and bearing (Isaiah 7:14), and the birth record cites that row as fulfilled in "a virgin shall be with child" (Matthew 1:23). Precept upon precept: the stated frame of this conception is a virgin conceiving and bearing.

## LINK VI — The answer the links carry

[L4] FOOTING, verse by verse: "she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:18 [L1]); "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20 [L3]); "before they came together" (Matthew 1:18 [L3]); "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25 [L1]); "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee" (Luke 1:35 [L3]); "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34 [L1]); "of whom was born Jesus" (Matthew 1:16 [L1]); "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph" (Luke 3:23 [L3]); "Behold, a virgin shall be with child" (Matthew 1:23 [L3]) — each with its lexical ground exhibited in LINKS I–V (ἐκ G1537; γεννάω G1080 in γεννηθέν, γεννώμενον, ἐγεννήθη; συνελθεῖν G4905; ἐγίνωσκεν G1097; ἐπισκιάζω G1982; ἧς G3739; ἐνομίζετο G3543; παρθένος G3933; עַלְמָה H5959). CHAIN: the record states the source of the begetting in Mary — the Holy Ghost (I); it states Joseph's coming together and knowing as wholly after the birth (II); it states the means — the coming upon and the overshadowing — and ties the Sonship-naming to that means (III); it carries Joseph's tie as husband and supposal, with the begetting-word for Jesus passive and out of her (IV); it frames the whole as a virgin conceiving and bearing (V). In the record's own begetting register (Matthew 1:2–16), a father is a man whose "begat" stands behind the child; the begetting stated to stand behind this child is out of the Holy Ghost, in a virgin who had not known a man, before her espoused husband came together with her. Therefore Christ had no biological father.

DECLARED CHAIN

1. The stated source of the child in Mary is the Holy Ghost — Matthew 1:18 [L1] with ἐκ (ek, G1537, "out of, from"); Matthew 1:20 [L3] with γεννηθέν (to gennēthen, G1080, "the thing begotten").
2. Joseph's coming together and knowing are stated to stand wholly after the birth — Matthew 1:25 [L1] with ἐγίνωσκεν (eginōsken, G1097, "knew") and ἕως οὗ ("until"); Matthew 1:18 [L3] with συνελθεῖν (synelthein, G4905, "come together"); Luke 2:5 [L3].
3. The stated means of the conception are the Holy Ghost coming upon and the power of the Highest overshadowing, with the Sonship-naming tied to them by "therefore" — Luke 1:34 [L1] with γινώσκω (G1097) and ἀνήρ (G435); Luke 1:35 [L3] with ἐπισκιάζω (episkiazō, G1982, "overshadow"), γεννώμενον (G1080), διό (dio, G1352); Luke 1:31, 1:37 [L3].
4. The genealogies carry Joseph's tie as "the husband of Mary" and "as was supposed," the begat-series (39 tokens, Matthew 1:2–16) ending at Joseph, with Jesus' link passive and out of the feminine relative — Matthew 1:16 [L1] with ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē, G1080, passive "was born") and ἧς (hēs, G3739, feminine "of whom"); Luke 3:23 [L3] with ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, G3543, "was supposed"); Matthew 1:24 [L3].
5. The stated frame of the conception is a virgin conceiving and bearing — Luke 1:27 [L1] with παρθένος (parthenos, G3933, "virgin"); Isaiah 7:14 [L3] with עַלְמָה (almah, H5959) and הָרָה (harah, H2030); Matthew 1:23 [L3]; Luke 1:31 [L3].
6. NO — Christ had no biological father: the child in Mary was begotten out of the Holy Ghost, before union with Joseph, by the power of the Highest overshadowing her, with Joseph's tie carried as "the husband of Mary" and "as was supposed" (Links 1–5).
