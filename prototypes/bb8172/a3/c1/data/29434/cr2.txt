# ROUND 2: DEFENDER'S ANSWER

The challenger's method is to take each link alone and say "the verse doesn't state it." The case is the chain, so I re-seat each term in its chain below.

---

## Link 1: Matthew 1:16 does not stand alone

**[L1]** Matthew 1:2-16 has thirty-nine occurrences of "begat" (ἐγέννησεν, egennēsen, "fathered"), the last being "Jacob begat Joseph." The fortieth step reads: "Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus." The challenger says non-narration is not denial. But the denial is not left to the grammar alone. Verses 18-20 follow at once: "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise." The word γέννησις (gennēsis, "origin, generation") introduces the account of how, and the account names no man.

**[L2]** ἧς (hēs, "of whom") is feminine singular. The relative pronoun could have been masculine had the writer wished to attach the child to Joseph. Matthew chose the grammar that cannot.

**[L3]** The challenger cites Numbers 1:2, 18 and Ezra 2:59-62 to show reckoning is by the father's house. I agree that this is the rule of registers, and it is the reason Matthew keeps Joseph in the register. Joseph is the legal head through whom the child is "son of David" (Matthew 1:20), and the angel addresses him as such because he must take the child in. Ezra 2:61 also works against the challenger: a man "took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai… and was called after their name." Israel's own register allowed a man to be named after his wife's house.

**[L4]** Footing: [L1] the formula running thirty-nine times and then changing, plus Matthew 1:18-20; [L2] feminine ἧς against ἐγέννησεν. Chain: a formula that never varies and then varies at the single point where the sense would otherwise be fatherhood is a deliberate exception, and the following verses give the reason. I do not rest on the verb in isolation.

## Link 2: "of the Holy Ghost" in its chain

**[L1]** Matthew 1:18 says she was "found with child of the Holy Ghost" **"before they came together"** (πρὶν ἢ συνελθεῖν αὐτούς, prin ē synelthein autous, "before their coming together"). The challenger reads Matthew 1:25 as bounding Joseph's abstinence "to the pregnancy." Take that reading as given. It puts the pregnancy before the union, and 1:25 says Joseph "knew her not" (οὐκ ἐγίνωσκεν, ouk eginōsken, "was not knowing her") until the birth. **[L3]** That is the same verb the canon uses for begetting at Genesis 4:1, which the challenger himself calls "the canon's stated means of begetting." By that stated means, the text says Joseph did not use it before the child was born. So on the challenger's own reading of 1:25 and 1:18 together, the conception was not through Joseph.

**[L2]** The challenger says ἐκ (ek, "out of") with the Spirit means "covenant origin." The row is τὸ ἐν αὐτῇ γεννηθὲν (to en autē gennēthen, "that which is begotten in her"), and ἐκ is applied to the thing **begotten in a womb**, not to a believer. In John 1:13 the "born of God" are set against "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh." That is a contrast with fleshly begetting, which does not help the idea that Matthew 1:20 allows it.

**[L1]** Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (ἄνδρα, andra, "a man, a husband"). The question is about the **manner**. Verse 35 answers it: the Holy Ghost "shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: **therefore also** that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." **[L2]** διὸ καί (dio kai, "for which reason also") makes the title a **consequence of the conception**. Solomon's sonship in 2 Samuel 7:14 is a future covenant promise ("I will be"), with no "therefore." The challenger's parallel lacks the causal clause that Luke has. The overshadowing verb in Mark 9:7 is not asked to answer "how can this be?", while here it is the angel's whole answer to Mary's question.

## Link 3: "father" and "supposed"

**[L1]** Luke 3:23 is the narrator's own voice: "being (**as was supposed**) the son of Joseph." **[L3]** The same writer wrote Luke 1:34-35. He is not carelessly inserting a hedge in a genealogy, where a fact would be stated plainly.

**[L1]** The challenger lists the voices that say "father." Take them in context:
- **[L1]** The townspeople: "Is not this the son of Joseph?" (Luke 4:22). John 6:42 goes on: "whose father and mother we know? how is it then that he saith, I came down from heaven?" These are voices of unbelief, wrong about origin, so they cannot adjudicate it.
- **[L3]** Mark 6:3: "Is not this the carpenter, **the son of Mary**?" The same crowd, with the mother named and no father.
- **[L1]** Luke 2:48-49: Mary says "thy father and I," and the child answers in the same scene, "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?" (τοῦ πατρός μου, tou patros mou, "of my Father"). The text sets the two titles side by side, and **[L1]** Luke 2:50-51 adds that they "understood not the saying which he spake" and "his mother kept all these sayings in her heart." Mary spoke the household title and did not understand the correction, which is what the narrative records.
- **[L3]** "Father" is also a forefather's title: "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32), "our father Abraham" (Luke 1:73). So "father" proves no begetting.

**[L2]** Granted that νομίζω (nomizō, "to suppose, reckon") takes its verdict from context, as at Luke 2:44 and Acts 14:19, where the supposers are wrong. The context at Luke 3:23 is Luke 1:34-35.

## Link 4: the seed and the bar

**[L1]** Acts 2:30 says "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh." **[L2]** ὀσφύς (osphys, "loins") is the descent-word, but it names descent from David, not **through which parent**. **[L3]** Genesis 29:14, Laban to his sister's son Jacob: "Surely thou art my bone and my flesh." Flesh-kinship in Israel's own text runs through a mother. **[L1]** Romans 9:5: "of whom as concerning the flesh Christ came" (ἐξ ὧν, ex hōn, "out of whom," the Israelites), and no father is named.

**[L4]** Footing: [L1] Luke 1:35, "that holy thing which shall be born **of thee**"; [L1] Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30, the flesh being David's; [L3] Genesis 29:14, flesh through a mother. Chain: if the child's flesh is David's flesh (Romans 1:3) and it came to him of Mary (Luke 1:35, Galatians 4:4, "made of a woman," γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός, genomenon ek gynaikos, "come into being out of a woman"), then Mary is of David's seed. The challenger says no row gives her a David descent. That is correct as a single statement, but the chain gives it.

**[L3]** Luke 3:23 reads "which was the son of Heli," while Matthew 1:16 has "Jacob begat Joseph." Two fathers for one Joseph. **[L3]** Ezra 2:61 (a man "called after their name"), Numbers 27:4-7 and 36:6-8 (daughters keeping the house within the tribe) fit Joseph reckoned in Heli's line through marriage. **[L4]** Footing: [L1] Luke 3:23 against Matthew 1:16, [L3] Ezra 2:61 and Numbers 36:6-8. Chain: two fathers named for Joseph are explained by a marriage-reckoning that the law already allowed. I mark this as a chain inference, not a stated fact.

**[L1]** Jeremiah 22:30 in full: "Write ye this man childless (עֲרִירִי, ʿărîrî, 'stripped of heirs'), a man that shall not prosper in his days: for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah." The challenger reads "in his days" over both clauses. The second clause begins with its own "for" and is general ("no man of his seed"). **[L1]** The challenger's own proofs fit the bar: Zerubbabel (Haggai 1:1, "governor of Judah") held a signet but no throne. **[L1]** Jeremiah 18:8 is conditioned on turning, and the text records no turning by Coniah after 22:28. Matthew places Jechonias in Joseph's line (1:11-12), and it is **Matthew who also writes 1:16 with ἧς**. The challenger says the curse is never cited against Christ. The grammar of 1:16 is the answer: the register gives the legal title through Joseph and withholds the begetting at the single point that would bring a descendant of Jechonias onto the throne. That is a reading of Matthew's own construction, not an argument from silence.

**[L2]** Isaiah 7:14, עַלְמָה (ʿalmāh, "young woman of marriageable age"): **[L3]** Genesis 24:43 uses the same word of Rebekah, whom 24:16 calls "a virgin, neither had any man known her" (בְּתוּלָה, bethulah, "virgin"). So the word is used of one who is a virgin. **[L1]** The child of Isaiah 8:3 is named Maher-shalal-hash-baz, not Immanuel. **[L1]** Isaiah 8:8 has "thy land, O Immanuel," addressed to a ruler of the land, and 9:6 says "unto us a child is born… The mighty God." The sign at 7:14 is given to "the house of David" (7:13), the very house in question.

## Link 5: the keystone

**[L4]** Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:18, 20, 25 and Luke 1:34-35; [L2] ἐκ with γεννηθέν, and διὸ καί; [L3] Genesis 4:1 and 29:14. Chain: the conception was before the union (1:18), Joseph did not know her until the birth (1:25), the angel answers Mary's "I know not a man" with the Spirit's power and a causal "therefore" (Luke 1:35), and the flesh-kinship to David can pass through a woman. The challenger's "of his loins" requires Joseph to have been the begetter, which is exactly what Matthew 1:16 and 1:18-25 deny.

## DECLARED CHAIN (updated)

1. Matthew 1:16 breaks a thirty-nine-fold "begat" formula with feminine ἧς (hēs, "of whom"), and verses 18-20 give the account of the birth.
2. The conception is "of the Holy Ghost" before they came together, and Joseph "knew her not" until the birth (Genesis 4:1 idiom). Luke 1:35 gives the causal "therefore."
3. Joseph is father "as was supposed" (Luke 3:23). The household and crowd voices are answered by Luke 2:49 and Mark 6:3.
4. David's flesh reaches the child through Mary (Luke 1:35, Romans 1:3, Genesis 29:14), and Matthew's grammar avoids the bar of Jeremiah 22:30.
5. Therefore Christ had no human biological father.
