DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

# LINK ROUND

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED.**
Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:2-15 runs on one verb, "begat" (Matthew 1:2, "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob"). Matthew 1:16 reads: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
- [L2] The verb in 1:2-15 is ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat"). In 1:16 the verb is ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē, "was born"), and the relative is ἐξ ἧς (ex hēs, "out of whom"), feminine singular. It can refer only to Mary, not to Joseph, who is masculine.
- Chain: the formula "X begat Y" holds until the last link, where Joseph is "the husband of Mary" and the birth is "of whom," meaning her. [L1] Matthew 1:18 then says "the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise," and 1:20 gives the reason: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
- Verses pressed against this link: Matthew 1:2 ("Isaac begat Jacob") and Matthew 1:19-20 ("Joseph her husband… Mary thy wife"). Husband and wife are stated, and I do not deny them. They do not state that Joseph begat. The change of verb and the feminine relative are textual features, not silence.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED.**
Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:20 says "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." Luke 1:35 says "that holy thing which shall be born of thee."
- [L2] γεννηθὲν ἐν αὐτῇ (gennēthen en autē, "begotten in her") is followed by ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (ek pneumatos hagiou, "out of the Holy Ghost"). Matthew's own genealogy uses ἐκ (ek, "out of") for the source of the begotten child in the womb: "Judas begat Phares and Zara of Thamar" (1:3), and likewise Rachab and Ruth (1:5).
- Chain: the same word that names the woman's contribution in 1:3 names the Spirit's in 1:20. The object is "that which is conceived in her," not a believer.
- Verses pressed: John 1:13, 1 John 4:7, and "Matthew 1:20 carries no such mark." John 1:13 and 1 John 4:7 speak of those who receive him and become God's children, not of a thing conceived in a womb. Romans 1:3 marks "according to the flesh" for David's seed, which I take through Mary (LINK 4). I do not read the Spirit's origin in Matthew 1:20 as a figure for covenant.
- Luke 1:35 and Solomon: the challenger says "A title's causal clause does not decide begetting." Luke 1:35 does not attach the title to a decree. It says "therefore" (διό, dio, "wherefore") because of what comes upon Mary: "the Holy Ghost shall come upon thee."

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED, narrowed.**
I do not rest this link on the unbelief of the speakers. I rest it on the text's own marking of the parentage as a supposition.
- [L1] Luke 3:23 says "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph."
- [L2] ὡς ἐνομίζετο (hōs enomizeto, "as was supposed"). The root νομίζω (nomizō, "to suppose, think") is used of a wrong supposition at Luke 2:44, "supposing him to have been in the company," when he was not.
- Chain: Luke, who writes "thy father" in Mary's mouth (2:48), brackets Joseph's sonship as supposed in his own narration. Mary's "thy father" and the town's "whose father and mother we know" are therefore household usage and not a statement of begetting.
- Verses pressed: Acts 16:13 ("where prayer was wont to be made"), John 6:42 ("Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? how is it then that he saith, I came down from heaven?"), John 1:45, Luke 2:27, Luke 2:48, Matthew 13:55. Acts 16:13 shows the wider range of the verb. Luke 3:23 supplies the "as" and Luke 2:44 shows the supposition can be wrong.

**LINK 4 [premise 4]: SUPPORTED, as a chain.**
I grant that no verse states Mary's descent from David in so many words.
Footing:
- [L1] Romans 1:3 says "made of the seed of David according to the flesh." Galatians 4:4 says "made of a woman." Luke 1:35 says "born of thee." Luke 1:32 says "the throne of his father David," spoken to Mary.
- [L2] γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός (genomenon ek gynaikos, "come into being out of a woman").
- Chain: by LINK 1 and LINK 2, Joseph is not the source of the flesh. The flesh that is David's came "of a woman," so the woman carries David's seed.
- Verses pressed: Ezra 2:61-62 ("which took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai… and was called after their name… these sought their register… but they were not found") and Numbers 36:6-8. Ezra 2:61-62 records a missing register. It does not say a descent through a mother is void, and it shows a man "called after their name," meaning the wife's house. Numbers 36:6-8 is a rule about inheritance and marriage within the tribe. It does not address descent.

**LINK 4 [premise 5]: SUPPORTED, as a marked chain inference.**
It is not a stated fact, and I do not rest the keystone on it.
Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:16 says "Jacob begat Joseph." Luke 3:23 says "the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli."
- [L3] Deuteronomy 25:6 says "shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead." Ezra 2:61 has a man "called after their name." Both show law and Scripture reckoning a man's line through a name other than his begetter's.
- Chain: given two named fathers for Joseph, a reckoning by name, not begetting, in one line is a pattern Scripture already uses.
- Verses pressed: Deuteronomy 25:6 and Luke 3:23. The dead brother is not called "father," but the child is reckoned in his name, which is my point.

**LINK 5: CONCEDED.**
Matthew 1:25 follows the taking in 1:24. It bounds the marriage after the taking and does not reach back over the espousal. The premise as worded, that the abstinence of 1:25 covers the conception period, floats on that verse alone. The keystone does not rest on 1:25. It rests on Matthew 1:19-20 (Joseph "minded to put her away privily"; "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost") and Luke 1:34-35 ("seeing I know not a man"; "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee").

## CHALLENGER'S UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES

1. The inference that Joseph begat within the espousal: "Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal." The challenger admits "No row narrates any knowing then; no row denies it; and the begetting is nowhere narrated at all." This props the denial of "no human biological father."
2. The inference that Luke 1:35 is only a title: "A title's causal clause does not decide begetting." It rests on Solomon rows that carry no conception clause, whereas Luke 1:35 says "therefore… born of thee." This props the dismissal of Luke 1:35.
3. The inference that ἐκ in Matthew 1:20 is covenant origin: "Matthew 1:20 carries no such mark". It transfers believers' birth in John 1:13 and 1 John 4:7 to a thing "conceived in her." This props the reading of "of the Holy Ghost" as non-biological.
4. The inference that descent through Mary cannot count: "So a flesh descent through a mother is not registrable". Ezra 2:61-62 records only that a register "was not found," and the man was "called after their name." This props the denial of David's flesh through Mary.
5. The inference that the named-father witnesses stand uncorrected: "the text never calls the parentage statement an error". This is an argument from silence, and Luke 3:23 marks it "as was supposed." It props the weight given to John 6:42 and Luke 2:48.
6. The inference that the alternative leaves no flesh source: "leaving the child's flesh with no source but an unregistrable, unrowed descent." Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman" and Luke 1:35 "born of thee" name the source. This props the claim that the Mary-line is no line at all.
