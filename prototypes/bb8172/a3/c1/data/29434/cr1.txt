DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

# OPENING: Did Christ have a biological father?

**Position.** No. Jesus had no human biological father. He was conceived in Mary by the Holy Ghost, and Joseph was his father only by legal and reputed standing. By "biological father" I mean a man who contributed to the generation of the child.

---

## 1. The genealogy changes its verb at Joseph

**[L1]** Matthew 1:2-16 runs on one formula: "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob…" Verse 16 then breaks it: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ**."

**[L2]** The verb throughout is ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat, fathered"). In verse 16 the relative pronoun is ἧς (hēs, "of whom"), **feminine singular**. It can only refer to Mary, not to Joseph. The verb becomes the passive ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē, "was born"). Joseph is called ἀνήρ (anēr, "husband"), not the one who begat.

**[L4]** From [L1] Matthew 1:16 and [L2] the feminine ἧς, the evangelist who wrote "begat" thirty-nine times withheld it at the one point where it would make Joseph the father. The break is deliberate.

## 2. The stated source of the conception is the Holy Ghost

**[L1]** Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was found with child **of the Holy Ghost**." Verse 20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."

**[L2]** τὸ γεννηθὲν (to gennēthen, "that which is begotten/conceived") is qualified by ἐκ πνεύματός ἐστιν ἁγίου (ek pneumatos estin hagiou, "is out of Holy Spirit"). The preposition ἐκ (ek, "out of") names the source.

**[L1]** Luke 1:34-35: "How shall this be, seeing I **know not a man**?" The answer is: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: **therefore also** that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."

**[L2]** οὐ γινώσκω (ou ginōskō, "I do not know") uses the biblical idiom for sexual relations. The overshadowing is ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, "will cast a shadow over"). The causal link is διὸ καί (dio kai, "for which reason also"), so the child's identity as Son of God follows from the manner of conception.

**[L3]** Genesis 4:1, "Adam **knew** Eve his wife; and she conceived" (Hebrew יָדַע, yadaʿ, "to know"), fixes the idiom. Mary's question asks how conception could occur without it. The angel does not correct her premise. He supplies a different agent.

## 3. Joseph is "supposed," not named, as father

**[L1]** Luke 3:23: "Jesus… being (**as was supposed**) the son of Joseph." Matthew 1:19: Joseph "was minded to put her away privily." Matthew 1:25: Joseph "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son."

**[L2]** ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "was reckoned, was thought") describes a reputation, not a fact. Joseph's plan to divorce fits only if he knew he was not the father. The angel's reassurance in Matthew 1:20, "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife," presupposes the same.

**[L3]** Luke 2:48-49 shows the same gap. Mary says, "thy father and I have sought thee." The child answers, "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?" He corrects who his Father is.

## 4. The prophetic and genealogical chain requires it

**[L1]** Genesis 3:15: "the **seed of the woman**." **[L2]** The Hebrew זֶרַע (zeraʿ, "seed") is ordinarily reckoned through the male, yet here it is attributed to the woman alone. Isaiah 7:14, quoted in Matthew 1:23, reads: "Behold, a virgin shall be with child." Matthew's Greek is παρθένος (parthenos, "virgin, unmarried maiden"). Isaiah's Hebrew is עַלְמָה (ʿalmāh, "young woman of marriageable age"), and Matthew's Spirit-given rendering fixes the sense.

**[L1]** Jeremiah 22:30 says of Coniah: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." Matthew 1:11-12 places Jechonias (the same king) in Joseph's line.

**[L3]** Luke 3:31 traces a line through Nathan, not Solomon's line through Jechonias. Romans 1:3 says Christ was "made of the seed of David according to the flesh," and Galatians 4:4 says he was "made of a woman," γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός (genomenon ek gynaikos, "come into being out of a woman").

**[L4]** From [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 with Matthew 1:11-12, and [L3] Luke 3:31 with Romans 1:3, Christ's right to David's throne cannot rest on Joseph's blood. A blood-descent from Joseph through Jechonias would fall under the bar. David's seed "according to the flesh" must come through the mother, which is consistent with Galatians 4:4 and Hebrews 10:5, "a body hast thou prepared me."

---

## DECLARED CHAIN -- the links your case stands on:

1. Matthew 1:16 breaks the "begat" formula at Joseph, using the feminine ἧς (hēs, "of whom") so that Jesus is "born" of Mary and not begotten by Joseph. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:2-16; [L2] ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat") against ἧς and ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē, "was born").
2. The source of the conception is stated as the Holy Ghost, before Joseph and Mary came together. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:18, 20 and Luke 1:34-35; [L2] ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (ek pneumatos hagiou, "out of Holy Spirit"), οὐ γινώσκω (ou ginōskō, "I do not know"), and διὸ καί (dio kai, "therefore also"); [L3] Genesis 4:1 for the idiom of "know."
3. Joseph stands as father only by reputation and legal standing. Footing: [L1] Luke 3:23, Matthew 1:19 and 1:25; [L2] ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "was supposed"); [L3] Luke 2:48-49, where the child names a different Father.
4. The prophetic and genealogical requirements are met only through the woman, not through Joseph's blood. Footing: [L1] Genesis 3:15, Isaiah 7:14 and Jeremiah 22:30 with Matthew 1:11-12; [L2] זֶרַע (zeraʿ, "seed") and παρθένος (parthenos, "virgin"); [L3] Luke 3:31, Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4.
5. Therefore Christ had no human biological father: he was conceived of the Holy Ghost in Mary, "made of a woman." Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:20 and Luke 1:35; [L2] ἐκ (ek, "out of") and γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός (genomenon ek gynaikos, "come into being out of a woman"); [L3] Hebrews 10:5, "a body hast thou prepared me."
