# FINAL: THE ANSWER STANDS — JOSEPH, NAMED

**Yes. Joseph, of the house of David.** The defender's case now rests on reading two silence-rows as denials, while the text names the father in five registers and states the promised seed out of a man's body. Closing on the links.

---

## 1. The timing rows, read in their own sequence (LINK 2, LINK 5)

The defender's strongest move is Genesis 4:1 turned against the father. Take the verses in narrative order **[L1]**:

> Matthew 1:24 — *"And Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife:"*
> Matthew 1:25 — *"And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS."*

The abstinence clause **follows the taking**. It bounds the marriage after the taking to the birth: *"till"* (ἕως οὗ, heōs hou). It does not reach backward over the espousal **[L4]** (footing: [L1] the sequence of 1:24-25; [L2] the imperfect οὐκ ἐγίνωσκεν, ouk eginōsken, "was not knowing," a state clause in sequence after the verb "took").

And *"before they came together"* **[L1]** (Matthew 1:18) dates the **finding**. The verb συνέρχομαι (synerchomai, "come together") names married union elsewhere — *"Come together again"* (1 Corinthians 7:5) — and Matthew sets what came together by what follows: *"took unto him his wife"* (1:24), her coming into his house.

Inside that sequence the record's reading stands **[L4]** (footing: [L1] *"Joseph her husband,"* Matthew 1:19; *"Mary thy wife,"* Matthew 1:20; *"till,"* Matthew 1:25; [L3] *"his neighbour's wife"* of a betrothed damsel, Deuteronomy 22:24; *"Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived,"* Genesis 4:1) — **Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal.** No row narrates any knowing then; no row denies it; and the begetting is nowhere narrated at all. Non-narration is not denial — that is the defender's own principle at Matthew 1:16, applied evenly.

## 2. "Of the Holy Ghost" (LINK 2)

**[L2]** ἐκ (ek, "out of") with God or Spirit names **covenant origin**: *"born of God"* (John 1:13; 1 John 3:9; 5:1), *"is of God"* (1 John 4:7). John 1:13's contrast with *"blood"* and *"the will of the flesh"* is about **who becomes a child of God** (1:12, receiving him) — no believer's physical father is unmade by being *"born of God"* **[L3]** (1 John 4:7; 5:1 of believers with earthly fathers). Where the flesh is meant Paul marks it: *"according to the flesh"* (Romans 1:3); Matthew 1:20 carries no such mark **[L1]**.

**[L1]** The angel's *"for"* grounds the **taking** — *"fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost"* — and the same verse gives the father the naming: *"THOU shalt call his name JESUS"* (1:21), the father's act in the canon (Luke 1:62-63, *"they made signs to his father, how he would have him called"*).

**[L1]** Luke 1:35 ends in a **title**: *"shall be CALLED the Son of God"* — the same title given to Solomon, who had a father: *"Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son"* (1 Chronicles 28:6); *"I will be his father, and he shall be my son"* (2 Samuel 7:14) **[L3]** (Psalm 2:7 "this day have I begotten thee" applied at the **resurrection**, Acts 13:33). A title's causal clause does not decide begetting. **[L2]** The two verbs are presence and power: ἐπέρχομαι (eperchomai, "come upon") as at Acts 1:8 to the twelve; ἐπισκιάζω (episkiazō, "overshadow") as the cloud at Mark 9:7 and the shadow at Acts 5:15. Neither denotes begetting at any row.

## 3. The father, named (LINK 3)

**[L1]** Not a crowd: Mary — *"thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing"* (Luke 2:48); the narrator — *"the parents brought in the child Jesus"* (2:27), *"his parents went to Jerusalem"* (2:41), *"Joseph and his mother"* (2:33); a disciple — *"Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph"* (John 1:45); the town — *"whose father and mother we know"* (John 6:42), *"Is not this the carpenter's son? is not his mother called Mary?"* (Matthew 13:55) — and Mark 6:3's *"the carpenter, the son of Mary"* stands beside that row as written; the town knew the household.

Their stumbling is over *"I came down from heaven"* (John 6:42) — the text never calls the parentage statement an error **[L1]**. And *"as was supposed"* (ἐνομίζετο, enomizeto, "was reckoned, was wont") is the same form as *"where prayer was wont to be made"* (Acts 16:13) **[L2]** — a reputation word, not a verdict.

## 4. The seed rows and the reckoning (LINK 4, LINK 5)

**[L1]** The promised seed is out of a man's body: *"which shall proceed out of thy bowels"* (2 Samuel 7:12); *"Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne"* (Psalm 132:11); *"of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne"* (Acts 2:30); *"made of the seed of David according to the flesh"* (Romans 1:3); *"it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda"* (Hebrews 7:14). **[L2]** ὀσφύς (osphys, "loins") is the generation word of Hebrews 7:9-10, *"Levi... in the loins of his father."* Genesis 29:14's *"my bone and my flesh"* is Laban's word for **kinship** with his sister's son; the generation of that same Jacob is recorded *"Isaac begat Jacob"* **[L1]** (Matthew 1:2) — kinship through a mother is not generation through a mother.

**[L1]** Reckoning is by the father's house: *"by the house of their fathers"* (Numbers 1:2, 18). The register was **struck** — not kept — for the wife's house: *"which took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai... and was called after their name. These sought their register... but they were not found: therefore were they, as polluted, put from the priesthood"* (Ezra 2:61-62). Numbers 36:6-8 preserves the father's tribe: *"only to the family of the tribe of their father shall they marry... every one of the children of Israel shall keep himself to the inheritance of the tribe of his fathers."* So a flesh descent through a mother is not registrable — and no row gives Mary a David descent (Luke 1:36 gives her a kinswoman *"of the daughters of Aaron"*) **[L1]**.

**[L1]** The two-fathers divergence (Matthew 1:16 Jacob; Luke 3:23 Heli) is stated and never explained by the text; and the one institution the law gives for a dead man's name provides **one begetter** and a preserved name: *"the firstborn which she beareth shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead"* (Deuteronomy 25:6) — the dead brother is never called the father. A marriage-reckoning father is a construct the text does not state, and on that construct Joseph's blood is not the line at all — leaving the child's flesh with no source but an unregistrable, unrowed descent.

**[L1]** Jeremiah 22:30 read whole: *"Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper in his days: for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah"* — and the sons of Jeconiah are **named** (1 Chronicles 3:17-18), and the signet is **restored** to Zerubbabel (Haggai 2:23), *"governor of Judah"* (Haggai 1:1), not king. **[L3]** The name appears twice in the New Testament (Matthew 1:11-12) and the curse is never cited against Christ; Jeremiah's own rule bounds pronounced sentences (18:8). The grammar of 1:16 does not carry a curse the canon never applies.

## 5. The keystone

**[L1]** *"of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh"* (Acts 2:30); *"made of the seed of David according to the flesh"* (Romans 1:3); *"Joseph, thou son of David"* (Matthew 1:20); *"of the house and lineage of David"* (Luke 2:4); *"the throne of his father David"* (Luke 1:32). **"No human biological father" cannot stand beside a seed proceeding out of David's loins and a father named "thou son of David."** LINK 5 is broken at [L1].
