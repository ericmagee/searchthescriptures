CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

LINK 1: SUPPORTED — the union as held: "Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal" [L4]. Layer footing, three registers:
[L1] Genesis 4:1 — "And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain." The canon's stated means of begetting is the husband knowing his wife.
[L1] Matthew 1:19 — "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a public example, was minded to put her away privily." Matthew 1:20 — "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife." Deuteronomy 22:24 — the law calls the betrothed woman "his neighbour's wife": the two are husband and wife from the espousal.
[L1] Matthew 1:24 — "took unto him his wife"; Matthew 1:25 — "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." The stated abstinence is bounded by "till" and covers the pregnancy after the taking.
[L2] The seed is the man's, in the text's own anatomy words: 2 Samuel 7:12 "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (mimme'eykha, "out of thy bowels"); Acts 2:30 "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (osphys, "the loins"); Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (ek spermatos Dauid kata sarka, "out of the seed of David according to the flesh").
[L3] Psalm 132:11 "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne"; Hebrews 7:14 "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda."
Chain: the promise requires seed out of a man's body [L1-L2]; the stated means of begetting is knowing [L1]; the man named is her husband from the espousal, whom the law names her husband before the wedding [L1]; the one abstinence row stops at the birth [L1]; therefore the knowing falls within the espousal [L4]. That inference exhibits two prior layers and does not float.
Verses pressed against it, answered: Matthew 1:19-20 "Joseph her husband... Mary thy wife" — affirmed as stated; that is the footing, not a contrary. Matthew 1:25 "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" — taken as written, bounded by "till." Luke 1:34 "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (andra ou ginōskō, "a man I know not") — present, her state when the angel spoke, and in the same speech the child is promised "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32). The begetting is not narrated anywhere; no row denies it; and the seed rows require it.

LINK 2: SUPPORTED — the causal clause of Luke 1:35 grounds the naming and does not decide begetting.
[L1] Luke 1:32 — "He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David." Title and throne come before the question of verse 34. Luke 1:35 — "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." The product of the clause is a name: "shall be called" (klēthēsetai, "shall be called") — the same naming word as 1:32.
[L2] Both verbs are measured at every row: "come upon" (eperchomai, "come upon") is 10 rows — Acts 1:8 "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you," of the apostles who are to "endued with power from on high" (Luke 24:49); "overshadow" (episkiazō, "overshadow") is 5 rows — Matthew 17:5; Mark 9:7; Luke 9:34, the cloud of the transfiguration, and Acts 5:15, Peter's shadow healing the sick. No row of either verb is a begetting.
[L3] The title is given to a son who has a father: 2 Samuel 7:14 "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (of Solomon); 1 Chronicles 28:6 "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son"; and "this day have I begotten thee" (Psalm 2:7) is applied at the resurrection (Acts 13:33), at the priesthood (Hebrews 5:5), and beside 2 Samuel 7:14 (Hebrews 1:5).
Chain: the clause's verbs are anointing and presence at every row [L2]; the clause's outcome is a naming [L1]; the name is the covenant title borne by a son with a father [L1-L3]; therefore the clause grounds the title and does not narrate or exclude a begetting [L4].
Verses pressed, answered: Luke 1:35 "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee" — "born of thee" names Mary as mother, which no one disputes; the verse states no absence of a father. 2 Samuel 7:14 "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" — as written, of Solomon, whom David begat; the title runs alongside a biological father rather than replacing him.

LINK 3: SUPPORTED — Matthew 1:20's "of the Holy Ghost" is the covenant-origin marker, not the begetter.
[L1] Matthew 1:20 — "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (ek pneumatos hagiou, "out of the Holy Ghost"). The clause answers "take unto thee Mary thy wife": it grounds the taking.
[L2] The preposition with God or Spirit names origin of being in the covenant register: John 1:13 "born of God"; 1 John 3:9 "born of God"; 1 John 4:7 "is of God"; 1 John 5:1 "born of God." And when the text means biological flesh origin it marks it: Romans 1:3 "according to the flesh" (kata sarka). Matthew 1:20 carries no such mark — the text's own marker for flesh origin is absent from the row.
[L3] The claim the clause carries is the covenant formula given to a son with a father: 2 Samuel 7:14 "I will be his father, and he shall be my son."
Chain: with God as source-word, the preposition is covenant origin at every row [L2]; biological origin is flagged kata sarka when meant and is unflagged here [L2]; the attached claim is the covenant formula of Solomon [L1-L3]; therefore "of the Holy Ghost" names the child as God's own claim, not God's biological begetting [L4].
Verses pressed, answered: John 1:13 and 1 John 4:7 — the objection is the referent; the footing is the register of the construction, and its counter-mark is Romans 1:3's kata sarka, absent at Matthew 1:20. That the Spirit is the biological conception is nowhere stated in the row, and it would assign to the Spirit the very thing the seed rows assign to a man's body (Acts 2:30, "the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh").

LINK 4: SUPPORTED — the stated reckoning is the father's house; no row registers a mother-line.
[L1] Numbers 1:2 — "by their families, by the house of their fathers... every male by their polls." Numbers 1:18 — "they declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers."
[L1] Ezra 2:59 — "they could not shew their father's house, and their seed, whether they were of Israel." Ezra 2:61-62 — certain priests "called after the name" of the wife's house (Barzillai) "sought their register... but they were not found: therefore were they, as polluted, put from the priesthood."
[L2] The 1611 margin at Ezra 2:59 on "their seed" reads "or, pedigree" — the reckoning word's own gloss.
[L3] Numbers 36:6-8 — "the children of Israel may enjoy every man the inheritance of his fathers. And the inheritance shall not remove from one tribe to another; but every one of the children of Israel shall keep himself to the inheritance of the tribe of his fathers."
Chain: the census and pedigree are declared "by the house of their fathers" [L1]; where the father's house cannot be shewn the register fails and the man is excluded [L1]; the one row where a man is called after the wife's house is that failing row [L1]; the seed-word's margin gloss is "pedigree" [L2]. So the text's own register carries the father's house, and descent through a mother is not the stated reckoning [L4].
Verses pressed, answered: Ezra 2:61-62 "called after their name... their register... were not found" — this is the row on my side: the wife's-house calling is exactly where the register fails and the man is put from the priesthood. Numbers 36:6-8 — it keeps every man's inheritance with "the tribe of his fathers," which is the reckoning in question. And the corollary is on the page: Matthew 1:16 "Jacob begat Joseph"; Luke 3:23 "Joseph, which was the son of Heli" — no row puts a woman in a descent line, so "Mary's genealogy" is not a text at all.

LINK 5: SUPPORTED — the named-father witnesses are affirmative and stand, and "as was supposed" is not a correction of them.
[L1] Five registers name the father: Mary's own mouth, "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48); the narrator, "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33), "his parents went to Jerusalem" (2:41), "the parents brought in the child Jesus" (2:27); the town, "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" (John 6:42), "Is not this the carpenter's son?" (Matthew 13:55), "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22); a disciple, "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45); the angel, "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20).
[L2] The marking verb carries no verdict of its own: "suppose, think, be wont" (nomizō, "suppose, think, be wont"); at Acts 16:13 the same form (enomizeto, "was wont / was supposed") is "where prayer was wont to be made" — custom; at Luke 2:44 "supposing him to have been in the company" the supposition is corrected inside its own passage. The row decides, not the word.
[L3] Luke 3:23 runs the reckoning itself through Joseph — "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" — and the names after it reach Adam and God; the parenthesis does not remove him from the line.
Chain: five registers affirm the fatherhood [L1]; the marking verb is neutral and elsewhere names usage, not error [L2]; the same verse reckons the line through Joseph [L3]; therefore the mark identifies the register of the naming and does not state that the naming is wrong [L4]. That is affirmative witness plus a neutral verb, not silence.
Verses pressed, answered: Luke 3:23 "as was supposed" — as above; Luke 2:44 shows the verb *can* be wrong, and it also shows the text says so plainly when it is; it never says so here. Luke 2:27 "the parents brought in the child Jesus" — the narrator's own word. Luke 2:48 "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" — the mother's own word, the recipient of the announcement. John 6:42 "whose father and mother we know" — the verse asserts knowledge of both parents. John 1:45 "the son of Joseph" — a disciple's confession. Matthew 13:55 "Is not this the carpenter's son?" — the town's. Acts 16:13 — the same verb for custom. None of these rows is corrected by any other row.

LINK 6: SUPPORTED — without Joseph the Davidic flesh has no rowed source.
[L1] The flesh must be David's and must come out of a man's body: Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David according to the flesh"; Acts 2:30 "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ"; 2 Samuel 7:12 "which shall proceed out of thy bowels"; Hebrews 7:14 "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda."
[L2] "Made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4; genomenon ek gynaikos, "come into being out of a woman") is the mortality idiom and settles no father: it is said of John the Baptist — Matthew 11:11 "among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" — whose father is named: Luke 1:13 "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son." Compare Job 14:1 "man that is born of a woman"; Job 25:4.
[L1-L3] The genealogy rows name men only — Matthew 1:16 "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary"; Luke 3:23 "Joseph, which was the son of Heli" — and the one kin row Mary carries is "thy cousin Elisabeth" (Luke 1:36), "of the daughters of Aaron" (Luke 1:5).
Chain: the required flesh is a man's seed out of his body [L1-L2]; "made of a woman" is the birth-of-a-woman phrase used of a man whose father is named [L2-L3]; if Joseph is that man, the rows are complete; if not, the record names no other man and no row routes the flesh through the mother [L1]; therefore the alternative leaves the Davidic flesh with no rowed source [L4].
Verses pressed, answered: Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman" — answered by the idiom row: it is also said of John, son of Zacharias. Luke 1:35 "born of thee" — names the mother; names no father and denies none. And the anatomical requirement stands unmet without Joseph: "the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30) has no rowed loins outside the named husband.
