## Round 2 - Defender (MiMo)

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

**ROUND 2 — THE DEFENSE HOLDS: a gap is not a how, and the flesh rows will not yield**

**1. C1/C2 — the broken formula and the *ek*.** [L2] *Egennēthē* in Matthew 1:16 is the plain birth-word: "of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." It names the mother and narrates no source. The challenger reads the gap — thirty-nine active "begat" verbs, then a passive — as the text "narrating the how by exception." A gap bears no such weight: non-narration is not narration, and the record carries explicit flesh rows to weigh against an inference drawn from an omission: "made of the seed of David *according to the flesh*" (Romans 1:3), "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30). One passive verb does not outvote two *kata sarka* statements. [L2] As for *ek*: it names a source, and the question is what kind. Every New Testament *ek* + God/Spirit row is spiritual origin of people who HAVE fathers: "born of God" (John 1:13), "born of God" (1 John 3:9), "is of God" (1 John 4:7) — believers, begotten of their own fathers and "born of God" in the same row of existence. The challenger concedes these believers "are born twice" — which is exactly the point: the construction *ek theou* names the Godward origin and does not deny a human begetter. Where flesh is meant the writer marks it: *kata sarka* (Romans 1:3). Matthew 1:20 has no such marker. [L3] And the canon's own conception rows settle what divine-source language can and cannot decide: "Elkanah knew Hannah his wife; and the LORD remembered her. Wherefore it came to pass, when the time was come about, after Hannah had conceived" (1 Samuel 1:19–20) — divine causation named in the conception verse with the father named in the same breath. "The LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb" (Genesis 29:31) — father Jacob. "God remembered Rachel... opened her womb" (Genesis 30:22) — father Jacob. A divine act named in a conception never erases the father.

**2. C3 — the knowing rows.** [L1] Held exactly as written: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:18); "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (1:25); "seeing I know not a man" (Luke 1:34). [L2] Each states a window: the state at the discovery (1:18), the period bounded by the birth (1:25, *heōs hou eteken*), the state at the annunciation by an espoused woman (Luke 1:27, present indicative). The challenger's syllogism — union is the only route to fatherhood, union is denied, therefore no father — converts "no union in this window" into "no father at any time." No row says "no man." And the father-rows come AFTER these verses, in the same Gospels: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48) is written by the author of Luke 1:34. [L3] The canon also knows conception beyond that one idiom: the word of the LORD "opened her womb" (Genesis 29:31; 30:22; 1 Samuel 1:19–20) with fathers named — so Genesis 4:1 is not the exclusive grammar of begetting.

**3. C4 — Joseph's fear.** [L2] Matthew 1:19 at the word level: *dikaios ōn, mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai, eboulēthē lathra apolysai autēn* — righteousness, unwillingness to make a publick example, intent to put away. Two grounds and an intent; no verb of knowledge anywhere in the row. The row records what Joseph meant to do, not what he knew. [L3] And the angel's charge is a FATHER'S charge: "thou shalt call his name JESUS" (1:21), done at 1:25 — the identical form given to Zacharias, "thou shalt call his name John" (Luke 1:13), where no one doubts the father; and the naming sign is given to the father again (Luke 1:62–63, "signs to his father, how he would have him called"). A charge to name the child is the mark of the father's office, not its negation.

**4. C5 — the five voices.** [L1] The Boy's answer is a covenant title, not a correction: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" (Luke 2:49) — and the SAME narrator writes the next verse: "he went down with them, and came to Nazareth, and was SUBJECT UNTO THEM" (2:51), with "them" the *goneis* of 2:41, 43. Luke 2:49 no more cancels 2:48 than "Son of God" cancels 1 Chronicles 28:6, where Solomon is God's son and David's begetter in one verse. [L1] John 6:42 is not a rumor but a knowledge-claim — "whose father and mother we know" — and the fourth Gospel narrates no correction of Joseph's fatherhood; John 1:45 is a disciple's plain address. [L2] On *enomizeto*: even granting Luke's supposition-uses, the verse's work is the REGISTER: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was [the son] of Heli" — the line runs through Joseph's name, father to father, "the son of Nathan, the son of David" (Luke 3:31) — thirty-seven male names and not one woman. [L3] Reckoning in the record is by the father's house: "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18); those who could not "shew their father's house" were excluded (Ezra 2:59); a register that followed the wife's house was struck and its holders "put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61–62). A genealogy that ends in a woman would break the record's own reckoning rule — so "Mary supplies the seed of David" is the import here, not the text.

**5. C6 — Jeconiah, and the escape that is not free.** [L1] "Write ye this man childless... for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more IN JUDAH" (Jeremiah 22:30). The New Testament never cites this curse against Jesus' throne; Iechonias appears twice (Matthew 1:11–12) and the charge is never raised. [L3] The promised throne is GIVEN, not inherited by accumulation: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32). The signet plucked from Jeconiah (Jeremiah 22:24) is set on Zerubbabel (Haggai 2:23) — governor (Haggai 1:1; Ezra 3:2), never king — and the pronounced-sentence rule bounds the curse: "If that nation... turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil" (Jeremiah 18:7–8). **But the decisive point: the challenger's own escape runs through the same two names.** [L1] "Zorobabel, which was the son of Salathiel, which was the son of Neri" (Luke 3:27) — while Matthew writes "Jechonias begat Salathiel... Salathiel begat Zorobabel" (Matthew 1:12–13). Whether Luke's Salathiel and Matthew's are one man the text never says — but if the challenger needs the flesh line to run through Luke's register to escape Jeconiah, that register passes through a Salathiel and a Zorobabel too. The trap, if it is a trap, closes on both routes at once; and since the text does not say they are the same men, it does not close on either. The knot stays a knot.

**6. C6 second half — "her seed" and "made of a woman."** [L1] "her seed" (Genesis 3:15) never negates a father in its own family of rows: "I will multiply THY SEED exceedingly" (Genesis 16:10, to Hagar — father Abraham); "let thy seed possess the gate of his enemies" (Genesis 24:60, to Rebekah — father Isaac); "thy seed shall inherit the nations" (Isaiah 54:3, to Zion). Four rows, four cases where a woman's seed is spoken while the father stands named. [L3] "Made of a woman" is the mortality idiom: "man that is born of a woman is of few days" (Job 14:1; 15:14; 25:4 — all singular, so the singular of Galatians 4:4 proves nothing), and the Lord uses the phrase of John the Baptist (Matthew 11:11), whose father Zacharias is named at Luke 1:13. Galatians 4:4's point is the Son's entry into mortal, law-bound humanity: "made of a woman, made under the law." [L1] Hebrews states the flesh line plainly: "he also himself likewise took part of the same" (Hebrews 2:14), "he took on him the seed of Abraham" (2:16), "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (7:14).

**7. C7 — the title.** [L1] The title is given BEFORE the question, with the throne: "He shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32) — then Mary asks (1:34) — then "shall be CALLED" (*klēthēsetai*, 1:35). And the title is Solomon's FIRST: "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14) of the son David begat; "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6). A title Solomon bore with a living father cannot decide whether Jesus had one. [L1] On Acts 13:33 the context is Paul's own: "he hath raised up Jesus again; as it is also written... Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee. And as concerning that he raised him up from the dead" (13:33–34) — the begetting is tied to the raising, and Romans 1:4 seals it: "declared to be the Son of God with power... by the resurrection from the dead." "A body hast thou prepared me" (Hebrews 10:5, of the sacrifice) and "that holy thing which shall be born of thee" (Luke 1:35) name the body's holiness — not its having no father.

**THE CHAIN, STANDING**

D1: The promised seed is flesh seed out of the father's body — 2 Samuel 7:12; Psalm 132:11; Romans 1:3; Acts 2:30; Hebrews 7:14 [L1/L2]. *Untouched by the challenger.*
D2: The father is named Joseph in five voices, and the naming charge is a father's act — Luke 2:48; John 6:42; Luke 4:22; John 1:45; Luke 2:4; Matthew 1:20–25 [L1/L2/L3]. *The "correction" reading is answered by Luke 2:51 "subject unto them."*
D3: The named father stands in David's house and lineage; the registers run through Joseph by the father's house, no female name in either — Luke 1:27; 2:4; Numbers 1:2; Ezra 2:59, 61–62 [L1/L3]. *Mary's register is the import.*
D4: *ek* + Spirit names covenant origin; flesh is marked *kata sarka* and unmarked at Matthew 1:20 — John 1:13; 1 John 3:9, 4:7; Romans 1:3; 1 Samuel 1:19–20 [L2/L3]. *The canon's conception rows name God and a father together.*
D5: "Son of God" is a covenant and resurrection title borne by a son with a father — 2 Samuel 7:14; 1 Chronicles 28:6; Acts 13:33–34; Romans 1:4 [L1/L3]. *Settles nothing here.*
D6: **Christ had a biological father — Joseph, the named Davidic father; the WHO is named and the HOW is never narrated.** John 6:42; Romans 1:3; 2 Samuel 7:12 [L1/L2].

**THE CHALLENGER'S LINKS, AS MEASURED**

C1 — an inference drawn from a gap; the flesh rows (Romans 1:3; Acts 2:30) are the counter-witness, and the *ex hēs* names the mother only. C2 — *ek* measures source-type; the God/Spirit rows describe people with fathers (John 1:13), so the construction cannot mean "no father." C3 — the negation rows bound windows; "no man" is in no row; the father-rows follow them. C4 — Matthew 1:19 is intent without a knowledge-verb; the naming charge is the father's. C5 — Luke 2:51 restores the parents after 2:49; Luke 3:23's register runs through Joseph with zero female names. C6 — the Jeconiah escape runs through Salathiel and Zorobabel in Luke 3:27 as well; "her seed" rows have named fathers (Genesis 16:10; 24:60); Job 14:1 is singular. C7 — Solomon bore the title with a living father; the begetting is the resurrection's (Acts 13:33–34; Romans 1:4).
