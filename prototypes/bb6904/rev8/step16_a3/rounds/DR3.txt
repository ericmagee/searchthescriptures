## Round 3 - Challenger (Claude): link round

CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

### LINK ROUND: CHALLENGER

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:16 is the only place the chain's verb changes: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus." The defender counts thirty-nine "begat" verbs. The fortieth link does not say "Joseph begat Jesus."
- [L2] The relative pronoun is *ex hēs* (feminine singular, "of her"). The verb shifts from active *egennēsen* to passive *egennēthē*. A masculine plural *ex hōn* would have carried Joseph. The grammar takes the final birth out of Joseph's line of begetting.
- [L1] Scripture records the naming act where the namer did not beget, and says no more than the act itself.
  - Exodus 2:10: "he became her son. And she called his name Moses."
  - Ruth 4:17: "the women her neighbours gave it a name, saying, There is a son born to Naomi."
- [L1] In Matthew 1:20-21 the angel says "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." He then charges Joseph, "thou shalt call his name JESUS," and Joseph does so (1:25).
- Chain: the chain's last verb is passive and from the mother [L2]. Naming does not prove begetting [L1, Exodus 2:10; Ruth 4:17]. The angel assigns the conception to the Spirit and the naming to Joseph [L1]. So Joseph's fatherhood in the text is the naming and recognizing kind. I concede that no word "adopt" appears. The claim rests on these two footings and does not need that word.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED.**
- [L1] The text gives two fathers for one man: "Jacob begat Joseph" (Matthew 1:16) and "Joseph, which was the son of Heli" (Luke 3:23).
- [L1/L3] The Law supplies a mechanism for a man to stand in another's name: "the firstborn which she beareth shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead" (Deuteronomy 25:6).
- [L3] Jeremiah 22:30 says of Coniah: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." Matthew 1:11-12 places Jechonias in Joseph's line. Luke 3:27 gives a different line through Salathiel.
- Chain: one man has one begetter. Two named fathers therefore mean two names for one man, or one father by flesh and one by law. I state the disjunction narrowly. It does not claim to know which father is which. It claims only that the two verses cannot both be pure begetting. That is the footing on which the Jeconiah knot loosens.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED, with scope narrowed.**
- [L1] I disclaim "till means never." Matthew 1:25 reads "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son," and the word bounds the denial.
- The premise needs only the bounded window.
  - [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
  - [L1] Matthew 1:25: "knew her not till she had brought forth."
- [L2] *Heōs hou* with the imperfect *eginōsken* denies the knowing through the whole period up to the birth. That period contains the conception. The chain runs from the bounded denial to the conception window only. It does not extend past the birth.

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED.**
- [L1] Mary's question is "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34). She is already "espoused" (1:27). The angel does not answer "after the wedding." His answer names another agent: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee" (1:35).
- [L1] Joseph's own reaction is the second footing. Matthew 1:19: "Joseph her husband, being a just man... was minded to put her away privily." Matthew 1:20: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife." If the child were his, there would be nothing to put away.
- Chain: the angel's answer names an agent other than the husband [Luke 1:35]. The husband's reaction treats the pregnancy as not his [Matthew 1:19]. So the question was about her state as an espoused woman, and the answer excludes the husband. The defender's present-indicative point on *ginōskō* is granted. The exclusion rests on 1:35 and 1:19, not on that tense.

**LINK 5: CONCEDED.**
- [L1] John 1:12-13 speaks of "as many as received him... which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." The subject is believers, in the plural in the KJV text, and not the child in Mary.
- The contrast with *andros* cannot be turned into a conception row for Jesus. I withdraw it as a footing for Matthew 1:20. That verse stands on Luke 1:35 and Matthew 1:18-19 (Links 3-4).

**LINK 6: SUPPORTED, with scope narrowed.**
- I concede that the word *parthenos* alone does not narrate a mechanism, and that Isaiah 8:3-4 ("I went unto the prophetess; and she conceived") shows an Isaiah-day child.
- [L1] Matthew 1:22 binds the prophecy to the events just given: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled." "All this" includes "before they came together" (1:18), "of the Holy Ghost" (1:18, 1:20) and "knew her not" (1:25). Luke 1:27 uses *parthenos* twice of Mary, and 1:34 adds "I know not a man."
- [L2] Isaiah 7:14 offers an *'ôth* (sign) after Ahaz was told to ask "either in the depth, or in the height above" (7:11). A sign of that scope is not an ordinary birth.
- Chain: the word is read in the context Matthew 1:22 fixes [L1], and the context excludes Joseph's coming together with her [L1]. The inference from the word alone is withdrawn. The inference from the word in that context stands.
