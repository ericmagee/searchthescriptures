## Round 2 - Challenger (Claude)

CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 2, CLOSING AND PREMISE LIST

### CHALLENGER'S CLOSE: the defender's rows stop at David, and Matthew 1 and Luke 1 say what they do not say

#### 1. D1 was touched, and it stays touched

[L1] Every flesh row the defender cites ends at David or Judah, never at Joseph: "proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12), "the fruit of thy body" (Psalm 132:11), "the fruit of his loins" (Acts 2:30), "sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14).

[L2] *Ek spermatos Dauid kata sarka* (Romans 1:3) fixes the descent at David and does not say which parent carries it.

[L4] The step from "David's loins" to "Joseph's loins" is the defender's own. It rests on one layer only, the rows, and they stop short of Joseph. The defender cannot call D1 "untouched" and also supply the missing link by assertion.

[L2] Luke 3:23 hedges Joseph and nothing else: *hōs enomizeto huios Iōsēph, tou Hēli*. The Greek has no second "son"; the chain proceeds by bare genitive articles, and the defender's bracketed "[the son]" is his own insertion. The defender's concession stands in his own words: "even granting Luke's supposition-uses." Luke's three uses are false suppositions (Luke 2:44; Acts 14:19; 21:29), and the defender answered none of them.

#### 2. The defender's conception rows prove the challenger's point

[L1] The defender offers "Elkanah knew Hannah his wife; and the LORD remembered her" (1 Samuel 1:19). In the same row the divine act and the father's knowing both stand in the text. Genesis 29:30 reads "he went in also unto Rachel," and 29:31–30:22 then add the LORD's opening of the womb.

[L1] Matthew 1 reverses the pattern. The Spirit is named as the source (1:18, 20), and the knowing is denied: "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (1:25).

[L3] The defender's parallel rows show what a divine act plus a father looks like: the father's union is stated. The Matthew row is the reverse. So "a divine act named in a conception never erases the father" is true only where union is present. Here the text withholds it, and the rows cited only prove that.

[L2] The defender never answered *en autē* with *to gennēthen*. The conceived object is "in her" (Matthew 1:20), and "found with child" (*en gastri echousa*, 1:18) is a womb word. The defender's *ek* rows (John 1:13; 1 John 3:9; 4:7) describe persons regenerated after a human birth. Matthew 1:20 describes the first begetting, and John 1:13 itself sets "of God" against "the will of man" (*andros*).

#### 3. Luke 1:34–35: the window reading makes Gabriel a non sequitur

[L1] The defender grants a window ("the state at the annunciation by an espoused woman"). Mary is already betrothed (Luke 1:27) and Gabriel has said "thou shalt conceive" (1:31). If Joseph were the intended father, the window would close at the marriage and the question "How shall this be?" would be idle.

[L1] Gabriel answers with the Spirit: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee" (1:35). He does not answer "when thou art wed."

[L2] The defender again skipped *dio kai*, "Therefore also." The title "Son of God" in 1:35 is grounded in the conception event. 1:32 gives the title and 1:35 gives its ground, so the defender's order of verses does not touch the connective. Solomon's rows (2 Samuel 7:14; 1 Chronicles 28:6) carry no "therefore" tied to a conception, so they cannot match it.

[L3] Acts 13:33 reads "raised up Jesus again," and 13:34 adds, as a separate clause, "And as concerning that he raised him up from the dead." Acts 3:26 also has "raised up his Son Jesus" before the cross. The defender never answered Acts 3:26.

#### 4. Joseph's fear, and the naming charge

[L1] Matthew 1:20 reads "Fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."

[L2] *Gar* gives the reason for the command not to fear. The defender says there is "no verb of knowledge" in 1:19. But 1:18 *heurethē* ("was found") is the discovery, and 1:19 is Joseph's response to it. A father who knew the child was his needs no such reassurance.

[L1] The naming charge is not a father's mark exclusively. Mary is charged "thou shalt… call his name JESUS" (Luke 1:31), and Isaiah 7:14 says "she shall call." [L4] Joseph's naming (Matthew 1:21, 25) is the adopting father's act, which fits "Joseph, thou son of David" (1:20) and the legal line, and the footing is Luke 1:31 plus Matthew 1:20–21.

#### 5. Luke 2:49 against 2:48, and Luke 2:51

[L1] "Thy father and I" (Luke 2:48) is answered at once by "my Father's business" (2:49).

[L2] John 5:18 has *patera idion*, His own Father. [L1] The "subject unto them" of 2:51 shows that Joseph and Mary stood in the household as parents. It does not show that Joseph begat Him, and the same narrator says "Joseph and his mother" (Luke 2:33).

#### 6. D3: the "no woman" register rule

[L1] The defender writes "A genealogy that ends in a woman would break the record's own reckoning rule." But Matthew's register names women: "Phares and Zara of Thamar" (1:3), "Booz of Rachab… Obed of Ruth" (1:5), "Solomon of her that had been the wife of Urias" (1:6). It ends "Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus" (1:16), with the feminine *ex hēs*.

[L3] The defender's Numbers 1:2 and Ezra 2:59–62 are about priestly and tribal enrollment. They say nothing of how the royal line is recorded in Matthew.

#### 7. Jeconiah: the silence argument and the defender's own rule

[L1] Jeremiah 22:30 is unconditional: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." Jeremiah 22:24 says "though Coniah… were the signet upon my right hand, yet would I pluck thee thence."

[L3] Jeremiah 18:7–8 speaks of "that nation" and of conditional pronouncements. It does not touch 22:30. Haggai 2:23 sets the signet on a governor, never a king (Haggai 1:1).

[L1] The defender argues that the New Testament never cites the curse against Jesus. That is an appeal to silence. His own rule in Round 2 was "A gap bears no such weight: non-narration is not narration." He cannot use the gap against Matthew 1:16 and then use a gap here.

[L1] On Salathiel: Luke 3:27 gives Neri as his father and Matthew 1:12 gives Jechonias. [L4] The text gives two fathers for the name, so either they are two men or the descent is legal. In both cases Luke's line enters David through Nathan (Luke 3:31; 1 Chronicles 3:5), not Solomon. The defender concedes "the knot stays a knot." The knot sits on his D3, which puts Joseph's own seed in Jeconiah's line (Matthew 1:12–16). My reading does not tie it.

#### 8. What the defender never answered

[L1] Matthew 1:22–23 says "all this was done, that it might be fulfilled… Behold, a virgin shall be with child." [L2] The word is *hē parthenos*. The defender gave it no answer, and the word cannot stand beside a Joseph-begotten conception.

[L1] "A body hast thou prepared me" (Hebrews 10:5) names the prepared body, and the defender glossed it as "holiness" with no verse. [L1] "That holy thing which shall be born of thee" (Luke 1:35) sets Mary alone as the one the child is born of. [L1] "Her seed" (Genesis 3:15) is the one place the woman's seed is spoken without a man's, and the defender's rows (Genesis 16:10; 24:60) all carry a husband in the same context.

The defender must still produce one verse that says Joseph begat Jesus. Matthew 1:16 withholds it, Matthew 1:25 denies the knowing, and Luke 1:35 gives the source as the Spirit. Christ had no human biological father. His Father is God.

#### UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES

1. LINK 1: The inference from David's body to Joseph's body, in the words "cannot stand with no man," props the claim that the Davidic flesh seed passes through Joseph. Every cited row (2 Samuel 7:12; Psalm 132:11; Acts 2:30; Hebrews 7:14) stops at David or Judah, and no row exhibits Joseph's body.
2. LINK 2: "The Boy's answer is a covenant title, not a correction" props the claim that Luke 2:48 stands uncancelled as a father-voice. The only footing is the Solomon analogy, and Luke 2:49 sets "my Father's" against "thy father" in adjacent verses (John 5:18, *patera idion*).
3. LINK 3: "A genealogy that ends in a woman would break the record's own reckoning rule" props the claim that the registers run through Joseph and Mary cannot carry the seed. It is contradicted by Matthew 1:3, 5, 6, 16, and the cited rows (Numbers 1:2; Ezra 2:59–62) are priestly and tribal enrollment.
4. LINK 4: "the New Testament's *ek* + God/Spirit is covenant origin at every row" props the claim that Matthew 1:20 names no biological source. Every cited row concerns persons already born of fathers, and the generalization to *to en autē gennēthen* ("in her") supplies no footing for the first conception, which Matthew 1:20 alone describes.
5. LINK 5: "A title Solomon bore with a living father cannot decide whether Jesus had one" props the claim that "Son of God" settles nothing. It passes over *dio kai* in Luke 1:35 (the title grounded in the conception) and Acts 3:26 against the resurrection reading.
6. LINK 6: "the WHO is named and the HOW is never narrated" props the conclusion that Christ had a biological father. Matthew 1:18, 20, 25 and Luke 1:35 narrate the Spirit as source ("in her") and deny the knowing, and the defender conceded Luke's supposition-uses at Luke 3:23.
