## Round 3 - Defender (MiMo): link round

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

### LINK ROUND

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED.** [L1] The flesh seed is named as flesh: "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12); "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30). [L1] Joseph is placed in that line by the text itself: "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20); "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David" (Luke 1:27 — "of the house of David" attaches to Joseph); "Joseph also went up... because he was of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4). [L3] Reckoning runs by the father's house: "by the house of their fathers... every male by their polls" (Numbers 1:2, 18). Chain: the seed must be flesh from David's body [L1]; the named father is of the house and lineage of David [L1]; reckoning is by the father's house [L3] — therefore the flesh seed runs through the named father. The premise's demand for "Joseph's body" demands a narration the register gives for no father in it; the chain names the man and the line, which is what the text does.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED.** [L1] Mary's own mouth: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48). [L1] Scripture gives one man both voices: David, whose father Jesse "sent and called David" (1 Samuel 16:19), says to God "thou art my father, my God" (Psalm 89:26) — so "my Father's business" (Luke 2:49) is the covenant Son's claim and cancels no earthly father. [L1] The covenant title rides beside a living father in one verse: "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6); "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14). [L2] John 5:18's *patera idion* is the Jews' charge of making himself equal with God; it speaks nothing to the household of Luke 2. Two exhibited layers, and the premise's only footing (Luke 2:49 beside 2:48) is answered by Psalm 89:26.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED.** [L1] "By the house of their fathers... every male by their polls" (Numbers 1:2); "declared their pedigrees... by the house of their fathers" (1:18). [L3] The inverse register: those who "could not shew their father's house" were excluded, and priests named after a wife's house were "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:59, 61-62) — the wife's house cannot carry the register. [L1] Matthew's own chain is male throughout: thirty-nine "begat" verbs, "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus" (Matthew 1:16) — Thamar, Rachab, Ruth, and "her that had been the wife of Urias" are named as *mothers inside* the chain, never as its links; not one is a chain verb. [L2] Luke 3:23 "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was [the son] of Heli" — no female name in the line. Naming a mother is not reckoning through her: the chain's terminus is the father.

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED.** [L2] *Ek* + God/Spirit at every row is origin, never a paternity verb: "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 3:9; 5:1), "is of God" (1 John 4:7). The biological marker is *kata sarka* — present at Romans 1:3 ("according to the flesh") and absent at Matthew 1:20. [L1] Matthew 1:20's sentence hands the naming to the father: "thou shalt call his name JESUS" (1:21). Chain: the phrase "of (*ek*) the Holy Ghost" carries no flesh-marker at any row [L2]; where flesh is meant the text says "according to the flesh" [L1]; therefore 1:20 states covenant origin and the angel does not narrate a conception mechanism. The premise's objection — that my rows concern the already-born — misreads the claim, which is lexical: what the construction means wherever it appears. Its own reading (the Spirit as bodily source) has no row and adds a HOW.

**LINK 5: SUPPORTED.** [L1] "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6) — the son David begat and the son God made, in one verse; "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14). [L1] Luke 1:32 gives "the throne of his father David" and the Son-language *before* 1:34's question; 1:35 says "he shall be CALLED the Son of God." [L3] "This day have I begotten thee" is applied to the resurrection: "hath raised up Jesus again" (Acts 13:33; Romans 1:4 "declared to be the Son of God... by the resurrection"). On the two pressed objections: *dio kai* grounds the **called** title, and the title was already given at 1:32 with the throne; and the premise grants its own defeat — if Solomon's title "cannot decide," it cannot decide toward no father. Acts 3:26 "raised up his Son Jesus, sent him to bless you" uses the same "raised up" as Acts 3:22's quotation of Deuteronomy 18:15 ("A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto thee") — a sending row, not a conception row.

**LINK 6: SUPPORTED.** [L1] The father, four registers: Mary — "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48); the narrator — "Joseph and his mother" (Luke 2:33), "his parents" (2:41); the town — "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22), "Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know" (John 6:42); a disciple — "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45). [L1] The seed rows require flesh: Romans 1:3; Acts 2:30; "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14). [L2] The three rows the premise presses are held as written and none states its conclusion: Matthew 1:25 "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" is bounded by "till" within the sentence; 1:18 "before they came together"; Luke 1:34 "I know not a man" is present indicative — a state at the annunciation by an espoused woman (Luke 1:27). "Supposed" at Luke 3:23 is *nomizo* (G3543), the same verb form as Acts 16:13 "where prayer was **wont** to be made" — a reckoning word carrying no verdict of its own. Two exhibited layers: the named-father and seed rows at [L1], the word-level scope of 1:25/1:34 and *nomizo* at [L2]. The premise reads past "till" and past the present tense — those are the very narrations the text withholds.

### CHALLENGER'S UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES

1. That Joseph's naming is adoption, not fatherhood — "Joseph's naming (Matthew 1:21, 25) is the adopting father's act" — the exhibited footing (Luke 1:31; Matthew 1:20-21) gives a naming charge and a son-of-David address and no adoption verb anywhere; it props the claim that Joseph's fatherhood is legal only.
2. That the Salathiel divergence resolves into an either/or — "the text gives two fathers for the name, so either they are two men or the descent is legal" — the text states two fathers and states no alternative; the disjunction is constructed and props the claim that the Jeconiah knot dissolves the biological line.
3. That "till" means never — "the knowing is denied: 'knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son' (1:25)" — the word bounds the denial and the extension past the birth is not exhibited from any row; it props the no-father conclusion at premise 6.
4. That Mary's question excludes her espoused husband — "If Joseph were the intended father, the window would close at the marriage and the question 'How shall this be?' would be idle." — no verse states that a question about timing forbids the husband in the text's own picture of an espoused woman; it props the reading of Luke 1:34-35 as naming divine paternity.
5. That John 1:13's *andros* is the conception of Jesus — "John 1:13 itself sets 'of God' against 'the will of man' (*andros*)." — its subject is those who "were born, not of blood... but of God" after receiving him (John 1:12-13), believers, not the child in Mary; it props the "Spirit as bodily source" reading of Matthew 1:20.
6. That *parthenos* narrates a mechanism — "the word cannot stand beside a Joseph-begotten conception." — Matthew 1:23 names the sign's sign, bounded by Isaiah 7:16 and 8:3-4 ("Immanuel" recurs in Isaiah's own day), and no verse states that the word excludes the named father; it props the virgin-word as proof of how the child was begotten.
