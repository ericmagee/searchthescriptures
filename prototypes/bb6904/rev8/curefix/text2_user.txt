DEBATE TRANSCRIPT

=== TOPIC ===
Did Christ have a biological father?

=== [CR1] DEFENDER R1 ===
DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

# OPENING — THE DEFENDER: "Did Christ have a biological father?"

The question is answered by three kinds of rows: the rows that name the **seed**, the rows that name the **father**, and the rows that name the **titles**. I quote each and say what it states.

## 1. The descent is stated as FLESH, from a man's body [L1] [L2] [L3]

- **[L1]** Romans 1:3 — "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**." **[L2]** *ek spermatos Dauid kata sarka* — *sperma* (G4690) is the body's seed, and *kata sarka* is the marker of bodily origin Paul writes precisely where biological descent is meant.
- **[L1]** Acts 2:30 — "of the fruit of his **loins**, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne." **[L2]** *osphys* (G3751) is the same loins-word Hebrews 7:5 and 7:10 use of the sons "that come out of the loins of Abraham."
- **[L1]** 2 Samuel 7:12 — "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed **out of thy bowels**." **[L2]** *mimme'eykha* — from thy bowels, the body's own interior. **[L3]** Psalm 132:11 — "Of the **fruit of thy body** will I set upon thy throne."
- **[L1]** Hebrews 7:14 — "it is **evident** that our Lord sprang out of Juda"; 2 Timothy 2:8 — "Jesus Christ of the seed of David"; Genesis 49:10 — "The sceptre shall not depart from Judah."

A seed that proceeds out of loins and bowels "according to the flesh" and whose springing out of Judah is "evident" is a real descent from a man's body. No row states otherwise.

## 2. The father is NAMED, in every register of the text [L1] [L2] [L3]

Four registers name him: **Mary's own mouth** — "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48) [L1]; **the narrator** — "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33), "his parents went to Jerusalem" (2:41) [L1]; **the town** — "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" (John 6:42) [L1]; **a disciple** — "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45) [L1]; and **the angel** — "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20) [L1]. **[L3]** Luke 4:22 — "Is not this Joseph's son?"

**[L1]** Luke 1:27 — "a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, **of the house of David**"; Luke 2:4 — Joseph "was of the house and lineage of David"; Luke 1:32 — "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of **his father David**." **[L2]** *ex oikou David* follows the man Joseph in Luke 1:27. **[L3]** Lineage is reckoned "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 1:18). No row assigns Mary a lineage; the flesh is not routed through her.

## 3. The one passive verb is non-narration, not a negation [L1] [L2] [L3]

**[L1]** Matthew 1:16 — "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom was born Jesus**, who is called Christ." **[L2]** the register runs in the active *egennēsen* ("begat") through the generations; this clause alone is *egennēthē* (passive) with *ex hēs*. A passive that does not narrate the begetting says nothing about whether a man was involved; it cannot be set over the rows that name the father and the flesh. **[L1]** Luke 1:34 — "I know not a man" is present indicative (*andra ou ginōskō*): a state at the moment of speaking, she being espoused (Luke 1:27); **[L2]** a permanent vow would require *ou mē* + aorist subjunctive, which is not there. Matthew 1:19 records intent, not knowledge. **[L1]** Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman" is the mortality idiom — **[L3]** Job 14:1 "man that is born of a woman is of few days"; Matthew 11:11 uses it of John Baptist, whose father is named (Luke 1:13). None of these rows says "no man."

## 4. "Son of God" is a title, not a begetting [L1] [L2] [L3]

**[L1]** 1 Chronicles 28:6 — God to David: "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be **my son**" — in one verse the son David begat and the son God covenanted. **[L3]** 2 Samuel 7:14 — "I will be his father, and he shall be my son." Solomon had a living father. **[L1]** Acts 13:33 applies "this day have I begotten thee" to the resurrection; Romans 1:4 — "declared to be the Son of God... by the resurrection from the dead"; Luke 1:32 gives the throne "of his father David" before 1:34's question, and **[L2]** Luke 1:35 *klēthēsetai* — "shall be **called** the Son of God." The title therefore supplies no second, divine biological paternity.

## 5. The Spirit-rows name covenant origin and empowerment, never begetting [L1] [L2] [L3]

**[L1]** Matthew 1:20 — "that which is conceived in her is **of** (*ek*) the Holy Ghost." **[L2]** *ek* + God/Spirit across the New Testament is covenant/spiritual origin — John 1:13 "born of God," 1 John 4:7 "is of God," 1 John 5:1 "born of God" — never biological paternity; and where Paul means flesh he writes *kata sarka* (Romans 1:3), which that clause does not carry. **[L1]** Luke 1:35 — "the Holy Ghost shall **come upon** thee... the power of the Highest shall **overshadow** thee." **[L2]** *eperchomai* is the word of Acts 1:8 "the Holy Ghost is come upon you"; **[L3]** *episkiazō* is the word of the transfiguration cloud (Luke 9:34; Matthew 17:5) and of Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15). No row of either verb denotes begetting.

## ANSWER

**Yes.** Christ had a biological father: **Joseph**, the named Davidic father. The seed is David's "according to the flesh," proceeding out of loins and bowels, "evident" in its springing out of Judah. The record names the WHO and does not narrate the HOW; what it names forbids the claim that no man was involved.

## DECLARED CHAIN

D1. The descent is stated as flesh from a man's body — [L1] Romans 1:3 "of the seed of David according to the flesh," Acts 2:30 "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh"; [L2] *ek spermatos... kata sarka*, *osphys* (the loins-word of Hebrews 7:5, 10); [L3] 2 Samuel 7:12 "out of thy bowels," Psalm 132:11 "fruit of thy body," Hebrews 7:14 "evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda."

D2. That flesh-line is named to a named man, Joseph, of the house of David — [L1] Luke 1:27 "a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David," Luke 2:4 "of the house and lineage of David," Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David," Luke 1:32 "the throne of his father David"; [L2] *ex oikou David* follows the man Joseph; [L3] Numbers 1:2, 18 reckoning "by the house of their fathers."

D3. Joseph is called father in every register of the text — [L1] Luke 2:48 "thy father and I" (Mary's mouth), Luke 2:33 and 2:41 "Joseph and his mother" / "his parents" (narrator), John 6:42 "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know," John 1:45 "the son of Joseph" (disciple); [L2] the four rows in four speakers' voices; [L3] Luke 4:22 "Is not this Joseph's son?"

D4. "Son of God" is a covenant and resurrection title borne by a man with a living father, and so supplies no divine biological paternity — [L1] 1 Chronicles 28:6 "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son," Acts 13:33, Romans 1:4 "declared... by the resurrection"; [L2] *klēthēsetai* "shall be called" (Luke 1:35); [L3] 2 Samuel 7:14 "I will be his father, and he shall be my son."

D5. The Spirit-rows name covenant origin and empowerment, not begetting — [L1] Matthew 1:20 "of (*ek*) the Holy Ghost," Luke 1:35 "come upon" / "overshadow"; [L2] *ek* + God = spiritual origin (John 1:13; 1 John 4:7; 5:1) with *kata sarka* absent there and present at Romans 1:3, *eperchomai* = Acts 1:8, *episkiazō* = Luke 9:34 / Acts 5:15; [L3] Luke 2:48 "thy father," 1 John 3:9 "born of God."

D6. ANSWER — YES: Christ had a biological father, Joseph, the named Davidic father; the seed is David's according to the flesh, out of loins and bowels, and the text names the WHO without ever narrating the HOW — [L1] Romans 1:3, John 6:42, Luke 2:48, John 1:45, Matthew 1:20; [L2] *sperma* / *kata sarka* / *osphys*, the passive *egennēthē* non-narration at Matthew 1:16; [L3] 2 Samuel 7:12, Psalm 132:11, Hebrews 7:14.

=== [DR1] CHALLENGER R1 ===
CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

# CHALLENGER'S ANSWER: Christ had no biological father

I concede what the record states plainly. Joseph is called "father" in several voices, and Jesus is Joseph's son in law and by naming. I deny that any of these rows states biological paternity. The rows the Defender left out state the opposite.

## 1. The rows omitted from the opening

**[L1]** Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child **of the Holy Ghost**." **[L2]** *heurethē en gastri echousa ek pneumatos hagiou*. The pregnancy is found before any coming together, and its *ek* is the Spirit.

**[L1]** Matthew 1:25: Joseph "**knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son**." **[L2]** *ouk eginōsken autēn heōs hou eteken*. The imperfect is continuous non-knowledge up to the birth. **[L3]** *Ginōskō* is the sexual verb of Genesis 4:1 ("Adam **knew** Eve his wife"). The narrator closes the door on Joseph at the conception and the birth.

**[L1]** Matthew 1:23, Isaiah 7:14: "Behold, a **virgin** shall be with child." **[L2]** *parthenos*. Matthew says the whole sequence occurred "that it might be fulfilled" (1:22). The Defender's opening never quotes 1:22–23.

**[L1]** Luke 1:27: "a **virgin** espoused... the virgin's name was Mary." **[L2]** *parthenos* twice in one verse.

## 2. Matthew 1:16 is an exclusion, not a non-narration

**[L1]** Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom was born Jesus**." **[L2]** Thirty-nine times the chain runs *egennēsen* (active, masculine subject). At the last link the formula breaks. Joseph is *ton andra Marias* ("husband"), not "father of Jesus." The verb becomes passive *egennēthē*, and the relative is **feminine singular** *ex hēs*, so Mary is the only parent the grammar names. **[L4]** Footings: **[L2]** the break from active to passive and masculine to feminine at exactly this link, and **[L1]** Matthew 1:18–20, which the evangelist supplies immediately as the explanation of the break. A writer who wanted to say that Joseph begat Jesus had the formula in hand and did not use it. The Defender calls this "non-narration." It is the single place in the chain where narration is withheld, and verses 18–25 say why.

## 3. The "father" rows are the voices of supposition, and Jesus corrects them

- **[L1]** Luke 3:23: "being (**as was supposed**) the son of Joseph." **[L2]** *hōs enomizeto*, "as was supposed." Luke's own narrator qualifies the title the Defender relies on.
- **[L1]** Luke 2:48–49: Mary says "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." Jesus answers: "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?" **[L2]** *en tois tou patros mou*. The Defender cites "Mary's own mouth" and omits the very next verse, where the Boy sets the claim aside. **[L4]** Footings: **[L1]** 2:48 and 2:49 are adjacent, and **[L1]** Luke 1:35 has already told Mary whose Son he is. The contrast "thy father… my Father" is a correction, not an echo.
- **[L1]** John 6:42 and Luke 4:22 are the *crowd* ("they said"), who in the same verse stumble at "I came down from heaven." **[L1]** John 6:38 shows that their unbelief is the context. **[L4]** A row spoken by unbelief is not a row of testimony. **[L1]** John 8:41 shows the same crowd later insinuating "We be not born of fornication," which indicates the people knew the account of his birth did not run through Joseph.
- **[L1]** Matthew 1:21, 25: Joseph "**called** his name JESUS." **[L4]** Legal fatherhood is conferred by acknowledging and naming, on the angel's address "Joseph, thou son of David" (1:20). This explains every "father" row without biology. Footings: **[L1]** 1:20, 21, 25 and **[L2]** *goneis* "parents" (Luke 2:41) as the legal-household word.

## 4. Joseph's own conduct excludes him as father

**[L1]** Matthew 1:19: "Joseph her husband, being a just man... was **minded to put her away privily**." The Defender says this "records intent, not knowledge." **[L4]** A betrothed man does not resolve to dismiss a bride for a child he knows to be his. Footings: **[L1]** 1:18 ("before they came together") and **[L1]** 1:20 ("**fear not** to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost"). The angel answers Joseph's fear by naming the child's origin, and the origin named is not Joseph.

## 5. Luke 1:34–35 answers "how," and the answer excludes a man

**[L1]** Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" **[L2]** *ou ginōskō*. The Defender reads this as a present state only, with no vow. Grant that. The question is still *how shall this be*, set against a future conception. **[L1]** Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: **therefore also** that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." **[L2]** *dio kai*, "for which reason also." The title is grounded in the conception by the Spirit. **[L4]** If Mary expected Joseph's seed after the wedding, the question has no force, and Gabriel's reply would have been "Joseph." Footings: **[L1]** 1:27 (espoused, not yet married) and **[L1]** 1:35 (the answer names Spirit and power, not Joseph). **[L1]** Luke 1:37, "with God **nothing shall be impossible**," is the same formula as Genesis 18:14 ("Is any thing too hard for the LORD?") **[L3]**. It marks the event as a miracle, not as ordinary generation.

Against D5: **[L2]** *ek pneumatos hagiou* in Matthew 1:18, 20 is attached to *to gennēthen en autē*, "that which is begotten/conceived **in her**." It names the origin of a pregnancy. The Defender's comparison texts (John 1:13; 1 John 4:7; 5:1) are about regenerated believers, where the context says "not of blood" (John 1:13). **[L4]** These texts do not govern a clause about a womb. Footings: **[L2]** *en autē* and *en gastri echousa* locate the conception, and **[L1]** Matthew 1:25 separately excludes Joseph. The Defender's rule that Paul writes *kata sarka* where flesh is meant does not apply to Matthew, and Matthew 1:20 carries the Spirit as sole named source.

## 6. "According to the flesh" does not require Joseph's loins

**[L1]** Romans 1:3: "of the seed of David according to the flesh." **[L4]** This requires Davidic descent, not Joseph's. Footings: **[L3]** Genesis 3:15 names "her seed" with no man, so *sperma* is not male-only; **[L1]** Luke 1:42, "the fruit of **thy womb**" (*karpos tēs koilias sou*) speaks of Mary's body; **[L1]** Luke 3:31 gives a line "Nathan, which was the son of David," distinct from Matthew's line through Solomon (1:6).

**The decisive text is Jeremiah 22:30.** **[L1]** "Write ye this man (Coniah) childless... for **no man of his seed** shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." **[L2]** *zera'*, "seed." **[L1]** Matthew 1:11–12: "Josias begat **Jechonias**... Jechonias begat Salathiel," and Joseph is further down that same chain (1:16). **[L4]** If Jesus were Joseph's biological seed, he would be "of the seed" of the one barred from David's throne. Footings: **[L1]** Jeremiah 22:30 and **[L1]** Matthew 1:12–16, which exhibit the chain. Matthew's genealogy therefore reads as the **legal** line, by *Joseph's* naming and adoption. Luke's line through Nathan carries the Davidic flesh through Mary's house, "as was supposed the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" (Luke 3:23). The Defender's inference "Joseph, of David, therefore the flesh is Joseph's" is silent on Jeremiah 22:30, and the text refutes it.

**[L1]** Galatians 4:4: "God **sent forth** his Son, made of a woman." **[L2]** *exapesteilen... genomenon ek gynaikos*. The Son is sent before being born, so he pre-exists his mother, and only a woman is named at his coming. **[L1]** Hebrews 10:5: "a **body hast thou prepared me**" (*sōma katērtisō moi*) names God as the preparer of his body. The Defender's "mortality idiom" (Job 14:1) cannot explain why the Son is "sent forth" first.

## 7. "Son of God" is not Solomon's adoption

**[L1]** 1 Chronicles 28:6: "I have **chosen** him to be my son" is adoption by selection of an already-existing man. **[L1]** Romans 8:32: "He that spared not his **own** Son." **[L2]** *tou idiou huiou*, "his own." **[L1]** John 5:18: "God was his Father (*patera idion*), making himself equal with God," where the Jews understood the claim as proper sonship. **[L3]** Romans 8:15 reserves *huiothesia* (adoption) for believers. **[L1]** Hebrews 1:5: "unto which of the angels said he at any time, Thou art my Son, **this day have I begotten thee**?" is a sonship no angel and no adopted king shares.

Against Acts 13:33 as "resurrection begetting": **[L1]** Acts 13:33: "God hath raised up (*anastēsas*) Jesus," and **[L1]** v. 34 begins a new clause: "And **as concerning that he raised him up from the dead**" (*ek nekrōn*). The resurrection is introduced as a *further* point in 13:34. (KJV's "again" in 13:33 is a supplied word.) **[L3]** Acts 3:26, "having raised up his Son Jesus," uses the same verb of his appearing, before any death. **[L1]** Romans 1:4: "*declared* (*horisthentos*) to be the Son of God... by the resurrection." Something declared is already so.

## 8. The "no mention of Mary's lineage" argument

**[L1]** Luke 1:32: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David," spoken to *Mary* **[L1]** (1:30–31), not to Joseph. **[L4]** The promise of David's throne is made to the one through whom the Son comes. Footings: **[L1]** 1:30–32 (addressed to Mary) and **[L1]** 1:35 (the Son is born "of thee").

## ANSWER

**No.** Christ had no biological father. Joseph was his legal father by betrothal, naming, and acknowledgment. The text states the conception as *ek pneumatos hagiou* (Matthew 1:18, 20), excludes Joseph's knowledge to the birth (1:25), gives the virgin as the fulfillment (1:22–23), answers Mary's "how" with the Spirit and not with Joseph (Luke 1:34–35), and breaks its own begetting formula at Matthew 1:16.

## CHALLENGER'S CHAIN

C1. Matthew 1:18, 20, 23, 25 and Luke 1:27, 34–35 state a conception by the Holy Ghost, with a virgin and with Joseph not knowing her until after the birth. [L1] [L2] *parthenos*, *ouk eginōsken... heōs hou eteken*, *ek pneumatos hagiou*. [L3] Isaiah 7:14.

C2. Matthew 1:16 breaks the active, masculine chain to *egennēthē ex hēs* (feminine singular), and Matthew 1:18–20 explains why. [L1] [L2] [L4] (footings: the grammatical break and the explanatory verses).

C3. The "father" rows are the voices of supposition, and Jesus corrects them. [L1] Luke 3:23 *enomizeto*; Luke 2:48–49 "thy father... my Father"; John 6:42; [L2] *enomizeto*, *patros mou*.

C4. Joseph's resolve to put Mary away (Matthew 1:19) and the angel's "fear not" (1:20) exclude him as father. [L1] [L4] (footings: Matthew 1:18–20).

C5. Luke 1:34–35 (*dio kai*) answers "how" with the Spirit's coming and grounds the title "Son of God" in that conception. [L1] [L2] [L3] Genesis 18:14.

C6. Davidic flesh does not require Joseph's seed, and Joseph's seed would descend through Jechonias, barred by Jeremiah 22:30. [L1] Jeremiah 22:30, Matthew 1:11–16, Luke 3:23, 31; [L2] *zera'*; [L3] Genesis 3:15.

C7. Christ is God's "own Son" (*idios*), not Solomon's adopted kind, and is "sent forth" before being "made of a woman." [L1] Romans 8:32, John 5:18, Galatians 4:4, Hebrews 1:5, 10:5; [L2] *idiou huiou*, *exapesteilen*; [L3] Romans 8:15.

**Burden.** The Defender must exhibit a verse stating that Joseph's seed produced Jesus. Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30 state Davidic descent, not Joseph's, and Matthew 1:11–16 with Jeremiah 22:30 is the verse-level reason the descent cannot run through Joseph's loins. If those two statements are not answered by quoted text, the Defender's D1–D3 stand only as legal descent.

=== [CR2] DEFENDER R2 ===
DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

# ROUND 2 — THE DEFENDER: The record names the WHO and refuses both closures

The challenger's answer does two things no row does: it reads four narrative rows as **negations** ("therefore no father"), and it demands **one verse that states** Joseph's seed produced Jesus. Both are refused. The record's rule of engagement is precept upon precept, line upon line (Isaiah 28:10): the chain is the form of proof, and a demand for a single stating verse is not a standard the text meets for anything — no single verse states the Trinity, the resurrection body, or the seed's path. What the text gives is the chain in D1–D6, and no link of it has been answered with a quoted row.

## 1. C1 — the three narrative rows are held as written, and the addition is refused [L1] [L2] [L3]

**[L1]** Matthew 1:18 — "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost"; Matthew 1:25 — "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son"; Luke 1:34 — "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" These stand **as written**. What each states is a **state at a time**: a found state before the coming together, non-knowledge bounded by "till," and a present-tense "I know not" from an espoused virgin (Luke 1:27). **[L2]** Luke 1:34 is present indicative (*andra ou ginōskō*); a permanent vow requires *ou mē* + aorist subjunctive, which is absent. **[L2]** Matthew 1:25's *heōs hou* bounds the non-knowledge at the birth, and the same verse gives the father's act: "he called his name JESUS" — as the angel commanded "THOU shalt call his name JESUS" (1:21), and as Zacharias named John (Luke 1:13; 1:62-63). **[L1]** Luke 2:48, from the mouth of the woman who received the announcement, twelve years later in the same Gospel: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing."

Not one of these rows says "no man." The sentence that does is not in the text. The record refuses **both** closures — "no man was involved" and any narrated how — and I affirm only the WHO the text names.

## 2. The *ek* clause is covenant-origin, not paternity [L1] [L2] [L3]

**[L1]** Matthew 1:20 — "that which is conceived in her is of (*ek*) the Holy Ghost." **[L2]** Across the New Testament *ek* + God/Spirit is covenant/spiritual origin: John 1:13 "born of God," 1 John 4:7 "is of God," 1 John 5:1 "born of God." Where Paul means bodily descent he writes the marker *kata sarka* — Romans 1:3 "of the seed of David **according to the flesh**" — and that marker is absent from Matthew 1:20. **[L2]** The row names where the child was carried (*en gastri*, *en autē*); the womb is Mary's in every row — Luke 1:42 "the fruit of thy womb" — and no row names the Spirit as a biological begetter. The clause names the covenant origin of the holy thing (Luke 1:35 "that holy thing"); the row does not say "not Joseph," and the record does not supply a how in either direction.

## 3. Matthew 1:16 — one passive against the chorus [L1] [L2] [L3]

**[L1]** Matthew 1:16 — "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." **[L2]** the register runs *egennēsen* (active) thirty-nine times; at this link the birth is **from Mary**, so the clause names her — *egennēthē*, *ex hēs*. A passive that names the birth's source is not a denial of a father; it is the one link where the text does not narrate the begetting, and the same sentence names "Joseph the husband of Mary." **[L3]** the register's purpose is Matthew 1:1 — "Jesus Christ, the son of David" — the Davidic standing established **through the named line**. **[L1]** The chorus the one passive is set against: Mary's mouth (Luke 2:48), the narrator (Luke 2:33 "Joseph and his mother"; 2:41 "his parents"), the town (John 6:42 "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know"; Luke 4:22 "Is not this Joseph's son?"), a disciple (John 1:45), and the angel (Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David"). Enthroning one quiet verb over five named registers is the move the record forbids.

## 4. The "supposition" and "unbelief" arguments invert the text [L1] [L2] [L3]

**[L2]** *nomizo* (G3543) is the lexicon's "to do by law (usage)... to accustom (passively, be usual); by extension, to deem or regard" — the identical form *enomizeto* at Acts 16:13 "where prayer was **wont** to be made." It is a custom-word; the custom it names is the reckoning "by the house of their fathers" (**[L1]** Numbers 1:2, 18). **[L1]** Luke's own narrator writes "Joseph and his mother" (2:33) and "his parents" (2:41) — those are not the crowd's words. **[L1]** John 1:45 "the son of Joseph" is spoken by a disciple in the same confession that runs to "Rabbi, thou art the Son of God, thou art the King of Israel" (1:49) — both titles in one mouth, exactly as God gives David "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6). **[L1]** John 8:41 "we be not born of fornication" is a slur in a dispute — an insult, not testimony, and no row makes it a mechanism; the inference "the people knew the birth did not run through Joseph" is an addition no footing exhibits, and it floats.

## 5. Luke 2:48-49 — two fathers, one verse apart [L1] [L2] [L3]

**[L1]** Luke 2:48 "thy father and I"; 2:49 "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" **[L2]** *en tois tou patros mou*. The Boy does not say "Joseph is not my father"; he names a second Father's business — at the temple, at twelve. **[L3]** 1 Chronicles 28:6 holds the pair in one verse: the son David begat and the son God covenanted. **[L1]** Luke 1:32 gives the throne "of **his father David**" **before** 1:34's question, and **[L2]** 1:35 says the child "shall be **called**" (*klēthēsetai*) the Son of God — a title announced in the same breath as the father David. The *dio kai* cannot ground in a fatherless conception a title the same announcement already gave two verses earlier.

## 6. Isaiah 7:14 does not carry a how [L2] [L1] [L3]

**[L2]** Isaiah 7:14 in the received Hebrew reads *ha-almah* (H5959) — the young-woman word, 7 rows in the canon — while *bethulah* (H1330) is the virgin word at 50 rows across 19 books, 9 in the Torah; the 1611 margin reads "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call." **[L1]** the sign is bounded in Isaiah's own day: 7:16 "before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken"; 8:3-4 "I went unto the prophetess; and she conceived, and bare a son... before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother"; 8:8 "the stretching out of his wings shall fill the breadth of thy land, O Immanuel." **[L3]** the near-sign and the name recur in the prophet's own generation.
