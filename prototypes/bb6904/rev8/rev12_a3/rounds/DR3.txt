## Round 3 - Challenger (Claude): link round

CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED.**

*Footing.*
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." Matthew 1:25: Joseph "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"
- [L2] *sunelthein* (G4905, "come together") is the verb for marital union (1 Corinthians 7:5, "come together again"). *Ginōskō* (G1097) with *andra* (G435, "man") in Luke 1:34 is the Hebrew idiom *yada'* (Genesis 4:1, which the defender cited). *Heurethē en gastri echousa ek pneumatos hagiou* is a conception with *ek* + Spirit as its stated source.
- [L3] Matthew 1:20 uses *to gar en autē gennēthen* ("that which is conceived in her"). This is the same root *genna-* as the genealogy's "begat," and its source is *ek pneumatos hagiou*. The defender's own rule is that *ek* + Spirit is "covenant origin" (John 1:13). John 1:13 itself negates "the will of man" (*andros*) as the origin.

*Reply to the pressed sentence.* The defender says the interval "has narrated what the conception was not." That is not the whole of what I claim. Matthew 1:18 gives the interval and the source together. Matthew 1:20 gives the source in the begetting verb itself, which Joseph's fear at "take unto thee Mary thy wife" requires. [L4] The chain is: the interval (1:18, 1:25) leaves no room for Joseph. The verb *gennēthen* is the genealogy's begetting verb. Its source is *ek pneumatos* (1:20). So the begetting is assigned to the Spirit, and the premise stands on a stated source, not on silence.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED.**

*Footing.*
- [L1] Luke 1:34-35. Mary asks about a man. The answer is positive: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- [L2] *Dio kai* ("therefore also") is causal and ties the child's identity to the stated agent. *Episkiasei* (G1982) and *epeleusetai* (G1904) are agent verbs. Their subject is God, and the sentence has no male subject.
- [L3] Luke 1:36-37 supplies the contrast. Elisabeth conceived with her husband Zacharias, and her wonder lay in her age. Mary's case is introduced by "with God nothing shall be impossible" (cf. Genesis 18:14).

*Reply to the pressed sentence.* The defender says I make "omission decisive." The angel does not omit. He names the agent (Spirit and power) and the consequence (*dio kai*). [L4] The chain is: the question asks for a man (Luke 1:34). The answer supplies God as agent with a causal "therefore" (1:35). Elisabeth's contrasting case (1:36) shows that a husband's part is not the sign in Mary's case. So the answer settles the source by statement.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED, narrowed to what the verses state.**

I do not claim a stated lineage of Mary. I concede that none is named. The premise is that Mary is the only human source the text names for the body, and that *ek sou* belongs with Paul's pairing.

*Footing.*
- [L1] Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman." Romans 1:3: "concerning his Son… made of the seed of David according to the flesh." Luke 1:35: "that holy thing which shall be born of thee."
- [L2] Both Pauline clauses use *genomenon/genomenou ek* (G1096 + G1537). The first has *ek gynaikos* and the second *ek spermatos Dauid*. Luke 1:35 has *to gennōmenon ek sou*.
- [L3] Genesis 3:15 has "her seed" (*zar'ah*, a woman's seed). Hebrews 2:16 has "he took on him the seed of Abraham" (*epilambanetai*).

*Reply to the pressed sentence.* The defender says no lineage of Mary's is stated, and that is correct. [L4] But the chain does not need one. Paul uses the same verb and the same *ek* for one Son twice. The only human source named in Galatians 4:4 is a woman. The only line named in Romans 1:3 is David's, and Joseph is not called its begetter there. Luke 1:35 puts the birth *ek sou*. So the text names the woman as the human source and never names Joseph as begetter. The Davidic claim rests on this and on Joseph's legal reckoning (Matthew 1:20, "Joseph, thou son of David").

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED.**

*Footing.*
- [L1] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me." Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph." Matthew 1:16: "Joseph the husband of Mary."
- [L2] *Katērtisō* (G2675, "fit, frame") has God as its subject. *Enomizeto* (G3543, "was thought by custom") marks Joseph's sonship as a legal supposition, not as fact.
- [L3] Isaiah 7:14 (*'almah*, "a virgin shall conceive") is quoted at Matthew 1:23 with *parthenos* (G3933). That is a sign stated of the woman.

*Reply to the pressed sentence.* The defender says the premise "rests on what is not said." The text says what God did (Hebrews 10:5) and says Joseph's sonship was "supposed" (Luke 3:23). [L4] The chain is: Hebrews 10:5 gives God as framer of the body. Luke 3:23 marks Joseph's sonship as supposed, not begotten. Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30 name David's seed, and no verse names Joseph as the begetter. So the flesh rows do not supply a male source.

**LINK 5: SUPPORTED.**

*Footing.*
- [L2] In Matthew 1:2-16 the active *egennēsen* runs unbroken to "Jacob begat Joseph." Then *ex hēs egennēthē Iēsous* follows. *Hēs* is feminine singular, so its antecedent can only be Mary. *Ex hou* would have named Joseph.
- [L1] Matthew 1:18 reads "the birth (*genesis*) of Jesus Christ was on this wise," and 1:20 reads "that which is conceived (*gennēthen*) in her is of the Holy Ghost."

*Reply to the pressed sentence.* The defender says a passive "names no agent" and denies nothing. [L4] The chain is: the grammar is the observed fact (the verb, relative pronoun and subject all change at 1:16). The next verses name the agent of the same verb (*gennēthen*) as the Spirit (1:18, 1:20). So the verdict is stated by the passage's own interpretation of its break.

**LINK 6: SUPPORTED, narrowed.**

I concede that Ruth 4:13 narrates the begetting by Boaz. The premise is only that legal sonship can carry name and inheritance without the reckoned father's seed.

*Footing.*
- [L1] Deuteronomy 25:6: "the firstborn which she beareth shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead." Genesis 48:5: "Ephraim and Manasseh… are mine, as Reuben and Simeon." Esther 2:7: Mordecai "took for his own daughter." Exodus 2:10: "he became her son." Jeremiah 22:30: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David."
- [L2] *Qum* (H6965) in Deuteronomy 25:6 is "stand up/succeed" in the dead man's name. This is a legal succession.
- [L3] Matthew 1:11-12 puts Joseph in the line of Jechonias, the man of Jeremiah 22:30.

*Reply to the pressed sentence.* The defender says the premise "outruns both rows." [L4] The chain is: Deuteronomy 25:6 and Genesis 48:5 show reckoning, and no seed from the reckoned father, carrying name and tribal standing. Joseph is in Jechonias's line (Matthew 1:12), and Jeremiah 22:30 bars the throne to his seed. So the Davidic standing of Jesus can pass through Joseph's legal reckoning (Matthew 1:20), with the body from God's work in Mary.
