## Round 1 - Challenger (Claude)

CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

### CHALLENGER'S ANSWER: THE RECORD'S STANDARD READING

**Position:** No. Jesus had no biological human father. Joseph is his **legal** father: husband of the mother, namer of the child, and conduit of the Davidic reckoning. The seed of the body is Mary's, by the Holy Ghost. The defender's chain fails at D1 (flesh must come through Joseph), at D4 (*ek pneumatos* is only "covenant") and at D5 ("the text narrates no how"). The text narrates the how, in Matthew 1:18-25 and Luke 1:34-35.

---

#### I. The defender's "no how" is contradicted by the text's own announcement of a how

**1. Matthew 1:18 announces a narration.** [L1] "Now the birth of Jesus Christ **was on this wise**: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." [L2] *hē genesis houtōs ēn* ("the genesis was thus") picks up *biblos geneseōs* in 1:1. Matthew is saying that the genesis the genealogy has been tracing is now to be explained, and that it happened in this manner. [L4] A text that says "on this wise" and then gives a sequence cannot be "un-narrated." Footing: Matt 1:1 and 1:18 (the same noun, *genesis*), and the *houtōs* of 1:18 introducing 1:18-25.

**2. The sequence is bracketed against Joseph.** [L1] Matt 1:18 has "before they came together" (*prin ē synelthein autous*), and Matt 1:25 has "knew her not **till** she had brought forth." [L3] *ginōskō* is the Old Testament idiom for union. Gen 4:1: "Adam **knew** Eve his wife; and she conceived." Gen 4:17 and 1 Sam 1:19 have the same pattern, and Judges 11:39 has "she **knew no man**" for virginity. [L4] In Genesis, knowing precedes conceiving. In Matthew 1:18-25, conceiving precedes any knowing: she is "found with child" first, and Joseph "knew her not" until the birth. Footing: Gen 4:1 (the idiom) and Matt 1:18, 25 (the order reversed). On the defender's reading Joseph begat Jesus, yet the text says he did not know her before the birth.

**3. Joseph's own reaction, with the angel's reason.** [L1] Matt 1:19: "Joseph her husband... was minded to put her away privily." Matt 1:20: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: **for** (*gar*) that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." [L4] The angel's *gar* gives the reason for "fear not." If Joseph were the father, the reason would be empty. Footing: Matt 1:19 (Joseph's suspicion) and Matt 1:20 (the *gar* clause).

#### II. D4 fails: *ek pneumatos* in Matthew 1 is source of the begotten, not mere "covenant"

**4. The defender's own verb.** [L2] The defender calls *gennaō* the "begetting-verb" of the thirty-nine. Matt 1:20 reads *to gar en autē gennēthen ek pneumatos estin hagiou*: the thing **begotten in her** is **out of** the Holy Spirit. [L1] Matt 1:18 has *en gastri echousa ek pneumatos hagiou*, "with child (in womb having) of the Holy Ghost." [L4] This is womb-language and begetting-language, not "covenant origin." Footing: Matt 1:20 (*gennēthen*, *en autē*) and Matt 1:18 (*en gastri*).

**5. One preposition, one chapter.** [L1] Matt 1:16: "Mary, **of whom** (*ex hēs*) was born Jesus." [L4] The defender must read *ek* biologically for Mary at 1:16 and covenantally for the Spirit at 1:18 and 1:20, though both attach to the same child in the same section. Footing: Matt 1:16 (*ex hēs egennēthē*) and Matt 1:20 (*ek pneumatos*). His John 1:13 and 1 John 5:1 are regeneration of believers, where no womb and no *gennēthen en autē* appears.

**6. Matthew 1:16 is a break, not a silence.** [L2] *tou andra Marias, ex hēs egennēthē Iēsous*: the relative *hēs* is **feminine singular**, so "of whom" is Mary alone, not Joseph. The defender's own count is thirty-nine "Jacob begat Joseph" formulas. [L1] The fortieth, "Joseph begat Jesus," is the one the formula demands, and it is absent. [L4] The sentence changes in four ways at the one place a "begat" was due: it gives "husband" for "father," a feminine relative for a masculine antecedent, a passive for an active, and Mary as the source. Footing: Matt 1:2-16 (the pattern) and Matt 1:16 (*hēs*, *egennēthē*).

**7. Isaiah 7:14, which the defender never touches.** [L1] Matt 1:22-23: "Now **all this** was done, that it might be fulfilled... Behold, a **virgin** shall be with child." [L2] *parthenos* (G3933), and *ha'almah* (H5959) with the article, "*the* virgin." The "all this" (*touto holon*) takes in the whole sequence of 1:18-21, "before they came together" included. [L1] Isa 7:11 sets the sign's scale: "ask it either in the depth, or in the height above." [L1] Luke 1:27 uses *parthenos* twice, in the narrator's own voice and not Mary's, for Mary at the moment of the annunciation.

#### III. D5 fails: Luke 1:34-35 asks "how" and the angel answers it

**8.** [L1] Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: **therefore also** (*dio kai*) that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." [L2] *Dio* is causal: the Spirit's coming is the ground of the title. *Epei* ("seeing") in 1:34 states the obstacle. [L2] *Andra* is "a man," not "my husband," so she denies any man and the angel names no man. [L3] For *ginōskō* of virginity, see Judges 11:39 and Gen 19:8. [L4] The defender says the text gives "no how." Luke 1:35 is the angel's answer to "How?" and it names the Spirit and the power in place of a man. Footing: Luke 1:34 (*epei*, *andra*) and Luke 1:35 (*dio*, subject of the verbs).

**9. The Solomon parallel.** [L1] 1 Chr 28:6: "I have chosen him to be my son." [L4] Solomon's title is an election made after birth. Luke 1:35's title follows "therefore" from the conception event itself. The parallel fails at *dio*. Footing: 1 Chr 28:6 (election) and Luke 1:35 (*dio*). Acts 13:33 does not exclude the conception. [L1] Luke 3:22 and Luke 9:35 also give "my beloved Son" at other moments. A title spoken at several points is not confined to one.

**10. Hebrews 10:5.** [L1] "When he cometh into the world, he saith... **a body hast thou prepared me**." [L2] *katērtisō*, "fitted, prepared" (cf. Heb 11:3), is predicated of God. [L1] John 1:14, "the Word was made flesh," and Gal 4:4, "God **sent forth** his Son, made of a woman," show a Son who exists before he is sent. [L4] A person generated as Joseph's offspring is not "sent forth." Footing: Gal 4:4 (*exapesteilen*) and Heb 10:5 (*katērtisō*).

#### IV. D3 fails: "named father" is reported speech, and the text corrects it

**11. Luke 2:48-49 corrects itself in one exchange.** [L1] Mary says "thy father and I." [L1] Jesus answers at once: "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?" [L4] Mary's phrase is legal and household usage. The only answer the text records puts the two fatherhoods against each other. Footing: Luke 2:48 and 2:49. I grant that *goneis* ("parents," Luke 2:41) and Joseph's naming are real legal fatherhood.

**12. The narrator never says "his father."** [L1] Luke 2:33: "Joseph and his mother." Luke 2:43: "Joseph and his mother knew not of it." [L1] Luke 3:23 is the narrator's own gloss on all the "son of Joseph" lines: "being (**as was supposed**, *hōs enomizeto*) the son of Joseph." [L2] *Nomizō* is "to hold by custom or opinion," and Luke 3:23 qualifies the very phrase the defender quotes from Luke 4:22, John 1:45 and John 6:42. [L1] John 6:42 is a murmuring against Jesus' heavenly claim: "How is it then that he saith, I **came down from heaven**?" [L1] Mark 6:3 reports the same Nazareth speech as "the son of **Mary**."

**13. Luke 3:23 supports this without resting on it.** [L2] In the Greek, *Iōsēph* has no article, while every later name has *tou* (*tou Hēli, tou Maththat*...). [L4] I put this forward as support only, since the genealogy question is contested. The case stands on 1-12 without it.

#### V. D1 and D2 fail: Davidic standing is secured without Joseph's seed, and Joseph's seed would block it

**14. Jeremiah 22:30.** [L1] "Write ye this man (Coniah) childless... for **no man of his seed** shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." [L1] Matt 1:11-12, 16 puts Jechonias in Joseph's line. [L2] *mizzar'o*, "of his seed," is the same *zera'* the defender relies on. [L1] Luke 1:32 promises Jesus the throne of David. [L4] If Joseph's body supplied the seed, Jesus is "of his seed" in the curse's own terms. The text secures the throne by a different route: a legal line through Joseph (Matt 1:16, 20-21, 24-25) and flesh descent through Mary. Footing: Jer 22:30 (the bar) and Matt 1:11-16 (Joseph in Jechonias's line).

**15. The defender's own proofs are legal reckoning.** [L1] Ruth 4:10: Ruth is taken "to raise up the name of the dead." [L1] Ruth 4:17: "There is a son born to Naomi." [L3] Deut 25:6: the firstborn "shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead." [L3] Zerubbabel is "son of Pedaiah" in 1 Chr 3:19 and "son of Shealtiel" in Ezra 3:2, Matt 1:12 and Luke 3:27. [L3] Exod 2:10: "he became her son." Gen 48:5: "thy two sons... are mine." Esth 2:7: "took for his own daughter." [L1] Num 36:8: a daughter with an inheritance marries within her father's family. [L4] Reckoning "by the father's house" (Num 1:2, 18) is exactly what a legal father supplies. The defender's Ezra 2:61-62 shows that reckoning is by registered house, not that biology is required. Footing: Ruth 4:10, 17 and Deut 25:6 (legal father distinct from begetter) with Zerubbabel (Ezra 3:2 against 1 Chr 3:19).

**16. "Fruit of the loins" does not require Joseph.** [L1] Heb 7:10: Levi "was yet in the loins of his father" when Melchisedec met Abraham, generations earlier. [L1] Luke 3:31 puts Nathan, son of David, in the line (Luke 3:23-38 as Mary's descent is a reading, and 3:23 is not clear on it). [L1] Luke 1:32: "the Lord God shall **give** unto him the throne" (*dōsei*). [L2] Heb 7:14 *anatetalken* means "has risen" (as the sun or the branch), not "has been sired." [L4] "Loins of David" is satisfied by any descendant through either parent, and Rom 1:3 *genomenou* ("made") is not *gennaō*. Footing: Heb 7:10 (the loins idiom) and Luke 1:32 (the throne is given).

**17. Genesis 3:15 is not the only seed of a woman.** [L1] Gen 16:10 (Hagar, "thy seed"), Gen 24:60 (Rebekah, "let thy seed possess"), and Lev 12:2 ("If a woman have conceived seed," *tazria'*). [L4] The defender's claim of "the one row" in the early canon is false, so his argument from silence on women's seed is empty. Footing: Gen 16:10, 24:60 and Lev 12:2.

#### CHALLENGER'S DECLARED CHAIN

**C1.** The text narrates a how and excludes Joseph before the birth. [L1] Matt 1:18 "on this wise... before they came together"; Matt 1:25 "knew her not till"; Matt 1:19-20 (Joseph's fear and the *gar*). [L2] *hē genesis houtōs ēn*; *prin ē synelthein*. [L3] Gen 4:1 and Judges 11:39 for *ginōskō*. [L4] Conception precedes any union, so Joseph's union does not account for the child.

**C2.** *Ek pneumatos hagiou* names the source of the begotten in the womb. [L1] Matt 1:16, 18, 20. [L2] *gennēthen en autē*; *ex hēs* alongside *ek pneumatos*. [L4] One preposition and one verb mark source in the same section.

**C3.** The Matthew 1:16 break and Isaiah 7:14 mark a virginal conception. [L1] Matt 1:16, 22-23; Luke 1:27, 34-35. [L2] feminine *hēs*; *parthenos*; *ha'almah*; *dio*; *epei*; *andra*. [L3] Isa 7:11, 14; Judges 11:39.

**C4.** "Son of Joseph" is legal or reported, never the narrator's own claim. [L1] Luke 2:48-49; Luke 3:23; John 6:42; Mark 6:3. [L2] *hōs enomizeto*. [L4] Reported usage alone does not establish paternity, and Jesus' answer in Luke 2:49 sets his Father against it.

**C5.** Davidic standing is secured without Joseph's seed, and Joseph's seed would conflict with Jer 22:30. [L1] Jer 22:30; Matt 1:11-16; Luke 1:32; Heb 7:10. [L2] *zera'*; *dōsei*. [L3] Ruth 4:10, 17; Deut 25:6; Ezra 3:2 against 1 Chr 3:19.

**C6.** ANSWER: No. Christ had no biological human father. Joseph is the legal father who names him and carries the Davidic reckoning, and the flesh is Mary's by the Holy Ghost. The defender's D1, D4 and D5 are each answered at the verse level above.
