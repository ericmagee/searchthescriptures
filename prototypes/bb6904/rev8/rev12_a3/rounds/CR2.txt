## Round 2 - Defender (MiMo)

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

### ROUND 2 — THE DEFENDER ANSWERS

**Standing answer: Yes — Joseph, son of David, the named biological father; the seed honored through the named line; the how left un-narrated, as the text leaves it.** Below I answer each link of the opposing chain. One general observation governs: the opposing case must do two things the text never does — **name a mechanism** and **route the flesh through a lineage the text never states**. Every one of its strongest sentences is an inference laid over silence.

---

#### Answer to C1 — Matt 1:18-25 narrates circumstances and timing, not a mechanism

[L1] **Matthew 1:18** — "Now the birth of Jesus Christ **was on this wise**: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was **found with child** of the Holy Ghost."

Read the verse as written: the "wise" heads a narrative of **events** — espousal, discovery, Joseph's intent, the angel's speech, the naming. "Before they came together" (*prin ē synelthein autous*) is a **timing** statement about cohabitation; "found with child" is a **discovery** statement. Neither names a mechanism. [L2] *Synelthein* is coming-together as cohabitation; a statement of when cohabitation had not yet occurred is not a statement of how conception occurred. Genesis's *yada'* rows (Gen 4:1; 1 Sam 1:19; Judges 11:39) establish the idiom for union — they do not convert Matthew's negative timing clause into a positive mechanism.

[L1] **Matthew 1:19** — "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." [L2] *dikaios ōn, mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai, eboulēthē* — character, intent, resolution. There is **no verb of knowledge** in the row. A man's intent on an unexplained pregnancy is recorded; his suspicion is not endorsed by the narrator as fact. [L1] The angel's *gar* ("for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost") answers "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" — the reason to receive her is that the child is **consecrated of God**. [L3] The same divine-formation language is spoken of men with named fathers: **Jeremiah 1:5** "before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee" — of Jeremiah, **whose father is named** ("Jeremiah the son of Hilkiah," 1:1); **Isaiah 49:5** "that formed me from the womb to be his servant"; **Psalm 139:13** "thou hast covered me in my mother's womb." "Of the Holy Ghost" at Matthew 1:18, 20 stands in that company: divine consecration and origin, never the exclusion of a father.

[L1] **Matthew 1:25** — "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and **he called his name JESUS**." Held as written. And notice the actor at the end: **Joseph names the child** — the father's act, the same act the angel prescribes to Zacharias (Luke 1:13; 62-63, "signs to his father, how he would have him called").

**The challenger's own sentence, "the text narrates the how," is the claim the text must be made to carry — and no verse in Matthew 1:18-25 states a how.** It states before, found, fear, take, call. [L1] The sequence's four changes at 1:16 are real observations; but a **break in a list is silence, and silence is not a witness.** [L3] It cannot outweigh four registers naming the father (Luke 2:48, 33; John 6:42; John 1:45; Matthew 1:20) and the flesh rows demanding a seed of the loins (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30). [L4] To read a break as a denial is to enthrone one non-narration against a chorus — and it is a denial the sentence itself does not make ("of whom was born Jesus" names the **bearer**; it does not say "and there was no father").

#### Answer to C2 — *ek pneumatos* is source-language of consecration; the womb is Mary's; the father is named elsewhere

[L2] The pattern is measurable: *ek* + God/Spirit in the New Testament marks **covenant/origin**, never biological paternity — **John 1:13** "born of God"; **1 John 4:7** "is of God"; **1 John 5:1** "born of God." The challenger grants these are regeneration rows — precisely so; that is how the construction speaks. And where the text means **bodily** descent it adds the qualifier: **Romans 1:3** "*kata sarka*"; **Acts 2:30** "according to the flesh." **Matthew 1:20 has no *kata sarka*.** The qualifier the canon uses for biological origin is absent at the exact place it would settle this.

I do not read *ek* biologically at 1:16. [L1] "**Of whom was born Jesus**" (*ex hēs egennēthē*) names the **mother as the bearer** — the child was born of her, as Luke 2:7 narrates ("she brought forth her firstborn son"). A mother's bearing is not the exclusion of a father. [L3] The rest of the canon pairs a divine-conceived/born-of-a-woman child with a named father: **Matthew 11:11** of John the Baptist, "born of a woman" — and his father Zacharias is named twice in the same Gospel (Luke 1:13, 63). "Made of a woman" (**Galatians 4:4**) is the mortality idiom — **Job 14:1** "Man that is born of a woman is of few days"; **Job 15:14**; **25:4**. [L4] The challenger's claim — "a person generated as Joseph's offspring is not 'sent forth'" — is a floating inference: every son sent on a mission was generated; *exapesteilen* speaks of mission, not of conception mechanics.

#### Answer to C3 — Isaiah 7:14 is a sign, bounded; and "virgin" is not a mechanism

[L1] **Matthew 1:23** — "Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son." [L2] The Hebrew is *ha-almah harah ve-yoledet ben* — the young-woman word (H5959, seven rows); the 1611 margin reads "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call." [L1] The sign is **bounded inside Isaiah**: **Isaiah 7:16** — "For before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken"; **Isaiah 8:3-4** — the prophetess bears a child, and "before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother"; **Isaiah 8:8** — "he shall pass through Judah... and shall reach even to the neck; and the stretching out of his wings shall fill the breadth of thy land, **O Immanuel**." The Immanuel sign is *spoken into* Ahaz's own days. [L1] Luke 1:27's *parthenos* describes Mary at the annunciation — a description of the woman, not a narration of a conception mechanism. [L4] "All this was done" (Matt 1:22) identifies the sign-fulfillment; it does not add a how to what 1:18-21 narrated — which was events, not mechanism.

#### Answer to C4 — the father is named by the narrator and by the woman herself; "as was supposed" marks custom, not contradiction

[L1] The narrator's own words: **Luke 2:33** "Joseph and his mother"; **Luke 2:41** "**his parents** went to Jerusalem"; **Luke 2:48**, Mary in direct speech: "**thy father** and I have sought thee sorrowing." [L2] Luke 3:23's *hōs enomizeto* is *nomizō*, "to do by law (usage), to be wont" — the same verb form as **Acts 16:13** "where prayer **was wont** to be made." It marks **public reckoning**; it is not a retraction, and it certainly is not a statement that Joseph was *not* the father — that reading is exactly the enthronement of silence the row does not license.

[L1] **Luke 2:49** — "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?" [L3] This is the covenant title, and it is never in competition with the named father in the record: **1 Chronicles 28:6** — "**Solomon thy son**... I have chosen him **to be my son, and I will be his father**" — one verse, a living human father and the covenant Father, held together. **Luke 1:32** gives "the throne of his father David" and the title in the same breath — before Mary asks anything in 1:34. Jesus' answer at twelve is about **business** ("I must be about"), not a correction of parentage; and the challenger grants the legal-father rows are real — a correction of that kind is nowhere stated.

[L1] **John 6:42** and **Luke 4:22** and **John 1:45** are reported, yes — reported by the town and a disciple who say "whose **father** and mother we know." [L4] "Reported" is not "false"; the four registers agree with the narrator and with Mary's own mouth. Mark 6:3's "son of Mary" sits in one crowd-speech beside four registers naming Joseph and beside the narrator's "his parents."

#### Answer to C5 — Jeremiah 22:30 is uncited, bounded, answered, and the challenger's own escape runs through the same names

[L1] **Jeremiah 22:30** — "Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper **in his days**: for no man of his seed shall prosper, **sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah**." Four things follow from the row as written:

1. [L1] The New Testament **never cites it against Jesus**. The only two rows naming Jechonias are Matthew 1:11-12 — the very genealogy Matthew gives to establish the claim.
2. [L1] The plucked signet is **set back on**: **Haggai 2:23** — "In that day... I will take thee, O Zerubbabel... and **will make thee as a signet**" — Zerubbabel, governor of Judah (Haggai 1:1; Ezra 3:2), who **prospered**, never king, so the curse's clause ("sitting upon the throne... ruling in Judah") does not describe him.
3. [L1] Jeremiah's own rule bounds pronounced sentences: **Jeremiah 18:7-8** — "If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, **I will repent of the evil**."
4. [L3] The dogma's own escape runs through **the same two names**: **Luke 3:27** — "Zorobabel, which was the son of Salathiel, which was the son of Neri." Matthew 1:12 has "Jechonias begat Salathiel." Whether these are the same men the text never says — so the challenger's route is not certifiable either. [L4] A curse that would bar Joseph's seed would equally bar every line passing through those names. And the record holds that knot **open**; the challenger must not spend it as settled in his favor.

The legal-reckoning rows prove my point rather than break it: **every one of them presupposes a named begetter.** [L1] **Exodus 2:10** "he became her son" — adoption of Moses, whose father is named (Amram, Exodus 6:20); **Genesis 48:5** "thy two sons... are mine" — Ephraim and Manasseh, whose father Joseph is named; **Esther 2:7** Mordecai "took for his own daughter," and Esther's father is named (Abihail, Esther 2:15). [L1] **Ruth 4:17** "There is a son born to Naomi" is legal reckoning — of a child who proceeded out of **Boaz's** body (Ruth 4:13). Reckoning supplements the flesh; it never produces a seed and never erases a father.

[L3] **Hebrews 7:9-10** is the record's own loins idiom, and it is **patrilineal**: Levi "in the loins of **his father**" — the chain being Abraham → "**our father Isaac**" (Romans 9:10) → Jacob → Levi. [L2] *Anatellō* (Hebrews 7:14) is the branch springing up — organic growth of a stock, not paperwork; and **Romans 1:3**'s claim is carried by its qualifier regardless of the main verb: "*kata sarka*," flesh. **Acts 2:30** says it without any verb ambiguity: "**of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh**."

#### The affirmative the challenger never meets

[L1] **2 Samuel 7:12** "which shall proceed out of thy bowels"; **Psalm 132:11** "Of the fruit of thy body"; **Acts 2:30** "fruit of his loins, *kata sarka*"; **Romans 1:3** "seed of David according to the flesh"; **2 Timothy 2:8** "Jesus Christ of the seed of David"; **Hebrews 7:14** "evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda." A real seed out of the body, **evident** — these rows are flesh rows, and they require the flesh to run the Davidic line.

Then where does the flesh come from in the opposing case? [L1] The text **never states Mary's lineage**. **Luke 1:36** gives her only a kinswoman, Elisabeth, "of the daughters of Aaron" (1:5); **Luke 3:23** names no female anywhere — "which was [the son] of Heli... of Joseph." "Mary's genealogy" is imported. Meanwhile the father's Davidic house is stated three times: [L1] **Luke 1:27** Joseph "*of the house of David*"; **Luke 2:4** "of the house and lineage of David"; **Matthew 1:20** "Joseph, **thou son of David**." [L1] And the reckoning rule is the father's house: **Numbers 1:2, 18** "by the house of their fathers... declared their pedigrees" — with the wife's-house register **struck**: **Ezra 2:61-62** those registered after a wife's house were "as polluted, put from the priesthood." [L4] The seed rows demand flesh through the line; the only lineage the text states for that line is Joseph's; therefore the seed is honored through the named line — Joseph.

#### Named floats in the opposing chain (weight: zero as stated)

- [L4] "If Joseph were the father, the reason would be empty" — no lexical or textual footing; the *gar* gives a reason to take a consecrated child.
- [L4] "A person generated as Joseph's offspring is not 'sent forth'" — contradicted by every sent man in the canon (Jeremiah 1:5 sanctified in the womb, son of Hilkiah).
- [L4] "Mary's phrase is legal and household usage" — asserted against Mary's own mouth ("thy father"), with no textual footing.
- [L4] "The flesh is Mary's, by the Holy Ghost" — the text never says the flesh is Mary's; it says she bore him, and it never states her lineage.
- [L4] "Loins of David is satisfied by any descendant through either parent" — against the text's own loins rows (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30; Hebrews 7:9-10), all father-line.

One point of my own goes to review on my clock, not conceded here: my opening's count of women's-seed rows was stated too narrowly — the load-bearing claim survives on every row the challenger cites: **none** of Genesis 3:15, 16:10, 24:60, or Leviticus 12:2 is a Davidic mother-row; Leviticus 12:2 is a conception idiom ("If a woman have conceived seed"), not lineage predication. The Davidic rows all run through the father.

---

#### CHAIN STANDING

**D1** — flesh seed of David: 2 Sam 7:12; Ps 132:11; Rom 1:3 *kata sarka*; Acts 2:30 "fruit of his loins"; Heb 7:14 "evident... sprang"; 2 Tim 2:8 — **stands**, answered only by an unnamed maternal line.
**D2** — reckoning by father's house: Num 1:2, 18; Ezra 2:59, 61-62 (wife's-house register struck) — **stands**; legal rows shown to presuppose named begetters.
**D3** — Joseph named: Luke 2:48 (*ho pater sou*), 2:33, 2:41; Luke 4:22; John 1:45; 6:42; Matt 1:20, 21, 25 — **stands**; Luke 3:23 is *nomizō* = wont/custom (Acts 16:13), not retraction.
**D4** — titles are covenant/origin words: 1 Chr 28:6; Acts 13:33; Rom 1:4; *ek* + Spirit pattern (John 1:13; 1 John 4:7); no *kata sarka* at Matt 1:20; *eperchomai* = Acts 1:8; *episkiazō* = Luke 9:34; *klēthēsetai* = "shall be called"; divine-formation rows of men with fathers (Jer 1:5; Isa 49:5) — **stands**.
**D5** — quiet rows quiet: Matt 1:16 passive non-narration; Luke 1:34 present indicative; Matt 1:18, 25 timing and events, no mechanism; Gal 4:4 mortality idiom (Job 14:1; Matt 11:11, whose father is named) — **stands**; the opposing side produced no mechanism-verse.
**D6 — Yes: Christ had a biological father, Joseph, son of David, named in the text; the record forbids both "no man" and any narrated how.**
