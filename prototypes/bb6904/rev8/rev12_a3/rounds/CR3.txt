## Round 3 - Defender (MiMo): link round

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

LINK 4 [premise 1]: SUPPORTED. The footing is not the absence of a word but the canon's own marked construction. [L2] Lexical ground: *ek* + God/Spirit is covenant origin at every row — John 1:13 "born of God," 1 John 3:9, 4:7, 5:1 — and *ek* + flesh is marked when flesh is meant: Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David *according to the flesh*" (*genomenou ek spermatos Dauid kata sarka*), Acts 2:30 "of the fruit of his loins, *according to the flesh*." [L3] Cross-reference: Matthew 1:20 "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" carries the covenant-origin construction (1611 margin "Gr. begotten"), and the biological marker the canon supplies at Romans 1:3 is the marker in question. Chain: the canon marks biological origin with *kata sarka*; it marks divine origin with *ek* + Spirit; Matthew 1:20 carries the second. The premise stands on the marked rows, not on silence.

LINK 4 [premise 2]: SUPPORTED. [L1] Each womb-row has a named human father in the same book: Jeremiah 1:5 "before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee" belongs to Jeremiah 1:1 "Jeremiah the son of Hilkiah"; Isaiah 49:5 "formed me from the womb to be his servant" belongs to Isaiah 1:1 "Isaiah the son of Amoz"; Psalm 139:13 "thou hast covered me in my mother's womb" is David's, whose father is named at 1 Samuel 16:1 "Jesse the Bethlehemite." [L3] Cross-reference: the consecration-formation language never excludes a father where fathers are named. Chain: where Scripture says God sanctified, formed, covered the child in the womb, the text elsewhere names the child's father; so womb-language of God is not a denial of a father — Matthew 1:18's clause states the womb's consecration and states no exclusion.

LINK 2 [premise 3]: SUPPORTED. [L1] The reckoning rows trace the begetter: Ruth 4:13 "Boaz took Ruth... the LORD gave her conception, and she bare a son"; 4:17 "they called his name Obed: he is the father of Jesse, the father of David." Deuteronomy 25:6's own stated purpose is name-succession — "that his name be not put out of Israel" — and the text never calls the dead brother the father. [L2] Lexical ground: *logizomai* (G3049) is an accounting verb — "counted" (Romans 4:3; 9:8) reckons, it does not generate. [L3] Cross-reference: the seed rows state bodily descent — 2 Samuel 7:12 "which shall proceed out of thy bowels," Romans 1:3 "according to the flesh," Hebrews 7:14 "sprang out of Juda." Chain: reckoning names and preserves; the flesh rows name the begetter; where the record reckons an heir it still narrates the begetting (Ruth 4:13). Legal name does not produce seed.

LINK 2 [premise 4]: SUPPORTED. [L1] The named line: Luke 1:27 Joseph "*of the house of David*"; Luke 2:4 "of the house and lineage of David"; Luke 1:32 "the throne of his father David"; Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David"; the flesh claim Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David according to the flesh," Acts 2:30 "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh," Hebrews 7:14 "evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda." [L3] Cross-reference: reckoning runs "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18), and the inverse is exclusion (Ezra 2:59 "could not shew their father's house"). Chain: the seed must be David's *kata sarka*; the throne is "of his father David" (Luke 1:32, itself naming the father); the only house the text names for that line is Joseph's (Luke 1:27; 2:4). Luke 1:32 does not set that aside — it is the anchor: "his father David." No female lineage is stated anywhere.

LINK 5 [premise 5]: SUPPORTED. [L2] Lexical ground: Matthew 1:16's *egennēthē* is the passive after thirty-nine active *egennēsen* forms, with *ex hēs* naming the mother — a passive names the birth and names no agent, and a passive is not a denial. [L1] The verse reads "Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" — husband named in the same breath. [L3] Cross-reference: the record's other non-narration rows are held the same way — Genesis 3:15 "her seed," Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman" — none of the three says "no man," and the chorus of seed rows (2 Samuel 7:12; Romans 1:3; Acts 2:30; Hebrews 7:14) supplies what the list leaves quiet, precept upon precept (Isaiah 28:10). Chain: a changed verb is a fact of the text; the inference that it marks the absence of a father is not in the text, and the flesh rows answer it.

LINK 3 [premise 6]: SUPPORTED. [L1] Luke 2:48-51 read whole: Mary says "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing"; Jesus answers "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"; then "he went down with them, and came to Nazareth, and was subject unto them" — and Luke 2:33, 41, 43 keep naming Joseph in the same Gospel. The covenant Father carries a living human father at 1 Chronicles 28:6 "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son," and 2 Samuel 7:14 "I will be his father, and he shall be my son." [L3] Cross-reference: the title's other bearers had fathers and nations — "Israel is my son, even my firstborn" (Exodus 4:22), Adam (Luke 3:38) — and its resurrection occasion (Acts 13:33; Romans 1:4). [L2] *klēthēsetai* — "shall be *called*" (Luke 1:35). Chain: the title is covenant designation borne with a named father; "my Father's business" names the covenant office, and the text records no retraction of "thy father."

CHALLENGER'S UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES
1. That Matthew's stated sequence excludes a man from the conception — the leap from interval to exclusion; the words claim a narration the verses do not make ("a text that fixes conception before any union has narrated what the conception was not").
2. That the angel's answer to "how" settles the source by omission — absence pressed into witness; the words make omission decisive ("The angel answers 'how' with the Spirit and omits any man").
3. That *ek sou* makes Mary the flesh-source of the Davidic seed — no lineage of Mary's is stated anywhere; the words assign the source beyond the birth-channel ("*Ek sou* ('of thee') makes Mary the source.").
4. That God's preparing the body excludes a male seed — an absence-claim standing against the flesh rows; the words rest on what is not said ("and no verse in the record gives a man as source for the body.").
5. That the *genesis*-list verb change at Matthew 1:16 signals the absence of a begetter — morphology observed, paternity-verdict inferred; the words convert a grammar fact into a verdict ("The break changes the verb, the relative and the subject at the place where 'begat' was due.").
6. That legal sonship carries the Davidic line without the begetter's seed — Deuteronomy 25:6 states name-preservation and Ruth 4:13 narrates the begetting; the words outrun both rows ("Deut 25:6 and Ruth 4:10, 17 show legal sonship carrying the name without the begetter's seed.").
