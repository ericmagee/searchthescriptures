## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** All four judgments (the three panel judges and the single judge) apply these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession".
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

### OPENING — THE DEFENDER

**Position:** Yes. The text names Christ's biological father: Joseph, son of David. A man was involved; the seed is honored through the named line. The record forbids the two extremes equally — it forbids "no man was involved," and it forbids narrating the *how*. I affirm the **who** and leave the how where the text leaves it: un-narrated.

#### I. The seed rows are flesh rows — a real seed out of a real body [L1 + L2]

[L1] **2 Samuel 7:12** — "And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, **which shall proceed out of thy bowels**, and I will establish his kingdom." — The promised seed is a physical descendant proceeding from the body.

[L1] **Romans 1:3** — "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, **which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh**." — Paul names the descent as flesh, not paperwork.

[L1] **Acts 2:30** — "Therefore being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him, that **of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne." — The oath's fulfillment is fruit of the loins, *kata sarka*.

[L1] **Hebrews 7:14** — "For **it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda**; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood." — *Evident* — not legal, not adopted; sprung.

[L1] **Psalm 132:11** — "The LORD hath sworn in truth unto David... **Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne**."

[L2] Lexical floor: **sperma** (G4690) is the begetting-word; **kata sarka** (Rom 1:3; Acts 2:30) is the qualifier the text adds *precisely when bodily descent is meant*; **mimme'eykha** (2 Sam 7:12, "out of thy bowels") and **osphys** (Acts 2:30 / Heb 7:5, 10, the loins-word) are anatomical. A legal adoption produces no "fruit of the loins" and no seed "proceeding out of the bowels."

[L3] **Genesis 49:10** — "The sceptre shall not depart from Judah..." — the line is a tribal, flesh line.

#### II. The father is named, in every register the text speaks from [L1 + L2]

[L1] Mary's own mouth: **Luke 2:48** — "...his mother said unto him, Son, why hast thou thus dealt with us? behold, **thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing**."

[L1] The narrator: **Luke 2:33** — "And **Joseph and his mother** marvelled at those things which were spoken of him." **Luke 2:41** — "Now **his parents** went to Jerusalem every year at the feast of the passover."

[L1] The town: **Luke 4:22** — "And all bare him witness... And they said, **Is not this Joseph's son?**" **John 6:42** — "And they said, **Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?**"

[L1] A disciple: **John 1:45** — "Philip findeth Nathanael, and saith unto him, We have found him... **Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph**."

[L1] The angel: **Matthew 1:20** — "...saying, **Joseph, thou son of David**, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife..." — the angel addresses Joseph by the Davidic title, and Joseph names the child (**Matthew 1:21, 25**).

[L2] Luke 2:48's "thy father" is *ho pater sou* in direct speech from Mary to Jesus at twelve years old. [L3] **Luke 1:27** — Joseph is "*of the house of David*" (*ex oikou David*, attached to the man); **Luke 2:4** — "because **he was of the house and lineage of David**."

#### III. Reckoning is by the father's house — and the wife's register is struck [L1 + L2 + L3]

[L1] **Numbers 1:2** — "Take ye the sum of all the congregation of the children of Israel, **after their families, by the house of their fathers**... every male by their polls." **Numbers 1:18** — "And they assembled all the congregation together... and **declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers**."

[L3] The inverse rows: **Ezra 2:59** — those who "could not shew their father's house" are listed apart; **Ezra 2:61-62** — priests reckoned after a **wife's** house (Barzillai) were "as polluted, **put from the priesthood**"; **Ruth 4:10** — the name is acquired "that the name of the dead be not cut off." Davidic standing runs through the father's house, so a father must be *named* — and one is.

#### IV. The divine titles are covenant and origin titles, not a second paternity [L1 + L2 + L3]

[L1] **1 Chronicles 28:6** — "And he said unto me, **Solomon thy son**, he shall build my house and my courts: for **I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father**." — In one verse: the son David begat, and the son God *called* him. The title "Son of God" was borne by a man with a living human father.

[L1] **Matthew 1:20** — "for that which is conceived in her is **of** (*ek*) **the Holy Ghost**." [L2] *ek* + God/Spirit everywhere in the New Testament marks **covenant origin**, never biological paternity: **John 1:13** "born of God"; **1 John 4:7** "is of God"; **1 John 5:1** "born of God." Where the text means bodily descent it adds *kata sarka* (Rom 1:3; Acts 2:30) — and Matthew 1:20 does not.

[L1] **Acts 13:33** — "God hath fulfilled the same unto us their children, **in that he hath raised up Jesus again**; as it is also written... Thou art my Son, **this day have I begotten thee**." **Romans 1:4** — "And **declared to be the Son of God** with power... **by the resurrection from the dead**." — The begetting-word and the declaration belong to the resurrection.

[L2 + L3] The angel's own verbs are empowerment and presence, not begetting: *eperchomai* (Luke 1:35) is the word of **Acts 1:8** — "ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is **come upon** you"; *episkiazō* (Luke 1:35) is the word of **Luke 9:34** — "a cloud, and **overshadowed** them" (the transfiguration) and Acts 5:15 (Peter's shadow). Luke 1:35 concludes "**shall be called** the Son of God" (*klēthēsetai*) — a title, given.

#### V. The quiet rows are quiet — they say no "no man" and no how [L1 + L2]

## Round 2 - Defender (MiMo)

#### Answer to C1 — Matt 1:18-25 narrates circumstances and timing, not a mechanism

[L1] **Matthew 1:18** — "Now the birth of Jesus Christ **was on this wise**: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was **found with child** of the Holy Ghost."

Read the verse as written: the "wise" heads a narrative of **events** — espousal, discovery, Joseph's intent, the angel's speech, the naming. "Before they came together" (*prin ē synelthein autous*) is a **timing** statement about cohabitation; "found with child" is a **discovery** statement. Neither names a mechanism. [L2] *Synelthein* is coming-together as cohabitation; a statement of when cohabitation had not yet occurred is not a statement of how conception occurred. Genesis's *yada'* rows (Gen 4:1; 1 Sam 1:19; Judges 11:39) establish the idiom for union — they do not convert Matthew's negative timing clause into a positive mechanism.

[L1] **Matthew 1:19** — "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." [L2] *dikaios ōn, mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai, eboulēthē* — character, intent, resolution. There is **no verb of knowledge** in the row. A man's intent on an unexplained pregnancy is recorded; his suspicion is not endorsed by the narrator as fact. [L1] The angel's *gar* ("for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost") answers "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" — the reason to receive her is that the child is **consecrated of God**. [L3] The same divine-formation language is spoken of men with named fathers: **Jeremiah 1:5** "before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee" — of Jeremiah, **whose father is named** ("Jeremiah the son of Hilkiah," 1:1); **Isaiah 49:5** "that formed me from the womb to be his servant"; **Psalm 139:13** "thou hast covered me in my mother's womb." "Of the Holy Ghost" at Matthew 1:18, 20 stands in that company: divine consecration and origin, never the exclusion of a father.

[L1] **Matthew 1:25** — "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and **he called his name JESUS**." Held as written. And notice the actor at the end: **Joseph names the child** — the father's act, the same act the angel prescribes to Zacharias (Luke 1:13; 62-63, "signs to his father, how he would have him called").

**The challenger's own sentence, "the text narrates the how," is the claim the text must be made to carry — and no verse in Matthew 1:18-25 states a how.** It states before, found, fear, take, call. [L1] The sequence's four changes at 1:16 are real observations; but a **break in a list is silence, and silence is not a witness.** [L3] It cannot outweigh four registers naming the father (Luke 2:48, 33; John 6:42; John 1:45; Matthew 1:20) and the flesh rows demanding a seed of the loins (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30). [L4] To read a break as a denial is to enthrone one non-narration against a chorus — and it is a denial the sentence itself does not make ("of whom was born Jesus" names the **bearer**; it does not say "and there was no father").

#### Answer to C2 — *ek pneumatos* is source-language of consecration; the womb is Mary's; the father is named elsewhere

[L1] "**Of whom was born Jesus**" (*ex hēs egennēthē*) names the **mother as the bearer** — the child was born of her, as Luke 2:7 narrates ("she brought forth her firstborn son"). A mother's bearing is not the exclusion of a father. [L3] The rest of the canon pairs a divine-conceived/born-of-a-woman child with a named father: **Matthew 11:11** of John the Baptist, "born of a woman" — and his father Zacharias is named twice in the same Gospel (Luke 1:13, 63). ## 3. The defender's divine-formation parallels lack the womb-begetting words

[L1] Jer 1:5, Isa 49:5 and Ps 139:13 speak of sanctifying, forming and covering a child in the womb. [L2] None of them has *to gennēthen en autē ek pneumatos* ("that which is begotten in her is out of the Spirit," Matt 1:20), and none is given as the answer to "found with child before they came together." [L3] Isa 7:14 pairs *parthenos* (G3933) with a sign "in the depth, or in the height above" (Isa 7:11). Hosea's and Isaiah's children come after union. Matthew's child is announced as the sign of a virgin who is with child (Matt 1:22-23). The defender's own Isaiah 7:16 and 8:3-4 rows show how a child follows a union, and Matthew 1:18-25 is built to exclude that sequence.

## 4. Mary's phrase and the narrator

[L1] The narrator says "Joseph and his mother" (Luke 2:33, 43) and never "his father." Luke 3:23 says "as was supposed." [L2] *Enomizeto* is the passive of *nomizō*, "to reckon, suppose." Acts 16:13 uses the same verb for what was "supposed" or customary. In both places the verb names an opinion held, and the verse leaves it to the reader to ask whether the opinion was true. That is why the KJV renders it "supposed." The defender's gloss of "custom" does not make "supposed" a confirmation. [L1] In Luke 2:49 Jesus answers "thy father" with "my Father's business," and the text records no retraction.

## 5. The defender cannot say which line carries the seed

[L1] Jer 22:30 bars Jechonias's seed from the throne. Matt 1:11-12 places him in Joseph's line. The defender says the knot is "held open." If it is open, the defender cannot treat Joseph's seed as the secure route, and the text states the other route: Luke 1:32, "the Lord God shall **give** unto him the throne of his father David." [L2] *Dōsei* is giving, not siring. [L3] Deut 25:6 and Ruth 4:10, 17 show legal sonship carrying the name without the begetter's seed. Hag 2:23 sets a signet on Zerubbabel, and Zerubbabel did not sit on the throne, so it does not lift the bar on seed.

## 6. Hebrews 10:5, Gal 4:4, Rom 1:3 and 1:4

[L1] Heb 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me." Gal 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman." Rom 1:3: *genomenou ek spermatos Dauid*, "made" and not *gennaō*. [L2] *Katērtisō* has God as its subject. [L4] The body is prepared by God and taken from the woman, and no verse in the record gives a man as source for the body.

Taken together: the text excludes union before the conception, answers "how" with the Spirit, makes Mary the source, and secures the throne by gift and legal name. The defender's chain depends on silence supplying what no verse supplies.

## Answer to C1 — Matt 1:18-25 narrates circumstances and timing, not a mechanism

[L2] *Synelthein* is coming-together as cohabitation; a statement of when cohabitation had not yet occurred is not a statement of how conception occurred. [L1] **Matthew 1:19** — "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." [L2] *dikaios ōn, mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai, eboulēthē* — character, intent, resolution. There is **no verb of knowledge** in the row. CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 2, CLOSING AND PREMISE LIST

# CHALLENGER'S CLOSING ROUND

The defender's reply grants the observations that carry my case, and then answers them with the claim that silence cannot witness. That claim has to be tested against what the text actually says.

## 1. Matthew 1:18-25 is narration, and the defender calls it only "timing"

[L1] Matt 1:18 reads "found with child of the Holy Ghost" and sets that discovery "before they came together." Matt 1:25 reads "knew her not till she had brought forth." [L3] In Gen 4:1, 4:17 and 1 Sam 1:19, knowing comes first and conceiving follows. [L4] In Matthew the order is reversed, and the text states the reversal. The defender calls this "timing," but a text that fixes conception before any union has narrated what the conception was not. It has also named the source, "of the Holy Ghost." The chain is Matt 1:18, 25 in the verse order, plus the Gen 4:1 idiom with its order of events.

## 2. Luke 1:34-35 stands unanswered

[L1] "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" The angel replies: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee... **therefore also** that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." [L2] *Dio kai* is causal. *Ek sou* ("of thee") makes Mary the source. The defender's round 2 never touches *dio*. He offers only *eperchomai* and *episkiazō* as lexical parallels, and he does not say why a power that "comes upon" and "overshadows" would be followed by a man's seed as cause. [L4] The angel answers "how" with the Spirit and omits any man. Footing: Luke 1:34, 35 (*epei*, *dio*, *andra*) and Judg 11:39 (*ginōskō* of virginity).

## 3. The defender's divine-formation parallels lack the womb-begetting words

[L1] Jer 1:5, Isa 49:5 and Ps 139:13 speak of sanctifying, forming and covering a child in the womb. [L2] None of them has *to gennēthen en autē ek pneumatos* ("that which is begotten in her is out of the Spirit," Matt 1:20), and none is given as the answer to "found with child before they came together." [L3] Isa 7:14 pairs *parthenos* (G3933) with a sign "in the depth, or in the height above" (Isa 7:11). Hosea's and Isaiah's children come after union. Matthew's child is announced as the sign of a virgin who is with child (Matt 1:22-23). The defender's own Isaiah 7:16 and 8:3-4 rows show how a child follows a union, and Matthew 1:18-25 is built to exclude that sequence.

## 4. Mary's phrase and the narrator

[L1] The narrator says "Joseph and his mother" (Luke 2:33, 43) and never "his father." Luke 3:23 says "as was supposed." [L2] *Enomizeto* is the passive of *nomizō*, "to reckon, suppose." Acts 16:13 uses the same verb for what was "supposed" or customary. In both places the verb names an opinion held, and the verse leaves it to the reader to ask whether the opinion was true. That is why the KJV renders it "supposed." The defender's gloss of "custom" does not make "supposed" a confirmation. [L1] In Luke 2:49 Jesus answers "thy father" with "my Father's business," and the text records no retraction.

## 5. The defender cannot say which line carries the seed
