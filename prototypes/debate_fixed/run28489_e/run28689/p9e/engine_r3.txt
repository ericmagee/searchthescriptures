DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED**

Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
- [L1] Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli."
- [L1] Joseph has two fathers in the two registers, Jacob and Heli.
- [L2] In Matthew 1:16 the "begat" pattern stops at Joseph, who is called *anēr* (husband), not father. The relative *hēs* (of whom) is feminine singular, so the one born is born of Mary.
- [L3] Ezra 2:61-62 says a man "took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai the Gileadite, and was called after their name." The penalty there was removal from the priesthood. But the verse attests that a man could be reckoned under his wife's father's name.
- [L3] 1 Chronicles 2:34-35: "Now Sheshan had no sons, but daughters... And Sheshan gave his daughter to Jarha his servant to wife; and she bare him Attai." Here a line runs through a daughter's house.
- [L3] Numbers 1:2, "by the house of their fathers," is the general rule. Numbers 27:7 gives the daughters of Zelophehad an inheritance "among their brethren," so daughters carried a father's house when there were no sons.

Chain: two registers give Joseph two fathers. Matthew breaks the "begat" pattern at him. Scripture has precedent for a son-in-law reckoned under his wife's father. So the Heli register can be Mary's line, reckoned under Joseph's name. This is an inference with two layers beneath it. No verse states it outright.

**LINK 6: SUPPORTED**

Footing:
- [L2] Romans 1:3, "made of the seed of David according to the flesh," uses *genomenou* (having become). Galatians 4:4, "made of a woman," uses *genomenon* (having become) with *ek gynaikos* (out of a woman). It is the same verb in both places.
- [L1] Luke 1:32 is spoken to Mary: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David."
- [L1] Luke 1:42: "the fruit of thy womb."
- [L3] Genesis 3:15 speaks of the woman's "seed."

Chain: if Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 describe one becoming, the Davidic seed came into being out of a woman. The seed-language of Romans 1:3 does not require a begetting male.

"Loins" and "body" do span generations. The challenger pressed these verses:
- Acts 2:30: "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne."
- 2 Samuel 7:12: "thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels."
- Psalm 132:11: "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne."
- Hebrews 7:14: "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda."
- Hebrews 7:9-10 and 7:10: "he was yet in the loins of his father."

Against these: Genesis 35:11, "kings shall come out of thy loins," is said to Jacob of kings generations later. Hebrews 7:5 says the sons of Levi "come out of the loins of Abraham." Acts 13:23 reads, "Of this man's seed hath God according to his promise raised unto Israel a Saviour, Jesus." Matthew 1:1 calls him "the son of David" across the whole register. Hebrews 7:14 says he "sprang out of Juda," the tribe, and does not name a father.

Mary's own descent is not stated by any single verse, and I do not claim it is. The conclusion rests on the chain above.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED**

Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- [L1] Matthew 1:19: "Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily."
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "Fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
- [L1] Deuteronomy 22:23-24, the stoning penalty, is the "example" a husband would want to avoid.
- [L2] *paradeigmatisai* (to make a public example) presupposes a suspected wrong.

Chain: Matthew 1:18 places the pregnancy "before they came together." It does more than date the finding. The angel's "for" in 1:20 names the ground of Joseph's fear, and that ground is origin: the child is "of the Holy Ghost." The answer to Joseph's troubled thought is that the origin is not a man. If Joseph knew he was the cause, there is no wrong to put away and no example to avoid.

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED**

Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
- [L2] The verb *gennēthen* (that which is begotten) is the same verb that Matthew uses for "begat" in 1:2-16.
- [L2] *ek* (out of) marks source in the register. Matthew 1:3, "of Thamar," and 1:5, "of Rachab," name the women as sources. Matthew 1:18 and 1:20 use the same *ek* for the Holy Ghost.
- [L3] Romans 1:3-4 sets "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" beside "declared to be the Son of God... according to the spirit of holiness." The text itself pairs *kata sarka* (according to flesh) with *kata pneuma* (according to spirit).

The challenger pressed John 1:13 and 1 John 3:9.
- John 1:13 reads: "Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." The verse itself puts "of God" in contrast with "the will of man."
- 1 John 3:9: "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him."

The challenger pressed Luke 1:62-63 for the father's naming act: "they made signs to his father, how he would have him called." Against this, Luke 1:60 has the mother naming: "his mother answered and said, Not so; but he shall be called John." Matthew 1:21 gives the angel's command, "thou shalt call his name JESUS," and does not call it a father's act.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED**

Footing:
- [L1] Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- [L2] *dio kai* (for which reason also) ties the child's holiness and sonship to the Spirit's coming.
- [L2] *andra ou ginōskō* (I do not know a man), with Mary already betrothed (Luke 1:27), asks about means.

Chain: the question is "how," and the answer gives a means. The means is the Holy Ghost and power. Joseph is not named in the answer. Luke 1:37 ("with God nothing shall be impossible") follows that answer. The sign given in 1:36 is the barren Elisabeth. The challenger pressed Acts 1:8, Luke 9:34, Acts 5:15, 2 Samuel 7:14 and 1 Chronicles 28:6 against these verbs and the title. 2 Samuel 7:14 reads "I will be his father, and he shall be my son," and 1 Chronicles 28:6 reads "I have chosen him to be my son." Those verses speak of Solomon, whose begetter is named. Luke 1:35 gives no begetter and states a different means.

**LINK 5: SUPPORTED**

Footing:
- [L2] *enomizeto* derives from *nomizō* (to deem by custom or law). It names what was held, not what was so.
- [L1] Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph."
- [L1] Mark 6:3: "Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary?" Here he is reckoned by the mother and no father is named.
- [L1] Luke 2:48-49: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing," answered by "my Father's business."

The challenger pressed John 6:42: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" The speakers are the murmuring Jews of John 6:41. Luke 3:23, written by the narrator of Luke 1:34-35, qualifies exactly that popular reckoning. The challenger also pressed:
- Luke 2:27 and 2:33: "the parents," "Joseph and his mother."
- John 1:45: "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph."
- Matthew 1:20: "Joseph, thou son of David."
- Luke 4:22: "Is not this Joseph's son?"
These are what the household and the town said. They are not the narrator's verdict.

**CHALLENGER'S UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES**

1. "the title is Solomon's covenant title, of one with a begetter" — this reads "Son of God" in Luke 1:35 as implying a human begetter. It props the LINK 3 objection.
2. "Luke 2:49 "my Father's business" is covenant speech" — this assumes the child's answer to "thy father" is only covenant idiom. It props the LINK 5 objection.
3. "it carries no verdict of its own" — this assumes *enomizeto* in Luke 3:23 adds nothing to the verse. It props the LINK 5 objection.
4. "it does not narrate when the child was begotten" — this reads Matthew 1:18 as silent on the time of conception, against "before they came together." It props the LINK 2 objection.
5. "Thirty-nine "begat" rows and then silence is non-narration" — this reads the broken "begat" pattern at Matthew 1:16 as having no meaning. It props the LINK 4 objection.
6. "The New Testament never cites the curse against Jesus' throne." — this uses silence to cancel the Jeconiah bar. It props the objection to the Jeconiah argument.
