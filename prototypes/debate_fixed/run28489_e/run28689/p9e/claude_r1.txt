DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

# Opening: Christ had no human biological father

**Thesis.** [L4] Christ was conceived in Mary's womb by the Holy Ghost, with no man's seed. Joseph was Mary's husband and the child's reputed father, not his begetter. I define "biological father" as the male who supplies the seed in conception. On that definition the answer is no, and the KJV gives it at four independent points.

## I. The conception is stated as "of the Holy Ghost," before Mary and Joseph came together

**1. [L1]** Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was found with child **of the Holy Ghost**."
**[L2]** *sunelthein* (to come together, here the verb for marital union) is placed before the pregnancy. *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of Spirit holy) uses *ek* (out of, from as source), which marks origin, not mere accompaniment.

**2. [L1]** Matthew 1:20: "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for **that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost**."
**[L2]** *gennēthen* (that which has been begotten or conceived) is an aorist passive participle, so the begetting is done to her, not by Joseph. It is again *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of Spirit holy).
**[L3]** Joseph's alarm in Matthew 1:19 ("minded to put her away privily") shows that he knew he was not the cause. The angel's answer names the true source and does not tell him to accept it as his own.

**3. [L1]** Matthew 1:25: "And [Joseph] **knew her not** till she had brought forth her firstborn son."
**[L2]** *ginōskō* (to know) is the common Scriptural idiom for marital intimacy. Its use here with *heōs* (until) denies it for the whole period of the conception and birth.
**[L3]** Compare Genesis 4:1, "Adam **knew** Eve his wife; and she conceived." Genesis ties conception to that knowing. Matthew denies the knowing, so Joseph is excluded as the cause.

## II. Mary's own testimony and the angel's explanation

**4. [L1]** Luke 1:34: "Then said Mary unto the angel, **How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?**"
**[L2]** *andra ou ginōskō* (a man I do not know), in the present tense, states her present state. She asks how a conception could occur without a man.
**[L1]** Luke 1:35 answers: "**The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee**: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
**[L2]** *episkiazō* (to overshadow) denotes divine power acting upon her, not union with a man. The "therefore" (*dio*, for which reason) makes this cause the ground of the title "Son of God."
**[L3]** Luke 1:27 calls her *parthenon* (virgin), and the angel never answers her question with Joseph.

## III. Prophecy and genealogy

**5. [L1]** Matthew 1:22-23: "...that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, **Behold, a virgin shall be with child**, and shall bring forth a son."
**[L2]** The Greek is *parthenos* (virgin, a maiden without sexual experience). The Hebrew in Isaiah 7:14 is *'almah* (a young woman of marriageable age). Matthew's inspired rendering fixes the sense as virgin, and the sign Isaiah calls *'oth* (a sign) would be no sign if it were ordinary conception.
**[L3]** Genesis 3:15 speaks of "the **seed of the woman**." Scripture usually reckons seed through the man (Genesis 12:7; 15:5), so naming the woman's seed alone is a notable exception.
**[L1]** Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, **made of a woman**."
**[L2]** *genomenon ek gunaikos* (having come to be out of a woman) names the woman as the only human source.

**6. [L1]** Matthew 1:2-16 repeats *egennēsen* (begat) throughout. At verse 16 the pattern breaks: "Jacob begat Joseph **the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus**."
**[L2]** *hēs* (of whom) is feminine singular, so it can only refer to Mary and not to Joseph. *egennēthē* (was born) is passive. Matthew changes verb and pronoun at exactly the point where he would otherwise have written "Joseph begat Jesus."
**[L3]** Jeremiah 22:30 says of Jeconiah: "no man of his seed shall prosper, **sitting upon the throne of David**." Matthew 1:11-12 places Jechonias in Joseph's line. If Joseph were the biological father, the throne promise of Luke 1:32 ("the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David") would meet that bar. The conception without Joseph's seed fits the facts, while Mary's descent from David (Luke 3, Romans 1:3) supplies the royal line.

## IV. "Father" language in the text answered

**7. [L1]** Luke 3:23: "Jesus... being (**as was supposed**) the son of Joseph."
**[L2]** *enomizeto* (was reckoned by custom or opinion) marks a belief held by others, which Luke qualifies.
**[L3]** Luke 2:48 ("thy father and I have sought thee") and John 6:42 ("Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph?") are the speech of Mary and the crowd. Luke 2:49 corrects this at once: "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?" Luke 2:33 ("Joseph and his mother marvelled") likewise calls Joseph a legal father within the household.
**[L1]** Hebrews 10:5: "a **body** hast thou prepared me." The body is prepared by God, not generated by a man.

## DECLARED CHAIN -- the links your case stands on

1. The conception was "of the Holy Ghost" and prior to Mary and Joseph's coming together. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:18, 20; [L2] *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of Spirit holy), *sunelthein* (to come together), *gennēthen* (that which is begotten, passive); [L3] Joseph's alarm in Matthew 1:19 and the angel's answer.
2. Joseph did not know Mary during the conception and birth, and Mary herself denies knowing a man. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:25, Luke 1:34; [L2] *ginōskō* (to know, marital sense) with *heōs* (until); [L3] Genesis 4:1 (knowing precedes conceiving).
3. The angel names the Holy Ghost's overshadowing as the cause and ground of the title "Son of God." Footing: [L1] Luke 1:35; [L2] *episkiazō* (to overshadow), *dio* (for which reason); [L3] Luke 1:27 (*parthenon*, virgin).
4. Matthew's genealogy deliberately switches from "begat" of Joseph to "of whom was born" of Mary. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:16; [L2] *hēs* (of whom, feminine singular) and *egennēthē* (was born, passive); [L3] Jeremiah 22:30 against Matthew 1:11-12.
5. Texts calling Joseph "father" are reputed or legal usage, which the text qualifies. Footing: [L1] Luke 3:23, Luke 2:48-49; [L2] *enomizeto* (was supposed); [L3] John 6:42.
6. Therefore Christ had no human biological father. Mary was the sole human parent, and the conception was by the power of God. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:18-25, Luke 1:34-35, Galatians 4:4; [L2] *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of Spirit holy), *genomenon ek gunaikos* (come to be out of a woman); [L3] Genesis 3:15 (the seed of the woman), Isaiah 7:14 as quoted in Matthew 1:23.
