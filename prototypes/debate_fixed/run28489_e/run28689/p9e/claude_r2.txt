# Round 2: Defender's Answer

Per DD-3, I answer the chain and not the isolated rows. The challenger has pressed rows of "seed", "loins" and "till". Each is re-seated below in its own chain.

## I. The seed rows name David, not Joseph (keystone)

**1. [L1]** Romans 1:3-4: "made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**; and declared to be the Son of God with power, **according to the spirit of holiness**."
**[L2]** *genomenou ek spermatos Dabid* (having come to be out of the seed of David) uses the participle of Galatians 4:4, *genomenon ek gunaikos* (having come to be out of a woman). The two participles share a verb, and both phrases are headed by *ek* (out of). *kata sarka* (according to the flesh) is paired with *kata pneuma hagiōsunēs* (according to the spirit of holiness). That is the two-source pattern of Matthew 1:18-20 and Luke 1:35: the flesh comes from David's line, and the Sonship is declared by the Spirit.

**2.** None of the challenger's rows names Joseph as the begetter.
- **[L1]** Acts 2:30, 2 Samuel 7:12, Psalm 132:11 and Hebrews 7:14 name David or Judah.
- **[L2]** *osphus* (loins) and *koilia* (belly, womb) are descent words that span generations. Hebrews 7:10 says Levi "was yet in the **loins** of his father Abraham" when Melchisedec met Abraham, which is several generations earlier.
- **[L3]** Loins reach back to the ancestor and do not identify the intermediate begetter. If the rows prove Joseph, they prove it only by importing "Joseph" from outside the rows.

**3. [L3]** Luke 1:42 uses the same body-word of Mary: "blessed is the **fruit of thy womb**" (*karpos tēs koilias sou*, fruit of thy belly). Psalm 132:11 has "fruit of thy body" in David's line, and Luke 1:42 applies the same fruit-of-the-belly language to Mary.

**4. [L4]** Mary is therefore of David by the chain's own rows, and not by a female name in a register. Footing: [L1] Romans 1:3 says Christ's flesh is "of the seed of David"; [L1] Galatians 4:4 says he came to be "of a woman"; [L3] Luke 1:42 says he is "the fruit of thy womb". If the flesh comes only through the woman, the woman supplies the Davidic flesh.

**5.** On "made of a woman" and "the seed of the woman" (Job 14:1; Matthew 11:11; Genesis 16:10; 24:60):
- **[L1]** Those rows have no pregnancy-without-a-man context. Luke 1:34-35 does.
- **[L3]** Luke 1:13 names Zacharias as John's father. Luke 1:18 gives the ordinary means: "I am an old man, and my wife well stricken in years."
- Galatians 4:4 is a corroborating link in my chain and not its only support. The chain stands on Links 1 to 5.

## II. "Knew her not till" and the question of Joseph's union

**1. [L1]** Matthew 1:18: "**before** they came together, she was found with child."
**[L2]** *prin ē sunelthein autous* (before they came together). *prin ē* (before that) with the infinitive places the pregnancy first. The challenger concedes that *sunerchomai* (come together) can name married union (1 Corinthians 7:5), and that is my reading. The challenger's [L4] says "Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal." It has no row. Matthew 1:18 puts the pregnancy before union, and 1:24-25 puts the taking of his wife after the angel's word, with no knowing "until" the birth.

**2.** The challenger's use of Deuteronomy 22:24 ("his neighbour's wife") actually helps my chain.
- **[L1]** Deuteronomy 22:23-24 treats a betrothed woman lain with by another man as an adulteress, and prescribes stoning.
- **[L1]** Matthew 1:19 says Joseph was "a just man" who was "not willing to make her a publick example" and "was minded to put her away privily."
- **[L4]** The text gives the object of his intent, *paradeigmatisai* (to make a public example), and the ground, her being "found with child." If Joseph knew himself to be the cause, there would be nothing to put away privily and no example to avoid. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:19; [L3] Deuteronomy 22:23-24 (the law for a betrothed woman found with another). Matthew 1:20 then answers his fear (*phobēthēs*, fear) with "that which is conceived in her is **of the Holy Ghost**." The angel does not say "it is yours."

**3. [L1]** Luke 1:34 ("seeing I know not a man") is answered at 1:35 by the Holy Ghost's coming, not by Joseph.
**[L2]** *dio kai* (for which reason also) at 1:35 makes the Spirit's coming the ground of the title.
**[L1]** Luke 1:37: "For with God nothing shall be impossible" (*ouk adunatēsei para tō theō pan rhēma*, no word shall be impossible with God). This was said to meet an impossibility. If Joseph were the begetter, no such answer was needed.

## III. Luke 1:35 and Solomon

**1. [L2]** *eperchomai* (to come upon) and *episkiazō* (to overshadow) are used of presence and power elsewhere (Acts 1:8; Luke 9:34). But in Luke 1:35 the same sentence names a conception ("which shall be born of thee") and makes the Spirit's coming its ground ("therefore also", *dio kai*).
**[L3]** 2 Samuel 7:14 ("I will be his father") is a promise. Luke 1:35 is a *dio* (for this reason) statement tied to the event of the conception. Solomon's sonship rests on God's covenant choice (1 Chronicles 28:6). The angel's *dio* rests it on the Spirit's coming upon Mary. The two grounds differ.

## IV. Matthew 1:16 and Jeremiah 22:30

**1. [L1]** Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom** was born Jesus."
**[L2]** *hēs* (of whom, feminine singular) cannot refer to Joseph. The challenger says this is "non-narration" after thirty-nine "begat" rows. But thirty-nine uniform rows followed by a break is exactly what a deliberate switch looks like.

**2. [L3]** Reckoning through a woman has a canonical pattern.
- **[L1]** 1 Chronicles 2:34-35: Sheshan "had no sons, but daughters. And Sheshan had a servant... Jarha. And Sheshan gave his daughter to Jarha his servant to wife; and she bare him Attai." The line runs through the daughter.
- **[L1]** Ezra 2:61, which the challenger cites, has a man who "took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai... and was called after their name."
- **[L4]** Matthew 1:16 gives Joseph by "begat" as Jacob's son, and Luke 3:23 gives him "of Heli." The two cannot both be his natural father. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:16 and Luke 3:23, whose fathers for Joseph differ, and [L3] 1 Chronicles 2:34-35 and Ezra 2:61. One register can be Joseph's legal line and the other Mary's through Heli, as sons-in-law were reckoned.

**3. [L1]** Jeremiah 22:30: "no man of his **seed** shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David."
**[L2]** *zera'* (seed, offspring) is the begotten line.
**[L3]** The challenger's rows confirm this. Haggai 1:1 and Ezra 3:2 call Zerubbabel governor and never king, so no Jechonias-descended man sat on David's throne. Jeremiah 18:7-8 is about a nation's repentance and says nothing of Jeconiah's seed.
**[L4]** Joseph's legal kingly reckoning (Matthew 1:20, "son of David") and his adoptive naming of the child (1:21, 25) meet the throne promise of Luke 1:32. The bar on Jeconiah's seed does not touch a child who is not Joseph's seed. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:20-25; [L1] Jeremiah 22:30; [L3] Haggai 1:1.

## V. "Father" language

**[L1]** Luke 2:48 ("thy father and I") is Mary's speech. Luke 2:49 gives the child's reply: "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?" Luke 3:23 says "as was supposed."
**[L2]** *enomizeto* (was reckoned) in Luke 3:23 is the passive of *nomizō* (to hold by custom or opinion). The challenger's parallel, Acts 16:13 ("where prayer was wont to be made"), also describes what was held by custom and not what was verified. Applied to Luke 3:23, it makes "son of Joseph" what was held by custom.
**[L3]** The four registers the challenger names (Mary, the narrator, the town, a disciple) all speak at the level of household and public reputation. John 6:42 says "whose father and mother we know", and the town's knowledge is the very "supposed" Luke qualifies.

## VI. "Of the Holy Ghost"

**[L2]** *gennēthen* (that which is begotten or born) is the root of Matthew's "begat" throughout the chapter. At 1:20 its source is *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of the Holy Spirit), not a man.
**[L1]** John 1:13's "born of God" is not paternity of the flesh. That is true, and it shows that *ek* marks source, whether of the flesh or of God. In Matthew 1:20 the child conceived in her is the subject, and the Spirit is the source.
**[L1]** Matthew 1:20-21 has the naming. But Luke 1:31 gives the same command to Mary: "thou shalt call his name JESUS." Naming is not exclusive to the father.

## VII. Isaiah 7:14

**[L1]** Matthew 1:22-23 quotes "a virgin" as the fulfilment.
**[L2]** *parthenos* (virgin) is Matthew's word under inspiration for Hebrew *'almah* (young woman of marriageable age). In Genesis 24:43 *'almah* is Rebekah, whom 24:16 calls *bethulah* (virgin) "neither had any man known her." No row in the canon uses *'almah* of a mother already married.
**[L1]** Isaiah 7:11 sets the scope: "ask it either in the depth, or in the height above." The *'oth* (sign) is of that order.
