**LINK ROUND**

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED**
Footing:
- [L1] 1 Chronicles 28:6, spoken to David of Solomon: "Solomon thy son, he shall build mine house and my courts: I will be his father, and he shall be my son: and I will establish the throne of his kingdom for ever." One verse names the son David begat and the title God gives him.
- [L1] 2 Samuel 7:12-14: "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels... I will be his father, and he shall be my son." The seed out of bowels and the title stand in one speech.
- [L2] Luke 1:35's own verb is "shall be CALLED the Son of God" (*klēthēsetai*, a designation conferred), and the row names no begetter.
- [L3] Psalm 2:7 "this day have I begotten thee" is applied at the resurrection (Acts 13:33) and beside 2 Samuel 7:14 (Hebrews 1:5).
Chain: the title was given to a man whose begetter is named in the same breath; a conferred title therefore states no begetting and decides nothing about the biological question. My premise is that the title does not SETTLE the question — not that it implies a begetter.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED**
Footing:
- [L1] Luke 2:48-49 in one exchange: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing"... "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" The child's answer concerns his work; the row does not answer Mary's "thy father" with a denial of Joseph's fatherhood.
- [L2] The phrase is *en tois tou patros mou* ("in the things of my Father"), and the 1611 margin reads "or, in my Father's house." Both readings concern the Father's affairs or abode, not a conception.
- [L3] 2 Samuel 7:14 and 1 Chronicles 28:6 (quoted above): Father-Son speech is covenant speech, used of a man whose father is named.
Chain: the phrase names the child's covenant business and kinship; it narrates no begetting; therefore it does not cancel the father Mary names in the dialogue before it.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED**
Footing:
- [L2] *nomizō* (G3543): "from νόμος; properly, to do by law (usage), i.e. to accustom (passively, be usual); by extension, to deem or regard:-- suppose, thing, be wont." The passive *enomizeto* names what was held or wont.
- [L1] The same form runs both ways in its own rows: Luke 2:44 "supposing him to have been in the company" (Joseph and Mary supposed wrongly) and Acts 16:13 "where prayer was wont to be made" (custom, as it was). The verb itself states no verdict; the row decides.
- [L3] Luke 3:23's own neighbours: Luke 2:48 "thy father" and Luke 2:27 "the parents brought in the child Jesus."
Chain: a verb that names what was deemed or custom-bound issues no verdict of its own; the naming rows beside it supply what the supposition is about. That is the premise, footed on the lexicon and the rows.

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED**
Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." The row's verb is the finding. Matthew 1:24 "took unto him his wife"; 1:25 "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son."
- [L2] *synerchomai* (come together) can name married union — 1 Corinthians 7:5 "come together again" — and Matthew sets what he means by what follows: her coming into his house at 1:24.
- [L3] Genesis 4:1 "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived"; Deuteronomy 22:24 calls a betrothed woman "his neighbour's wife"; to part Joseph must "put her away" (Matthew 1:19), and the angel says "Mary thy wife" (1:20).
Chain: 1:18 dates the finding, and 1:25 bounds the abstinence "till." So the row does not narrate the time of the begetting; and the stated means of begetting in the canon is a husband knowing his wife, so within the espousal — "Joseph her husband" (1:19), "Mary thy wife" (1:20) — Joseph knew Mary his wife [L4, on the rows just quoted]. The premise is a statement about what 1:18 narrates, not a claim that no man was involved.

**LINK 5: SUPPORTED**
Footing:
- [L2] Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." The register's chain is active *egennēsen* ("begat") for the fathers; at 1:16 it is passive *egennēthē* ("was born"), with the relative *hēs* ("of whom") feminine singular, attaching to Mary. By its own form the verb names the birth from Mary and no begetter.
- [L1] Matthew 1:1 names the register's verdict: "Jesus Christ, the son of David"; and Matthew 1:20, the angel's speech: "Joseph, thou son of David."
- [L3] The rows that name the father fill what the passive leaves unspoken: "the son of Joseph" (John 1:45; John 6:42; Luke 4:22; Luke 3:23). And the seed rows name the body it comes from: "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30).
Chain: a passive naming the mother is not a statement that no father exists; it is a verb not narrating him. The loins rows pressed against me do not weaken this: even where loins span generations — "kings shall come out of thy loins" (Genesis 35:11), Levi "in the loins of his father" (Hebrews 7:9-10) — the chain still runs through men's bodies; spanning never removes the male body from the row. Two-plus layers exhibited: the morphology [L2], the register and naming rows [L1], the seed rows [L3].

**LINK 6: SUPPORTED**
Footing:
- [L1] The only New Testament rows naming him: Matthew 1:11-12, "Josias begat Jechonias... And after they were brought to Babylon, Jechonias begat Salathiel" — the line continues through him to Jesus Christ "the son of David" (Matthew 1:1). No New Testament row cites the sentence against that throne; the rows that speak of his throne speak of David: "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32); "of the fruit of his loins... to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30).
- [L2] The sentence's own words and bounds: "Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper IN HIS DAYS: for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more IN JUDAH" (Jeremiah 22:30) — bounded to "in his days" and to "in Judah."
- [L3] Jeremiah 18:7-8: "If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil"; Haggai 2:23: "I will make thee as a signet: for I have chosen thee... Zorubbabel... the son of Shealtiel," set as governor (Haggai 1:1; Ezra 3:2), never king in Judah.
Chain: the premise rests not on silence alone but on the sentence's stated bounds and the New Testament's own usage: the bar is bounded to Jechonias's days and to ruling in Judah, the signet is restored on a governor, and the register passes through him to the son of David without one word of the curse.
