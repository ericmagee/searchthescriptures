CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

**Opening:** Christ had a biological father — Joseph, Mary's named husband, of the house of David. The text states the seed as fleshly descent out of a man's body, and no row on the floor says "no man." I press the declared chain keystone-first.

**D6 (keystone) — "Christ had no human biological father."** The seed rows state the contrary, anatomically:

- Romans 1:3 [L1]: "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh." [L2] *sperma* (G4690, seed); *kata sarka* (according to the flesh) is the marker of bodily descent.
- Acts 2:30 [L1]: "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne." [L2] *osphys* (G3751, loins).
- 2 Samuel 7:12 [L1]: "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels." [L2] *mimme'eykha* (out of thy bowels).
- Psalm 132:11 [L1]: "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne" (1611 margin "Heb. belly").
- Hebrews 7:14 [L1]: "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda."

Seed out of loins, bowels, body — "evident." A keystone asserting no begetter must answer these; the opening quotes none of them.

"Made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4) cannot carry the load [L3]: the phrase is the mortality idiom — Job 14:1 "man that is born of a woman is of few days"; Job 15:14; Job 25:4; and Matthew 11:11 "them that are born of women" is said of John the Baptist, whose father is named: "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son" (Luke 1:13). If "born of a woman" excluded a father, John had none. Likewise "the seed of the woman" (Genesis 3:15): seed predicated of a woman in the offspring sense appears in four rows — Genesis 16:10 (Hagar), 24:60 (Rebekah), Isaiah 54:3 (Zion) — and each child has a named father. Naming the mother's side negates no father.

**D2 — "knew her not till."** [L1] Matthew 1:25: "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." "Till" bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy; the sentence does not extend it past the birth. [L3] Genesis 4:1 is the canon's stated means of begetting — "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" — and Matthew names the two husband and wife in law from the espousal: "Joseph her husband" (1:19), "Mary thy wife" (1:20), and Deuteronomy 22:24 calls a betrothed woman "his neighbour's wife"; to part, Joseph must "put her away" (1:19) — a divorce. Standing on those rows (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30 [L1]; Genesis 4:1 [L1]; Matthew 1:19-20, 25 [L1]), the seed must be of the husband's body, and the abstinence is stated only across the pregnancy: [L4] Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal. Luke 1:34 [L1] "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" is [L2] *andra ou ginōskō* (a man I do not know), present indicative — her state at that hour, while espoused, and the angel's word of the conception is future ("thou shalt conceive in thy womb," 1:31). It is a "how," not a vow of never; the answer never says no man shall beget him.

**D3 — Luke 1:35's verbs.** [L2] *eperchomai* (G1904, come upon) is the Spirit's coming on the apostles — "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you" (Acts 1:8) [L3], anointing; *episkiazō* (G1982, overshadow) is the transfiguration cloud (Matthew 17:5; Luke 9:34) and Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15) [L3], divine presence. Neither denotes begetting at any row. What 1:35 states is a title: "shall be CALLED the Son of God" [L2] *klēthēsetai* (shall be called) — the title God gives Solomon, who had a father: "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14); "Solomon thy son ... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6) [L1]. A covenant title cannot decide begetting.

**D4 — Matthew 1:16 and the Jeconiah bar.** [L1] "of whom was born Jesus" after thirty-nine active "begat" rows is non-narration — it does not say "no man" — and the same passive (*egennēthē*, was born) stands at 1:20 of Mary's bearing. The register is headed "Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham" (Matthew 1:1) and counts the generations "unto Christ" (1:17) through Joseph's line; if that line did not reckon his descent, the chapter proves nothing. Reckoning is by the father's house [L1]: "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18); those who "could not shew their father's house" were excluded (Ezra 2:59); priests "called after the name" of a wife's house were "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61-62). Luke 3:23 [L1] reads "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was [the son] of Heli" — a male line; no woman is named in either register.

Jeremiah 22:30 answers its own bar [L1]: "Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper IN HIS DAYS: for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more IN JUDAH." Yet Jechonias is written childless while his sons are listed (1 Chronicles 3:17-18) and Matthew 1:12 says "Jechonias begat Salathiel"; the plucked signet goes to Zerubbabel (Haggai 2:23), who is governor, never king (Haggai 1:1; Ezra 3:2); Jeremiah's own rule: "If that nation ... turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil" (Jeremiah 18:7-8). The New Testament never cites that curse against Jesus' throne; instead the angel says "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20) and the child is given "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32). If the bar stood, Matthew 1:1 and Luke 1:32 are unintelligible.

**D5 — "as was supposed."** [L2] *enomizeto* (was reckoned, was wont) is the same verb form as Acts 16:13 "where prayer was wont to be made" [L3]; it carries no verdict of its own. Against it stand four registers naming the father [L1]: Mary — "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48); the narrator — "the parents brought in the child Jesus" (2:27), "Joseph and his mother" (2:33, 43); the town — "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22), "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know" (John 6:42); a disciple — "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45). Luke 2:49 "my Father's business" cancels none of them: God says "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" of Solomon (2 Samuel 7:14), whose begetter is David (1 Chronicles 28:6) [L3]. "Father" is covenant speech running beside the named begetter.

**D1 — "of the Holy Ghost."** [L2] *ek* (of, from) with God marks covenant origin, not begetting: "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 3:9; 5:1) is never biological paternity [L3]. Where fleshly descent is meant the text adds *kata sarka* (Romans 1:3). The angel's "for" grounds a command to TAKE her — "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" (Matthew 1:20) — and the same angel orders "THOU shalt call his name JESUS" (1:21), naming the child being the father's act (1:25; cf. Luke 1:62-63) [L1]. "Before they came together" (1:18) dates the finding ("she was found with child"), and *synerchomai* can name married union ("come together again," 1 Corinthians 7:5) [L3]; Matthew's own union row is 1:24 "took unto him his wife." Hebrews 10:5 is no witness either: God prepares every body — "thou hast covered me in my mother's womb" (Psalm 139:13) is David's, whose father is Jesse [L3].

**UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES (defender's words, verbatim):**
1. "Mary's descent from David (Luke 3, Romans 1:3) supplies the royal line." — no female name in either register.
2. "Matthew's inspired rendering fixes the sense as virgin" — the received Hebrew is *ha-almah* (the young woman); *bethulah* (the virgin word) is not used there, and the sign is bounded at Isaiah 7:16 and 8:8 in Isaiah's own day.
3. "Joseph's alarm in Matthew 1:19 ... shows that he knew he was not the cause" — the text states intent and two grounds and names no ground of the intent.
4. "the sign Isaiah calls *'oth* would be no sign if it were ordinary conception" — the sign in the text is birth plus timing (Isaiah 7:16).
5. "The conception without Joseph's seed fits the facts" — the seed rows state seed out of a man's body.
6. "The body is prepared by God, not generated by a man" — prepared-in-womb is universal (Psalm 139:13).
7. "Luke 2:49 corrects this at once" — the covenant title runs beside the named father (2 Samuel 7:14; 1 Chronicles 28:6).
