CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, CLOSING AND PREMISE LIST

**Closing challenge — keystone first.**

**LINK 6 (keystone) — "no human biological father."** The defender never lays beside the keystone the flesh rows, so they stand unanswered: Romans 1:3 [L1] "made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**"; Acts 2:30 [L1] "of the fruit of his loins, **according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne"; 2 Samuel 7:12 [L1] "thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels"; Psalm 132:11 [L1] "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne"; Hebrews 7:14 [L1] "it is **evident** that our Lord sprang out of Juda." The answer offered is that loins "span generations" (Hebrews 7:9-10, Levi in Abraham) [L3]. But Acts 2:30 speaks of raising **Christ** to **his throne** — the fruit of the loins, according to the flesh, is the King himself; and "fruit of thy body" (Psalm 132:11) is direct fruit, not a remote ancestor's. Where the text does span generations it says so ("in the loins of his father," Hebrews 7:10).

The daughter-row the defender brings proves my point [L1] 1 Chronicles 2:34-35: "Sheshan gave his daughter to Jarha his servant to wife; **and she bare him Attai**" — the line runs through the daughter only because the **begetting man is named** and the child is written as his. And Ezra 2:61-62 is not a pattern but a penalty: the man who took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai "was called after their name" and was "as polluted, **put from the priesthood**" [L1]. Reckoning runs "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18 [L1]); he who could not shew his father's house was excluded (Ezra 2:59 [L1]). Luke 3:23 [L1] reads "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, **which was [the son] of Heli**" — a male chain; no woman is named in either register. The claim that Mary supplies the Davidic flesh has no row: Luke 1:42 "fruit of thy womb" states a child in her womb, not her lineage.

**LINK 2 — "knew her not till."** [L1] "till she had brought forth her firstborn son" bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy. [L1] Matthew 1:18 dates the finding — "she was **found** with child" — it does not narrate when the child was begotten, and *synerchomai* (come together) names married union at 1 Corinthians 7:5 [L3]; Matthew's own union row is 1:24 "took unto him his wife." Against Deuteronomy 22:23-24 (stoning) the text sets Joseph's act: he "was minded to **put her away**" (Matthew 1:19) — divorce (Matthew 19:7 [L3]) — and the row states intent and two grounds and names no ground of the intent. Luke 1:34 [L1] is *andra ou ginōskō* (a man I do not know), present indicative — her state at the hour of a future promise ("thou shalt **conceive**," 1:31) — a "how," not a vow. The canon's stated means of begetting is knowing (Genesis 4:1 [L1]), and the two are "Joseph her husband" (1:19) and "Mary thy wife" (1:20) — Deuteronomy 22:24 [L1] calls a betrothed woman "his neighbour's wife."

**LINK 3 — Luke 1:35.** [L1] "therefore also that holy thing ... shall be **called** the Son of God" — the *dio kai* (for which reason also) grounds the **title**, and the title is Solomon's covenant title, of one with a begetter: "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14 [L1]); "Solomon **thy son** ... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6 [L1]). The verbs: *eperchomai* (come upon) is the apostles' empowering (Acts 1:8 [L3]); *episkiazō* (overshadow) is the transfiguration cloud (Luke 9:34) and Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15) [L3] — presence, not begetting. Luke 1:37 "nothing shall be impossible" answers a young woman's "how," and does not say no man shall beget him.

**LINK 4 — Matthew 1:16.** [L2] *egennēthē* (was born) is the mother's bearing verb — the same passive is used of Mary at 1:20 — and "of whom" refers to her. Thirty-nine "begat" rows and then silence is non-narration: it does not say "no man." The register heads "Jesus Christ, **the son of David**, the son of Abraham" (Matthew 1:1) and counts the generations "unto Christ" (1:17); if the line did not reckon him, the chapter counts nothing. The divergence (Jacob / Heli) is data the text supplies and never explains; "one register ... the other Mary's through Heli, as sons-in-law were reckoned" is a construct no verse states.

**The Jeconiah bar** [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 itself is bounded: "a man that shall not prosper **in his days** ... ruling any more **in Judah**"; yet Jechonias is written "childless" while his sons are listed (1 Chronicles 3:17-18 [L1]) and "Jechonias begat Salathiel" (Matthew 1:12 [L1]); the plucked signet goes to Zerubbabel (Haggai 2:23 [L1]), governor, never king (Haggai 1:1 [L1]); and Jeremiah 18:7-8 [L1] gives the rule for pronounced sentences. The New Testament never cites the curse against Jesus' throne. If it barred the line, Matthew 1:1 and Luke 1:32 ("the throne of **his father** David") [L1] would be void.

**LINK 5 — "as was supposed."** [L2] *enomizeto* (was reckoned / was wont) is the same form as Acts 16:13 "where prayer **was wont** to be made" [L3] — it carries no verdict of its own. Against one qualifier stand four registers [L1]: Mary — "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48); the narrator — "the parents brought in the child Jesus" (2:27), "Joseph and his mother" (2:33); the town — "the son of Joseph, **whose father and mother we know**" (John 6:42); a disciple — "the son of Joseph" (John 1:45); the angel — "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20). Luke 2:49 "my Father's business" is covenant speech: God says "I will be his father" of Solomon, whose begetter is David (1 Chronicles 28:6 [L3]).

**LINK 1 — "of the Holy Ghost."** [L2] *ek* with God marks covenant origin: "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 3:9 [L3]) is said of men with earthly fathers. Where fleshly descent is meant, the text adds *kata sarka* (Romans 1:3) — and there the flesh is reckoned "of the seed of David," a man's seed. The angel's "for" grounds the command to **take** her, and orders "**thou** shalt call his name JESUS" (1:21), done at 1:25 — the father's naming act (Luke 1:62-63, signs made to "his father, how he would have him called" [L3]).

**Isaiah 7:14:** the received Hebrew is *ha-almah* (the young woman) — the same woman is called *almah* (Genesis 24:43) and *bethulah* (24:16) [L1], so the words are not technical opposites; the sign is birth plus timing ("before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good," 7:16) and "Immanuel" recurs at 8:8 in Isaiah's own day [L1]; the 1611 margin reads "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call."

**UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES --**
LINK 4: "One register can be Joseph's legal line and the other Mary's through Heli, as sons-in-law were reckoned." — the sons-in-law reckoning, a construct no verse states, propping the answer to the divergent registers.
LINK 6: "If the flesh comes only through the woman, the woman supplies the Davidic flesh." — Mary's unattested Davidic descent propping the keystone's sole-parent claim.
LINK 2: "If Joseph knew himself to be the cause, there would be nothing to put away privily and no example to avoid." — reading a ground into Joseph's intent that Matthew 1:19 does not name.
LINK 1: "That is the two-source pattern of Matthew 1:18-20 and Luke 1:35: the flesh comes from David's line, and the Sonship is declared by the Spirit." — the two-source reading of Romans 1:3-4 propping the *ek pneumatos hagiou* begetting claim.
LINK 3: "If Joseph were the begetter, no such answer was needed." — silence of Luke 1:37 read as exclusion of Joseph.
LINK 5: "the town's knowledge is the very 'supposed' Luke qualifies." — the town's first-hand "we know" (John 6:42) demoted to custom by the *enomizeto* of Luke 3:23.
