## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession".
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Defender (MiMo)

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

**OPENING — THE QUESTION AND THE TEXT'S OWN CATEGORY**

The topic asks whether Christ had a biological father. The text has its own word for "biological": "according to the flesh" (kata sarka = "according to the flesh"), and it makes its lineage claim in that word. I hold that he did — Joseph, Mary's husband, of the house of David. Three registers of the text carry it: the seed promised out of a man's body [L1][L3], the father named in four voices [L1], and the stated means of begetting [L1][L3], with the contrary rows measured at the word level [L1][L2].

**I. THE SEED IS FLESH SEED OUT OF A MAN'S BODY [L1]**

2 Samuel 7:12 — "And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom." The promised seed proceeds out of the body of David's line.

Psalm 132:11 — "The LORD hath sworn in truth unto David; he will not turn from it; Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne." The throne takes the fruit of the body.

Acts 2:30 — "Therefore being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him, that of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne;" — "loins" is osphys (G3751 = loins), the same word used of Levi's sons coming "out of the loins of Abraham" (Hebrews 7:5, 10).

Romans 1:3 — "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh;" and 2 Timothy 2:8 — "Remember that Jesus Christ of the seed of David was raised from the dead according to my gospel". [L3] Hebrews 7:14 — "For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda". A seed that "proceeds out of the bowels," "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh," and is "evident," is seed out of a man's body [L3].

**II. THE FATHER IS NAMED, IN FOUR REGISTERS [L1]**

Mary's own mouth — Luke 2:48: "behold, thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." The narrator — Luke 2:33 "And Joseph and his mother marvelled"; Luke 2:41 "Now his parents went to Jerusalem". The town — John 6:42 "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?"; Luke 4:22 "Is not this Joseph's son?"; a disciple — John 1:45 "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph". The angel — Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife". And Joseph names the child: Matthew 1:21 "thou shalt call his name JESUS"; Matthew 1:25 "and he called his name JESUS" — the father's act, as with Zacharias, to whom the sign was given "how he would have him called" (Luke 1:13, 62-63) [L3]. One man is named father in every register; no other man is named anywhere.

**III. JOSEPH IS OF THE HOUSE OF DAVID, AND THE CHILD IS RECKONED TO THE FATHER'S HOUSE [L1][L2][L3]**

Luke 1:27 — "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David" — ex oikou David ("of the house of David") follows the man Joseph [L2]. Luke 2:4 — "because he was of the house and lineage of David"; Luke 1:32 — "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David".

The reckoning is by the father's house: Numbers 1:2 — "after their families, by the house of their fathers, with the number of their names, every male by their polls"; Numbers 1:18 — "they declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers". The inverse rows show the rule enforced: Ezra 2:59 "they could not shew their father's house, and their seed" (excluded); Ezra 2:61-62 the children of Barzillai, "called after their name" of the wife's house, "as polluted, put from the priesthood"; Ezra 10:3 puts away the wives "and such as are born of them" [L3]. Ruth 4:10 — "that the name of the dead be not cut off" [L3]. Mary's own lineage is never stated: Luke 1:36 gives her "thy cousin Elisabeth" (syngenēs = kinswoman), who is "of the daughters of Aaron" (Luke 1:5), and Luke 3:23 runs the line through "the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" — no female name in it [L1].

[L4] On those rows, plus the seed rows of section I which require seed out of a man's body: Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal. Nothing further is narrated, and nothing further is asserted.

**V. THE CONTRARY ROWS, MEASURED AT THE WORD LEVEL [L1][L2][L3]**

Three rows state present state and bounded time, and stand as written: Luke 1:34 "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" "of the Holy Ghost" — Matthew 1:18, 20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost". "of" here is ek (G1537 = "of," "out of"). Everywhere in the New Testament where ek marks origin from God or the Spirit it marks covenant birth, not flesh: John 1:13 "Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God"; 1 John 5:1 "born of God". Where the text marks biological flesh it says kata sarka ("according to the flesh," Romans 1:3); Matthew 1:20 carries no such marker [L2][L3].

[L2] The Greek is to gar en autē gennēthen ek pneumatos hagiou estin (= for that which is begotten in her is out of Holy Spirit). [L2] The relative is hēs (= of whom), feminine singular. It can refer only to Mary. Joseph is "the husband of Mary" (ton andra Marias = the husband of Mary), not "begat Jesus."

#### 5. MOST VULNERABLE

**The defense's seam is the hinge between Matthew 1:19 and Matthew 1:20.** The L4 the record holds — Joseph knew Mary within the espousal — must be placed somewhere, and every placement costs something the defense has not established. Before the conception: then "before they came together" cannot denote union, which requires the assembly sense the marriage row resists, and it leaves a just man planning to put away a woman he had himself come in unto, with the angel's *gar* grounding nothing. After the birth: then the narrative states no begetter at all for the conception it says was "found" — the very gap the defense says the seed rows close. Between them is barred by "knew her not till." The defense has not chosen, and its zero-concession posture is why: no link was re-footed after a hit, and the footing that most needed re-footing — the sense of *synerchomai* — stands as first offered.

**The challenger's seam is that its conclusion rests on three rows, and none of them is a statement about a begetter.** "Before they came together" is a timing; "of the Holy Ghost" is an *ek*-origin whose idiom over believers the challenger itself conceded; "knew her not till" is a bound with a stated end and no stated start. None says "no man." Strip those and the case is a sequence inferred, not a source stated — and its two load-bearing bridges (Heli; legal-versus-flesh) are unrowed, so the sequence must carry everything. Genesis 3:15 and Leviticus 15:16 were left on the table after the defense's turn and never picked back up.

**The shape of the disagreement, unresolved:** the defense argues from what the seed rows *require* (a body, loins, bowels, flesh) plus the one man the registers name; the challenger argues from what the conception rows *state* (a timing, an origin-word, a bound) and from the silence where a begetting would be narrated. Both are arguing from the text's own kind of evidence — requirement on one side, stated sequence on the other — which is why twenty-one links closed with one concession and no collapse.

*No verdict is delivered here.*

[L1] Matthew 1:25 — "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." [L2] heōs hou ("until which") bounds the abstinence by the birth. The row states no *start* for the abstinence. The challenger supplies one — the espousal — and that supply is the whole of their step 1. Their own words concede the bound: the abstinence runs "until she had brought forth."

[L1] The narrative's ordering: v.18 the finding; vv.19-21 the angel; v.24 "took unto him his wife"; v.25 "knew her not till." [L4] The text narrates abstinence across the pregnancy and dates it nowhere before; the seed rows (below) require flesh seed; therefore the record holds Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal and narrates no act, time or place past those rows. [L1] Matthew 1:19 — "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." [L2] dikaios ōn, mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai, eboulēthē lathra apolysai autēn — being just, not wishing to make a public example, he intended to put away quietly. Intent and two grounds; **no verb of knowledge** in the row. Their claim — "the reaction of a man who knew he was not the cause" — is [L4] with no word-level footing: it reads a knowledge claim into a verse that states intent. [L3] The law's public route for a taken betrothed damsel was stoning (Deuteronomy 22:23-24: "then ye shall bring them both out unto the gate of that city, and ye shall stone them"), and the putting-away route is Deuteronomy 24:1; Joseph's choice between them is the choice Matthew narrates. ## II. THE BEGETTING VERB AND "OF THE HOLY GHOST"

Every one of those "born of God" has a human father; [L1] 1 John 5:1 "born of God"; John 3:6 "born of the Spirit" — said of believers begotten of men. [L3] Where the text marks biological descent it says kata sarka ("according to the flesh") — Romans 1:3 "of the seed of David **according to the flesh**"; Romans 9:3 "my kinsmen according to the flesh." [L4] ek + Spirit is the covenant-birth marker, not a begetter: footings (a) [L1] John 1:13, 1 John 5:1 — the same idiom over men with fathers; (b) [L2] ek ("of," origin) set beside kata sarka, the flesh-marker absent at Matthew 1:20. They answer that John 1:13 "excludes the man's act" — that verse is written *of believers*, whose fathers the text never excludes. Their reading proves too much again: it would make every believer fatherless.

[L1] Matthew 1:16 — "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." [L2] after thirty-nine active egennēsen ("begat"), the passive egennēthē ("was born") with hēs ("of whom"). [L4] Passive non-narration, not denial: the verse says "was born," and never "Joseph begat not." Footings: (a) [L2] the passive states the birth, not the begetter; (b) [L1] the named-father rows stand — Luke 2:48 "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing", John 6:42 "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know" — and the register is by the father's house ([L3] Numbers 1:2 "by the house of their fathers"; 1:18 "declared their pedigrees... by the house of their fathers"). CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 2, CLOSING AND PREMISE LIST

# FINAL ROUND: THE DEFENDER'S WINDOW DOES NOT EXIST, AND THE ANGEL'S "FOR" CLOSES IT

## I. THE DEFENDER'S "UNDATED START" HAS NO ROOM IN THE TEXT

The defender says Matthew 1:25 "states no start for the abstinence," so Joseph's knowing can sit inside the espousal. The sequence rules this out.

- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." [L2] The Greek is prin ē synelthein autous (= before they came together). [L3] The verb synerchomai (= come together) is the marriage-union verb. 1 Corinthians 7:5 uses it for husband and wife who "come together again."
- [L1] Matthew 1:20 speaks of what "is conceived" (gennēthen, = begotten) already "in her." So at the finding the conception had already happened, and by the text's own order no coming together had happened.
- [L1] Matthew 1:24–25 then has Joseph take his wife and know her "not till she had brought forth." So the sequence is conception with no union, then taking her, then no knowing until the birth.
- [L4] The defender's window for Joseph's knowing would have to fall between the conception (already present at 1:18, before union) and the birth (1:25, no knowing). The footings are Matthew 1:18 with the sense of synelthein shown at 1 Corinthians 7:5, and Matthew 1:25 with heōs hou (= until which). The text leaves no gap. The defender's "undated start" is an invented interval, and the defender admits that "nothing further is narrated."
- The defender's Genesis 4:1 pattern gives knowing, then conceiving. Matthew gives conceiving, then no knowing.

## II. THE ANGEL'S "FOR" TURNS THE DEFENDER'S OWN PREMISE

The defender says Joseph reasoned from "the norm the whole canon states": pregnancy comes of a man. I accept that, and the text answers it.

- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: **for** that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." [L2] The word gar (= for) gives the ground of the command. The reason Joseph need not fear is the origin of the conception.
- [L4] If the origin were a covenant marker over Joseph's own begetting, the "for" would give no ground. Joseph would have had nothing to fear, since by the defender's account he knew her. The footings are the gar of Matthew 1:20 and Matthew 1:19 (his intent to put her away privily). The angel overturns the canon's norm for this one conception, and the defender's norm cannot be the answer to the angel's "for."
- [L1] The defender's reading of ek (= out of) fails on John 1:13. The verse says "**nor of the will of man**, but of God." [L2] The text sets ek theou (= out of God) against ek thelēmatos andros (= out of the will of a man). The defender says the verse is "written of believers, whose fathers the text never excludes," but the verse itself excludes the will of man as the source of that birth. Its contrast is the one Matthew 1:20 makes.

## III. LUKE 1:34–37: THE REPLY STATES A MEANS

- [L1] Luke 1:27 says Mary was already "espoused." [L1] Luke 1:31 says "thou shalt conceive." [L1] Luke 1:34 asks "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" [L2] The word epei (= since) makes her present state the ground of the question.
- [L4] If the promised conception were to follow marriage in the ordinary way, the question would not arise, because an espoused woman expects marriage to bring children. The footings are Luke 1:27 (espoused) and Luke 1:34 (epei, "since I know not a man"). Her question shows she understood the promise as not waiting on a man.
- [L1] The reply, Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also." [L2] dio kai (= for which cause also) makes this the cause. The defender says the angel's "silence is not a statement." But the angel did state a cause, and it names no man.
- [L1] Luke 1:37: "For with God nothing shall be impossible." [L3] The same answer is given to Sarah's "how" at Genesis 18:14, "Is any thing too hard for the LORD?" A reply of impossibility to a question of means points to means beyond the ordinary.
- [L1] Luke 1:35 says "born **of thee**" (ek sou, = out of you). Only one human parent is named as the source.
- [L3] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me" (katērtisō, = thou hast prepared). [L1] Galatians 4:4 says "made of a woman." Neither names a man.

## IV. THE SEED ROWS NAME NO BEGETTER, AND THE DEFENDER'S OWN RUTH ROW SHOWS RECKONING WITHOUT BEGETTING

- [L1] Romans 1:3 says "made" (genomenou, = came to be), not "begat." [L1] Acts 2:30 and 2 Samuel 7:12 say "David." None names Joseph's body.
- [L3] The defender cites Ruth 4:10: "that the name of the dead be not cut off." In that row the child was begotten by Boaz and reckoned to the dead man's house. The defender's reckoning rule shows reckoning without begetting.
- [L1] Luke 3:23: "as was supposed" (hōs enomizeto, = as was reckoned). The narrator himself qualifies "son of Joseph." The defender replies that the word "carries no verdict," but the qualification would have no purpose if the sonship needed none.
- [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 says "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." The defender says the curse also cuts the legal line. [L4] The legal line is not the flesh line. The footings are Jeremiah 22:30 (the bar is on "seed") and Matthew 1:16 ("husband of Mary, of whom was born"). A throne passed by law and not by Joseph's seed does not pass through the barred seed. The defender's own flesh-seed reading puts the child inside the barred seed.
- [L1] Isaiah 7:14 is applied at Matthew 1:22–23: "that it might be fulfilled… Behold, a virgin shall be with child." [L2] The defender concedes that Genesis 24:43 (ha-almah, = the maiden) and Genesis 24:16 (a virgin) cover the same person. The lexical objection therefore does not move Matthew's application. The defender quotes Matthew's application "as given," and as given it names a virgin.
- [L1] Luke 2:48–49: Mary says "thy father," and the child says "my Father's business." I do not deny Joseph was called father. The text sets his fatherhood by marriage and law beside the other Father, and the defender's own 2 Samuel 7:14 pairing of two fathers confirms two registers. The question concerns the biological register, and the stated causes there are Matthew 1:20 and Luke 1:35.

## V. CLOSE

- [L1] Matthew 1:18: pregnancy before coming together.
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: gar, the conceived thing is of the Holy Ghost.
