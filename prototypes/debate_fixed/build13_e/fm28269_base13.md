## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession".
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Defender (MiMo)

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

The question is asked at the floor of the text: Did Christ have a biological father? The answer the words give is **yes** — and the father is named. Method: precept upon precept, line upon line (Isaiah 28:10); each claim below carries its layer.

**THE FATHER IS NAMED, IN FIVE MOUTHS.** [L1] The child's own mother, to the child: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48). [L1] The narrator: "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33); "his parents went to Jerusalem" (Luke 2:41). [L1] The town: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22); "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" (John 6:42). [L1] A disciple: "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45). [L1] The angel: "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20). [L1] And the line is his: "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David" (Luke 1:27); "Joseph also went up ... of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4). [L2] Luke 1:27's "of the house of David" is *ex oikou David* (G1537 + G3624 + G1138), and it follows the man's name, not the woman's. [L4] A father named by the mother, the narrator, the town, a disciple and the angel, in the same house the throne was promised to, is not a metaphor; footing: [L1] the five registers above and [L2] the Davidic clause attached to the man.

**THE SEED IS A FLESH SEED, AND IT COMES OUT OF THE FATHER'S BODY.** [L1] "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12). [L1] "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne" (Psalm 132:11). [L1] "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3). [L1] "Of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30). [L1] "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14). [L2] Acts 2:30's "loins" is *osphys* (G3751), the same word Paul uses for the Levites "who receiveth the tithes" and "whose is Abraham" (Hebrews 7:5, 10) — the anatomy-word of descent, not a figure. [L4] A seed "proceeding out of the bowels," "of the fruit of the body," "according to the flesh," and "evident" is bodily descent from a father; footing: [L1] the four stating rows and [L2] the loins-word.

**THE THREE QUIET ROWS SAY NO MORE THAN THEY SAY — AND NOT "NO MAN."** [L1] "of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matthew 1:16). [L2] *egennēthē* (G1080, passive) stands after thirty-nine active *egennēsen* rows in the same genealogy — the verb turns passive exactly where the begetting is not narrated, which is non-narration, not denial. [L1] "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34). [L2] *andra ou ginōskō* (G435 + G1097) is present active indicative — "I do not know a man" at the moment of speaking — while she is already "espoused" (Luke 1:27), the same woman of whom the same Gospel later says "Joseph and his mother" (2:33). [L1] "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25). [L2] *heōs hou eteken* (G2193 + G3757 + G5088) bounds the sentence at the birth itself. [L4] These three rows state a present state, a postponed marriage, and a verb the text declines to narrate — none of them states that no man fathered the child; footing: [L2] the three forms and [L1] their own context (espousal at Luke 1:27; the birth at Matthew 1:25).

**"OF THE HOLY GHOST" IS COVENANT ORIGIN, NOT BEGETTING.** [L1] "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20; the 1611 margin "Gr. begotten"). [L2] *ek* (G1537) with God or Spirit in the New Testament is origin-of-covenant every time it is used: "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 3:9; 5:1), "is of God" (1 John 4:7) — spiritual birth, never bodily paternity. [L2] Paul marks bodily descent with a second phrase — "according to the flesh," *kata sarka* (G2596 + G4561), Romans 1:3 — and that qualifier is absent from Matthew 1:20. [L3] The angel's two verbs carry the same reading: "the Holy Ghost shall come upon thee" is *eperchomai* (G1904), the word of "the Holy Ghost is come upon you" (Acts 1:8, empowerment) and "the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee" is *episkiazō* (G1982), the word of the transfiguration cloud (Luke 9:34) and Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15). [L3] And the angel's title is no paternity verdict: "he shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35) is the title Solomon received while his father David lived — "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14); "Solomon thy son ... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6). [L4] So the Holy Ghost sentence names covenant origin and anointing, and the biological father question is answered by the named Joseph above; footing: [L2] *ek*/*kata sarka* and the two angel-verbs, and [L3] Acts 1:8, Luke 9:34, 2 Samuel 7:14.

**THE RECKONING RUNS BY THE FATHER'S HOUSE, AND IT IS JOSEPH'S.** [L1] "by the house of their fathers ... every male by their polls" (Numbers 1:2); "declared their pedigrees ... by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:18). [L1] Joseph is "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4), and "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20). [L3] The text strikes the inverse register: those who "could not shew their father's house" were excluded (Ezra 2:59, margin "or, pedigree"), and priests reckoned after the wife's house were "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61-62). [L4] A line reckoned "by the house of their fathers" and drawn through the named Davidic father is a biological line; footing: [L1] Numbers 1:2, 18 and [L3] Ezra 2:59, 61-62.

DECLARED CHAIN
D1. Joseph is named as the father by the mother, the narrator, the town, a disciple and the angel (Luke 2:48; 2:33; 2:41; 4:22; John 6:42; 1:45; Matthew 1:20) and is "of the house of David" (Luke 1:27, *ex oikou David*) — footing: [L1] five registers + [L2] the Davidic clause on the man.
D2. The promised seed is a flesh seed proceeding out of the father's body (2 Samuel 7:12; Psalm 132:11; Romans 1:3; Acts 2:30; Hebrews 7:14) with *osphys* (G3751, loins) as the descent word (Hebrews 7:5, 10) — footing: [L1] four stating rows + [L2] the anatomy word.
D3. **THE THREE QUESTIONS.**

**First — the "for" of Matthew 1:20.** Joseph's fear is the fear his own row records: he "was minded to put her away" (1:19) after the discovery "before they came together" (1:18). The angel answers that fear: "fear not to **take** unto thee Mary thy wife" — and the ground given is the child's covenant holiness ("of the Holy Ghost") and Joseph's own office: "**thou** shalt call his name JESUS" (1:21), which he does (1:25). The message gives the naming to Joseph — the father's act (Luke 1:62-63) — so the "for" grounds the taking and the naming, not a verdict on the seed.

**Second — *dio kai* of Luke 1:35.** It is grounded on the **setting-apart** ("the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee") and issues a **title**: "shall be called the Son of God" — the title already given in v.32 ("he shall be called the Son of the Highest"), before v.34's question. And the title is the one a son with a living father bore: "Solomon thy son ... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6). *Klēthēsetai* is naming, not begetting.

**Third — Jeremiah 22:30 and the line.** The curse is bounded ("in his days ... in Judah"), never cited against this throne, answered by Haggai 2:23's signet, bounded again by Jeremiah 18:7-8, and joined to sons actually listed (1 Chronicles 3:17-18). The route that avoids Joseph runs through a Salathiel "of Neri" (Luke 3:27) whose identity with Matthew's the text never states — and the throne was promised through the named house of David (Luke 1:27, 32), which is Joseph's.

**Floating inferences in the challenger's case** (each assumes what it must prove): "a just man does not put away the mother of his own child" (Matthew 1:19 has intent, not knowledge); "her question assumes conception without a man" (Luke 1:34 is present indicative, asked while espoused); "Davidic flesh can come through Mary" (the four woman-seed rows are none Davidic); "the child redirects 'father'" (Luke 2:49 is the mission register, beside 2:51 "subject unto them"); "they are the wrong witnesses" (unbelief on one subject does not erase an observation on another).

**D1-D6 stand.** The text names Joseph as father in five mouths, requires a flesh seed out of the father's body, holds the three quiet rows as non-narration and present-state, reads "of the Holy Ghost" as covenant origin at every parallel row, and reckons by the father's house. C7 — "no human biological father" — is stated by no verse; the seed rows exclude it, and no mechanism is narrated in either direction.
