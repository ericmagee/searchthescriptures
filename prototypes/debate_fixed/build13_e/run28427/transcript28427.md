## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession".
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Defender (MiMo)

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

**OPENING — THE DEFENDER**

The question is plain: did Christ have a biological father? The text answers yes. A man was involved, the text names him, and the seed is David's own flesh. The same text refuses to narrate the *how* of the conception — so neither will I. What I affirm is the WHO the verses affirm; what I do not do is supply a mechanism the verses never supply.

**D1 — The seed is bodily flesh, out of a man's body.**

[L1] "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3). "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12). "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne" (Psalm 132:11). "Of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30). "It is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14).

[L2] sperma (G4690) = seed; kata sarka = according to the flesh; osphys (G3751) = loins, the same word in Acts 2:30 and Hebrews 7:5, 10; mimme'eykha = out of thy bowels. These rows are anatomical: bowels, body, loins, flesh. A seed that "proceeds out of the bowels" and is "the fruit of the loins" is a real bodily seed.

[L3] "The sceptre shall not depart from Judah... until Shiloh come" (Genesis 49:10) — the line is a flesh line through Judah; "evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14) is that promise seen fulfilled.

A real seed proceeding out of a body, according to the flesh, evident, cannot stand with "no man."

**D2 — The father is named, and named in every register.**

[L1] Mary's own mouth: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48). The narrator: "And Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33); "his parents" (Luke 2:41); "Joseph... being of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4). The town: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22); "Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know" (John 6:42). A disciple: "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45). The angel: "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20); "thou shalt call his name JESUS" (Matthew 1:21); "and he called his name JESUS" (Matthew 1:25).

[L2] The naming is the father's act — ekalesen (G2564) = he called; the angel gives the name to Joseph ("thou shalt call"), and Joseph does it.

[L3] In Luke's own narrative the naming belongs to the father: "thou shalt call his name John" (Luke 1:13), and the neighbours "made signs to his father, how he would have him called" (Luke 1:62).

Mary, the narrator, the town, a disciple, and the angel all give the child a father, and the father names him.

**D3 — The line is reckoned by the father's house.**

[L1] "By the house of their fathers... every male by their polls" (Numbers 1:2); "they declared their pedigrees after their families, by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:18). Joseph is "of the house of David" (Luke 1:27), "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4), and the promise is "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32).

[L2] ex oikou David = of the house of David; the throne is called "the throne of his father David."

[L3] The inverse rows show the same rule by its failures: they who "could not shew their father's house" are excluded (Ezra 2:59, margin "or, pedigree"), and those called after the wife's house are "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61-62); Ruth is taken to wife "that the name of the dead be not cut off" (Ruth 4:10).

**D4 — "Son of God" answers a different question.**

[L1] God says to David of Solomon, "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father" (1 Chronicles 28:6); "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14) — a son with a living father. And the begetting is placed at the resurrection: "Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee" (Psalm 2:7), applied at Acts 13:33, and "declared to be the Son of God... by the resurrection from the dead" (Romans 1:4). In Luke the title and throne come *before* the question: Luke 1:32-33 then 1:34, and 1:35 "he shall be called the Son of God."

[L2] klēthēsetai (G2564) = he shall be called — a called name, not a begetting. eperchomai (G1904) = come upon: "ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you" (Acts 1:8) — the apostles; episkiazō (G1982) = overshadow: "a cloud overshadowed them" (Luke 9:34). Neither verb denotes begetting at any row in the canon.

[L3] The title belongs to Israel ("my son, even my firstborn," Exodus 4:22) and to the king ("I will make him my firstborn," Psalm 89:27) as well. "Of the Holy Ghost" is covenant-origin language — "born of God" is said of believers (John 1:13; 1 John 5:1), never of biological begetting, and Romans 1:3 marks biological flesh with "according to the flesh."

So the title does not answer who begat the child; the seed rows and the named father answer that.

**D5 — The quiet rows are non-narration, not denial.**

[L1] "of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matthew 1:16) is one passive line after thirty-nine active begetting lines in the same genealogy — it does not narrate the begetting; it does not say "no man." "Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily" (Matthew 1:19) records intent, and names no verb of knowledge. "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34) is spoken while she is betrothed (Luke 1:27) and is present tense — "I do not now know a man." "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25) is bounded by "till," and then names the son whom Joseph names.

[L2] andra ou ginōskō = I know not a man — present active indicative, the state at the hour of speaking; prin ē synelthein autous = before they came together.

[L3] "Man that is born of a woman is of few days" (Job 14:1); "born of a woman" is the mortality idiom said of John the Baptist (Matthew 11:11), whose father is named in the same book (Luke 1:13, 57-63).

Rows held as written, none of them saying "no man."

**D6 — The answer.**

[L1] "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3); "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48); "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4). [L2] sperma = seed, kata sarka = according to the flesh. [L3] 2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30.

Christ had a biological father. A man was involved; the seed is David's own flesh out of bowels and loins; the father is named Joseph in every register of the narrative; and the text states the WHO without narrating the HOW — which is exactly where this answer stops.

**DECLARED CHAIN**

1. The seed of Christ is bodily flesh out of a man's body — [L1] Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David according to the flesh"; 2 Samuel 7:12 "proceed out of thy bowels" — [L2] sperma (G4690) = seed, osphys (G3751) = loins, kata sarka = according to the flesh — [L3] Psalm 132:11; Acts 2:30; Hebrews 7:14.
2. The father is named in the text's own registers — [L1] Luke 2:48 "thy father and I"; John 6:42 "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know"; Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David" — [L2] ekalesen (G2564) = he called, the father's act of naming at Matthew 1:25 — [L3] Luke 1:13; 1:62.
3. The line is reckoned by the father's house — [L1] Numbers 1:2, 18 "by the house of their fathers"; Luke 2:4 "of the house and lineage of David"; Luke 1:32 "the throne of his father David" — [L2] ex oikou David = of the house of David — [L3] Ezra 2:59 (margin "or, pedigree"), Ezra 2:61-62, Ruth 4:10.
4. "Son of God" is a covenant and resurrection title, not a paternity statement — [L1] 1 Chronicles 28:6 "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son"; Acts 13:33; Romans 1:4 "by the resurrection" — [L2] klēthēsetai (G2564) = shall be called; eperchomai (G1904) = come upon (Acts 1:8); episkiazō (G1982) = overshadow (Luke 9:34) — [L3] Exodus 4:22; Psalm 89:27.
5. The quiet rows are non-narration and held as written, not a denial of a man — [L1] Matthew 1:16 "of whom was born Jesus"; Matthew 1:19 (intent); Luke 1:34 "I know not a man"; Matthew 1:25 "knew her not till" — [L2] andra ou ginōskō = present indicative; prin ē synelthein = before they came together — [L3] Job 14:1 with Matthew 11:11 (John, born of a woman, father named).
6. Christ had a biological father: a man was involved, the father is named Joseph, the seed is David's flesh, and the text states the WHO while never narrating the HOW.

## Round 1 - Challenger (Claude)

CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

### CHALLENGER: THE TEXT SAYS THE CHILD WAS CONCEIVED OF THE HOLY GHOST, NOT BEGOTTEN BY JOSEPH

The defender says the text "refuses to narrate the how" and calls the quiet rows "non-narration." That is false at three points: Matthew 1:16, 1:18-25, and Luke 1:34-35. The defender's D1-D6 never quotes Matthew 1:18, 1:20, 1:22-23, Luke 1:35 in full, Luke 2:49, or Luke 3:23. Under the rules, what is not challenged stands as held for the side that presented it, so I press these.

#### C1 — Matthew 1:16 breaks the begetting pattern, and the break is the narration

[L1] "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matthew 1:16). Every preceding line reads "A begat B" (Matthew 1:2-15).

[L2] The preceding lines use egennēsen (begat, active, with the father as subject). At verse 16 the verb changes to egennēthē (was born, passive), and the relative is ex hēs (out of whom, feminine singular). Joseph is called ton andra Marias (the husband of Mary), not the begetter of Jesus. The Greek relative is feminine, so it cannot refer to Joseph.

[L4] The defender calls this "one passive line" that "does not narrate." But the chain is: (a) the pattern of verse 2-15 (L1) makes "begat" the expected verb for every father; (b) verse 16 withholds that verb in the one place where the defender's thesis needs it, and the feminine ex hēs (L2) assigns the birth to Mary alone. A writer who believed Joseph begat Jesus would have written "Joseph begat Jesus." The departure from the pattern is itself the statement.

#### C2 — Matthew 1:18-25 fixes the order of events, and the order excludes Joseph

[L1] "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:18). "That which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20). "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25).

[L2] Matthew 1:18 has prin ē synelthein autous (before they came together), then heurethē en gastri echousa ek pneumatos hagiou (she was found having in womb out of spirit holy). Matthew 1:20 has to gar en autē gennēthen ek pneumatos estin hagiou (for that which in her is begotten is out of spirit holy). The defender quotes prin ē synelthein in D5 and so concedes the timing. The verb gennēthen (begotten/conceived) is the same root as the genealogy's egennēsen (begat). The source is ek (out of) pneumatos hagiou (Holy Spirit). In the genealogy the father is the source of gennaō. Here the Holy Ghost is.

[L3] John 1:13 is the defender's own row for "born of God." It says "which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." The ek theou (out of God) source there is stated as excluding the will of man. The defender cannot use it to flatten ek pneumatos into something compatible with a human begetter.

[L4] Chain: (a) the pregnancy is found before any coming together (Matthew 1:18, L1, with prin ē synelthein, L2); (b) Joseph "knew her not" until after the birth (Matthew 1:25, L1); (c) the conceived child is gennēthen ek pneumatos hagiou (L2, Matthew 1:20). So Matthew places the conception before any union and gives its source as the Holy Ghost. The defender must say that Joseph is the biological father by some unnamed means, with no union and no verse. That is a mechanism the defender says the text does not supply, and the defender supplies it anyway.

#### C3 — The angel's answer to Joseph answers the very question the defender calls "intent"

[L1] "Joseph her husband, being a just man... was minded to put her away privily" (Matthew 1:19). "Fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20).

[L2] The angel gives the reason for Joseph's fear with gar (for): "for that which is begotten in her is out of the Holy Spirit."

[L3] Deuteronomy 22:23-24 treats an unfaithful betrothed woman as a capital matter. This explains why Joseph, "just," would seek to put her away.

[L4] The defender says this verse "names no verb of knowledge." The footing is: Joseph's plan (Matthew 1:19, L1) is to put her away privily, which presupposes he is not the father; the angel's remedy (Matthew 1:20, L1, with gar, L2) is the Holy Ghost as the source. If Joseph were the biological father, the remedy would be "the child is yours." The angel never says that.

#### C4 — Luke 1:34-35 does supply the how, and Mary's question requires it

[L1] "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34). "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35). "For with God nothing shall be impossible" (Luke 1:37).

[L2] Luke 1:34 has epei andra ou ginōskō (since a man I know not): epei is causal. Luke 1:35 has dio kai (wherefore also), tying the title to the Holy Ghost's coming; and to gennōmenon ek sou hagion (the being-begotten out of thee, holy). The source named is ek sou (out of thee), Mary alone. Luke 1:13's contrast is telling: "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son" is gennēsei huion soi (shall bear a son to thee). Matthew 1:21 is tex-etai de huion (she shall bring forth a son), with no soi (to thee). The defender uses Luke 1:13 as a parallel. The Greek shows the exact parallel is missing in Joseph's case.

[L3] Luke 1:36-37 pairs this with Elisabeth's barrenness and "nothing impossible." Hebrews 10:5, "a body hast thou prepared me" (katērtisō, thou hast fitted), puts the preparation of his body with God.

[L4] The defender reads Luke 1:34 as "I do not now know a man," spoken by a betrothed woman. The chain: (a) Mary asks "how shall this be" about a conception announced as imminent (Luke 1:31, "thou shalt conceive," L1); (b) the angel does not answer "after marriage," but names the Holy Ghost's coming and the Highest's overshadowing (L1), with dio kai (therefore also, L2) as the ground; (c) the question and answer would be pointless if the means were ordinary, and Luke 1:37 appeals to "nothing impossible" (L1, L3). So the angel's answer supplies the mechanism. The defender's claim that the text "refuses to narrate the how" is refuted by Luke 1:35.

#### D1 answer — "seed" and "loins" are ancestral language and do not name Joseph

[L1] "Levi also, who receiveth tithes, payed tithes in Abraham. For he was yet in the loins of his father, when Melchisedec met him" (Hebrews 7:9-10). [L2] Hebrews 7:10 uses the defender's own word osphys (loins) of a great-grandson, so the word spans generations. Galatians 4:4 has genomenon ek gynaikos (coming to be out of a woman), with the same verb genomenon as Romans 1:3 (genomenou ek spermatos Dauid, coming to be out of seed of David).

[L3] Isaiah 7:14: "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel," with Matthew 1:22-23: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled... Behold, a virgin shall be with child." The Hebrew is ha'almah (the maiden); Rebekah is 'almah (Genesis 24:43) after being described as "neither had any man known her" (Genesis 24:16). The Greek of Matthew 1:23 is hē parthenos (the virgin).

[L4] The defender's chain 1 goes from "seed of David" to "Joseph's seed." Footing against it: (a) Hebrews 7:10 (L1) with osphys (L2) shows "loins" language reaches back generations, so Acts 2:30 and 2 Samuel 7:12 do not require the immediate father to be David's descendant by begetting; (b) Galatians 4:4 (L1) with ek gynaikos (L2) names only the woman as the human source of the coming. No verse says "Joseph's seed." The text says "seed of David," and that fits descent through Mary, whom Luke 1:27 describes in the same sentence as "of the house of David." Luke 3:23-38 gives a second genealogy, and Luke 3:23 is where the line reaches Joseph with the qualifier below.

#### D2 answer — the named "father" is legal and supposed, and Luke says so

[L1] "Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph" (Luke 3:23). "Wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" [L3] Elisabeth names the child: "Not so; but he shall be called John" (Luke 1:60). Isaiah 7:14 puts the naming in the mother's mouth (weqara't, and she shall call). Genesis 4:25: "she called his name Seth." The defender cites Luke 1:62 but passes over Luke 1:60. So naming is not exclusively the father's act.

[L4] Chain: (a) the town's and Mary's usage is "father" in the household sense (Luke 2:48, John 6:42, L1); (b) Luke 3:23 (L1, with hōs enomizeto, L2) explicitly tells the reader that the Joseph-sonship was a supposition; (c) the child's reply at Luke 2:49 (L1) corrects "thy father" to "my Father." The defender collects voices that call Joseph father. The narrator's own correction (Luke 3:23) and the child's own correction (Luke 2:49) are in the same text, and the defender never answered them.

#### D3 answer — reckoning by the father's house is legal, not biological

[L1] "Thy wife... Ruth... have I purchased to be my wife, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance" (Ruth 4:10). "The firstborn which she beareth shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead" (Deuteronomy 25:6). "There is a son born to Naomi" (Ruth 4:17). "Boaz begat Obed of Ruth" (Matthew 1:5). "The priests... which took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai the Gileadite, and was called after their name" (Ezra 2:61).

[L2] Ruth 4:10 and Deuteronomy 25:6 use shem (name) as the legal carrier of the line.

[L4] The defender's own rows (D3, Ruth 4:10 and Ezra 2:61) show that the house or name to which a child is reckoned can differ from the biological father: Obed is reckoned to Naomi and the dead, though Matthew 1:5 says Boaz begat him (L1, with Deuteronomy 25:6, L3). So "of the house of David" for Joseph (Luke 2:4) supports Joseph's legal fatherhood and the throne's legal title. It does not prove Joseph begat Jesus. Matthew 1:24 "took unto him his wife" is the act that makes the child his in law.

#### D4 answer — the "Son of God" title is tied to the conception in Luke

[L1] "Therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35). "Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee" (Psalm 2:7), applied in Acts 13:33 to the raising up, and "declared to be the Son of God with power... by the resurrection" (Romans 1:4).

[L2] Romans 1:4 has horisthentos (declared, marked out): a declaration of what he already was, not the origin of his sonship. Luke 1:35 has dio kai (therefore also): the title follows from the Holy Ghost's coming.

[L4] The defender says the title answers a different question, and uses 2 Samuel 7:14 for Solomon. Chain: Luke 1:35 (L1) with dio kai (L2) places the cause of the title in the Holy Ghost's coming, and Romans 1:4 (L1) with horisthentos (L2) says the resurrection declared what was already so. Solomon had a named father, David, who is addressed ("Solomon thy son," 1 Chronicles 28:6). In Luke 1:35 the one addressed is Mary, and the cause named is the Holy Ghost, not any man.

#### Concession and boundary

I concede that Joseph is husband, guardian, and legal father (Matthew 1:24-25, Luke 2:48). I concede that "the text does not narrate a mechanism beyond the Holy Ghost's coming and the overshadowing" (Luke 1:35). That is exactly what I affirm, and I add no mechanism of my own.

## Round 2 - Defender (MiMo)

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

**ROUND 2 — DEFENDER**

The challenger's case closes with a sentence the text never writes: "Christ had no biological human father." No verse says it. It is assembled out of one passive verb, two timing clauses, and two empowerment verbs — and then asserted as if a verse had stated it. Below I answer the presses.

**On C1 — Matthew 1:16.** The verse reads: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." [L1] The passive "was born" (egennēthē, G1080, passive) follows thirty-nine active "begat" lines and narrates no begetting in either direction: it does not say Joseph begat him, and it does not say a man did not. Non-narration is not a denial. [L2] ex hēs (out of whom) is grammatical agreement with Mary, the nearest named woman — and the very clause names "Joseph the husband of Mary" at the point where the challenger says Joseph is excluded. A writer making the negative statement the challenger wants would not need to name the man there at all. [L3] What fills the one quiet line is the chorus the challenger never answered: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48); "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know" (John 6:42); "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22); "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45). One passive verb does not outvote four named registers.

**On C2 — Matthew 1:18, 1:20, 1:25.** I hold each row as written: "before they came together, she was found with child" (prin ē synelthein autous); "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (ek pneumatos hagiou); "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." [L1] What these state is (a) when she was *found* with child, (b) the child's holy origin, (c) a bounded period of non-union ending at the birth. None of the three says "no man," and none names Joseph's person as excluded from the seed. [L2] "Of the Holy Ghost" is ek + Spirit, and ek + God or Spirit in the New Testament marks covenant or spiritual origin, never biological paternity: "born of God" is said of believers (John 1:13; 1 John 5:1), "is of God" of those that love (1 John 4:7). When the text means biological flesh it adds the marker — "made of the seed of David *according to the flesh*" (Romans 1:3, kata sarka); Matthew 1:20 has no kata sarka. [L3] The challenger's own proof shows the point: John 1:13 is spoken of those who "received him" (v.12) — believers' new birth, "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh." If ek + Spirit were biological begetting, every believer "born of God" would be biologically begotten of God. The row is origin-language, and applying it to a birth narrative as paternity is the import.

**On C3 — Matthew 1:19.** The row reads: "Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." [L2] dikaios ōn (being just), mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai (not willing to make her a publick example), eboulēthē (he planned) — intent and two grounds. There is no verb of knowing in the row. The challenger's claim that the plan "presupposes he is not the father" reads a belief into a text that records a plan. [L3] Deuteronomy 22:23-24 and 24:1 are not named by Matthew; the angel's word is "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife," and it closes by assigning Joseph the father's act: "*Thou* shalt call his name JESUS" (Matthew 1:21), and "he called his name JESUS" (1:25). A remedy that ends in the man naming the child does not exclude the man.

**On C4 — Luke 1:34-35.** [L1] "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" is spoken by a betrothed woman (Luke 1:27), and andra ou ginōskō (I know not a man) is present active indicative — the state at the hour of speaking, not a vow. The answer is: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." [L2] eperchomai (G1904, come upon) is the empowerment verb of Acts 1:8 — "ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you," said of the apostles; episkiazō (G1982, overshadow) is the cloud of Luke 9:34 ("a cloud overshadowed them") and Peter's shadow at Acts 5:15. At no row in the canon does either verb denote begetting. And the sentence ends "shall be *called* (klēthēsetai) the Son of God" — a title, given with the throne at Luke 1:32-33 *before* the question at 1:34. [L3] The challenger's soi-argument answers itself: naming is assigned to Joseph in the birth account ("thou shalt call his name JESUS," Matthew 1:21), and naming is not exclusive in the record anyway (Elisabeth, Luke 1:60; Eve, Genesis 4:25) — so the dative pronoun proves nothing in either direction. Hebrews 10:5 "a body hast thou prepared me" says God prepared the body; it does not say by what means, and the challenger's own closing concession admits it: "the text does not narrate a mechanism beyond the Holy Ghost's coming and the overshadowing." Granted as stated — and that is my very point. A text that narrates no mechanism has not said "no man."

**On the seed answer (their D1 reply).** [L1] The rows stand: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ" (Acts 2:30); "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12); "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14). [L2] Hebrews 7:9-10, Levi "in the loins of his father," shows osphys (loins) is *bodily descent language* spanning generations — it expands the flesh line, it does not dissolve it. [L3] And the flesh line is not routed through Mary: Luke 1:27 reads "a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David" (ex oikou David follows the man), and Luke 3:23 runs the line through Joseph — "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" — with no female name anywhere in it. Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman" is the mortality idiom, said in the same form of John the Baptist, "born of a woman" (Matthew 11:11; Job 14:1 "man that is born of a woman"), and John's father is named in Luke 1:13.

**On the "supposed" and "my Father" answer (their D2 reply).** [L1] Luke 3:23's hōs enomizeto (as was supposed) carries no verdict of its own — the same verb form appears at Acts 16:13, "where prayer *was wont* to be made" — and the verse then reckons the line through Joseph anyway. Luke 2:49 is not a correction of "thy father": the child answers "about my Father's business," and the same Gospel then records "he went down with them, and came to Nazareth, and was subject unto them" (Luke 2:51) — "them," the parents of 2:48 and 2:41, after the answer. John 6:41's murmuring does not cancel the fact the speakers state ("whose father and mother we know"), and it stands beside Nathanael's "the son of Joseph" (John 1:45) spoken without murmuring.

**On Isaiah 7:14 (their D1/D5 row).** [L2] At the word level the verse reads ha-almah (the young woman) — seven rows in the canon — while bethulah (the virgin-word, fifty rows) is not used there; the 1611 margin reads "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call." [L1] The sign is bounded by Isaiah 7:16 ("before the child shall know to refuse the evil") and Isaiah 8:3-4, and the name recurs at Isaiah 8:8 in Isaiah's own day. And the verse says the woman "shall conceive, and bear a son" — conceiving is predicated of the woman, and not one word of it excludes a husband.

**WHERE IT STANDS.** The challenger's links 1-4 rest on the same three rows — Matthew 1:16, 1:18-25, Luke 1:34-35 — and each is held *as written* by this side; what none of them says is "no man." Their link 7 asserts "no biological human father," which is not a row at all: it is a conclusion drawn from a passive verb, two timing clauses, and two verbs of empowerment and presence. Against it stand the anatomical seed rows (bowels, body, loins, flesh, "evident... sprang out of Juda") and the named father in Mary's mouth, the narrator's, the town's, a disciple's, and the angel's. The challenger's own concession sets the ceiling of their case: no mechanism is narrated. At that ceiling, the text names the WHO and withholds the HOW — and this side asserts the WHO and narrates nothing the text does not.

## Round 2 - Challenger (Claude)

CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 2, CLOSING AND PREMISE LIST

### CHALLENGER: FINAL ROUND

The defender's last move is that the text never writes "no man," and a text that gives no mechanism has not excluded one. That is a standard no doctrine could meet, and the defender does not apply it to his own thesis. No verse says "Joseph begat Jesus." The defender's thesis is assembled from "seed," "father," and "house," and the chain from those words to Joseph's own body is an inference. The text assigns the conception a source, and the source is not Joseph.

#### 1. The defender's own words fix the sequence

[L1] "Before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:18). "Knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25).

[L2] prin ē synelthein autous means "before they came together." heurethē en gastri echousa means "she was found having in womb." ek pneumatos hagiou means "out of the Holy Spirit." The same verb gennēthen (that which is begotten) in Matthew 1:20 is the root of the genealogy's egennēsen (begat).

[L4] The defender's Round 2 grants "a bounded period of non-union ending at the birth." That grant carries the chain:
- (a) The child is in the womb before union (Matthew 1:18, L1; prin ē synelthein, L2).
- (b) There is no union through the birth (Matthew 1:25, L1; the defender's own words).
- (c) The text names the source of that conceived thing as ek pneumatos hagiou (Matthew 1:20, L1, L2).

A biological father for a child conceived before any union and without union afterward is a mechanism the text nowhere supplies. The defender says he "narrates nothing the text does not," yet he supplies exactly that.

#### 2. Matthew 1:16 and the ek of John 1:13

[L2] The defender grants that ex hēs (out of whom) is "grammatical agreement with Mary." That concedes the clause names only the woman as the one out of whom the birth came. Every earlier line names the man as subject of egennēsen (begat). Joseph is here called ton andra Marias (the husband of Mary), not a begetter.

[L4] The chain is the pattern of Matthew 1:2-15 (L1), plus the feminine relative (L2), plus the defender's concession (L2). The writer withholds "begat" at the one place the defender needs it. A departure from thirty-nine repetitions is not silence.

[L3] The defender says ek theou (out of God) in John 1:13 proves ek Spirit is never about origin of a body. John 1:13 itself states the exclusion: "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man." The text there writes the negation the defender demands, and ek theou stands opposite the human male will. For believers the exclusion is of a figurative birth, since they also have human fathers (John 3:6, "that which is born of the flesh is flesh"). In Matthew 1:20 the thing "conceived in her" is gennēthen (begotten) in the womb. A verb of womb-conception cannot be flattened into covenant language by pointing to a spiritual rebirth.

#### 3. Luke 1:34-35: the how is given

[L1] "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34). "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee... therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35).

[L2] epei andra ou ginōskō means "since I know not a man." epei (since) is causal. dio kai means "wherefore also." to gennōmenon hagion means "the holy thing being begotten."

[L4] The defender reads the clause as a present state ("I do not now know a man"). Grant it. The chain:
- (a) The angel has just said "thou shalt conceive" (Luke 1:31, L1).
- (b) Mary names her lack of union as the obstacle (epei, L2).
- (c) The angel does not answer "after the wedding." He answers with the Holy Ghost's coming and the Highest's overshadowing (Luke 1:35, L1).
- (d) He grounds the child's title in that coming with dio kai (L2).

If Joseph's seed were the means, the answer would be beside the question. The defender says eperchomai (come upon) and episkiazō (overshadow) "never denote begetting" elsewhere. That is an argument from silence, and the verbs are not on trial. The question is what the angel's answer accounts for. It accounts for the conception without the man.

#### 4. The seed rows and the line

I withdraw one point. [L1] Luke 1:27 reads "a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David," and the phrase attaches by proximity to Joseph. I do not rest descent through Mary on that verse.

[L2] Romans 1:3 has genomenou ek spermatos Dauid, "coming to be out of seed of David," with ginomai (to come to be), not a verb of begetting by a man. Galatians 4:4 has genomenon ek gynaikos, "coming to be out of a woman," the same verb with the woman as source. The defender grants that osphys (loins) spans generations (Hebrews 7:9-10). So the seed rows say David's descendant, and not "Joseph's seed."

[L3] Genesis 3:15 speaks of "her seed," and zera' (seed) is there attributed to the woman. The defender calls Galatians 4:4 "the mortality idiom." But the sentence is "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman," and it names God as sender and the woman as the human source, with no man.

[L4] Joseph's genealogy cannot be a plain begetting chain for two reasons.
- (a) Matthew 1:16 gives Joseph's father as Jacob, while Luke 3:23 says "the son of Heli" (L1). Two fathers for one man means at least one line is reckoned, not begotten, as Deuteronomy 25:5-6 shows (L3).
- (b) Matthew 1:11-12 runs Joseph's line through Jechonias, and Jeremiah 22:30 says "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David" (L1; zera', seed, L2).

Luke 1:32 promises this child "the throne of his father David." The defender's chain from the house of David to Joseph's body is therefore an inference that Matthew and Jeremiah press against.

#### 5. Naming, "supposed," and Isaiah 7:14

[L1] "Thou shalt call his name JESUS" is said to Mary as well (Luke 1:31). "His name was called JESUS, which was so named of the angel before he was conceived in the womb" (Luke 2:21). Pharaoh's daughter named Moses (Exodus 2:10), and Hagar was told to name Ishmael (Genesis 16:11). The defender concedes naming is "not exclusive in the record." So "the father names him" proves legal act and not begetting.

[L2] hōs enomizeto means "as was supposed" or "as was reckoned." The defender cites Acts 16:13 ("was wont"). The same verb root nomizō (to think, reckon) is at Luke 2:44: "supposing him to have been in the company" (nomisantes). That supposition was wrong. The narrator writes it at Luke 3:23 immediately after the voice from heaven, "Thou art my beloved Son" (Luke 3:22).

## C1 — Matthew 1:16 breaks the begetting pattern, and the break is the narration
