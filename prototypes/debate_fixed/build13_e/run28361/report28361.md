## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession".
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

### OPENING — THE DEFENDER

The question is a "who" question, and the record answers a who question with names. It answers it in four voices, from the body of the promised seed, and from the naming act the text gives to Joseph. What it never does is narrate the how — and I will not narrate it either. I affirm the who; I state no mechanism.

---

#### 1. THE FATHER IS NAMED — FOUR VOICES [L1]

The mother speaks: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48) [L1] — she says this to the child at twelve years old, and the whole gospel has stood beside "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33) [L1] and "his parents went to Jerusalem" (Luke 2:41) [L1].

The town speaks: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22) [L1]; "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" (John 6:42) [L1].

A disciple speaks: "We have found him, of whom Moses in the law, and the prophets, did write, Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45) [L1].

The angel speaks: "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" (Matthew 1:20) [L1] — and Joseph is "of the house of David" (Luke 1:27, *ek oikou David*, out of the house of David) [L2], "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4) [L1]. Not one of these four voices is ever corrected [L3]. And the naming of the child is Joseph's act: "and he called his name JESUS" (Matthew 1:25) [L1] — the father's act throughout, as with Zacharias, "how he would have him called" (Luke 1:62-63) [L3].

#### 2. THE PROMISED SEED IS A FLESH SEED OUT OF A FATHER'S BODY [L1-L2-L3]

"I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12) [L1]. "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne" (Psalm 132:11) [L1]. "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3) [L1] — *kata sarka*, according to the flesh, the marker of bodily descent [L2]. "Of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30) [L3] — the same loins-word of the priesthood reckoning (Hebrews 7:5, 10, *osphys*, the loins) [L2]. "It is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14) [L1]. Seed that proceeds out of bowels and loins, evident and sprung out, is not produced by a legal act; a man's body is in the sentence [L4: resting on 2 Samuel 7:12 and Romans 1:3, both stating out-of-the-body flesh descent, with Acts 2:30 as the cross-reference].

#### 3. "SON OF GOD" NAMES A COVENANT, NOT A BEGETTER [L1-L3]

God says of Solomon, with David alive and named as his father: "Solomon thy son, he shall build my house... for I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father" (1 Chronicles 28:6) [L1]; "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14) [L1]. The same title is applied to Jesus at the resurrection: "God hath fulfilled the same unto us their children, in that he hath raised up Jesus again; as it is also written... Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee" (Acts 13:33) [L3]; "declared to be the Son of God with power... by the resurrection from the dead" (Romans 1:4) [L1]. Luke 1:32 gives the throne "of his father David" and the title together, before the question in verse 34 [L1]; verse 35 says he "shall be CALLED the Son of God" [L1]. A title Solomon bore with a living earthly father, and a title declared at the resurrection, names no begetter of flesh [L4: resting on 1 Chronicles 28:6 [L1] and Acts 13:33 [L3]].

#### 4. THE ANGEL'S VERBS ARE PRESENCE AND POWER [L1-L2-L3]

"The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee" (Luke 1:35) [L1]. "Come upon" (*eperchomai*, to come upon) is the same verb as "ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you" (Acts 1:8) [L2-L3] — the apostles at Pentecost, of whom the text says "ye shall be witnesses," not that they were begotten. "Overshadow" (*episkiazō*, to overshadow) is the same word as the transfiguration cloud: "a cloud, and overshadowed them" (Luke 9:34) [L2-L3], and of Peter's healing shadow (Acts 5:15) [L3]. Neither verb denotes begetting at any row where the text uses them [L4: resting on the Luke 1:35 [L1] verb pair and the Acts 1:8 / Luke 9:34 [L3] parallels].

#### 5. THE QUIET ROWS STATE A STATE AND A MORTALITY, NOT "NO MAN" [L1-L2-L3]

"Of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matthew 1:16) [L1] — one passive verb (*egennēthē*, was born) after thirty-nine active "begat" rows in the same list [L2]: non-narration, not a denial. "Before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:18) [L1] and "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25) [L1] — held as written: a state at the time, with the naming act given to Joseph in the same verse. "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34) [L1] — present tense of a betrothed woman ("espoused to a man whose name was Joseph," Luke 1:27) [L1-L2], and the same Mary calls Joseph "thy father" twelve years later [L3]. "Made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4) [L1] is the mortality idiom: "Man that is born of a woman is of few days" (Job 14:1) [L1]; "he which is born of a woman" (Job 15:14) [L1]; "Among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" (Matthew 11:11) [L1] — and John's father was named and struck dumb for disbelief (Luke 1:13) [L3]. None of these rows says "no man"; the record simply does not write the how, and neither will I [L4: resting on Matthew 1:18, 1:25 [L1] as stating rows and the Job / Matthew 11:11 [L3] mortality parallel].

The one row the other side will press, "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20) [L1], is origin-language: "of" (*ek*, out of) with God or the Spirit is covenant origin throughout — "born of God" (John 1:13) [L1] — and the biological marker is absent there and present at Romans 1:3 ("according to the flesh") [L1-L2]. The text requires the flesh seed and names the man; it never says no man was involved [L4: resting on John 1:13 [L1] and Romans 1:3's *kata sarka* [L2]].

#### 6. THE RECKONING IS BY THE FATHER'S HOUSE [L1-L3]

"By the house of their fathers... every man by their polls" and "declared their pedigrees... by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18) [L1]. Where the register ran by a wife's house, it was struck: those who "could not shew their father's house" were excluded (Ezra 2:59) [L1]; priests named after the wife's house were "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61-62) [L1]. Joseph is enrolled accordingly: "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4) [L1]. [L4: resting on Numbers 1:2 [L1] and the Ezra 2:59, 61-62 [L3] inverse rows.]

I hold both fences: I will not say Mary bore a child without a man, and I will not construct a mechanism the text does not write.
