## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession".
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Defender (MiMo)

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

**OPENING — THE DEFENDER**

The question is narrow and the text answers it: did Christ have a biological father? Yes. A man is named in the text, the seed is spoken of as flesh and bone out of a father's body, and the reckoning runs by the father's house. I affirm the *who* the text affirms — Joseph, of the house of David. I do not narrate the *how*: the text does not narrate it, and this defense will not supply what the pages leave quiet.

---

**THE SEED IS BODILY [L1]**

> 2 Samuel 7:12 — "And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, **which shall proceed out of thy bowels**, and I will establish his kingdom."

The promised son is a seed proceeding out of the father's own body.

> Psalm 132:11 — "The LORD hath sworn in truth unto David; he will not turn from it; **Of the fruit of thy body** will I set upon thy throne."

The throne is set on fruit of the body. The same words run elsewhere: "kings shall come out of thy loins" (Genesis 35:11 [L3]); "thy son that shall come forth out of thy loins" (1 Kings 8:19 [L3]).

**THAT SEED IS CHRIST, ACCORDING TO THE FLESH [L1 + L2]**

> Romans 1:3 — "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was **made of the seed of David according to the flesh**."

> Acts 2:30 — "...of the fruit of his loins, **according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne."

> Hebrews 7:14 — "For it is **evident** that our Lord **sprang out of Juda**."

[L2] The words: *sperma* (seed, G4690), *osphys* (loins, G3751), *kata sarka* (according to flesh). *Sperma* is the offspring word; *osphys* is the anatomical loins-word of Hebrews 7:5, 10; *kata sarka* is the text's own marker of bodily descent. Paul adds it deliberately where flesh is meant. A seed that is "evident" and "sprang out of" a tribe is a bodily descent, and a bodily descent names a father.

**THE FATHER IS NAMED [L1]**

> Luke 2:48 — "and his mother said unto him, Son, why hast thou thus dealt with us? behold, **thy father and I** have sought thee sorrowing."

Mary's own mouth.

> John 6:42 — "And they said, **Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?**"

The town's mouth.

> Matthew 1:20 — "behold, the angel of the Lord appeared unto him in a dream, saying, **Joseph, thou son of David**, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife..."

The angel's mouth — and Joseph is addressed as the son of David, the line the seed is sworn to. [L3] A disciple says the same: "Jesus of Nazareth... the son of Joseph" (John 1:45); the town again: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22). Joseph is called "of the house of David" (Luke 1:27) and "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4).

**THE RECKONING RUNS BY THE FATHER'S HOUSE [L1 + L2]**

> Luke 3:23 — "And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being **(as was supposed) the son of Joseph**, which was the son of Heli."

> Numbers 1:2 — "Take ye the sum of all the congregation of the children of Israel, **after their families, by the house of their fathers**... every male by their polls."

[L2] *Nomizō* (suppose, be wont, G3543) is the word of custom — the same form as "where prayer was wont to be made" (Acts 16:13). "As was supposed" names the public custom, and the line then runs through Joseph. The enumeration is by the house of the fathers; the register gives the father's line (Matthew 1:16, "Jacob begat **Joseph** the husband of Mary").

**THE ONE ROW THAT NAMES THE SPIRIT [L1 + L2 + L3]**

> Matthew 1:20 — "...for that which is conceived in her **is of the Holy Ghost**."

[L2] *Ek* (of) the Holy Ghost, with **no** *kata sarka* (according to flesh) — the biological marker Romans 1:3 carries is absent here. [L3] Everywhere *ek* + God marks origin of a spiritual kind, not begetting of flesh: "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 5:1), "is of God" (1 John 4:7). This row marks the child's covenant origin. It does not name a begetter, and the text never calls the Spirit a father.

**THE THREE QUIET ROWS SAY NOTHING AGAINST THIS [L1]**

Matthew 1:16 — "of whom was born Jesus" — is non-narration: it does not say "no man." Luke 1:34 — "seeing I know not a man" — is a present tense ("I do not now"), of a woman already espoused to Joseph (Luke 1:27), and Luke has Mary call Joseph "thy father" later (Luke 2:48). Matthew 1:25 — "and knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" — holds a stated order and says nothing after. Silence is not testimony; a verse that does not narrate the begetting does not deny the begetter the rest of the text names.

**FLOOR STATEMENT** [L1] The promised seed proceeds out of the father's body; that seed is Christ "according to the flesh"; the father is named in four voices — Mary, the town, a disciple, the angel; the reckoning is by the house of the fathers and the register gives Joseph. A named father, a bodily seed, an evident sprouting out of Judah: a man was involved, and the text names him.

**DECLARED CHAIN**

D1. The Davidic promise names a bodily seed proceeding out of the father's own body — [L1] 2 Samuel 7:12 ("out of thy bowels"), Psalm 132:11 ("fruit of thy body"); [L3] Genesis 35:11, 1 Kings 8:19 (out of the loins).
D2. The Christ is that seed "according to the flesh," sprung out of Judah — [L1] Romans 1:3, Acts 2:30, Hebrews 7:14 ("evident... sprang out of Juda"); [L2] sperma (seed), osphys (loins), kata sarka (according to flesh).
D3. A father is named in the text in four voices, Joseph, son of David — [L1] Luke 2:48 ("thy father and I"), John 6:42 ("the son of Joseph"), Matthew 1:20 ("Joseph, thou son of David"); [L3] Luke 4:22, John 1:45, Luke 1:27, Luke 2:4.
D4. The genealogical reckoning runs by the house of the fathers and gives Joseph's line — [L1] Luke 3:23 ("as was supposed the son of Joseph"), Numbers 1:2 ("by the house of their fathers"), Matthew 1:16 ("Joseph the husband of Mary"); [L2] nomizō (suppose, be wont).
D5. The "of the Holy Ghost" row marks covenant origin and names no begetter — [L1] Matthew 1:20; [L2] ek (of) without kata sarka (according to flesh), the flesh-marker Romans 1:3 carries; [L3] John 1:13, 1 John 4:7 (ek + God = born of God, spiritual origin).
D6. Christ had a biological father: Joseph, the named Davidic father, and Christ was made of the seed of David according to the flesh — [L1] Romans 1:3, Luke 2:48, Matthew 1:16, Luke 3:23; [L2] kata sarka (according to flesh), sperma (seed); [L3] 2 Samuel 7:12, Acts 2:30, Hebrews 7:14.

**2. MATTHEW BREAKS THE BEGETTING CHAIN AT JOSEPH [L1 + L2]**

[L4] But the formula "X begat Y" runs unbroken through the chapter (Matthew 1:2–16, L1), and the grammar of 1:16 (feminine relative, L2) breaks it at exactly this point. ## Round 2 - Defender (MiMo)

DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

**ROUND 2 — THE DEFENDER ANSWERS**

The challenger's case runs on two moves, and the text breaks both: **silence read as negation** (a verse that does not narrate Joseph's begetting is read as saying he did not beget), and **source-language read as paternity-denial** (*ek*, "of" the woman, read as excluding the father). Both fail against the pages themselves.

---

**A. THE SEED ROWS ARE NOT ANSWERED [L1]**

> Romans 1:3 — "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**."

> Acts 2:30 — "...of the fruit of his loins, **according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne."

> Hebrews 7:14 — "For it is **evident** that our Lord sprang out of Juda."

A seed that is "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" is bodily descent, and bodily descent passes through a body. The challenger grants the Davidic flesh but routes it through Mary. That route has no verse.

---

**B. "FLESH THROUGH MARY" IS AN IMPORT THE TEXT NEVER STATES [L1 + L2]**

The challenger says: "The text never says Mary is outside David's house. Her flesh can carry David's seed." [L4] That is an inference whose stating verse does not exist, and the record's own practice says otherwise:

> Luke 1:27 — "...to a virgin espoused to a man **whose name was Joseph, of the house of David**..."

The house is named for **Joseph**. Luke 2:4-5: "And **Joseph** also went up... with Mary his espoused wife... **because he was of the house and lineage of David**" — the census reason given is Joseph's line.

> Numbers 1:2 — "by the **house of their fathers**... every male by their polls." Numbers 1:18 — "...declared their pedigrees **after their families, by the house of their fathers**." [L3]

Where a register gave the **wife's** house, it was struck: priests "called after the name" of the wife's house were "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61-62; the whole row, Ezra 2:59, "could not shew their father's house"). No female name enters any register of Christ (Luke 3:23 gives Heli, no mother named; Matthew 1:16 names no lineage of hers). An [L4] resting on the absence of a verse has one layer of footing — silence — and floats. The challenger's own C1 depends on it.

---

**C. MATTHEW 1:16 AND *EK*: SOURCE AND BEGETTER COEXIST IN THE SAME VERSES [L1 + L2]**

The challenger proves too much. In the very rows he cites for *ek* (out of) as bodily source, **the father is the named begetter and the mother is the named source at once**:

> Matthew 1:3 — "And **Judas begat** Phares and Zara **of Thamar**."

> Matthew 1:5 — "And **Salmon begat** Booz **of Rachab**... and **Booz begat** Obed **of Ruth**."

"Of Thamar" is *ek*, and Judah still begets. *Ek* names the source of the child's coming-out; it never excludes the begetter in these rows. So "that which is conceived in her is **of** (*ek*) the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20) names source — and by Matthew's own usage it says nothing against a named father.

And what of Matthew 1:16? "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." The passive is non-narration: it does not say "Joseph begat him not." What the text **does** give Joseph in the same chapter is the **father's act**:

> Matthew 1:21 — "...**thou shalt call his name JESUS**." Matthew 1:25 — "...and **called his name JESUS**."

The naming of the child belongs to the father in this canon — to Zacharias the father it is said, "thou shalt call his name John... and they made signs to his father, how he would have him called" (Luke 1:13, 62-63). [L3] The text gives that act to Joseph and addresses him as "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20). [L1]

---

**D. MATTHEW 1:18-19: STATE-ROWS AND INTENT, NOT A PATERNITY VERDICT [L1 + L2]**

> Matthew 1:18 — "...before they came together (*prin ē synelthein autous*) she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."

> Matthew 1:19 — "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man (*dikaios ōn*), and not willing to make her a publick example (*mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai*), was minded to put her away privily (*eboulēthē lathra apolysai autēn*)."

The challenger says Joseph's distress "shows he knew the child was not his." Matthew 1:19 contains **no verb of knowing** — it records a just man's intent and two grounds (not to expose her; to put her away quietly). The row states an order in 1:18; it does not issue the verdict "no man," and I will not narrate what it does not. Matthew names neither the stoning statute (Deuteronomy 22:23-24) nor the divorce statute (Deuteronomy 24:1); he records the intent. [L3] Verse 1:25's "till" states the order and says nothing after — held as written.

---

**E. "HOW SHALL THIS BE" — THE ANSWER'S VERBS ARE PRESENCE AND POWER [L1 + L2 + L3]**

> Luke 1:35 — "The Holy Ghost shall **come upon** (*eperchomai*) thee, and the power of the Highest shall **overshadow** (*episkiazō*) thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be **called** (*klēthēsetai*) the Son of God."

[ L2] *Eperchomai* (come upon) is the Spirit's coming on the apostles: "after that the Holy Ghost is **come upon** you" (Acts 1:8). *Episkiazō* (overshadow) is the transfiguration cloud (Luke 9:34) and Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15). Neither word denotes begetting anywhere it appears. The result clause is a **title** — "shall be called the Son of God" — the covenant title Solomon bore while David his father lived: "Solomon **thy son**... I have chosen him to be **my son**, and I will be his father" (1 Chronicles 28:6). [L3] A title that coexists with a named human father cannot decide who that father is.

**The supporting rows the challenger leans on are the mortality idiom and the mother-inclusive idiom:**

> Galatians 4:4 — "made of a woman." It is said of every man: "Man that is **born of a woman** is of few days" (Job 14:1; so 15:14; 25:4) — and of John the Baptist: "Among them that are **born of women** there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" (Matthew 11:11), whose father is named — Zacharias (Luke 1:13; 57-63). [L3] "Of a woman" never excludes a father.

> Genesis 3:15 — "her seed." The same idiom is used of Hagar ("I will multiply **thy seed** exceedingly," Genesis 16:10 — Hagar's seed is Abram's) and Rebekah ("**thy seed** may possess the gate," Genesis 24:60 — Rebekah's seed is Isaac's). [L3] "Her seed" names the mother's part; it never denies the father.

> Hebrews 10:5 — "a body hast thou prepared me." God prepares every body: "thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother's womb. I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made" (Psalm 139:13-14). [L3] Prepared by God and fathered by a man are not opposites; "no father's seed is mentioned" is an argument from silence.

---

**F. THE FATHER-VOICES: NARRATION GOVERNS — AND THE NARRATOR SAYS IT TOO [L1 + L2]**

The challenger's own rule: "A speaker's assertion is not narration, and the narration governs." Apply it:

> Luke 2:27 — "And when **the parents** brought in the child Jesus." Luke 2:41 — "Now **his parents** went to Jerusalem." Luke 2:33 — "And **Joseph and his mother** marvelled."

These are Luke in **narrator voice**. [L1] Luke 3:23's "as was supposed" is *nomizō*, the word of usage — "where prayer **was wont** to be made" is the same verb form (Acts 16:13, *enomizeto*). [L2] The word carries no verdict; each row's context decides — and this context is a genealogy running through Joseph ("which was the son of Heli") after a census taken of Joseph "because he was of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4-5). [L1]

> Luke 2:48-49 — "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing... wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?"

"My Father" is the covenant title running **alongside** the named father, exactly as in 1 Chronicles 28:6 — Solomon "thy son" to David and "my son" to God, in one verse. Jesus' reply names his mission; it does not withdraw Joseph's fatherhood, and Luke keeps calling Joseph the parent afterwards (2:27, 2:41). [L3] The angel's "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20) and the naming act (1:21, 1:25) remain unanswered.

---

**G. JEREMIAH 22:30 — THE CURSE THE NEW TESTAMENT NEVER CITES [L1 + L3]**

> Jeremiah 22:30 — "Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper **in his days**: for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more **in Judah**."

The New Testament never cites this against Christ's throne — "Jechonias" appears twice (Matthew 1:11, 12), and the curse is never applied to him. The signet plucked from Jeconiah (Jeremiah 22:24) is set on another: "I will take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant, the son of Shealtiel... and will make thee as a signet" (Haggai 2:23) — governor of Judah (Haggai 1:1), never king. [L3] Jeremiah's own rule bounds a pronounced sentence: "If that nation... turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil" (Jeremiah 18:7-8). [L3] And the challenger's own escape route is sprung: he sends the flesh through Mary and cites "Luke 3:31... through Nathan" — but that line runs "Zorobabel, which was the son of Salathiel, which was the son of **Neri**" (Luke 3:27) — the same two names as Matthew's, with a different father given and no narration of why. The knot is left open; it is not Joseph's alone.

**THE FIVE CHALLENGES**

1. *"Quote the verse that says Joseph begat Jesus."* That demand is refused: the framework never requires one verse to state a chain — precept upon precept, line upon line (Isaiah 28:10). What I exhibit: the promised seed proceeding out of the father's body (2 Samuel 7:12; Psalm 132:11) [L1]; that seed "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3; Acts 2:30; Hebrews 7:14) [L1]; a father named in four voices (Luke 2:48; John 6:42; Matthew 1:20; Luke 2:27, 41) [L1]; reckoning "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18) with Joseph's line given (Luke 2:4-5; Luke 3:23; Matthew 1:16) [L1]; and the father's naming act given to Joseph (Matthew 1:21, 25; cf. DEFENDER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

**ROUND 2 — THE DEFENDER ANSWERS**

The challenger's case runs on two moves, and the text breaks both: **silence read as negation** (a verse that does not narrate Joseph's begetting is read as saying he did not beget), and **source-language read as paternity-denial** (*ek*, "of" the woman, read as excluding the father). Both fail against the pages themselves.

**A. THE SEED ROWS ARE NOT ANSWERED [L1]**

> Romans 1:3 — "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**."

> Acts 2:30 — "...of the fruit of his loins, **according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne."

> Hebrews 7:14 — "For it is **evident** that our Lord sprang out of Juda."

A seed that is "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" is bodily descent, and bodily descent passes through a body. The challenger grants the Davidic flesh but routes it through Mary. That route has no verse.

---

**B. "FLESH THROUGH MARY" IS AN IMPORT THE TEXT NEVER STATES [L1 + L2]**

The challenger says: "The text never says Mary is outside David's house. Her flesh can carry David's seed." [L4] That is an inference whose stating verse does not exist, and the record's own practice says otherwise:

> Luke 1:27 — "...to a virgin espoused to a man **whose name was Joseph, of the house of David**..."

The house is named for **Joseph**. Luke 2:4-5: "And **Joseph** also went up... with Mary his espoused wife... **because he was of the house and lineage of David**" — the census reason given is Joseph's line.

> Numbers 1:2 — "by the **house of their fathers**... every male by their polls." Numbers 1:18 — "...declared their pedigrees **after their families, by the house of their fathers**." [L3]

Where a register gave the **wife's** house, it was struck: priests "called after the name" of the wife's house were "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61-62; the whole row, Ezra 2:59, "could not shew their father's house"). No female name enters any register of Christ (Luke 3:23 gives Heli, no mother named; Matthew 1:16 names no lineage of hers). An [L4] resting on the absence of a verse has one layer of footing — silence — and floats. The challenger's own C1 depends on it.

---

**C. MATTHEW 1:16 AND *EK*: SOURCE AND BEGETTER COEXIST IN THE SAME VERSES [L1 + L2]**

The challenger proves too much. In the very rows he cites for *ek* (out of) as bodily source, **the father is the named begetter and the mother is the named source at once**:

> Matthew 1:3 — "And **Judas begat** Phares and Zara **of Thamar**."

> Matthew 1:5 — "And **Salmon begat** Booz **of Rachab**... and **Booz begat** Obed **of Ruth**."

"Of Thamar" is *ek*, and Judah still begets. *Ek* names the source of the child's coming-out; it never excludes the begetter in these rows. So "that which is conceived in her is **of** (*ek*) the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20) names source — and by Matthew's own usage it says nothing against a named father.

And what of Matthew 1:16? "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." The passive is non-narration: it does not say "Joseph begat him not." What the text **does** give Joseph in the same chapter is the **father's act**:

> Matthew 1:21 — "...**thou shalt call his name JESUS**." Matthew 1:25 — "...and **called his name JESUS**."

The naming of the child belongs to the father in this canon — to Zacharias the father it is said, "thou shalt call his name John... and they made signs to his father, how he would have him called" (Luke 1:13, 62-63). [L3] The text gives that act to Joseph and addresses him as "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20). [L1]

**D. MATTHEW 1:18-19: STATE-ROWS AND INTENT, NOT A PATERNITY VERDICT [L1 + L2]**

> Matthew 1:18 — "...before they came together (*prin ē synelthein autous*) she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."

> Matthew 1:19 — "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man (*dikaios ōn*), and not willing to make her a publick example (*mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai*), was minded to put her away privily (*eboulēthē lathra apolysai autēn*)."

The challenger says Joseph's distress "shows he knew the child was not his." Matthew 1:19 contains **no verb of knowing** — it records a just man's intent and two grounds (not to expose her; to put her away quietly). The row states an order in 1:18; it does not issue the verdict "no man," and I will not narrate what it does not. Matthew names neither the stoning statute (Deuteronomy 22:23-24) nor the divorce statute (Deuteronomy 24:1); he records the intent. [L3] Verse 1:25's "till" states the order and says nothing after — held as written.

**E. "HOW SHALL THIS BE" — THE ANSWER'S VERBS ARE PRESENCE AND POWER [L1 + L2 + L3]**

> Luke 1:35 — "The Holy Ghost shall **come upon** (*eperchomai*) thee, and the power of the Highest shall **overshadow** (*episkiazō*) thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be **called** (*klēthēsetai*) the Son of God."

[ L2] *Eperchomai* (come upon) is the Spirit's coming on the apostles: "after that the Holy Ghost is **come upon** you" (Acts 1:8). *Episkiazō* (overshadow) is the transfiguration cloud (Luke 9:34) and Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15). Neither word denotes begetting anywhere it appears. The result clause is a **title** — "shall be called the Son of God" — the covenant title Solomon bore while David his father lived: "Solomon **thy son**... I have chosen him to be **my son**, and I will be his father" (1 Chronicles 28:6). [L3] A title that coexists with a named human father cannot decide who that father is.

**The supporting rows the challenger leans on are the mortality idiom and the mother-inclusive idiom:**

> Galatians 4:4 — "made of a woman." It is said of every man: "Man that is **born of a woman** is of few days" (Job 14:1; so 15:14; 25:4) — and of John the Baptist: "Among them that are **born of women** there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" (Matthew 11:11), whose father is named — Zacharias (Luke 1:13; 57-63). [L3] "Of a woman" never excludes a father.

> Genesis 3:15 — "her seed." The same idiom is used of Hagar ("I will multiply **thy seed** exceedingly," Genesis 16:10 — Hagar's seed is Abram's) and Rebekah ("**thy seed** may possess the gate," Genesis 24:60 — Rebekah's seed is Isaac's). [L3] "Her seed" names the mother's part; it never denies the father.

> Hebrews 10:5 — "a body hast thou prepared me." God prepares every body: "thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother's womb. I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made" (Psalm 139:13-14). [L3] Prepared by God and fathered by a man are not opposites; "no father's seed is mentioned" is an argument from silence.

[L2] The word carries no verdict; each row's context decides — and this context is a genealogy running through Joseph ("which was the son of Heli") after a census taken of Joseph "because he was of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4-5). [L1]

[L3] The angel's "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20) and the naming act (1:21, 1:25) remain unanswered.

**G. JEREMIAH 22:30 — THE CURSE THE NEW TESTAMENT NEVER CITES [L1 + L3]**
