"How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34). Andra ou ginosko is present active indicative: her state when she spoke to the angel, not a vow (a permanent negation would take ou me + aorist subjunctive). ESPOUSED IS MARRIED: before they came together the text already says "Joseph her husband" (Matt 1:19) and "Mary thy wife" (Matt 1:20); the law calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's wife" (Deut 22:24); to part, Joseph must "put her away" (Matt 1:19). "Before they came together" (Matt 1:18, synerchomai) is her coming into his house: "took unto him his wife" (Matt 1:24). "Knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matt 1:25) covers the pregnancy, not the time before. THE DAVIDIC SEED: "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Sam 7:12); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30); "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Rom 1:3). The one man the text names is Joseph, of the house of David, her husband, and seed out of a man's loins comes by his knowing his wife: "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" (Gen 4:1). [L4, footed on these rows] Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal. "Of the Holy Ghost" (Matt 1:18, 20) is the covenant-origin marker, not the begetter (DG-18). WHERE "VIRGIN" CAME FROM: Isaiah 7:14's Hebrew is ha-almah, young woman (H5959); bethulah (H1330), the word for a virgin, is not used. Matthew 1:23's "a virgin" is parthenos, the Septuagint's Greek rendering of almah, and the KJV's "virgin" in Isaiah 7:14 follows that Greek word, not the Hebrew. Luke 2:48: Mary calls Joseph "thy father."
