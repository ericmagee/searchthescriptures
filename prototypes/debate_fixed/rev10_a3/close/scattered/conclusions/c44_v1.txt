Matthew 15:24, apolōlota (G622) = perfect participle: a completed state of lostness, "utterly lost." L2 LIMIT: the participle is lexically flexible; the same word serves the individually lost sheep, coin and son (Luke 15:4-7) and "that which was lost" (Luke 19:10), so the corporate-scatter reading is supplied by CONTEXT (Ezekiel 34 shepherd/scattered backdrop; Jeremiah 50:6 "lost sheep" language), not by the lexeme alone. The clause "completely without identity / people with no knowledge of who they are" is L3/L4 inference (Ezekiel 37:13; Hosea 2), not lexically entailed: hold it labeled, never argue it as the word's meaning. The Shepherd knows them (John 10:14) even when they do not know themselves (Ezekiel 37:13), an L3 arrangement. The verse names the people: "the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matthew 15:24; DG-20). Who and where they are today is the Shepherd's to name (DG-20).
