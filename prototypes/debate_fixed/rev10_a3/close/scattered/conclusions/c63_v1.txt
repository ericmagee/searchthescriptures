SEED COUNTED BY PROMISE: the Romans 9:6 cut (pairs with DG-60). 'Counted by promise, not flesh' (Rom 9:8; DG-12/DG-60) is neither a neutral void nor an open door; it is the two-edged cut of Rom 9:6, 'they are not all Israel, which are of Israel', closing two cracks the word 'promise' otherwise opens. (1) ADDRESS: 'of Israel' presupposes an Israel the promise was sworn to: the covenants and promises are Israel's possession (Rom 9:4 hōn 'whose ARE', #60; epangelia sworn to the fathers, #48); so 'promise' can never be read as address-less faith that erases the recipient. (2) NARROWS WITHIN: 'not all Israel which are of Israel': natural descent alone does not qualify (Rom 9:7-8, the children of the promise, not of the flesh, are counted for the seed); so 'promise' can never be read as flesh-automatic. NET: the promise is neither guaranteed by flesh nor dissolved into adoption; it is the covenant line, addressed to Abraham's seed, discriminated within by promise. This closes the two vocabulary misreadings. The address names the recipients: the promises pertain to "Israelites" (Rom 9:4), and the Gentiles Paul writes to are scattered Israel, Lo-ammi called "my people" again (Rom 9:25-26; DG-1; Conclusion #2). Who and where they are today is the Shepherd's to name (DG-20). Use Rom 9:6 to deny both the flesh-automatic and the adoption-erasing readings of 'promise.' --- APPENDED 2026-07-27, ruled ("append ... keep it all together tight"). THE OFFENSIVE FORM: WHY BLOOD IS THE WRONG INSTRUMENT, NOT MERELY THE WRONG ANSWER. All floor-verified from kjv_verses and original_words this session. ---
The body above is defensive: it closes two misreadings of "promise." This states the positive case that makes the blood-frame refusable at the door.

(1) THE VERB IS AN ACCOUNTING VERB. Rom 9:8 "are counted for the seed" is logizetai (G3049, V-PNI-3S): reckoned, imputed, put to account; the same verb used of faith counted for righteousness. Seed-status is CREDITED, not inherited. And sperma there is G4690 N-ASN, accusative SINGULAR: plural tekna (G5043, N-NPN) are reckoned into ONE seed; the same singular Paul insists on at Gal 3:16 ("not seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ") and carries to Gal 3:29 (sperma G4690 N-NSN singular, klēronomoi G2818 plural: plural "ye" are a singular seed, and the qualifier is "if ye be Christ's").

(2) PAUL NEVER REACHES FOR BLOOD. In the one passage explicitly about descent, his contrast pair is sarx (G4561) against epangelia (G1860). He negates FLESH. haima does not occur in the argument. He had the word and did not use it.

(3) CANON-WIDE, BLOOD IS NOT A DESCENT CATEGORY. Direct query of original_words: only FIVE NT verses carry haima (G129) in the same verse as genos (G1085), sperma (G4690) or ethnos (G1484): Acts 17:26, Acts 18:6, Acts 21:25, Gal 1:16, Rev 5:9. Not one makes blood a category of descent: 18:6 is guilt, 21:25 is diet, Gal 1:16 is human counsel ("conferred not with flesh and blood"), Rev 5:9 is Christ's redeeming blood with the nations as what men are redeemed OUT OF. The single positive contact, Acts 17:26, makes all nations ONE blood, the opposite of a dividing census (#20).

(4) THE TEXT'S OWN EXAMPLES HOLD BLOOD CONSTANT. Rom 9:7 divides Isaac from Ishmael (Gen 21:12, "in Isaac shall thy seed be called"), both Abraham's own offspring. Rom 9:10-11 tightens it: Rebecca "conceived by one, even by our father Isaac," twins, "being not yet born, neither having done any good or evil." Blood does not vary where the division falls; promise and calling do. A variable held fixed cannot be the criterion.

CONSEQUENCE (pairs with DG-60): an opponent arguing blood is arguing an instrument the text does not use to divide. Refusing that frame is fidelity, not evasion.
BLOOD REMAINS ADMISSIBLE IN ITS TWO STATED SENSES, unchanged: universal one-blood (Gen 9:19 / Acts 17:26; #20) and the go'el kinsman-redeemer (#50). "Away from blood" must NOT harden into "never blood": Acts 17:26 is a witness FOR this position, and surrendering the word surrenders the verse.
This addition sharpens the instrument; the recipients stand as named above (DG-1; DG-20).
