Romans 9:4 vs Ephesians 2:12: "pertaineth" vs "strangers from the covenants of promise" is not a contradiction; it is possession vs condition, and the grammar of both sides is on rows. Rom 9:4: hoitines eisin Israēlitai, hōn (G3739, relative genitive, "whose ARE") the adoption, glory, covenants, law, service, promises: ontological possession stated in Paul's own voice. Eph 2:12: apēllotriōmenoi (G526, perfect passive participle), "having been ALIENATED": a completed estrangement FROM a standing one was previously in; the participle presupposes the prior relation, it does not deny it (cf. Col 1:21, alienated by wicked works; #2, status/condition not origin). The possession is irrevocable while the possessors stand estranged: Rom 11:29 ametamelēta (G278), the gifts and calling of God are without repentance, and Rom 11:24's natural branches are graffed into idia elaia (G2398+G1636), THEIR OWN olive tree; the wild branch is graffed eis kallielaion (G2565), "a good olive tree" (Conclusion 65, citation caution). Paul's own interpretive key sits in the same chapter: Rom 9:25-26 quoting Hosea: not-my-people called children of the living God. LIMIT: this resolves the alleged L1-L3 closure ("strangers proves never-Israel"). The Ephesians 2 recipients are addressed as those who were "in time past Gentiles in the flesh" (Eph 2:11): Gentile is their former condition, not their origin (DG-1; Conclusion #2). Who and where the scattered are today is the Shepherd's to name (DG-20). SCOPE-NOTE (2026-07-14, W1 row da8d08e2, ratified): Eph 4:18 uses the same participle, same form (apēllotriōmenoi, G526 V-RPP-NPM, row-verified) for alienation "from the life of God": a non-ethnic object. Same-author usage therefore makes the separative force object-neutral: the participle presupposes A prior standing whose nature is supplied by its object, not by the participle. In Ephesians 2:12 the object is named, "aliens from the commonwealth of Israel": by this note's own rule, the prior standing is the commonwealth of Israel (L3); the core also rests on hōn (Rom 9:4) and ametamelēta (Rom 11:29).
