John 11:51-52: the redundancy objection fails on the pericope's own structure, and the gathering is the mission's stated shape. "That nation" (tou ethnous) is defined by the immediate context as the perishable local Judean polity ("the Romans shall come and take away both our place and nation," 11:48; "that the whole nation perish not," 11:50), while "the children of God that were scattered abroad" (dieskorpismena, perfect passive, an already-scattered state) are a second body by the verse's own not-only/but-also. Caiaphas' one sentence (11:50) carries both target/field nouns, hyper tou laou ... holon to ethnos, and its "perish" verb is the very verb of Matt 15:24's apolōlota. The gather-verb of 11:52 (synagagē) is the verb of Christ's own antithesis "he that gathereth not with me scattereth" (Matt 12:30), and its "into one" (eis hen, G1520) is the numeral of John 10:16's one flock, one shepherd (mia, heis); the numeral stands in 355 rows and is said of "he that planteth and he that watereth" (1 Cor 3:8; Paul and Apollos at 3:6; Sight 1579), so the shared word carries no identity by itself. The scatter-word attaches to sheep at the smitten-shepherd sites (Matt 26:31; Mark 14:27; Zech 13:7). The prophecy Paul quotes names the scattered: "the number of the children of Israel ... in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living God" (Hosea 1:10); "there shall they be called the children of the living God" (Romans 9:26). Joining that naming to John 11:52 is an L3 arrangement (DG-1; Conclusion #2). Who and where they are today is the Shepherd's to name (DG-20).
