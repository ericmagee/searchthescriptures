TWO VERBS, NOT TWO PEOPLES: the GRAFT restores the broken-off house to its own named tree; the JOINING is the stranger's verb; each verb stands in its own rows, and the text never makes the two verbs a line between two peoples (DG-3). The olive tree carries God's own naming, "The LORD called thy name, A green olive tree... and the branches of it are broken" (Jer 11:16), owner named as both houses (Jer 11:17), and the wild branches are grafted "among them" (Rom 11:17), "contrary to nature into a good olive tree" (Rom 11:24; "their own olive tree" in the same verse is said of the natural branches): Lo-ammi Israel returning to its own covenant (Hos 1:10; Deut 32:21 quoted at Rom 10:19; ruled Conclusion #65). The stranger's entrance runs on a different verb: "the sons of the stranger, that JOIN themselves to the LORD... taketh hold of my covenant" (Isa 56:6), "the strangers shall be JOINED with them, and they shall cleave to the house of Jacob" (Isa 14:1), circumcised into the passover as one born in the land, one law (Ex 12:48-49), inheriting among the tribes as born in the country (Ezek 47:22-23: ger, not nokri, per Conclusion #28). The graft (egkentrizō G1461) is written of the wild olive (Romans 11:17, 19, 24) and of the natural branches graffed in again (11:23, 24); the record identifies the wild branches as Lo-ammi Israel called back, by the jealousy chain (Hosea 1:9-10; Romans 9:24-26; 10:19; #65), into the named tree (Jeremiah 11:16-17). The son of the stranger (ben ha-nekar H5236, Isaiah 56:3, 6) and the ger (H1616, Isaiah 14:1) enter by joining (lavah H3867). Obadiah 1:12 "the day of thy brother in the day that he became a stranger", said of "the children of Judah", is neker H5235 (a calamity; Job 31:3) in the Hebrew, a different word from nekar H5236. THE STRANGER IS NOT SHOWN TO BE AN OUTSIDER (DG-3): the joining stranger is not named by origin, and the text puts the stranger-words on Israel itself ("ye were strangers in the land of Egypt," Exodus 22:21; "an alien unto my mother's children," Psalm 69:8; "his seed should sojourn in a strange land," Acts 7:6; the patriarchs "strangers and pilgrims," Hebrews 11:13). Two verbs do not make two peoples: do not set the joining stranger against the grafted branch (DG-3). The text's category is seed, counted by promise (Rom 9:8). Who and where they are today is the Shepherd's to name (DG-20).
