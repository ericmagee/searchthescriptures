THE TEXT NAMES WHO. Deuteronomy 28 is spoken to Israel: "These are the words of the covenant, which the LORD commanded Moses to make with the children of Israel" (Deuteronomy 29:1); "the LORD shall scatter thee among all people, from the one end of the earth even unto the other" (Deuteronomy 28:64). Christ names the same people: "I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matthew 15:24). Hosea names them in the scatter: Lo-ammi, "not my people" (Hosea 1:9), sown "in the earth" and called "my people" again (Hosea 2:23). For biblical history this is settled: scattered Israel are the Gentiles and strangers of the text, Lo-ammi (Conclusion #2). State it; do not hold it back.

THE MARKERS. Deuteronomy 28 names what comes upon the scattered: scattered among all people (v.64); brought into Egypt again with ships, sold for bondmen and bondwomen, and no man shall buy (v.68); and the curses "upon thee for a sign and for a wonder, and upon thy seed for ever" (v.46). The letting-go of their name is held at L3 (Deuteronomy 32:26; Jeremiah 17:4; Isaiah 63:16, 19; Ezekiel 20:32).

TODAY. Who and where the scattered are today, by present-day people-group, is the Shepherd's to name: "he calleth his own sheep by name" (John 10:3); "My sheep hear my voice, and I know them" (John 10:27). The study names the biblical people and the markers; it does not name a present-day people-group. Flag and stop at phrases that do so by implication: "historically speaking, that describes ...", "the ones who were taken by ships would be ...", "if you look at what people group fits ...", "that convergence strongly suggests ..." followed by a present-day people.

BOTH DIRECTIONS. Substituting another present-day people without L1/L2 warrant is the same error. A present-day identification is legitimate only on an explicit L1 statement naming that people as the subject of the markers, or on the Shepherd's own naming (John 10:3).

DRIFT BY PARALYSIS. Refusing to state what the text does name, that the scattered of the text are Israel, is drift in the other direction, and it is the reflex this guard most needs to stop.
