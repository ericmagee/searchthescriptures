ONE CHAIN, THE DAVIDIC SEED. (1) The promise requires seed out of a man's body: "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Sam 7:12); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30); "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Rom 1:3). (2) The canon's stated means of begetting is the husband knowing his wife: "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" (Gen 4:1). (3) The one man the text names is Joseph, her husband from the espousal: "Joseph her husband" (Matt 1:19); "Mary thy wife" (Matt 1:20); the law calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's wife" (Deut 22:24); Mary herself says "thy father and I" (Luke 2:48). (4) Therefore [L4, footed on these rows] Joseph knew Mary his wife, and the child is of his loins. No row says "no man." THE TIMING ROWS, READ INSIDE THE CHAIN: "Before they came together, she was found with child" (Matt 1:18) dates the finding. The verb (synerchomai) can name married union ("come together again," 1 Cor 7:5); Matthew sets what came together by what follows: "took unto him his wife" (Matt 1:24), her coming into his house. "Knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matt 1:25) covers the pregnancy after the taking. "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34): andra ou ginosko is present active indicative, her state when the angel spoke, not a vow. THE ANGEL'S "FOR": "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matt 1:20). The "for" (gar) grounds the command to take her: the child is the seed God claims as his own, "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Sam 7:14), given "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32). "Of the Holy Ghost" is the covenant-origin marker, not the begetter (DG-18). Joseph's intent to put her away (Matt 1:19) states intent and two grounds, just and not willing to make her a public example; no row states the ground of the intent. WHERE "VIRGIN" CAME FROM: Isaiah 7:14's Hebrew is ha-almah, young woman (H5959); bethulah (H1330), the word for a virgin, is not used. Matthew 1:23's "a virgin" is parthenos, the Septuagint's Greek rendering of almah, and the KJV's "virgin" in Isaiah 7:14 follows that Greek word, not the Hebrew.
