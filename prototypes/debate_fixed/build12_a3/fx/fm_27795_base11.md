## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** All four judgments (the three panel judges and the single judge) apply these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession".
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

**OPENING — DEFENDER**

The question is whether Christ had a biological father. **I. THE SEED IS FLESH-SEED, DAVID'S OWN — [L1] + [L2] + [L3]**

[L1] "And Jacob begat **Joseph** the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matthew 1:16).

[L1] "The LORD telleth thee that he will make thee an house. And when thy days be fulfilled... I will set up thy seed after thee, **which shall proceed out of thy bowels**, and I will establish his kingdom" (2 Samuel 7:11-12).

[L1] "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, **which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh**" (Romans 1:3).

[L1] "Therefore being a prophet... he, **speaking of the resurrection of Christ**... spake thus, That **of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30-31).

[L1] "For it is **evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda**" (Hebrews 7:14).

[L2] The words are anatomical and flesh-marked: sperma (G4690, "seed") paired with **kata sarka ("according to the flesh")** at Romans 1:3; osphys (G3751, "loins") at Acts 2:30 is the same loins-word used of Levi in Hebrews 7:5, 10 ("Levi... out of the loins of Abraham"). [L3] "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne" (Psalm 132:11).

A seed that proceeds out of bowels and loins, **according to the flesh**, evident to sight (Hebrews 7:14), is the seed of a man. [L4, on the two footings above: the phrase "according to the flesh" would be empty if no flesh-line of a father stood behind it; the record's own words place the origin in David's body.]

**II. THE FATHER IS NAMED, IN FOUR REGISTERS — [L1] + [L3]**

[L1] Mary's own mouth: "thy **father** and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48).

[L1] The narrator: "And **Joseph and his mother** marvelled at those things which were spoken of him" (Luke 2:33); "his parents went to Jerusalem" (2:41).

[L1] The town: "Is not this the carpenter's son? is not his mother called Mary?" (Matthew 13:55); "Is not this Jesus, **the son of Joseph**, whose **father and mother we know**?" (John 6:42).

[L1] The angel: "Joseph, **thou son of David**, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" (Matthew 1:20) — and Joseph names the child: "**thou shalt call his name JESUS**" (1:21), done at 1:25. [L3] "Jesus of Nazareth, **the son of Joseph**" (John 1:45); "Is not this **Joseph's son**?" (Luke 4:22); Joseph "was of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4), and the throne is "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32).

Four independent registers — the mother, the narrator, the town, the angel — name one man as father. That is not a title; it is the WHO. [L4, on these footings: a man named "father" by the mother herself, and called "son of Joseph" by those who know both parents, is the biological father the question asks about.]

---

**III. THE SPIRIT-ROWS SPEAK COVENANT ORIGIN AND EMPOWERING PRESENCE, NOT BEGETTING — [L1] + [L2] + [L3]**

[L1] "that which is conceived in her is **of (ek) the Holy Ghost**" (Matthew 1:20); "The Holy Ghost **shall come upon** thee, and the power of the Highest **shall overshadow** thee" (Luke 1:35).

[L2] ek (G1537, "of") with God or Spirit is the covenant-origin idiom throughout: "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 3:9; 5:1), "is of God" (1 John 4:7) — spiritual origin, never biological paternity. When Paul means flesh he marks it: "made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**" (Romans 1:3, kata sarka, "according to the flesh"). Matthew 1:20 carries **no kata sarka** — the flesh-qualifier is absent.

[L2] The two verbs of Luke 1:35 are empowerment and presence words at every occurrence: eperchomai (G1904, "come upon") is the Pentecost word — "after that the Holy Ghost is **come upon** you, and ye shall be witnesses" (Acts 1:8; the power of Luke 24:49); episkiazō (G1982, "overshadow") is the transfiguration cloud (Luke 9:34 "a cloud overshadowed them") and Peter's healing shadow (Acts 5:15). [L3] 1 Samuel 16:13 "the Spirit of the LORD came upon David" — anointing.

These are covenant-origin and anointing words. The record does not make the Spirit a begetter of flesh. [L4, on the L2 verb-catalogue and the absent kata sarka marker above: the words the angel used are the words of anointing and divine presence, and the biological marker is missing where a paternity claim would require it.]

#### 1. Matthew 1:16 breaks the genealogy's pattern, and the defender concedes it

[L1] "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom** was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matthew 1:16).

[L2] The relative pronoun hēs (G3739, "of whom") is **feminine singular**. It can only refer to Mary. The text does not say "Joseph begat Jesus." Joseph is called andra (G435, "husband"), not father. [L1] Luke supplies the explicit marker: "Jesus... being (**as was supposed**) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" (Luke 3:23). [L2] enomizeto (G3543, "was thought, was reckoned by custom") is the verb of opinion. The narrator himself classifies the sonship as supposition.

The text puts the conception **inside** the stretch where Joseph did not know her. What he does afterward is irrelevant to the question.

[L4, on the 1:18 chronology ("before they came together... found with child"), the 1:25 bound ("knew her not till"), and the gennēthen/ek pneumatos language]: the conception falls between two verses that both exclude Joseph's union with her, and the text assigns it to the Holy Ghost.
