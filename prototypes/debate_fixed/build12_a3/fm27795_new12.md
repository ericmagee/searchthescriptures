## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession".
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Challenger (Claude)

CHALLENGER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

### CHALLENGE: Christ had no biological father. Joseph was his legal father, and the conception was of the Holy Ghost.

The standard reading holds that the child was conceived in Mary while she was a virgin, by the Holy Ghost, with no human father's seed. I take each of the defender's six sections in turn.

---

#### 1. Matthew 1:16 breaks the genealogy's pattern, and the defender concedes it

[L1] "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom** was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matthew 1:16).

[L2] The relative pronoun hēs (G3739, "of whom") is **feminine singular**. It can only refer to Mary. The text does not say "Joseph begat Jesus." Joseph is called andra (G435, "husband"), not father. The defender admits that egennēthē (G1080, "was born") is "the one passive after thirty-nine active egennēsen ('begat')." He calls this "non-narration." But a writer who has said "begat" thirty-nine times and then changes verb, voice and pronoun gender at the one point where the chain would naturally continue has marked a break.

[L1] Luke supplies the explicit marker: "Jesus... being (**as was supposed**) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" (Luke 3:23). [L2] enomizeto (G3543, "was thought, was reckoned by custom") is the verb of opinion. The narrator himself classifies the sonship as supposition.

[L4, on Matthew 1:16 (feminine hēs, passive after thirty-nine actives) and Luke 3:23 (enomizeto, "was supposed")]: two inspired genealogies decline to say Joseph begat him. One of them calls the Joseph-sonship a supposition. That is a positive limit on Joseph's fatherhood, not mere silence.

#### 2. Jeremiah 22:30 bars a biological Joseph-line from the throne

[L1] Matthew traces Joseph through "Jechonias" (Matthew 1:11-12, 16). [L1] Of Coniah the LORD said: "Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper in his days: for **no man of his seed** shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah" (Jeremiah 22:30). [L2] zera (H2233, "seed") is the same word as in 2 Samuel 7:12.

[L4, on Matthew 1:12, 16 (Joseph descends from Jechonias) and Jeremiah 22:30 (no seed of Coniah sits on David's throne)]: if the child were Joseph's biological seed, he would fall under that bar, yet Luke 1:32-33 promises him "the throne of his father David." The defender's reading collides with Jeremiah. The standard reading does not. Joseph supplies legal standing, and the seed comes by another route.

#### 3. Matthew 1:18-25 locates the conception before Joseph and without him

[L1] "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost**" (1:18). [L1] "Joseph... was minded to put her away privily" (1:19). [L1] "**for** that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (1:20). [L1] "And knew her not **till** she had brought forth her firstborn son" (1:25).

- [L2] The verb in 1:20 is gennēthen (G1080, "that which is begotten, conceived"). It is the same verb as the "begat" of the genealogy, and here the agent is ek pneumatos hagiou ("out of the Holy Ghost"), the object is "in her" (en autē), and the thing is a conceived child, not an anointing.
- [L1] Joseph's problem in 1:19 is that she was found with child. If the child were his, there would be no dilemma. [L4, on 1:19 and the explanatory gar ("for") of 1:20]: the angel's "for" supplies the reason Joseph may take her. That reason works only if the pregnancy was not from a man.
- [L2] heōs hou (G2193 + G3739, "until which") in 1:25 bounds the entire period of conception and gestation: Joseph "knew her not." [L3] ginōskō (G1097, "know") carries the marital sense, as yada (H3045, "know") does in "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1). The text puts the conception **inside** the stretch where Joseph did not know her. What he does afterward is irrelevant to the question.

[L4, on the 1:18 chronology ("before they came together... found with child"), the 1:25 bound ("knew her not till"), and the gennēthen/ek pneumatos language]: the conception falls between two verses that both exclude Joseph's union with her, and the text assigns it to the Holy Ghost. The defender's section IV calls these "sequencing statements." But the sequence is the point. It puts the conception on the pre-union side of the line.

#### 4. Luke 1:34-35 answers the how, and ties the title to it

[L1] "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34). The angel does not say "Joseph shall come to thee." [L1] He says: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: **therefore also** that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (1:35).

- [L2] dio kai (G1352 + G2532, "wherefore also") makes the manner of conception the **cause** of the title. The defender's section V says the title "never answers the biological one." Luke 1:35 attaches the title to the conception.
- [L2] She is betrothed (1:27), so a question about "how" would be idle if Joseph were the expected means. The question presumes the conception is to be without a man, and the angel confirms it. - [L2] The defender catalogues episkiazō (G1982, "overshadow") as presence-and-power. I accept that. But the effect stated in the verse is "that holy thing which shall be born of thee." [L3] Hebrews 10:5 says: "a body hast thou **prepared** me" (katērtisō, G2675, "thou hast fitted, prepared"). The body is God's preparation.

[L1] Romans 1:3 reads "of the seed of David according to the flesh," and verse 4 follows with "according to the spirit of holiness." [L4, on Romans 1:3-4]: Paul sets two spheres side by side, flesh-descent from David and Spirit-sonship. He is not describing a single paternity.

[L1] Levi was "in the loins of Abraham" (Hebrews 7:10) across several generations. [L1] David is called Jesus' "father" (Luke 1:32) across about a thousand years. A conception by an ordinary husband would be no sign. [L2] Matthew 1:23 renders the Hebrew almah (H5959, "young woman") by parthenos (G3933, "virgin"). Luke 1:27 uses the same word of Mary.

[L4, on Galatians 4:4 (woman only), Genesis 3:15 (the woman's seed), Isaiah 7:14 with Matthew 1:23 (sign; virgin)]: the Davidic flesh-line can come through Mary. Section I proves David's seed, not Joseph's.

[L1] Mordecai "took [Esther] for his own daughter" (Esther 2:7).

[L4, on Luke 2:48-49 (father/Father), Luke 3:23 (supposed), Exodus 2:10 and Deuteronomy 25:6 (legal naming and adoption)]: Joseph is father in the legal and household sense. The text sets that sense beside a limit ("supposed"), and it has the child himself answer "my Father."

[L1] Solomon's "I will be his father" (2 Samuel 7:14) was spoken of a son with a living human father. But Luke 1:35 says "**therefore** also... the Son of God" and ties the title to the conception. [L2] anastēsas (G450, "having raised up") in 13:33 is not the verb phrase of verse 34. [L4, on Luke 1:35 (dio kai) and Acts 13:33-34 (v.34 adds "from the dead")]: the defender's section V rests on one verse he reads as resurrection, while Luke 1:35 answers the origin question directly.

#### 8. The defender's own parallel

[L1] The defender cites John 1:13: "born, **not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man**, but of God." [L2] There ek (G1537, "out of") with God is set in **antithesis** to ek thelēmatos andros ("out of the will of a man"). #### What I concede

[L1] Joseph is called "husband" (Matthew 1:16), "father" in the household sense (Luke 2:48), "son of David" (Matthew 1:20), and he names the child (1:25). All of that stands as **legal and household fatherhood**.

#### Challenge chain

- **C1.** Matthew 1:16: hēs is feminine ("of whom"), Joseph is "husband" not begetter. Luke 3:23: "as was supposed" (enomizeto). The genealogies decline to say Joseph begat him.
