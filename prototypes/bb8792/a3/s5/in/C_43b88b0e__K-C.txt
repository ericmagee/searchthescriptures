## A. THE KEYSTONE (D6): the throne-seed and the man's loins

**A1. The seed texts cannot run through Joseph's body.**
[L1] Jeremiah 22:30: "Write ye this man childless… for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." [L2] *zera'* (seed) is the word the text uses, and *'ariri* (childless, stripped) is the sentence on Coniah. [L1] Matthew 1:11-12 places Jechonias in Joseph's line, and Matthew 1:16 has "Jacob begat Joseph." [L4] If Joseph begat Jesus, then Jesus is of Jechonias's seed (Matt 1:12, 16), and Jeremiah 22:30 bars that seed from the throne. Matthew gives Joseph's line but withholds "begat" at its last link (Matt 1:16). The text itself therefore keeps the throne-right and the barred seed apart. This rests on [L1] Jer 22:30 + [L1] Matt 1:11-16 + [L2] *zera'* and the verb of verse 16.

**A2. In the Davidic promise the Father is named, and he is God.**
[L1] 2 Samuel 7:12 says "thy seed… out of thy bowels." Verse 14, the same promise, says "I will be his father, and he shall be my son." [L3] Hebrews 1:5 quotes 2 Samuel 7:14 of the one "better than the angels": "I will be to him a Father, and he shall be to me a Son." [L1] Luke 1:32, 35 joins "the throne of his father David" to "the Son of the Highest" and "the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee." [L4] The father assigned in the throne-promise itself is God (2 Sam 7:14; Heb 1:5), and Luke 1:35 places the Highest in the conception. The challenger cites 2 Samuel 7:12 but passes over verse 14 in the same promise.

**A3. "Loins" and "fruit of the loins" mark descent across generations, not the immediate begetter.**
[L1] Hebrews 7:10: Levi "was yet in the loins of his father [Abraham]," four generations before. [L3] Genesis 35:11: "kings shall come out of thy loins." Genesis 46:26 speaks of "all the souls that came with Jacob into Egypt, which came out of his loins." [L2] *osphys* (loins) in Acts 2:30 and Hebrews 7:5, 10 therefore reckons David's descendants. It does not require that no woman stands between David and the child. [L1] Hebrews 7:14, "sprang out of Juda" (*anatetalken*, has risen up), names a tribe and says nothing of the immediate begetter.

**A4. "According to the flesh" is satisfied by the woman.**
[L1] Romans 1:3 says "made of the seed of David according to the flesh." [L2] *genomenou* (coming to be, being made) is the same participle family as Galatians 4:4, *genomenon ek gynaikos* (coming to be out of a woman). [L3] Hebrews 2:14, 16: "he also himself likewise took part of the same [flesh and blood]… he took on him the seed of Abraham." [L2] *metesche* (partook) and *epilambanetai* (takes hold of) speak of a taking-on of flesh and say nothing of a human father's act. [L4] "Flesh" in Romans 1:3 means real human nature of David's line (Heb 2:14, 16), and it is said of the one "made," not of a begetter.

**A5. Mary's line is in the text.**
[L1] Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli." Matthew 1:16 gives Joseph's father as Jacob. [L2] Luke 3 has only *tou* (of the), with no *egennēsen* (he begat). [L1] Luke 3:31 names "Nathan, which was the son of David," a different son of David from Solomon (cf. Matt 1:6). [L4] Two lists, two fathers for Joseph, and Luke's parenthesis "(as was supposed)" placed at the very point where sonship to Joseph is named. These footings point to Luke giving the line through Mary, with Heli as her father, and Joseph entering as husband. I rest on [L1] Luke 3:23-31 + Matt 1:16 + [L2] the bare genitive. I do not rest the keystone on this alone, since A1–A4 and A2 stand independently.

**A6. The Aaronic kinswoman does not displace this.**
[L1] Luke 1:36, 5 makes Elisabeth of Aaron's daughters. [L1] Exodus 6:23: Aaron "took him Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab"; [L1] Numbers 1:7 makes Nahshon son of Amminadab "prince of the children of Judah." [L3] Priest and Judah intermarried at the root. Kinship in Luke 1:36 therefore does not cut Mary off from Judah.

## B. D5: "of a woman" and the seed texts

[L4] I do not rest the case on Galatians 4:4 alone, and the challenger's Job rows are noted and not conceded as isolated idiom. The chain matters here:

[L1] Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman." [L2] *exapesteilen* (sent forth) comes before *genomenon* (being made), so the one sent is a Son before he is made. [L1] John is "born of women" (Matt 11:11) and the text names his father (Luke 1:13, 57). Jesus is "conceived… of the Holy Ghost" (Matt 1:20), and the text names no father. [L4] The idiom does not carry the claim alone, and I do not need it to. The claim stands on Matthew 1:18-20 and Luke 1:35, and Galatians 4:4 is consistent with it. ## C. D1: the passive verb, the register, and the naming

**C1. The evangelist announces that the genealogy is not the account of the begetting.**
[L1] Matthew 1:1: "The book of the generation of Jesus Christ." [L1] Matthew 1:18: "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise." [L2] *geneseōs* (of generation) in verse 1 and *gennēsis* (birth, coming to be) in verse 18 are the same root. *Houtōs* (thus, in this manner) introduces a manner distinct from the begetting chain just given. [L4] The chain ends at verse 16 with a woman as the antecedent. Verse 18 then says how the birth came about, and gives the Holy Ghost (v. 18, 20). So the passive at verse 16 is part of an announced shift of manner. It is not a stray silence. [L4] A formula repeated through the generations and altered at its last member is a datum.

**C2. The canon reckons through a woman at the roots.**
The challenger says the canon's own rule runs against the wife's house.
[L1] Ezra 2:61 itself reports a man who "took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai… and was called after their name." [L1] 1 Chronicles 2:34-35: Sheshan "gave his daughter to Jarha his servant to wife; and she bare him Attai," and the line is carried on. [L1] 1 Chronicles 2:21-22 reckons Jair through Hezron's daughter, and Numbers 32:41 says "Jair the son of Manasseh." [L1] Numbers 27:1-7 and 36:6-8 treat daughters as carrying a father's inheritance. [L4] The register has precedent for reckoning through the woman, and the challenger's "rule against" overstates it (Ezra 2:61; 1 Chr 2:21-22, 34-35; Num 27, 32:41).

**C3. The naming is not the father's act only.**
[L1] Luke 1:31 says to Mary, "thou shalt call his name JESUS." [L1] Matthew 1:21 says the same to Joseph. [L1] Genesis 29:32 has Leah calling Reuben's name, and Luke 1:60 has Elisabeth giving John's name. [L1] Isaiah 7:14 says "she shall call his name Immanuel." [L4] Naming is attested of the mother as well as the father, so it does not establish a begetting row.

## D. D2: "of the Holy Ghost" and the two verbs

**D1. *Ek* (out of) is read in its sentence.**
[L1] John 1:13 is about believers: "which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." [L4] That verse sets *ek theou* (out of God) against "the will of man." It does not make *ek* with God mean "no real origin." In Matthew 1:20 the sentence says what is "conceived in her" (*to gennēthen en autē*, that begotten in her), and verse 18 says *en gastri echousa* (having in womb). [L4] Believers' new birth is not a conception in a womb. Matthew 1:20 is. The challenger's covenant-origin reading of *ek* strips the term from its sentence. In Matthew 1:18-20, *ek* names the source of what is in the womb.

**D2. The "therefore."**
[L1] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: *therefore also* that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." [L2] *dio kai* (for which reason also) joins the title to the coming and the overshadowing. [L4] In 1 Chronicles 28:6, Solomon's title rests on *bachar* (to choose, "I have chosen him"), with no "therefore" tied to a conception. In Luke 1:35 the title follows from the event. The answer to "how shall this be" (Luke 1:34) is the event itself. [L4] Mary asked about a conception with no man, and the angel gave a cause that names no man. I rest this on [L1] Luke 1:27, 31, 34-35 + [L2] present-tense *ginōskō* (I know), which the challenger grants as her state at that time.

## E. D3: the sequence in Matthew 1 defeats the challenger's L4

The challenger's conclusion is: "the child of Mary, wife of Joseph… is of his loins within the espousal." Its own footings contradict it.

[L1] Matthew 1:18 separates two things: "espoused to Joseph" and "before they came together." [L2] *Mnēsteutheisēs* (having been betrothed) and *sunelthein* (to come together) are distinct, and the pregnancy was found before the second. [L1] Matthew 1:24 says "took unto him his wife," and verse 24 comes after the angel's word in verse 20. [L3] Genesis 4:1 gives the order *knew, then conceived*. [L1] Matthew 1:25 gives the opposite order: "knew her not *till* she had brought forth." [L4] The challenger's own Genesis 4:1 pattern is absent from Matthew 1. The text has the finding (v. 18), the angel's account (v. 20), the taking (v. 24), and the knowing only after the birth (v. 25). So "within the espousal" has no row of union before the finding. [L3] Deuteronomy 22:23-24 does call a betrothed damsel a neighbour's wife, but that same law treats the betrothal and the lying with her as separate acts. The title "wife" in Matthew 1:20 therefore does not supply a union.

**On the listed floating premises (3 and 4 in D3 of my case):**
[L1] Matthew 1:19: "Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." [L2] *apolusai* (to dismiss) and *paradeigmatisai* (to make a public example, expose) are the verbs.
