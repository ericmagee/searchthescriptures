# Round 2: re-seating the chain

Noted: the challenger's central weight is "according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3; Acts 2:30). Per DD-1, I answer by re-seating that phrase in its chain, not by judging it alone. Nothing below is conceded on this clock.

## A. "Seed of David according to the flesh" (the keystone, Link 6)

**1. The verb is "made," not "begat."**
- [L1] Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh."
- [L2] γενομένου (genomenou, "having come to be / made") is the same verb as γενόμενον (genomenon, "made") in Galatians 4:4, "made of a woman." It is not ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat"), the father's verb in Matthew 1:2-16.
- [L3] Paul's two "made" clauses, "made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4) and "made of the seed of David" (Romans 1:3), describe one event. If the flesh is "of a woman" and "of David's seed," the seed came to David's line through the woman.
- [L4] So Romans 1:3 does not state a father's begetting. It says the child came to be, in flesh, of David's line, and Galatians 4:4 names the woman as the human source. Footings: Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 [L1], genomenos against egennēsen [L2], the two "made" clauses [L3].

**2. "According to the flesh" classifies descent. It does not describe a mechanism.**
- [L1] Romans 9:3: "my kinsmen according to the flesh." Romans 9:5: "of whom as concerning the flesh Christ came." Romans 4:1: "Abraham our father, as pertaining to the flesh."
- [L2] κατὰ σάρκα (kata sarka, "according to flesh") sorts a person into a kindred, as with Paul's "kinsmen." In Romans 1:3-4 it is set against κατὰ πνεῦμα ἁγιωσύνης (kata pneuma hagiōsynēs, "according to the spirit of holiness"). Two columns are named, the flesh column and the Spirit column, and the child stands in both.
- [L4] The phrase puts the child in David's kindred. It does not say a man's seed formed him, and Luke 1:35 and Matthew 1:20 supply the formation. Footings: Romans 1:3-4; 9:3, 5 [L1], kata sarka against kata pneuma [L2].

**3. Descent is reckoned by the name, and the law separates the name from the begetter.**
- [L1] Deuteronomy 25:6: "the firstborn which she beareth shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead." Genesis 48:5: "Ephraim and Manasseh... are mine; as Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine."
- [L3] The challenger's Numbers 1:2, "by the house of their fathers," is a reckoning, a counting. These two texts show the reckoning can run apart from the begetter, which is how Joseph's house carries the child.
- [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 says of Coniah: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." Matthew 1:11-12 places Jechonias in Joseph's line.
- [L4] If Joseph begat the child, the child would be Jechonias's seed under that ban (Jeremiah 22:30, Matthew 1:11-12). The throne right (Luke 1:32) then comes by the legal reckoning of Deuteronomy 25:6 and Genesis 48:5, not by Joseph's seed.

**4. Loins and body language runs through women too.**
- [L1] Genesis 17:16: "she shall be a mother of nations; kings of people shall be of her." Genesis 25:23: "two nations are in thy womb."
- [L2] Hebrews 7:5, 10, "out of the loins of Abraham" and "yet in the loins of his father," states the ancestor's line, not who performed the begetting in a given birth.
- [L4] 2 Samuel 7:12, Psalm 132:11 and Acts 2:30 state that the promised king comes out of David's line. They do not state that Joseph is the begetter. They are also silent on Mary's line, which is why Luke 1:32, the angel's words to Mary, "the throne of his father David," matters: it is said to her before any union.

**5. Romans 1:4 "declared" is not "made."**
- [L2] ὁρισθέντος (horisthentos, "marked out / declared") names a public demonstration. The origin of the sonship is a different matter, and Luke 1:35 gives it: διὸ καί (dio kai, "for which reason also") ties "Son of God" to the Holy Ghost's coming.

**6. The five "naming" speakers.**
- [L1] Luke 3:23, ὡς ἐνομίζετο (hōs enomizeto, "as it was being reckoned"), is the same narrator who wrote "his parents" in Luke 2:41. Luke himself marks the household title as a reckoning.
- [L1] Luke 2:49-50: the child says "my Father's business," and "they understood not the saying." Two registers stand in the same exchange.
- [L1] John 6:42, "we know": John 7:27 has the crowd say "we know this man whence he is," and the child answers in 7:28-29, "ye both know me, and know whence I am: and I am not come of myself." A crowd's "we know" is answered in the same book.
- [L2] On Acts 16:13 versus Luke 2:44: agreed that ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "was reckoned by custom") takes its verdict from context. Luke 3:23 sits after Luke 1:27, "a virgin," and Luke 1:34-35. That context decides it.
- [L1] Matthew 1:21, 25 has Joseph "call his name JESUS." The naming is the legal act of acknowledgement.

## B. Link 2: "knew her not till"

- [L1] Matthew 1:25: "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." The challenger says "till" bounds the span to the pregnancy.
- [L2] ἐγίνωσκεν (eginōskon, "he was knowing," imperfect) with ἕως οὗ (heōs hou, "until which time") says that through the whole pregnancy Joseph was not knowing her.
- [L3] The challenger offers Genesis 4:1, "Adam knew Eve his wife: and she conceived," as the canon's means of begetting. Matthew 1:25 states the means was absent for the entire gestation.
- [L4] By the challenger's own span and standard, Joseph did not know Mary before the birth, so Joseph is not the begetter. The conclusion offered, "Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal," contradicts Matthew 1:25 as the challenger reads it. Footings: Matthew 1:25 [L1], eginōskon with heōs hou [L2], Genesis 4:1 [L3].

**Contrast with Solomon.**
- [L1] 2 Samuel 12:24: "David... went in unto her, and lay with her, and she bare a son... and he called his name Solomon." The covenant sonship in 2 Samuel 7:14 and 1 Chronicles 28:6 sits beside a narrated going-in.
- [L3] Where a covenant title and a begetting coincide, the text narrates the begetting. In Matthew 1:18-25 it narrates the opposite.

**The Greek of Matthew 1:20.**
- [L2] τὸ ἐν αὐτῇ γεννηθὲν (to en autē gennēthen, "that which has been begotten in her") uses the begetting root of the genealogy's ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat"), with ἐκ πνεύματός ἐστιν ἁγίου (ek pneumatos estin hagiou, "is out of holy spirit"). In the genealogy the begetter is the father. Here the source is the Holy Ghost.
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise." γένεσις (genesis, "origin / beginning") is the title word of 1:1, followed by οὕτως (houtōs, "in this manner").
- [L4] The evangelist announces that this birth has a different manner from the fifteen before it (1:1, 1:18) and then says what it is (1:20). Footings: Matthew 1:1, 18, 20 [L1], genesis, houtōs, gennēthen [L2].

**Joseph's conduct.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:19: Joseph, "being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." Deuteronomy 22:23-24 (the challenger's own law) sets the penalty for a betrothed damsel found with child by another.
- [L4] A just man does not put away the mother of his own child. The angel's answer is not "the child is yours." It is "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (1:20). Footings: Matthew 1:19-20 [L1], Deuteronomy 22:23-24 [L3].

**Luke 1:34-35.**
- [L1] Mary asks "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" and the answer is "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee."
- [L4] Whether the present tense is a state or a vow, it makes the question about a near-future conception. The answer names a different agent and does not say "when Joseph takes thee." The challenger's point that ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, "will overshadow") is not a begetting verb is granted for argument. The load is carried by γεννηθέν (gennēthen, "begotten"), ἐκ (ek, "out of") and διὸ καί (dio kai, "for which reason also").

## C. Link 4: Isaiah 7:14

Noted: the challenger is accurate that Genesis 24:16 uses בְּתוּלָה (bethulah, "virgin") and Genesis 24:43 uses עַלְמָה (almah, "young woman"). Not conceding here. Goes to review. The link rests on what follows.

- [L1] Genesis 24:16 calls Rebekah "a virgin, neither had any man known her," and 24:43 calls the same woman almah. Almah is therefore used of a woman who is a virgin.
- [L1] Isaiah 7:11: "ask it either in the depth, or in the height above." The offer was on a scale of deep or high, and Ahaz refused. Then "the Lord himself shall give you a sign" (7:14), and the "you" is plural, to the house of David (7:13).
- [L2] Isaiah 8:3 gives the prophetess's child the name מַהֵר שָׁלָל חָשׁ בַּז (Maher-shalal-hash-baz), not Immanuel. It is a different child with a different name. Isaiah 8:18's "signs" are children whose names are the sign.
- [L1] Isaiah 8:8: "thy land, O Immanuel." The land is addressed to Immanuel as its owner. Isaiah 9:6-7 follows, with the child on "the throne of David."
- [L1] Matthew 1:22-23: "all this was done, that it might be fulfilled." The challenger grants the application "stands as written." Matthew's ἵνα πληρωθῇ (hina plērōthē, "in order that it might be fulfilled") points to 1:18-21 ("all this").
- [L4] A near fulfilment in Isaiah's day and the fulfilment Matthew states do not conflict. The chain is Isaiah 7:11, 8:3, 8:8, 9:6-7 together with Matthew 1:22-23.

## D. Link 3: "of a woman" and "a body prepared"

- [L1] Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman." The sending precedes the being made, so the one sent is already the Son.
- [L2] On Job 14:1 and Matthew 11:11: the generic plural "born of women" states mortality. That is granted for argument. Galatians 4:4 carries γυναικός (gynaikos, "a woman") in the singular, and the verse names no father.
- [L1] Hebrews 2:14: "the children are partakers of flesh and blood; he also himself likewise took part of the same."
- [L2] κεκοινώνηκεν (kekoinōnēken, "have shared," perfect, an inherited state) is said of the children, and μετέσχεν (meteschen, "took part," aorist, a single act) of him. The text distinguishes the two.
- [L1] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me," said "when he cometh into the world," so before he comes. Hebrews 10:10 follows: "the offering of the body of Jesus Christ."
- [L4] The sacrifice context fixes what the body is for. It does not erase what Hebrews 10:5 says God did: God prepared it. Re-seated in the chain with Matthew 1:20 and Luke 1:35, "no room" is the consequence, not a free-standing claim. Footings: Galatians 4:4, Hebrews 2:14, 10:5 [L1], kekoinōnēken against meteschen and katērtisō [L2].

## E. Link 1

- [L1] Matthew 1:16 names a birth "of whom," ἐξ ἧς (ex hēs, "out of whom," feminine). Whatever the verse does not deny, it grants the begetting to no one.
- [L2] Active ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat") governs 1:2-15. The passive ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē, "was born") governs the last link.
- [L4] Matthew 1:16 alone does not exclude Joseph. Read with 1:18 ("the birth... on this wise"), 1:20 ("begotten in her... of the Holy Ghost") and 1:25 ("knew her not"), the change of verb is the first line of an explanation the evangelist then gives.

## Chain standing

1. Matthew 1:16, 18, 20, 25 together exclude Joseph as begetter. The challenger's "till" concedes the span.
2. Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30 state the child's descent from David. They use "made," and they classify by kindred. Descent by name, by legal reckoning and through Mary is stated elsewhere (Deuteronomy 25:6; Genesis 48:5; Jeremiah 22:30; Luke 1:32).
3. Isaiah 7:14, Matthew 1:22-23 and Luke 1:34-35 stand as one witness to the conception.
4. Joseph's title is household and legal usage, and Luke himself marks it at 3:23.

Conclusion: Christ had no biological father.

**LINK 6 [premise 2]: SUPPORTED, narrowed to the flesh-source named in the narrative.**

- [L3] Luke 1:42 "fruit of thy womb" and Acts 2:30 "fruit of his loins" use the same noun, καρπός (karpos, "fruit"). The chain is: the fruit is of her womb, the fruit is of David's loins, and the narrative names no other parent for it.
- Against Matthew 11:11 "born of women" with Luke 1:5, 13: that idiom leaves a father named in the narrative. Here the narrative names none: "seeing I know not a man" (Luke 1:34).
