# LINK ROUND

**LINK 5: NARROWED TO:** the limbs that the throne-seed's father is God and that "loins" is generational. The limb that Joseph's seed is barred by the curse is dropped.

Dropped limb: [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 says "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." The challenger pressed Jeremiah 18:7-8, "If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil," and Haggai 2:23, "I will take thee, O Zerubbabel... and will make thee as a signet: for I have chosen thee." Together with 1 Chronicles 3:17-18 and Matthew 1:12 ("Jechonias begat Salathiel"), these show the sentence is not shown to be unbroken. I do not rest the keystone on it.

Kept limb 1, the father of the throne-seed is God:
- [L1] Luke 1:32: "shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David."
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- [L3] 2 Samuel 7:14: "I will be his father, and he shall be my son."
- Chain: the throne-promise names God as father, and Luke applies it to this child with the angel's own explanation.
- On 1 Chronicles 28:6, "I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father": Solomon's row shows the title added to a human begetter. It does not show the title excludes one. Here Luke 1:34-35 and Matthew 1:20 supply the exclusion, which the Solomon row never does.

Kept limb 2, "loins" is generational:
- [L1] Hebrews 7:10: "he was yet in the loins of his father."
- [L1] Acts 2:30: "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh."
- [L1] 2 Samuel 7:12: "thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels."
- Chain: Levi was in Abraham's loins across generations (Hebrews 7:5, 10), and David's loins reach the child across many generations the same way. The text does not require Joseph as the immediate begetter. [L2] *sperma* (seed) in Romans 1:3 and 2 Timothy 2:8 is descent, not a single act.
- Psalm 132:11, "Of the fruit of thy body," and Hebrews 7:14, "our Lord sprang out of Juda," state the descent. They do not state who the immediate begetter was.

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:2-15 repeats "begat" ([L2] *egennēsen*, begat, active) fifteen times. Matthew 1:16 changes it: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus." The verb is [L2] *egennēthē* (was born, passive), and the relative is [L2] *ex hēs* (out of whom, feminine singular), which gives one parent.
- [L1] Matthew 1:18 announces the shift: "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise" ([L2] *houtōs*, thus, in this manner).
- Chain: the altered formula is followed at once by an announced manner of birth, and Matthew 1:20 gives the source ("that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost").
- The pressed verses do not touch the verb. Matthew 1:21 "thou shalt call his name JESUS" and 1:25 "he called his name JESUS" give Joseph the naming, not the begetting. 2 Samuel 7:12, Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30 give the descent. None of them says Joseph begat the child.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." [L2] *to en autē gennēthen* (that which is begotten in her) places the child in the womb, and *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of the Holy Spirit) names the source of that child. [L1] Matthew 1:18 repeats "found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- [L1] Luke 1:34-35 sets the angel's reply against Mary's question: "seeing I know not a man... The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee... that holy thing which shall be born of thee."
- Chain: a stated source for the child in the womb, in answer to a question about a man.
- The pressed verses do not carry covenant-origin into this sentence. John 1:13, "born of God," itself denies "blood... the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man" as the source. 1 John 3:9 is about believers' new birth, not a conception. Acts 1:8, "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you," speaks of power for witness. Luke 1:35, "shall be called the Son of God," follows "that holy thing which shall be born of thee," so the calling rests on the birth. Romans 1:3 names the flesh-line, which Mary supplies as the one "of whom" in Matthew 1:16.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED.**
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." [L2] *synelthein* (to come together) is the verb of 1 Corinthians 7:5, "come together again." The text itself puts the finding before the coming together.
- [L1] Luke 1:27, "a virgin espoused," and Luke 1:34, "I know not a man," give the same state at the annunciation.
- Chain: the challenger's own pressed word, *synerchomai*, is dated after the finding in Matthew 1:18 and fulfilled in 1:24, "took unto him his wife."
- Matthew 1:19, "Joseph her husband," and 1:20, "Mary thy wife," with Deuteronomy 22:24, "his neighbour's wife," give the espousal's legal status, not an act of union. Genesis 4:1, "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived," has the knowing before the conceiving, and Matthew 1:25, "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son," states no knowing before the birth.

**LINK 4: NARROWED TO:** the limb that [L1] Luke 3:23, "(as was supposed) the son of Joseph," is the narrator's own qualifier within a narrative that has already said Luke 1:34-35. The limb that [L2] *nomizō* (to suppose) alone marks a corrected belief is dropped.

**LINK 6: NARROWED TO:** the limb that Luke 3 permits a line through Heli as Mary's father. It is an inference and is not stated. The limb that it is stated, or that the keystone rests on it, is dropped.

- Chain: the canon has precedents for reckoning through the wife's father, so Luke 3:23 does not by itself exclude the reading.
