TOPIC: Did Christ have a biological father?

DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

# Opening: Jesus had no biological father

**Definition.** "Biological father" means a male parent whose seed begets the child. I defend the standard reading: the child Jesus had a mother, Mary, and no human male parent. Joseph was his reputed and legal father only. His conception was "of the Holy Ghost."

## 1. The genealogy breaks its own pattern at Matthew 1:16

- [L1] Matthew 1:2-15 runs "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob..." down to "Jacob begat Joseph." Verse 16 then reads: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
- [L2] The verb ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat") is active and is used for every father-to-son link in verses 2-15. In verse 16 the verb changes to the passive ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē, "was born"). The relative pronoun ἐξ ἧς (ex hēs, "out of whom") is feminine singular, so it can refer only to Mary, not to Joseph. Joseph is called ἀνήρ (anēr, "husband"), not the one who "begat."
- [L3] Compare Matthew 1:18-20 below. The evangelist's grammar anticipates his explanation: the line passes through Joseph's house legally, and the birth comes out of Mary alone.
- [L4] Because the one verb "begat" governs fifteen links and is deliberately dropped at the sixteenth, and because the pronoun is feminine (footings: Matthew 1:2-16 and the Greek grammar of ex hēs), the text withholds from Joseph the begetting it grants to every other father in the list.

## 2. The conception is "of the Holy Ghost," before any union

- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." Matthew 1:25: Joseph "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son."
- [L2] In 1:18, συνελθεῖν (synelthein, "to come together," here as man and wife) is placed before the pregnancy. ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (ek pneumatos hagiou, "out of spirit holy") gives the source of the conceiving. In 1:20, τὸ γεννηθέν (to gennēthen, "that which has been begotten/conceived") takes the same ἐκ (ek, "out of"). In 1:25, ἐγίνωσκεν (eginōsken, "he was knowing") is imperfect, "was not knowing her," up to the birth, and ἕως οὗ (heōs hou, "until which time") marks the span.
- [L3] Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" Mary's question uses ἄνδρα (andra, "man/husband") with γινώσκω (ginōskō, "I know," the same verb as Matthew 1:25). Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, "will overshadow") and διὸ καί (dio kai, "therefore also") make the divine act the stated reason for the child's sonship.
- [L4] Matthew 1:18-20, 25 and Luke 1:34-35 agree in three points: the time (before they came together), the source (ek pneumatos hagiou), and the question and answer (no man known, so the Holy Ghost). Together they exclude Joseph, or any man, as the begetter.

## 3. The child is "of a woman," and his body is "prepared"

- [L1] Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law." Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me." Genesis 3:15 speaks of "her seed," where seed is ordinarily reckoned through the man.
- [L2] In Galatians 4:4, γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός (genomenon ek gynaikos, "coming to be out of a woman") names only the woman as the human source. In Hebrews 10:5, κατηρτίσω (katērtisō, "you fitted/prepared") makes the body something God prepared. In Genesis 3:15, זַרְעָהּ (zar'āh, "her seed") is the feminine-possessive form of "seed."
- [L3] Paul's phrase in Galatians 4:4 matches Matthew 1:16 (ex hēs) and Luke 1:35 (born "of thee"). Three witnesses name the mother alone as the human source.
- [L4] Galatians 4:4 and Genesis 3:15 name a woman as the origin (L1, L2), and Hebrews 10:5 says God prepared the body (L1, L2). The text therefore gives no room for a human father's seed in the body of the child.

## 4. The sign of Isaiah 7:14 is fulfilled

- [L1] Isaiah 7:14: "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." Matthew 1:22-23: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled... Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son."
- [L2] Hebrew הָעַלְמָה (hā'almāh, "the young woman of marriageable age") with הָרָה (hārāh, "conceiving/with child") is the word of the sign. Matthew renders it with παρθένος (parthenos, "virgin"), and he applies it explicitly to the events of 1:18-21.
- [L3] Genesis 24:43 uses the same Hebrew word for Rebekah, whom Genesis 24:16 describes as "a virgin, neither had any man known her." The word is used elsewhere of an unmarried maiden. A "sign" (Isaiah 7:14, אוֹת, 'ôṯ, "sign") means something beyond ordinary generation.
- [L4] Isaiah 7:14 stands on its Hebrew word, its Genesis 24 usage, and Matthew's inspired application. The sign is a woman conceiving without a man, and Matthew declares it fulfilled in Mary.

## 5. Joseph is "supposed" father, not begetter

- [L1] Luke 3:23: Jesus "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph." John 6:42: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" Luke 2:48-49: Mary says "thy father and I have sought thee," and he answers, "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" Matthew 1:19-20: Joseph is "minded to put her away privily," and then the angel says "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife."
- [L2] In Luke 3:23, ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "it was being thought/reckoned by custom") marks a public opinion, not a fact. In John 6:42, the speakers are the crowd, whose knowing is only their claim. In Matthew 1:19, ἀπολῦσαι (apolysai, "to release/divorce") shows Joseph concluding the child was not his.
- [L3] Luke 2:49 corrects the "thy father" of verse 48 by naming another Father. Matthew 1:20 shows God correcting Joseph's own assumption.
- [L4] The text itself separates the opinion of the crowd from the fact, so every "father" passage naming Joseph rests on legal reckoning and public supposition (L1, L2). It cannot be pressed as a statement of begetting.

The conception was "of the Holy Ghost" before Mary and Joseph came together, and Mary knew no man; footing: [L1] Matthew 1:18, 20, 25 and Luke 1:34-35, [L2] ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (ek pneumatos hagiou, "out of holy spirit"), συνελθεῖν (synelthein, "to come together"), ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, "will overshadow"), [L3] Matthew and Luke agreeing on time, source, and Mary's question.
3. The human source of the child is the woman alone, and his body was prepared by God; footing: [L1] Galatians 4:4, Hebrews 10:5, [L2] γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός (genomenon ek gynaikos, "made out of a woman") and κατηρτίσω (katērtisō, "you prepared"), [L3] Genesis 3:15 "her seed."
4. Isaiah 7:14's sign of a virgin conceiving is applied by the text to Mary; footing: [L1] Isaiah 7:14, Matthew 1:22-23, [L2] הָעַלְמָה (hā'almāh, "the young woman") and παρθένος (parthenos, "virgin"), [L3] Genesis 24:16, 43.
5. Joseph's "fatherhood" is supposition and legal standing, not begetting; footing: [L1] Luke 3:23, John 6:42, Luke 2:48-49, Matthew 1:19-20, [L2] ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "was reckoned"), [L3] Luke 2:49 correcting Luke 2:48.
6. Answer: Christ had no biological father; he was conceived in Mary by the Holy Ghost, and Joseph was only his reputed and legal father.

# The Challenge: the seed is David's, and the father is named

# Round 2: re-seating the chain

Noted: the challenger's central weight is "according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3; Acts 2:30). Per DD-1, I answer by re-seating that phrase in its chain, not by judging it alone. Nothing below is conceded on this clock.

## A. "Seed of David according to the flesh" (the keystone, Link 6)

**1. The verb is "made," not "begat."**
- [L1] Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh."
- [L2] γενομένου (genomenou, "having come to be / made") is the same verb as γενόμενον (genomenon, "made") in Galatians 4:4, "made of a woman." It is not ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat"), the father's verb in Matthew 1:2-16.
- [L3] Paul's two "made" clauses, "made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4) and "made of the seed of David" (Romans 1:3), describe one event. If the flesh is "of a woman" and "of David's seed," the seed came to David's line through the woman.
- [L4] So Romans 1:3 does not state a father's begetting. It says the child came to be, in flesh, of David's line, and Galatians 4:4 names the woman as the human source. Footings: Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 [L1], genomenos against egennēsen [L2], the two "made" clauses [L3].

**2. "According to the flesh" classifies descent. It does not describe a mechanism.**
- [L1] Romans 9:3: "my kinsmen according to the flesh." Romans 9:5: "of whom as concerning the flesh Christ came." Romans 4:1: "Abraham our father, as pertaining to the flesh."
- [L2] κατὰ σάρκα (kata sarka, "according to flesh") sorts a person into a kindred, as with Paul's "kinsmen." In Romans 1:3-4 it is set against κατὰ πνεῦμα ἁγιωσύνης (kata pneuma hagiōsynēs, "according to the spirit of holiness"). Two columns are named, the flesh column and the Spirit column, and the child stands in both.
- [L4] The phrase puts the child in David's kindred. It does not say a man's seed formed him, and Luke 1:35 and Matthew 1:20 supply the formation. Footings: Romans 1:3-4; 9:3, 5 [L1], kata sarka against kata pneuma [L2].

**3. Descent is reckoned by the name, and the law separates the name from the begetter.**
- [L1] Deuteronomy 25:6: "the firstborn which she beareth shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead." Genesis 48:5: "Ephraim and Manasseh... are mine; as Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine."
- [L3] The challenger's Numbers 1:2, "by the house of their fathers," is a reckoning, a counting. These two texts show the reckoning can run apart from the begetter, which is how Joseph's house carries the child.
- [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 says of Coniah: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." Matthew 1:11-12 places Jechonias in Joseph's line.
- [L4] If Joseph begat the child, the child would be Jechonias's seed under that ban (Jeremiah 22:30, Matthew 1:11-12). The throne right (Luke 1:32) then comes by the legal reckoning of Deuteronomy 25:6 and Genesis 48:5, not by Joseph's seed.

**LINK 5 [premise 6]: SUPPORTED.**

DEBATE RULE VIOLATION (Isaiah 28:10: "here a little, [and] there a little"). The rule on a BROKEN ground binds you as it binds the debaters. On every COLLISION POINTS line you grade DEFENDER LINK BROKEN or CHALLENGER LINK BROKEN, exhibit on that line itself at least two lower layers of DIFFERENT kinds, each tagged: [L1] with the KJV words quoted; [L2] with the Hebrew or Greek word in its own script, with its transliteration marks, or with its Strong's number; [L3] with the cross-reference named. Two quotations at one layer are one layer. A BROKEN line without them is a DEBATE RULE VIOLATION. The system checks every BROKEN line after you answer; a violating line is returned to you to cure, by exhibiting the two layers, or to answer in writing why it stands. An answer that does not exhibit them leaves the violation standing, and your judgment is then not counted. The same holds when your CONCEDED LINKS or CHALLENGER CONCEDED LINKS count is 0 while you grade a link of that side BROKEN for a witness left unanswered. Both sides alike.

DEFENDER CHAIN MARK: <FOOTED|STRAINED|SLACK> -- <one clause naming the load-bearing link that decides the mark>.
CHALLENGER CHAIN MARK: <FOOTED|STRAINED|SLACK> -- <one clause naming the load-bearing link that decides the mark>.
These two lines come last, after OUT-OF-ROUND CONCESSIONS, the defender's first. One definition marks each side's own constructed chain, the defender's declared chain (D1 to the conclusion link) and the challenger's own alternative reading: FOOTED = every load-bearing link of the side's chain is footed on an exhibited L1-L3 layer; its reading stands as a built structure. STRAINED = the chain is built but one or more load-bearing links float: a link floats only when the block that states it exhibits fewer than two prior layers; the mark names the layers exhibited and the layer lacking; it stands only under charity. SLACK = the side advanced no coherent chain of its own: objections without a constructed reading, or a construction that never cohered. Each mark describes ONLY how well that side built its own chain. It is descriptive, not a verdict. It MUST NOT change DEFENSE STANDING, CHALLENGE STANDING, CONCEDED LINKS, CHALLENGER CONCEDED LINKS, the CLAUSE line, KEYSTONE or the top-line verdict. A FOOTED chain is fully compatible with its side losing the debate; a SLACK chain is fully compatible with its side winning it.
