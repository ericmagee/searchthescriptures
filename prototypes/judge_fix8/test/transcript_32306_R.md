## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept must be upon precept" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds seven rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED (no footing offered or fewer than two prior layers exhibited, or a pressed witness left unanswered) "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all or exhibited fewer than two prior layers (the burden rule, the same in every round: a load-bearing [L4] counts only when it exhibits at least two prior layers and the chain from them; fewer floats and carries zero weight), or when a witness pressed against that premise, a verse carried under PRESSED WITH: in the list against that side, was left unanswered"; it counts as conceded.
- **AN UNANSWERED PRESSED WITNESS (operator rulings 372 and 392).** "A premise whose pressed witness goes unanswered is UNEXHIBITED: it counts toward CONCEDED LINKS and the standings." A non-response is never neutral: a side could otherwise win by not responding. But "a press must have a premise behind it; a verse pressed without one isn't a witness" (ruling 392): only a verse carried under PRESSED WITH: beside a numbered premise of the other side's list is a pressed witness. A verse cited elsewhere, on the ledger's "cited in" line or pressed in prose against a link, is not a witness, and leaving it unanswered never counts; the "cited in" line is for information only.
- **CONCEDED (stated).** A link is graded CONCEDED (stated) "when the block itself states the surrender, only when the block surrenders the link itself; a block that drops a limb and keeps the link in a narrowed form is not a concession: grade the narrowed link".
- **VERSE CITATION (operator ruling 3).** "A verse counts as evidence, as footing, as a witness or as an answer only when its book, chapter and verse are stated and the words quoted are exactly the words of the KJV; a quotation may be part of a verse; paraphrased or misquoted words do not count"; an answer in substance without the reference named does not count, and a quotation the citation check marks NOT COUNTED is not counted.
- **CLOSE ROUNDS.** Round 4 gives each side a close: the defender's close answers witnesses still pressed against its chain and counts as a later defender round; the challenger's close is its final words, answers nothing new and may not press, and a verse it names that its earlier rounds never named is marked NOT COUNTED. The defender's close counts only as an answer to a witness already pressed: a verse it names that no earlier round of either side names is marked NEW IN CLOSE, NOT COUNTED, and a sentence in it that grades or presses against a challenger link is disregarded. Both closes keep the declared D numbers and the round-3 C numbers; a close that renumbers is flagged.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession". It adds: "A charge is stronger than its quote only when it asserts what the quote does not: a negation, a universal (all, none, only, never), or a claim the quote's own words do not make. Dropping, reordering or paraphrasing the quote's words is not stronger. A limb the link round narrows counts in its narrowed form." and "A premise's quote is checked against every earlier round of the opponent; it is verbatim if found in any of them, and the judge names that round."
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Defender (Claude)

DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

### Opening: Christ had no biological father

**Thesis.** [L4] The KJV answers no. Jesus was conceived in Mary without any man's seed, by the power of the Holy Ghost. Joseph was his father only by legal standing and in common supposition, not by generation. By "biological father" I mean a man whose seed generated the child. The text excludes that.

#### 1. The woman was a virgin when she conceived

**[L1] Luke 1:27:** "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin's name was Mary."
- **[L2]** The word is παρθένος (parthenos, "a virgin, a maiden who has not had relations with a man").

**[L1] Luke 1:34:** "Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"
- **[L2]** The verb is γινώσκω (ginōskō, "to know"), here in its sexual sense.
- **[L3]** Genesis 4:1 uses the same idiom: "And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived."
- Mary's question rules out a human explanation. Gabriel's answer does not say "a man shall come." It names another source (see 3 below).

**[L1] Matthew 1:23:** "Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son."
- This quotes **[L1] Isaiah 7:14:** "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son."
- **[L2]** The Hebrew is עַלְמָה (ʿalmāh, "a young woman of marriageable age, unmarried").
- **[L3]** Genesis 24:43 uses the same word for Rebekah: "when the virgin cometh forth to draw water."
- **[L3]** Genesis 24:16 describes her: "a virgin, neither had any man known her."
- So the word is used of a woman the text itself says no man had known.
- [L4] The chain is this. Matthew 1:22 introduces the quotation as fulfilment ("Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled"). The Greek parthenos in Matthew 1:23 fixes the sense. Genesis 24:16,43 shows ʿalmāh applied to a woman declared unknown by any man. Together these show the prophecy was of a conception without a man.

#### 2. The source of the conception is stated as the Holy Ghost, not Joseph

**[L1] Matthew 1:18:** "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- **[L2]** "Of" translates ἐκ (ek, "out of, from the source of"). The source is πνεύματος ἁγίου (pneumatos hagiou, "Holy Spirit").
- The timing is "before they came together."

**[L1] Matthew 1:20:** "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."
- **[L2]** The participle is γεννηθέν (gennēthen, "begotten, brought into being"). It is again ἐκ πνεύματος (ek pneumatos, "out of the Spirit").
- The angel gives this as the reason Joseph should not fear to take Mary.

**[L1] Matthew 1:19:** Joseph was "minded to put her away privily."
- [L4] Joseph's own reaction shows he knew the child was not his. That is why the angel's explanation was needed.

**[L1] Matthew 1:25:** "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son."
- **[L2]** The verb ἐγίνωσκεν (eginōsken, "was knowing") is in the imperfect, describing continued non-relations. Its limit is the birth ("till she had brought forth").
- Joseph had no union with Mary before the birth.

#### 3. The power by which the child came to be

**[L1] Luke 1:35:** "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- **[L2]** The verb ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, "will overshadow") is not language of marital union. In the Greek Old Testament it is used of the cloud over the tabernacle (Exodus 40:35).
- **[L3]** Exodus 40:35 in the KJV reads: "the cloud abode thereon, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle."
- The word marks divine power at work, not the act of a man.
- "Therefore" (διό, dio, "for which reason") ties the child's title to this origin.

**[L1] Galatians 4:4:** "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law."
- **[L2]** γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός (genomenon ek gynaikos, "coming to be out of a woman") names only the woman as the human source.
- **[L3]** Genesis 3:15 foretells this: "between thy seed and her seed." The Hebrew zeraʿ (seed) is attributed to the woman, which is unusual because seed is normally reckoned through the man.
- [L4] Two footings support the inference. Galatians 4:4 names only the woman as the human origin. Genesis 3:15 assigns the promised seed to the woman. So the deliverer's human origin is traced through her alone.

#### 4. Joseph's fatherhood is legal and supposed, not generative

**[L1] Matthew 1:16:** "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."
- **[L2]** Matthew 1:2–15 repeats ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat") for every link. At verse 16 the pattern changes. The text does not say "Joseph begat Jesus." It says ἐξ ἧς ἐγεννήθη (ex hēs egennēthē, "out of whom was born"), with the relative pronoun ἧς (hēs) in the feminine singular. It refers to Mary alone.
- The same genealogy that gives Joseph's line breaks its formula at the one point where the generative claim would have been made.

**[L1] Luke 3:23:** "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph."
- **[L2]** ὡς ἐνομίζετο (hōs enomizeto, "as was thought, as was supposed") marks a popular belief, not a fact.

**[L1] Mark 6:3:** "Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary?"
- Here the townspeople name only the mother.

**[L1] Luke 2:48–49:** Mary says "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." Jesus answers, "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"
- [L4] Two footings support the inference. Mary's phrase uses the speech of ordinary supposition, as in Luke 3:23. Jesus's reply redirects "father" to God. So the title "father" for Joseph is real in the household but is not the claim of generation.

#### 5. Consistency with the royal line

**[L1] Jeremiah 22:30:** "Write ye this man childless... for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David."
- **[L1] Matthew 1:11–12** records "Josias begat Jechonias" and "Jechonias begat Salathiel."
- **[L1] Matthew 1:16** names Joseph as in that royal line.
- **[L1] Luke 1:32** promises: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David."
- [L4] The chain is this. Jeremiah 22:30 forbids Jechonias's seed from the throne. Matthew 1:11–16 puts Joseph in Jechonias's line. Luke 1:32 promises the throne to Jesus. If Joseph's seed generated Jesus, that promise would collide with Jeremiah 22:30. If Joseph is father only by legal standing, Jesus's right to the throne comes through the husband's line without his seed. This is a corroborating fit, not the main proof.

#### Summary

[L4] Every verse that speaks to the child's origin traces it to Mary and to the Holy Ghost. None traces it to a man.

#### DECLARED CHAIN -- the links your case stands on
