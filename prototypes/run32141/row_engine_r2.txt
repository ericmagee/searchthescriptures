CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, CLOSING AND PREMISE LIST

# Closing: the text names the father, and the seed is out of a man's loins

The declared chain asks the panel to accept one withheld verb over five registers that name the man, one covenant-origin phrase over the seed rows, a prophecy-scope leap over Isaiah's own bounds, a curse no verse ever lays on this throne, and a fatherhood made of reputation. Set the rows side by side and the answer stands.

**The seed rows name the body.** "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12) [L1]. "Of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30) [L1-L2]. "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3) [L1-L2]. "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14) [L1] — and evident by the law's reckoning: "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18) [L1]. Bowels and loins are a man's body. The reply that Acts 2:30's loins are David's, not Joseph's, only moves the question one generation back and does not answer it: David's flesh seed reaches this child through a father of David's house, and the text names him.

**The father is named, in five registers.** Mary's own mouth: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48). The narrator: "the parents brought in the child Jesus" (Luke 2:27), "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33). The town: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22); "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" (John 6:42). A disciple: "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45). The angel: "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20). [L1] Mary, who received the angel's own words, calls Joseph "thy father"; the child's answer, "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" (Luke 2:49), speaks of business and denies nothing.

**The marriage and the timing rows hold.** "Joseph her husband" (Matthew 1:19), "Mary thy wife" (1:20) — and the law calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:24) [L1]. "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (1:25) bounds the abstinence to the birth — the defender's own gloss, "until the birth" [L1]. The canon's stated means of begetting is knowing: "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1) [L1]. Mary's question is her state when the angel spoke: "seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34), present tense [L2].

**"Of the Holy Ghost" is covenant origin, not paternity.** "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 5:1) is the same idiom for believers, whose fathers are named throughout the epistles' congregations; where the text marks biological flesh it adds kata sarka (according to flesh): "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3) [L2]. And the angel's own verbs are presence and power: "come upon" is Acts 1:8's word of the apostles; "overshadow" is Luke 9:34's cloud [L2]. The child's title is given, not a sire's: Solomon is "my son" while David begets him (2 Samuel 7:14; 1 Chronicles 28:6) [L1-L3].

**The curse is never laid here.** Jechonias appears in the New Testament at Matthew 1:11-12, and no row cites Jeremiah 22:30 against Jesus; the signet is set on Zerubbabel (Haggai 2:23), governor (Haggai 1:1), never king; and Jeremiah carries his own rule: "If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil" (Jeremiah 18:8) [L1]. "Mary's line" is not in the text; Luke 3:23 gives the line to Joseph, "the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" [L1].

**Answer: Yes. Christ had a biological father — Joseph, of the house of David, named in the text. The seed is of his loins, and the record forbids "no man was involved."**
