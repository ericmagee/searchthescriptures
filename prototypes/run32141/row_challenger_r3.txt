**LINK 1: SUPPORTED**

*Premise 1 — the child's answer speaks of business and denies nothing.* Footing:

[L1] The two verses, quoted whole: "his mother said unto him, Son, why hast thou thus dealt with us? behold, thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing. And he said unto them, How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" (Luke 2:48-49). Mary's sentence names "thy father." The child's sentence states a necessity about "my Father's business" and contains no negation verb against her word.

[L3] The same two registers stand side by side in the angel's one announcement: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32) and "that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35). A named human father in 1:32 and the divine title in 1:35, in one speech, neither erasing the other.

Chain: if "the Son of God" beside "his father David" does not deny David, then "my Father's business" beside "thy father" does not deny Joseph. The premise rests on [L1] the words of Luke 2:48-49 and [L3] the paired registers at Luke 1:32/35.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED**

*Premise 2 — "of the Holy Ghost" is covenant origin, not paternity.* (This states the held reading; footing as those rows exhibit it.) Footing:

[L1] "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20), beside "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14, spoken of Solomon) and "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father" (1 Chronicles 28:6) — in one verse the son David begat and the title God gave him. The Spirit-rows name the claim God takes on the child.

[L2] "of" is ek (out of) with the genitive. Across the received Greek, ek + God/Spirit is origin-language — "born of God" (John 1:13), "is of God" (1 John 4:7) — never biological paternity; where biological flesh is meant the text adds kata sarka (according to the flesh): "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3). Matthew 1:20 carries no kata sarka.

[L3] Luke 1:35's verbs are power and presence: "shall come upon" is eperchomai (come upon), the verb of "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you" (Acts 1:8); "shall overshadow" is episkiazō (overshadow), the verb of "a cloud overshadowed them" (Luke 9:34) and "the shadow of Peter... overshadow" (Acts 5:15) — count as served (S-9): episkiazō = 5 rows, every use the cloud or the shadow.

Matthew 1:18 "she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" and Luke 1:35 "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee" are quoted as written; they state the origin God claims, and no row re-reads them as a begetting.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED**

*Premise 3 — David's flesh seed reaches this child through a father of David's house whom the text names.* (The held reading; footing as those rows exhibit it.) Footing:

[L1] The named man of David's house: "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" (Matthew 1:20); "Joseph her husband" (1:19); Joseph "was of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4); "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48); "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know" (John 6:42).

[L1] The seed rows are anatomical: "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30); "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3); "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14).

[L2] Matthew 1:16 "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus" — the passive egennēthē (was brought forth) names no male subject: a silence in the begetting beside a named husband, not a denial. The row does not say "no man."

[L3] The canon's stated means: "Adam knew Eve his wife: and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1); the law calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:24).

[L4], footed on those rows: the record holds that Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal, and the seed is of the named man's body.

The defender's press is answered where it is pressed: Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman" (genomenon ek gynaikos — come to be out of a woman) is the mortality idiom — "Man that is born of a woman is of few days" (Job 14:1; Job 15:14; 25:4); it is said of John, "Among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" (Matthew 11:11), and John's father is named: "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son" (Luke 1:13). "Born of a woman" names the mother's side of birth and excludes no father. Romans 1:3 is itself the seed row, kata sarka included. Matthew 1:16 is non-narration, answered above.

**LINK 6: SUPPORTED**

*Premise 6 — Mary's question is her state when the angel spoke.* (The held reading; footing as those rows exhibit it.) Footing:

[L2] "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34) — andra ou ginōskō (a man I know not) is present active indicative: her state when the angel spoke; a standing negation would take ou mē (not at all) with the aorist subjunctive.

[L1] "before they came together, she was found with child" (Matthew 1:18) dates the finding; "took unto him his wife" (1:24); "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (1:25) bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy; "Joseph her husband" (1:19); "Mary thy wife" (1:20).

[L4], footed on those rows: the record holds that Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal. The timing rows are read inside that chain as written: 1:18 dates the finding; 1:25 covers the pregnancy after the taking (1:24); 1:34 is the state when the angel spoke.

Luke 1:35 pressed here answers itself at Link 2 (eperchomai = Acts 1:8; episkiazō = the cloud), and the title is given with the throne BEFORE the question — "he shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32) precedes 1:34.

**THE FLOOR, and the aim at the keystone**

The seed is David's according to the flesh, out of loins and bowels (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30; Romans 1:3; Hebrews 7:14); the father is named — by Mary ("thy father," Luke 2:48), by the narrator ("the parents," Luke 2:27), by the town ("the son of Joseph," Luke 4:22; John 6:42), by a disciple ("the son of Joseph," John 1:45), and by the angel ("Joseph, thou son of David," Matthew 1:20); Joseph names the child (Matthew 1:21, 25); and the reckoning is "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18). The canon's stated means of begetting is knowing (Genesis 4:1), and the abstinence is bounded by "till" to the pregnancy (Matthew 1:25). The seed rows require seed out of a man's body: the record forbids "no man was involved" — the seed rows forbid it.

So the keystone — that no man begat the child — is pressed at three links. **LINK 1** rests on counting household and town speech as an exclusion while the narrator's own qualifier is enomizeto (was reckoned by custom — the verb of "where prayer was wont to be made," Acts 16:13), which carries no verdict and stands beside Mary's "thy father" (Luke 2:48) and the reckoning "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2). **LINK 3** rests on one silent passive ("of whom was born Jesus," Matthew 1:16) read against the anatomical seed rows and against Genesis 4:1. **LINK 6** rests on the abstinence row read outside the chain, "till" unbent.

**UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES IN THE DEFENDER'S CASE** (their words, verbatim):

1. "None says a man begat the last generation." — floating: it asserts the very gap the pressed rows fill ("which shall proceed out of thy bowels," 2 Samuel 7:12; "Adam knew Eve his wife: and she conceived," Genesis 4:1; "Joseph her husband," Matthew 1:19).
2. "so David's flesh reached this child through the woman." — floating: Galatians 4:4 is the mortality idiom (Job 14:1; Matthew 11:11, of John whose father is named at Luke 1:13), Romans 1:3 marks flesh by kata sarka, and the defender concedes "I drop any claim that a verse names Mary's descent."
3. "the abstinence runs through the birth, so Joseph could not have begotten the child." — floating: "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25) bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy; "Joseph her husband" (1:19), "Mary thy wife" (1:20), "his neighbour's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:24) name the marriage from the espousal.
