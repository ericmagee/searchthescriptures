DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

# Opening: Did Christ have a biological father?

**Position.** No. Jesus had no human biological father. Mary supplied his flesh. The Holy Ghost, and no man, was the source of the conception. By "biological father" I mean a man whose seed begets the child. Joseph was the husband of Mary and the supposed father, never the begetter.

---

## 1. The genealogy breaks its own pattern at Matthew 1:16

[L1] Matthew 1:2-16 runs "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob…" and ends: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matt 1:16).

[L2] Every earlier link uses ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat," active, with a male subject). Verse 16 does not say Joseph "begat" Jesus. It says ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē, "was born," passive) ἐξ ἧς (ex hēs, "out of whom"). The relative pronoun ἧς is feminine singular, so it can only refer to Mary and cannot include Joseph. Joseph is called ὁ ἀνήρ (ho anēr, "the husband") of Mary, not the begetter of Jesus.

[L3] Luke confirms the same qualification: Jesus "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph" (Luke 3:23). The Greek ὡς ἐνομίζετο (hōs enomizeto, "as was reckoned/supposed") marks Joseph's fatherhood as a popular opinion. It is not stated as fact.

[L4] Matthew keeps the verb "begat" for 39 generations of fathers and then changes it. This is a deliberate break in the line. Its footings are the verb change at Matt 1:16 [L1/L2] and Luke's "as was supposed" [L3].

## 2. The text names the source of the conception, and it is not a man

[L1] "Before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matt 1:18). The angel told Joseph: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matt 1:20). "[Joseph] knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matt 1:25).

[L2] In Matt 1:18, 20 the phrase is ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (ek pneumatos hagiou, "out of spirit holy"). The preposition ἐκ marks source or origin. In Matt 1:20 γεννηθὲν (gennēthen, "that which is begotten/conceived") is passive, with the Holy Ghost as the source. In Matt 1:25 οὐκ ἐγίνωσκεν (ouk eginōsken, "he was not knowing her"), in the imperfect tense, means no marital union continued until the birth.

[L1] Mary's own words: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34). The answer: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35).

[L2] ἄνδρα οὐ γινώσκω (andra ou ginōskō, "a man I do not know") is a present-tense statement of Mary's condition. ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, "will overshadow") is the verb used for the cloud of the Lord's presence in Luke 9:34. It describes divine power, not a bodily union.

[L3] God is a spirit (John 4:24), and "a spirit hath not flesh and bones" (Luke 24:39). The Holy Ghost therefore acts as the power that causes the conception, and does not stand in the place of a male sire. The flesh is Mary's, and Galatians 4:4 says "made of a woman" (γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός, genomenon ek gunaikos, "coming-to-be out of a woman"), with no mention of a man.

[L4] Matt 1:18-20, Luke 1:34-35, the ἐκ plus passive grammar [L2], and John 4:24 with Luke 24:39 [L3] together lead to this: the origin is God's creative act upon Mary's body. A human sire is excluded, and no divine "biological" act in a physical sense is implied.

## 3. The promise was made beforehand: the seed of the woman and the virgin

[L1] "I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed" (Gen 3:15). "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel" (Isa 7:14). Matthew applies this directly: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled…" (Matt 1:22-23).

[L2] In Gen 3:15 זַרְעָהּ (zar'āh, "her seed") is unusual, because seed is normally reckoned to the man. In Isa 7:14 הָעַלְמָה (ha'almāh, "the young woman, the maiden") is the one who הָרָה (hārāh, "is with child"). Matthew quotes it with παρθένος (parthenos, "virgin"), and Luke 1:27 calls Mary a παρθένον (parthenon, "virgin") who was "espoused."

[L3] The promise was kept. The seed is traced to the woman in Gal 4:4. Mary is the one "of whom was born Jesus" (Matt 1:16).

[L4] A promise of the woman's seed, joined to the virgin conceiving, shows that the prophecy points to a conception without a male contributor. The footings are Gen 3:15 and Isa 7:14 [L1/L2] and Matt 1:22-23 [L1/L3].

## 4. A biological descent from Joseph would have defeated the throne promise

[L1] Joseph's line ran through Jechonias: "Josias begat Jechonias… Jechonias begat Salathiel" (Matt 1:11-12). The Lord said of Coniah (Jeconiah): "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah" (Jer 22:30).

[L2] Jeremiah's word is מִזַּרְעוֹ (mizzar'ô, "from his seed"). The curse falls on his seed, meaning his biological descendants.

[L3] Luke's genealogy runs through Nathan, David's son (Luke 3:31), and so avoids that line. Yet the angel promised that "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32). Jesus is therefore of David by Mary's line (Luke 3:23-31), and he is legally Joseph's by Joseph's naming and acceptance of him (Matt 1:20-21, 25).

[L4] The throne could not pass by seed through Jechonias, so a biological sonship from Joseph would conflict with Jer 22:30 [L1/L2]. A legal claim through Joseph, together with a physical descent from David through Mary (Luke 3:31) [L3], satisfies both the promise and the curse.

## 5. Texts that seem to name a father

**"Thy father and I have sought thee" (Luke 2:48).** [L1] Mary's words are met at once by the child: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" (Luke 2:49). [L3] The child corrects the title and claims another Father, while Luke already said Joseph was only "supposed" (Luke 3:23).

**"Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph?" (John 6:42; Matt 13:55).** [L1] These are the words of the crowd. [L3] They carry the same "supposed" reckoning as Luke 3:23, so they report opinion, not the begetting.

**"Of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Rom 1:3; Acts 2:30).** [L2] κατὰ σάρκα (kata sarka, "according to flesh") and ἐκ σπέρματος Δαυίδ (ek spermatos Dauid, "out of the seed of David") require physical descent from David. [L3] Mary supplies that descent (Luke 3:31; Gal 4:4). The passages do not say the descent came through a male parent.

**"Only begotten" (John 3:16).** [L2] μονογενής (monogenēs, "only-born, one of a kind") speaks of a uniqueness that no other son has. [L3] It matches Luke 1:35: "that holy thing… shall be called the Son of God."

## DECLARED CHAIN -- the links your case stands on

1. Matthew 1:16 breaks the "begat" pattern: ἐγέννησεν (begat) gives way to ἐγεννήθη ἐξ ἧς (was born out of whom, feminine singular), and Joseph is only "the husband of Mary" — footing: [L1] Matt 1:16, [L2] the verb change and feminine ἧς, [L3] Luke 3:23 "as was supposed."
2. The conception is stated to be ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (out of the Holy Ghost), with Mary a woman who "knew not a man" and Joseph not knowing her until the birth — footing: [L1] Matt 1:18, 20, 25; Luke 1:34-35, [L2] ἐκ plus passive γεννηθέν, ἄνδρα οὐ γινώσκω, ἐπισκιάσει, [L3] John 4:24 with Luke 24:39.
3. The woman's seed and the virgin conceiving were promised beforehand and are applied to Jesus — footing: [L1] Gen 3:15, Isa 7:14, [L2] זַרְעָהּ (her seed) and הָעַלְמָה/παρθένος (the maiden/virgin), [L3] Matt 1:22-23 and Luke 1:27.
4. Biological descent from Joseph would conflict with the curse on Jechonias's seed, while Mary's line through Nathan carries David's seed — footing: [L1] Jer 22:30, Matt 1:11-12, Luke 3:31, [L2] מִזַּרְעוֹ (from his seed), [L3] Luke 1:32 with Luke 3:23-31.
5. The texts that name Joseph as father report supposition or legal standing, and Rom 1:3 and Acts 2:30 are met by descent through Mary — footing: [L1] Luke 2:48-49, John 6:42, Rom 1:3, [L2] ἐνομίζετο (was supposed), κατὰ σάρκα (according to flesh), [L3] Luke 3:23 and Gal 4:4 γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός.
6. Answer: No. Christ had no human biological father, because his conception was ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (out of the Holy Ghost) in Mary, and Joseph was his husband-guardian and supposed father — footing: [L1] Matt 1:16, 18, 20, Luke 1:35, [L2] ἐγεννήθη ἐξ ἧς and ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου, [L3] Isa 7:14 with Matt 1:22-23 and Gal 4:4.
