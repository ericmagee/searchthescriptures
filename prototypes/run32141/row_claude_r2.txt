DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

# Round 2: Re-seating the Chain and the Link Round

Each premise the challenger lists is a term with its chain stripped off. I put each one back in its chain below. Where the challenger presses a verse, I quote it.

**One opening note on Link 1 (Matthew 1:16).** The challenger says the passive is only silence about one act. The silence is selective. Matthew's pattern is "begat" for the father plus ἐκ (ek, "out of") for the mother:
- [L1] "Judas begat Phares and Zara of Thamar" (Matt 1:3).
- [L1] "Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth" (Matt 1:5).
- [L1] "David the king begat Solomon of her that had been the wife of Urias" (Matt 1:6).

[L2] In every row the father is the subject of ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat") and the mother follows with ἐκ. In verse 16 the mother's ἐξ ἧς (ex hēs, "out of whom," feminine) is kept, and the father's "begat" is withheld for Joseph alone.
[L4] That is a break in a pattern, and it does not come from the text merely being silent. Its footings are Matt 1:3, 5, 6 [L1] and the verb withheld at 1:16 [L2].

The challenger never touched the fact that "begat" is withheld only where the Spirit-conception is introduced (Matt 1:18-20). That stands.

## LINK 1 (premise 1): the woman's seed and the virgin

**Pressed against it:** "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Sam 7:12); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30).

- [L1] Loins name the ancestor's body for remote descent, not the immediate parent. "Levi… was yet in the loins of his father [Abraham], when Melchisedec met him" (Heb 7:10). "Kings shall come out of thy loins" (Gen 35:11), said to Jacob of kings generations later.
- [L1] In Acts 2:30 the loins are David's ("his"), not Joseph's. These rows require David's descent and are silent on which parent carried it.
- [L3] The throne phrase also names a mother's body. "Of the fruit of thy body" (Ps 132:11) is in Hebrew מִפְּרִי בִטְנֶךָ (mipperî viṭnekhā, "from the fruit of thy belly"). The same word בֶּטֶן (beṭen, "belly, womb") gives "the fruit of the womb" (Gen 30:2). Luke 1:42 says to Mary, "blessed is the fruit of thy womb."
- [L2] On Isaiah 7:14, the challenger says the word for virgin is bethulah. But עַלְמָה (ʿalmāh, "young woman") is Rebekah in Gen 24:43, and the same Rebekah is בְּתוּלָה (bĕthûlāh, "virgin") in Gen 24:16: "a virgin, neither had any man known her." On the challenger's own reported margin, "thou, O virgin, shalt call," the word is still virgin.
- [L1] The sign is not bounded by Isaiah's day: "the Lord himself shall give you a sign" (Isa 7:14), after the offer "ask it either in the depth, or in the height above" (7:11). Matthew then states the application: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled" (Matt 1:22-23).

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED.**
[L4] The footings are:
1. Isa 7:11, 14 [L1], a sign from the height or the depth;
2. Matt 1:22-23 [L1], the application;
3. Gen 24:16, 43 [L2], almah and bethulah for one woman;
4. Gal 3:16 [L3], "to thy seed, which is Christ," a single seed.

The chain runs from a sign of that magnitude, through the application, to a conception with no male contributor. The loins and bowels rows name David's body and so do not exclude this.

## LINK 2 (premise 2): the curse on Jechonias's seed

**Pressed against it:** "If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil that I thought to do unto them" (Jer 18:8); the sons of Jeconiah (1 Chr 3:17-18); "I will make thee as a signet" (Hag 2:23).

- [L1] Jeremiah 18:8 is conditioned on "that nation… turn." No row records Coniah turning. Jer 22:30 reads: "Write ye this man childless… for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah."
- [L2] מִזַּרְעוֹ (mizzarʿô, "from his seed") is the stated ground.
- [L1] Jeconiah's sons existing (1 Chr 3:17) does not touch the sentence, which concerns the throne, not offspring. Haggai names "Zerubbabel… governor of Judah" (Hag 1:1), never king, which is consistent with the sentence standing.
- [L1] Matthew puts Joseph's line through "Jechonias begat Salathiel" (Matt 1:11-12).
- [L3] Scripture records naming and legal sonship apart from begetting. "There is a son born to Naomi" (Ruth 4:17), of Ruth's child. "The sons of Pedaiah were, Zerubbabel" (1 Chr 3:19), though Ezra 3:2 calls Zerubbabel "the son of Shealtiel."

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED.**
[L4] The footings are:
1. Jer 22:30 with מִזַּרְעוֹ [L1/L2];
2. Matt 1:11-12 [L1], Joseph's line through Jechonias;
3. Matt 1:21, 25 [L1], where Joseph names the child;
4. Ruth 4:17 and 1 Chr 3:19 [L3], where legal reckoning stands apart from begetting.

If Joseph begat Jesus, the child is Jechonias's seed and Jer 22:30 bars the throne. Legal sonship by naming avoids the bar. The conflict is conditional on the sentence being unrevoked, and no text records a revocation.

## LINK 3 (premise 3): "Mary's line"

**Pressed against it:** "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" (Luke 3:23).

Noted. Not conceding here. Goes to review. The footings follow.

- [L2] In the Greek of Luke 3:23 the article τοῦ (tou, "of the") attaches to every name from Heli onward, but Ἰωσήφ (Iōsēph, "Joseph") stands without it, right after ὡς ἐνομίζετο (hōs enomizeto, "as was supposed"). This is grammar that sets Joseph apart. It is not proof by itself.
- [L1] Matthew gives Joseph's father as "Jacob begat Joseph" (Matt 1:16), and Luke gives "Heli." The text itself records two fathers for Joseph.
- [L3] The challenger's own rows show a man reckoned by his wife's father. "Took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai… and was called after their name" (Ezra 2:61). Numbers 36:8 has the heiress daughter "wife unto one of the family of the tribe of her father."
- [L2] Rom 1:3 reads γενομένου ἐκ σπέρματος Δαυίδ κατὰ σάρκα (genomenou ek spermatos Dauid kata sarka, "come to be out of the seed of David according to flesh"). Gal 4:4 reads γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός (genomenon ek gunaikos, "come to be out of a woman"). Matt 1:16 reads ἐξ ἧς (ex hēs, "out of whom").
- [L1] The angel spoke to Mary of "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32).

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED on the chain, not on the phrase "Mary's line through Nathan."**
[L4] The footings are Rom 1:3, Gal 4:4 and Matt 1:16 [L2], with Luke 1:32 [L1]. If the flesh is from David's seed and no male begat it, Mary is the only channel of that flesh. Luke 3:23-31 is consistent with this and is not its ground.

## LINK 4 (premise 4): the father-rows as opinion

**Pressed against it:** "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48); "where prayer was wont to be made" (Acts 16:13).

- [L2] ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "was reckoned, supposed") has a root the text lets us test. Where the supposition can be checked, it fails.
  - "Supposing him to have been in the company" (Luke 2:44), said of this same child.
  - "Supposing he had been dead" (Acts 14:19).
  - "Supposing that the prisoners had been fled" (Acts 16:27).
  - "They supposed that they should have received more" (Matt 20:10).
- [L2] Acts 16:13 is a reputed place of prayer, a reputation that is then tested by going out to it. It carries a custom held at arm's length and no verdict, and Luke 3:23 adds ὡς (hōs, "as") in front of it.
- [L1] Luke's own narrator writes "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33), not "his father and his mother." Mary's "thy father" (2:48) is met in the next verse by "my Father's business" (2:49), the pairing the text itself sets.
- [L1] The angel never calls Joseph the child's father: "take the young child and his mother" (Matt 2:13, 20, 21).
- [L1] Even the crowd's "whose father and mother we know" (John 6:42) is an objection to "I came down from heaven," and the reply points to the Father (John 6:46).

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED.**
[L4] The footings are Luke 2:44, Acts 14:19, 16:27 and Matt 20:10 [L2/L3] for the verb's tested use, and Luke 2:33 and Matt 2:13 [L1] for the narrator's and angel's own voice. The father-rows report reputation and household speech. The narrator and the angel do not use them.

## LINK 5 (premise 5): "A human sire is excluded"

**Pressed against it:** "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30); "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 5:1); "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Rom 1:3).

- [L2] John 1:13 is the challenger's own parallel, and it states the exclusion: "which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." The Greek is οὐκ ἐκ θελήματος ἀνδρός ἀλλ' ἐκ θεοῦ ἐγεννήθησαν (ouk ek thelēmatos andros all' ek theou egennēthēsan, "not out of the will of a man but out of God were begotten"). It uses the same verb as Matt 1:16, 20, and ἐκ θεοῦ is set against a man's willing.
- [L2] Matt 1:20 locates the begetting in her body: τὸ ἐν αὐτῇ γεννηθέν (to en autē gennēthen, "that which in her is begotten"). The new birth of believers has no womb.
- [L2] Rom 1:3-4 sets κατὰ σάρκα (kata sarka, "according to flesh"), which is David's seed, beside κατὰ πνεῦμα ἁγιωσύνης (kata pneuma hagiōsunēs, "according to the spirit of holiness") in one sentence. The absence of kata sarka at Matt 1:20 therefore proves nothing.
- [L1] "Therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35). The Greek διὸ καί (dio kai, "wherefore also") grounds the title in the Holy Ghost coming upon her.
- [L1] Solomon's sonship comes with no "therefore" tied to conception (2 Sam 7:14), and his human father is named.
- [L1] Mary's "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34) is answered by "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee" (1:35). The answer does not say "by Joseph."

**LINK 5: SUPPORTED.**
[L4] The footings are John 1:13 and Matt 1:20 [L2], with Luke 1:34-35 [L1] and Rom 1:3-4 [L2]. The challenger agrees that eperchomai (come upon) and episkiazō (overshadow) are presence and power. None of the angel's verbs is a verb of knowing, and the angel's answer to "How?" does not name Joseph.

## LINK 6 (premise 6): "Joseph her husband… Mary thy wife"

**Pressed against it:** "Joseph her husband" (Matt 1:19); "Mary thy wife" (1:20); "his neighbour's wife" (Deut 22:24); "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matt 1:25).

- [L1] Deut 22:23-24 speaks of "a damsel that is a virgin betrothed unto an husband" and calls her "his neighbour's wife." Wife there names legal standing with no union.
- [L2] Matt 1:18 μνηστευθείσης (mnēsteutheisēs, "having been betrothed"). Matt 1:20 παραλαβεῖν (paralabein, "to take to oneself") is still future. Matt 1:18 says "Before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- [L1] Even at the birth journey she is "Mary his espoused wife, being great with child" (Luke 2:5).
- [L2] οὐκ ἐγίνωσκεν (ouk eginōsken, "he was not knowing her") is the negative of Gen 4:1 "Adam knew Eve… and she conceived," and it is set directly after "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matt 1:20).
- [L1] The word "guardian" is not in the text. The angel's own phrase is "take the young child and his mother" (Matt 2:13), and Joseph "called his name JESUS" (Matt 1:25).

**LINK 6: SUPPORTED.**
[L4] The footings are Matt 1:18, 20, 25 [L1/L2], Deut 22:23-24 [L1] and Luke 2:5 [L1]. Joseph was husband by betrothal-law and fathered the child legally by naming. The conception was already in Mary before they came together, and the text never gives it to him.

---

## Closing

The challenger needs Joseph's loins. The text gives David's loins. It never names Joseph as begetter, withholds "begat" at Matt 1:16, and puts the conception in Mary "of the Holy Ghost" before they came together.

**Answer: No. Christ had no human biological father.**
