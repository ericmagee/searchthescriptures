CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

# The Challenge: the text names the father, and the seed comes out of a man's body

**Answer to the topic.** Yes. Christ had a biological father, and the text names him: Joseph, of the house of David. The promise requires seed out of a man's body, and the record names the man. Below I press the declared chain link by link.

---

## Against LINK 1 — the passive at Matthew 1:16

[L1] The row reads: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matthew 1:16). It names the birth out of Mary; it does not narrate the begetting. Silence about one act is not denial of it.

The chorus does name the father, in four registers:
- Mary's own mouth: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48).
- The narrator: "the parents brought in the child Jesus" (Luke 2:27); "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33); "his parents went to Jerusalem" (Luke 2:41).
- The town: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22); "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" (John 6:42).
- A disciple: "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45).
- The angel: "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20).

[L2] "as was supposed" is enomizeto (was reckoned) — the same verb form as Acts 16:13, "where prayer was wont to be made." The word carries no verdict of its own; each context decides. And Luke 3:23 assigns the whole line to Joseph: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli."

[L3] Reckoning is by the father's house: "by the house of their fathers... every male by their polls" (Numbers 1:2); "declared their pedigrees... by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:18). That is how descent becomes evident: "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14).

[L4] One verb that declines to narrate an act cannot overturn five registers naming the actor (footings: the rows above at [L1], and the nomizo form at [L2]).

## Against LINK 2 — "of the Holy Ghost" and the angel's verbs

[L2] ek (out of) plus God or Spirit is origin-of-covenant language, never biological paternity: "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 5:1). When the text marks biological flesh it says kata sarka (according to flesh): "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3). Matthew 1:20 has no kata sarka — the qualifier that marks biological origin is absent there and present where the flesh is named.

[L2] The angel's two verbs do not denote begetting anywhere: eperchomai (come upon) is the word of Acts 1:8, "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you" — of the apostles, with the power of Luke 24:49; episkiazō (overshadow) is the word of Luke 9:34, the cloud at the holy mount, and of Acts 5:15, Peter's shadow. Presence and power, at every row.

[L1] The timing rows, read together: "before they came together, she was found with child" (Matthew 1:18) dates the finding; "Joseph her husband" (1:19), "Mary thy wife" (1:20) name the marriage, and the law calls a betrothed woman "his neighbour's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:24); "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (1:25) bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy — "till," and the defender's own gloss agrees ("until the birth"). Mary's question is present tense: "seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34) — her state when the angel spoke. The canon's stated means of begetting is knowing: "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1).

[L4] So the record reads: Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal, and the child is of his loins (footings: Matthew 1:19, 1:20, 1:25 with Deuteronomy 22:24 at [L1]; the covenant-origin pattern at [L2]). The Spirit is named as the child's covenant origin, exactly as believers are "born of God" (John 1:13) — and that the Spirit is not a bodily sire is what Luke 24:39, "a spirit hath not flesh and bones," already tells us.

## Against LINK 3 — "her seed" and the young woman

[L2] Isaiah 7:14 reads ha-almah (the young woman); the word for a virgin, bethulah, is not used there. The 1611 margin reads "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call."

[L1] The sign is bounded in Isaiah's own day: "before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken of both her kings" (Isaiah 7:16); "before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother" (Isaiah 8:4); "O Immanuel" recurs at Isaiah 8:8.

[L1] "her seed" (Genesis 3:15) is seed — and the Davidic promise puts that seed in the man's body: "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12); "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne" (Psalm 132:11); "kings shall come out of thy loins" (Genesis 35:11). "Her seed" is one of four woman-seed rows in the offspring sense (Genesis 16:10; 24:60; Isaiah 54:3) and none of the four is the throne seed.

[L4] A woman's seed does not delete the man; the throne rows name the man's bowels and loins (footings: [L1] the seed rows with Genesis 3:15; [L2] almah at Isaiah 7:14).

## Against LINK 4 — the curse and "Mary's line"

[L1] The curse is never laid on this child's throne: Jechonias appears in the New Testament at Matthew 1:11-12, "Jechonias begat Salathiel," and no row cites Jeremiah 22:30 against Jesus. The signet that was plucked (Jeremiah 22:24) is set on another: "Zerubbabel... the son of Shealtiel" (Haggai 2:23) — governor of Judah (Haggai 1:1; Ezra 3:2), never king. And Jeremiah carries the rule for a pronounced sentence: "If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil that I thought to do unto them" (Jeremiah 18:8). Jeconiah's sons are named at 1 Chronicles 3:17-18.

[L1] Luke 3:27 reads "Zorobabel, which was the son of Salathiel, which was the son of Neri" — the same two names; the text never says whether the same men, and the divergence is held open as written.

[L1] The phrase "Mary's line through Nathan" is not in the text. Luke 3:23 gives the line to Joseph: "the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli." Mary's lineage is never named; Luke 1:36 gives her a kinswoman, Elisabeth, "of the daughters of Aaron" (Luke 1:5), and says nothing of Mary's own descent. The reckoning rule runs by the father's house (Numbers 1:2, 18), and the inverse rows confirm it: those who "could not shew their father's house" were excluded (Ezra 2:59), and the priest called by the wife's house was "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61-62).

[L4] The throne promise runs to the child through the named house: Joseph "was of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4), the angel addresses him "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20), and the promise is "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32) (footings: [L1] these rows with Numbers 1:2, 18; [L3] Ezra 2:59, 61-62).

## Against LINK 5 — the father-rows waved off as opinion

[L1] Luke 2:48 is direct speech of Mary to the child, and the child's answer is about his business: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" (Luke 2:49) — no refusal of Joseph stands in the verse beside "thy father and I." Mary herself, who received the angel's words, calls Joseph "thy father."

[L1] The narrator names Joseph beside the mother (Luke 2:27, 33, 41), not as rumor.

[L2] Rom 1:3 and Acts 2:30 are answered by their own words: "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30) — the loins-word of Hebrews 7:5, 10 — and "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3). Loins and bowels are a man's body. The reply that these passages "do not say the descent came through a male parent" cannot stand beside the words "his loins" and "thy bowels."

[L1] "made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4) is the mortality idiom: "man that is born of a woman is of few days" (Job 14:1; Job 15:14; 25:4). It is said also of John, "Among them that are born of women" (Matthew 11:11), whose father is named (Luke 1:13). It never adds "and not of a man."

## Against LINK 6 — the keystone

[L1] The keystone says no human father. Set beside it: "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30); "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3); "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14).

[L3] Put the question the text invites: how is it evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda, given the law's reckoning — "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18)? Descent evident by the father's house requires a father whose house is Judah's.

[L2] "Son of God" is the covenant title and does not settle the question: God says of Solomon, "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14) — and Solomon's father is David: "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6). Luke 1:35 gives the title as a naming: "shall be called the Son of God."

[L4] The seed rows require seed out of a man's loins, and the stated means of begetting is knowing (Genesis 4:1); Joseph and Mary were husband and wife from the espousal (Matthew 1:19, 1:20; Deuteronomy 22:24), and 1:25 bounds the abstinence to the birth. Therefore the child is of Joseph's loins (footings: [L1] 2 Samuel 7:12, Acts 2:30, Matthew 1:25, Genesis 4:1; [L2] ek spermatos with kata sarka at Romans 1:3).

## UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES

1. **LINK 3, verbatim:** "A promise of the woman's seed, joined to the virgin conceiving, shows that the prophecy points to a conception without a male contributor." — The exhibited rows say "her seed" and "conceive." The Davidic seed rows say the opposite about the body: "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30). The exclusion is the debater's, running against those rows.

2. **LINK 4, verbatim:** "so a biological sonship from Joseph would conflict with Jer 22:30." — Set beside Jeremiah 18:8 ("I will repent of the evil"), 1 Chronicles 3:17-18 (his sons), and Haggai 2:23 (the signet set on Zerubbabel), and beside the fact that no row applies the curse to this throne.

3. **LINK 4, verbatim:** "Jesus is therefore of David by Mary's line (Luke 3:23-31)." — Luke 3:23 assigns the line to Joseph: "the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli." No row names Mary's lineage.

4. **LINK 5, verbatim:** "They carry the same 'supposed' reckoning as Luke 3:23, so they report opinion, not the begetting." — Set beside Luke 2:48 (Mary's own mouth: "thy father and I") and Acts 16:13 (the same verb form, "where prayer was wont to be made," carrying no verdict).

5. **LINK 2, verbatim:** "A human sire is excluded, and no divine 'biological' act in a physical sense is implied." — The affirmative rows name the sire's body (Acts 2:30, "the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh"); ek plus Spirit is covenant origin at its every row (John 1:13; 1 John 5:1), which is why kata sarka appears at Romans 1:3 and not at Matthew 1:20.

6. **LINK 6, verbatim:** "Joseph was his husband-guardian and supposed father." — The rows say "Joseph her husband" (Matthew 1:19), "Mary thy wife" (1:20), "his neighbour's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:24), and 1:25 bounds the abstinence by "till."
