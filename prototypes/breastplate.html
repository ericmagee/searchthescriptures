Stone names follow the KJV of Exodus 28:17–20. The text does not give the stones’ colors; the colors shown are display choices.

Light and color

  • White light holds every color. Sunlight is a continuous spectrum from about 380 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red); a prism spreads it into its colors.
  • Light adds. Overlapping beams of colored light add together; the whole spectrum, or as few as three colors (red, green and blue), is seen as white.
  • Stones filter. A gemstone makes no light of its own. It looks colored because it absorbs some wavelengths and passes or reflects the rest. Light sent through several colored stones in turn grows darker; stones set side by side do not, and from a distance their colors blend in the eye toward an average.
  • Lamps fill the gaps. Common white LEDs leave gaps in the blue-green and deep red. Better lamps mix several narrow colored channels to come closer to natural daylight.
A comparison, not a claim of the text. The breastplate’s twelve names and the Urim, “lights”, invite reflection alongside how many colors relate to one white light. The animation above shows colored light adding to white as an illustration; the stones themselves filter light and do not make it.
