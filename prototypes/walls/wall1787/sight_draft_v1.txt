SUMMARY:
ROWS FIRST, each pulled live. (1) Isaiah 7:14 "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son" is almah (H5959, lexicon "young woman, maid, virgin"), 7 rows: Genesis 24:43; Exodus 2:8 "the maid went"; Psalm 68:25 "the damsels"; Proverbs 30:19 "the way of a man with a maid"; Song of Solomon 1:3; 6:8; Isaiah 7:14 (margin "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call"). The sign carries its own clock (7:16; 8:3, "I went [Heb. approached] unto the prophetess; and she conceived"). (2) Genesis 24:16 "a virgin, neither had any man known her" is bethulah (H1330, lexicon "a virgin (from her privacy); sometimes (by continuation) a bride"), 50 rows (50 verses); 24:43 calls the same Rebekah almah. (3) Matthew 1:23 "Behold, a virgin shall be with child" is parthenos (G3933, lexicon "a maiden; by implication, an unmarried daughter"), 14 tokens. THE CLAUSE ROWS: bethulah + "neither had any man known her" (Genesis 24:16); bethulah + "that had known no man by lying with any male" (Judges 21:12); bethulah + "which hath had no husband" (Leviticus 21:3); parthenos + "not defiled with women", said of men (Revelation 14:4). THE HUSBAND ROW: "Lament like a virgin [bethulah] girded with sackcloth for the husband of her youth" (Joel 1:8). THE STATION ROWS: bethulah paired with "young men" (e.g. Deuteronomy 32:25; Psalm 148:12; Lamentations 2:21); named of a city or people ("the virgin of Israel," Jeremiah 31:4; Amos 5:2); a marriage status in the law ("a virgin be betrothed unto an husband," Deuteronomy 22:23; "the dowry of virgins," Exodus 22:17). parthenos the same: "difference ... between a wife and a virgin" (1 Corinthians 7:34); "if a virgin marry" (7:28); "a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph" (Luke 1:27). [L4, labelled] On these rows each noun names a woman's station, and where the text means that no man had known her it writes that in a clause beside the noun. So the chain "Matthew's parthenos, defined by Genesis 24:16" carries a clause that neither almah nor parthenos carries. The bridge from the Greek word of Matthew 1:23 to the Hebrew row of Genesis 24:16 runs only through the Greek translation of the Hebrew scriptures, which the record does not weigh (DG-52); through Rebekah the link is one woman under two words, a cross-reference, not a definition of almah. WHAT THIS SIGHT DOES NOT TOUCH: Matthew 1:18 "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost," 1:20, and 1:25 "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" stand as written (DG-59). The record forbids "no man was involved" and forbids narrating the how (DG-8; Sight 1578); this sight writes neither.

HISTORY (CONTEXT ONLY -- NOT L1, NOT L2, NOT GROUND; it authorizes no claim and no conclusion rests on it; DG-49, DG-52, DG-62): Outside histories (carried, not pulled; source: the screenshot shared in the operator's thread 2026-10-26, citing Reddit and Jews for Judaism) say that Jewish translators in Alexandria rendered the Hebrew scriptures into Greek before the New Testament, a version called the Septuagint, and that it renders Isaiah 7:14's almah as parthenos; the same sources say Matthew quoted that Greek version. The record weighs none of this: Sight 1578 struck "Matthew quotes the Septuagint verbatim" as off the floor, and the record's authority is the received Hebrew and the received Greek. It is recorded here so that a reader, or a judge who already carries this history, can see where the parthenos-to-Genesis-24:16 bridge comes from and why it is not ground.
