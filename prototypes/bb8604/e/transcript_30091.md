## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession". It adds: "A charge is stronger than its quote only when it asserts what the quote does not: a negation, a universal (all, none, only, never), or a claim the quote's own words do not make. Dropping, reordering or paraphrasing the quote's words is not stronger. A limb the link round narrows counts in its narrowed form." and "A premise's quote is checked against every earlier round of the opponent; it is verbatim if found in any of them, and the judge names that round."
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Defender (Claude)

DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

### Opening: Christ had no biological father

**Scope.** "Biological father" means a human male whose seed contributed to the conception. The standard reading is that Christ had none. His conception was by the Holy Ghost in a virgin, and Joseph was his reputed father only. The case is a chain, not a single verse.

#### I. The conception is stated as "of the Holy Ghost," not of a man

**[L1]** "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:18). "That which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20).

**[L2]** The preposition is *ek* (out of, from as source), in *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of Spirit holy). The verb in 1:20 is *gennēthen* (that which has been begotten or conceived), a word the same chapter uses for a father's begetting. Matthew gives the Holy Ghost, not Joseph, as the source of the begetting.

**[L1]** Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."

**[L2]** *Episkiazō* (to overshadow, cast a shade over) is the cloud-language of Exodus 40:35 (LXX) and Luke 9:34. It describes God's presence acting, and it is not a euphemism for ordinary generation. *Dio kai* (wherefore also) makes the divine conception the cause of the Sonship named.

#### II. Mary's virginity is stated before and after the conception

**[L1]** "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34). Joseph "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25).

**[L2]** *Ginōskō* (to know) is the Hebrew-idiom word for marital intimacy (*yādaʿ*, to know, in Genesis 4:1). Luke 1:34 uses *andra* (a man, a male) and the present tense, *ou ginōskō* (I do not know), which states her present state. Matthew 1:25 has *eōs hou* (until which), which marks the point up to which the sentence speaks.

**[L3]** Isaiah 7:14, "a virgin shall conceive," is quoted in Matthew 1:22-23 with *parthenos* (virgin). The Hebrew is *ʿalmāh* (a young woman of marriageable age). Genesis 24:43 uses *ʿalmāh* of Rebekah, whom 24:16 calls *bethûlāh* (virgin) and describes as one "neither had any man known her." The usage supports the virgin sense, and Matthew's *parthenos* fixes it.

#### III. Matthew's genealogy breaks its own pattern at the end

**[L1]** Matthew 1:2-16 repeats "begat" from Abraham to Jacob: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (1:16).

**[L2]** The refrain is *egennēsen* (begat), a masculine active verb. In verse 16 it stops. The text gives *Iōsēph ho anēr Marias* (Joseph the husband of Mary), then *ex hēs* (out of whom), where *hēs* is feminine singular, so only Mary is the source. The verb is passive, *egennēthē* (was begotten or born).

**[L3]** Luke 3:23 says Jesus was "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph." *Enomizeto* (was reckoned, was thought by custom) separates the opinion from the fact. John 6:42 shows the crowd holding that supposition: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?"

#### IV. Joseph and Jesus both act as if Joseph were not the father

**[L1]** Joseph was "minded to put her away privily" (Matthew 1:19) until the angel said, "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" (1:20). In the temple Mary says, "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing," and Jesus answers, "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" (Luke 2:48-49).

**[L4]** Footings: Matthew 1:18-20 says the pregnancy preceded their coming together, and Luke 2:48-49 has Jesus correcting "thy father" with "my Father's." Joseph's reaction would be unintelligible if he were the father, and Jesus assigns the title "Father" elsewhere. So the text itself treats Joseph's fatherhood as legal and not generative.

#### V. The flesh is traced through the woman

**[L3]** Genesis 3:15 speaks of "her seed" (*zeraʿ*, seed or offspring, of the woman). Galatians 4:4 says "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman" (*genomenon ek gynaikos*, coming to be out of a woman). Romans 1:3 says "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (*kata sarka*).

**[L4]** Footings: Galatians 4:4 names only the woman as the source of his coming-to-be, and Romans 1:3 limits the Davidic descent to "according to the flesh." Taken together, the biological line is through Mary, and the title "son of David" given to Joseph in Matthew 1:20 is legal.

#### DECLARED CHAIN -- the links your case stands on

1. The text names the Holy Ghost as the source of the conception. This rests on Matthew 1:18, 20 [L1] with *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of Spirit holy) and *gennēthen* (begotten) [L2], and on Luke 1:35, "therefore also… the Son of God" [L1], with *episkiazō* (to overshadow) [L2].
2. Mary was a virgin when she conceived and remained so until the birth. This rests on Luke 1:34 and Matthew 1:25 [L1] with *ginōskō* (to know, of marital intimacy) [L2], and on Isaiah 7:14 with Genesis 24:16, 43 and Matthew 1:23 [L3].
3. Matthew 1:16 deliberately severs the begetting line at Joseph. This rests on Matthew 1:2-16 [L1] with the masculine *egennēsen* (begat) giving way to the feminine *ex hēs* (out of whom) [L2].
4. Joseph is called father only by supposition or custom. This rests on Luke 3:23 [L1] with *enomizeto* (was reckoned) [L2], and on John 6:42 and Luke 2:48-49 [L3].
5. The flesh of Christ is traced through the woman alone. This rests on Galatians 4:4 and Romans 1:3 [L1] with *genomenon ek gynaikos* (coming to be out of a woman) and *kata sarka* (according to the flesh) [L2], and on Genesis 3:15 [L3].
6. Christ had no biological father: no human male contributed to his conception, which came by the Holy Ghost in the virgin Mary.

## Round 1 - Challenger (MiMo)

CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

### Rebuttal: the chain asserts what no verse states

The keystone (D6) says "no human male contributed to his conception." That sentence is not in the text. It is an inference standing on D1, and D1's own rows do not carry it. I press the links in order.

---

#### D1 — "of the Holy Ghost" names the child's origin claim, not a begetting that excludes a man

The rows stand as written: "she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:18); "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20) [L1]. But *ek* (out of) plus God or Spirit is the covenant-origin idiom everywhere else in the New Testament: "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 3:9; 5:1), "is of God" (1 John 4:7) [L2]. Where the text means flesh-birth it adds the marker: "made of the seed of David **according to the flesh** (*kata sarka*, according to flesh)" (Romans 1:3) [L1-L2]. Matthew 1:20 has no such marker. So the row names the child God claims; it never says "not Joseph."

Luke 1:35's two verbs denote begetting at no row in the canon: "come upon" (*eperchomai*, to come upon) is "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you, and ye shall be witnesses" (Acts 1:8) — the apostles' anointing; "overshadow" (*episkiazō*, to overshadow) is the cloud of the mount (Luke 9:34; Matthew 17:5) and Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15) [L1-L3]. And "shall be **called** the Son of God" (Luke 1:35) is a title given — the same title given to Solomon while David begat him: "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6); "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14) [L1]. The title with the throne is given at Luke 1:32 **before** Mary's question at 1:34. *Dio kai* (wherefore also) cannot carry divine paternity.

#### D2 — the virginity rows are bounded rows, and bounded rows cannot carry D6

"Joseph knew her not **till** she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25) [L1] bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy and speaks of no time after. "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34) is present indicative (*andra ou ginōskō*, a man I do not know) [L2] — her state when the angel spoke, not a vow. So D2 establishes abstinence during the pregnancy. It cannot establish what D6 needs.

And the appeal to Isaiah's Hebrew undercuts D2 rather than supporting it: the word at Isaiah 7:14 is *ha-almah* (H5959, young woman), while the word for a virgin, *bethulah* (H1330), is used in fifty rows and is not used there; the 1611 margin reads "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call." The sign in Isaiah's own section is bounded by "before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good" (Isaiah 7:16) and by the child of 8:3-4, with "Immanuel" at 8:8 in Isaiah's day [L1-L2]. Matthew's use of the word is not in question; what is in question is D2's claim that the Hebrew proves more than the mother's own present-tense sentence and a bounded "till."

#### D3 — "of whom was born" is who bore; it does not answer who begat

Matthew 1:16 reads "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus" [L1]. Joseph is placed **inside** the begat line; then the passive *egennēthē* (was born) with *ex hēs* (out of whom, feminine) names the one who bore [L2]. That is non-narration of the begetting, not a denial of one. Silence is not a statement.

And the reckoning is the father's, all through: "by the house of their fathers... every male by their polls" (Numbers 1:2, 18); "they could not shew their father's house" — excluded (Ezra 2:59); priests "called after the name" of the wife's house — "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61-62) [L1]. Luke traces the same way: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" (Luke 3:23) [L1]. If the flesh ran through Mary alone, the canon would need a genealogy of Mary. There is none — the only kin named for her is Elisabeth, "of the daughters of Aaron" (Luke 1:5, 36) [L1].

#### D4 — Joseph is called father in four registers, not one supposition

Mary's own mouth: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48). The narrator: "the parents brought in the child Jesus" (Luke 2:27), "his parents went to Jerusalem" (2:41), "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (2:33, 43). The town: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22); "whose father and mother we know" (John 6:42). A disciple: "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45). The angel: "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20) [L1]. Against that chorus D4 sets one participle, *enomizeto* (was reckoned) — the same form as "where prayer **was wont** to be made" (Acts 16:13), a word carrying no verdict of its own [L2].

On Matthew 1:19: at the word level it is *dikaios ōn... eboulēthē lathra apolysai autēn* (being just... he was minded to put her away privily) — intent and two grounds, no verb of knowledge [L2]. And the angel's word grounds the **taking**: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: **for** that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20) — then "**thou** shalt call his name JESUS" (1:21), done at 1:25, the father's naming act, as Zacharias names John (Luke 1:13, 62-63) [L1].

#### D5 — "of a woman" is the mortality idiom, and it never means "and of no man"

"made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4) is spoken beside "made under the law"; the idiom is "man that is born of a woman is of few days" (Job 14:1; 15:14; 25:4) [L1]. Its clearest row is "them that are born of women" (Matthew 11:11) — of John, whose father is named and struck dumb (Luke 1:13, 57-63) [L1-L3]. So "of a woman" excludes no father.

Worse for D5: no text ever calls Mary David's seed. The promised flesh is "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3), "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30), "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12), "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14) [L1]. Joseph is "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4; 1:27), and the throne is "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32) [L1]. Route the flesh through Mary alone and the Davidic seed stands with no stated line at all.

#### D6 — the keystone fails: the seed rows state the contrary

The canon's stated means of begetting is knowing: "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1) [L1]. Joseph and Mary were husband and wife in law from the espousal: "Joseph her husband" (Matthew 1:19), "Mary thy wife" (1:20), and the law calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:24) [L1]. "Knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25) bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy [L1]. Therefore, at inference level footed on those three rows [L4, footed on Matthew 1:19, 20, 25 and Deuteronomy 22:24 (L1) with Genesis 4:1 (L1)]: Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal, and the child is of his loins — which is exactly what "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30) requires [L1-L2].

D6's sentence — "no human male contributed" — is stated by no verse, and these rows state the contrary about the same matter.

---

#### Premises I press, with the defender's words

- **D1 [L2]:** "Matthew gives the Holy Ghost, not Joseph, as the source of the begetting." The row says "of the Holy Ghost" and never says "not Joseph"; *ek* with God is covenant origin at John 1:13, 1 John 3:9, 5:1, 4:7, and the flesh-marker *kata sarka* (according to flesh) is absent from Matthew 1:20 but present at Romans 1:3.
- **D3 [L2]:** "so only Mary is the source." "Of whom was born" names who bore; the father's reckoning runs through Joseph (Numbers 1:2, 18; Luke 3:23), and no genealogy of Mary exists.
- **D4 [L4]:** "Joseph's reaction would be unintelligible if he were the father." Matthew 1:19 states intent and two grounds and no knowledge-verb; the angel's "for" grounds the taking of "Mary thy wife."
- **D5 [L4]:** "the biological line is through Mary." No text states Mary's Davidic descent; the flesh-seed rows run out of a man's bowels and loins (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30).
- **D6 [L4]:** "no human male contributed to his conception." No row states it; Genesis 4:1, Matthew 1:19-20, 25 and Deuteronomy 22:24 state the union of husband and wife.

## Round 2 - Defender (Claude)

DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

### Round 2: the chain stands, and the challenger's own rows close the gap

The challenger says no verse states "no human male contributed." The same standard applies to the keystone he proposes: no verse states "Joseph begat Jesus." Matthew 1:2-16 uses "begat" link after link and drops it where Joseph is named. Under DD-1, each term the challenger isolates goes back into its chain.

#### D1: the source of the conception

**[L1]** "Before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:18). "Fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20).

**[L2]** The challenger's own cross-reference works against him. John 1:13 says believers were born "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." Here *ek theou* (out of God) is set explicitly against *ek thelēmatos andros* (out of the will of a male). So the idiom he cites sets God's begetting against a man's. In Matthew 1:20 the participle is neuter, *to gennēthen* (that which has been begotten), and the source is *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of Spirit holy). **[L3]** That is the same contrast with a man's begetting, applied literally to the child. The absence of *kata sarka* (according to flesh) does not help him. Romans 1:3 adds that phrase because it names a second line, David's, and Galatians 4:4 shows how that line arrives, through *ek gynaikos* (out of a woman).

**[L1]** On Luke 1:35, the challenger says "Son of God" is a title, as with Solomon. But the verse is built on *dio kai* (wherefore also, for which reason): "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee... therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." The verse gives a cause, and in 2 Samuel 7:14 and 1 Chronicles 28:6 no "therefore" attaches the title to an act of conception. Luke 1:32 gives the title first, and Mary's question follows at 1:34, *pōs estai touto* (how shall this be?). Verse 35 is the answer to that how, and it names the Holy Ghost as agent, not Joseph.

#### D2: virginity, state and answer

**[L1]** Luke 1:27 calls Mary *parthenon* (a virgin) twice, "a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph; and the virgin's name was Mary." **[L2]** In Luke 1:34, *ou ginōskō* (I do not know) is a present state, as the challenger says. I need only the state, and I do not need a vow. She is told "thou shalt conceive" (1:31) and asks how it can happen in her present state. **[L4]** Footings: Luke 1:31 puts the conception ahead of her, 1:27 names her espoused to Joseph, and 1:35 answers with the Holy Ghost and 1:37 with "with God nothing shall be impossible." If conception by her betrothed were meant, the question would have no object and the answer would be no answer.

**[L3]** On Isaiah 7:14, Isaiah 7:11 frames the sign as something "in the depth, or in the height above," and 7:14 calls it a *ʾôt* (sign, wonder) given by "the Lord himself." An ordinary birth is not that kind of sign. Matthew 1:22-23 says "all this was done, that it might be fulfilled" (*hina plērōthē*, in order that it might be filled up), and the "this" it points back to is the conception of 1:18-20, the very thing the challenger wants to make ordinary. He grants that Matthew's use is not in question.

#### D3: the genealogy

**[L1-L2]** In Matthew 1:16 the pattern of *egennēsen* (begat) stops at Joseph, and the clause that follows is *ex hēs* (out of whom, feminine singular) with a passive verb. **[L3]** The genealogy has already named women, Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and "her that had been the wife of Urias" (Matthew 1:3, 5, 6), and in each case the male line is carried by the verb "begat." Verse 16 is the only place where the verb is withheld from the husband. That is a deliberate shift, and the challenger's rule that silence is not a statement cuts both ways.

On "no genealogy of Mary": **[L1]** Luke 1:32 promises Mary's child "the throne of his father David" in an annunciation addressed to Mary. Acts 2:30 says God swore to David that "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ." It names David's loins and does not name Joseph's. Hebrews 7:14 says "sprang out of Juda," which names the tribe and not Joseph. The rows the challenger cites for Davidic flesh do not name Joseph.

## D2: virginity, state and answer

I need only the state, and I do not need a vow. ## D3: the genealogy

Verse 16 is the only place where the verb is withheld from the husband. That is a deliberate shift, and the challenger's rule that silence is not a statement cuts both ways.

On "no genealogy of Mary": **[L1]** Luke 1:32 promises Mary's child "the throne of his father David" in an annunciation addressed to Mary. Acts 2:30 says God swore to David that "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ." It names David's loins and does not name Joseph's. Hebrews 7:14 says "sprang out of Juda," which names the tribe and not Joseph. The rows the challenger cites for Davidic flesh do not name Joseph.

**[L2-L3]** Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 share the same participle, *genomenou* and *genomenon* (coming to be), with the same preposition *ek* (out of). One reads "out of the seed of David according to the flesh," the other "out of a woman." **[L4]** Footings: both verses use the same verb of coming-to-be with the same preposition (L2), and Romans 1:3 limits the Davidic origin to flesh while Galatians 4:4 names the immediate source as the woman (L1). The inference is that the flesh descended from David reached him through the woman. The challenger needs "of a woman" to be only a mortality idiom, but in Galatians 4:4 it is paired with "God sent forth his Son," and the sending sets his origin apart from the ordinary run of those "born of women."

**[L4]** A further footing is Jeremiah 22:30 with Matthew 1:12. Jeremiah 22:30 says of Jechonias, "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." Matthew 1:11-12 places Jechonias in the line that reaches Joseph. Luke 1:32 gives the child that throne. If the child were of Joseph's seed, Jeremiah 22:30 would exclude him. ## D6: the challenger's keystone fails on his own rows

## D1 — John 1:13 is a birth of believers, and it excludes the woman's means too
