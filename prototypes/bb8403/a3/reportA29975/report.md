## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession". It adds: "A charge is stronger than its quote only when it asserts what the quote does not: a negation, a universal (all, none, only, never), or a claim the quote's own words do not make. Dropping, reordering or paraphrasing the quote's words is not stronger. A limb the link round narrows counts in its narrowed form." and "A premise's quote is checked against every earlier round of the opponent; it is verbatim if found in any of them, and the judge names that round."
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Defender (Claude)

DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

### Opening: Christ had no human biological father

**Standard reading defended:** Jesus was conceived in Mary by the Holy Ghost without a human father. Joseph was his father only by legal reckoning and by supposition.

#### 1. The conception is stated as "of the Holy Ghost," not of a man

[L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."

[L2] The word is γεννηθὲν (gennēthen, "begotten/conceived"). The source is ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (ek pneumatos hagiou, "out of Holy Spirit"). The preposition ἐκ (ek, "out of") names the origin. The same verb γεννάω (gennaō, "to beget") is used of a father throughout Matthew 1:2-16. In verses 18 and 20 it is attributed to the Holy Ghost, not to a man.

[L1] Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."

[L2] ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, "will overshadow") is a verb of divine presence. The Septuagint uses it for the cloud over the tabernacle in Exodus 40:35. Luke 1:35 gives the reason with διὸ καί (dio kai, "therefore also"). The child is called the Son of God because of this conception.

[L3] The two accounts, Matthew's from Joseph's side and Luke's from Mary's side, are independent. They agree on the one source.

#### 2. The genealogy breaks its own pattern at Joseph

[L1] Matthew 1:16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."

[L2] Fifteen times Matthew writes ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen, "begat") with a masculine father as subject. At verse 16 the pattern stops. Joseph is called ἀνὴρ (anēr, "husband/man") of Mary. Jesus was ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē, "was born") ἐξ ἧς (ex hēs, "out of whom"). The relative pronoun ἧς (hēs, "whom") is **feminine singular**, so it refers to Mary alone. Had Joseph been the father, the text would have said "Joseph begat Jesus," as it says of every other name.

[L1] Luke 3:23: "Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph."

[L2] ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "was thought/reckoned") marks a belief held by others, not a fact stated by the narrator.

#### 3. Mary had no relations with a man

[L1] Luke 1:34: "Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"

[L2] ἄνδρα (andra, "a man") is the word for a male person. γινώσκω (ginōskō, "I know") is the same word used for marital knowledge in Matthew 1:25. The present tense says that at that time she had no such relationship, and the angel's answer in Luke 1:35 does not correct her premise.

[L1] Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son."

[L2] ἐγίνωσκεν (eginōsken, "was knowing") is imperfect and denotes a continuing state. ἕως οὗ (heōs hou, "until which time") limits it to the period through the birth.

[L1] Matthew 1:22-23: "Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son," quoting Isaiah 7:14.

[L2] Matthew uses παρθένος (parthenos, "virgin"). Isaiah has הָעַלְמָה (ha-almah, "the young woman of marriageable age"). [L3] Genesis 24:43 uses the same noun עַלְמָה (almah, "young woman") of Rebekah. Genesis 24:16 describes her as "a virgin, neither had any man known her." Matthew's inspired rendering fixes the sense for this case.

#### 4. "Made of a woman" with no man named

[L1] Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law."

[L2] γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός (genomenon ek gynaikos, "come to be out of a woman") names only the woman. Paul does not name a human father even though he is stressing Christ's humanity.

[L3] Genesis 3:15: "the seed of the woman." The woman's seed is the term used, not the man's. [L3] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me." The Greek is σῶμα (sōma, "body"), and the verb is κατηρτίσω (katērtisō, "thou hast fitted/prepared"), attributed to God.

#### 5. Joseph's fatherhood is legal and supposed, and the child's own words point elsewhere

[L1] Matthew 1:20: "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife." Verse 21: "she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS." Verse 25: "he called his name JESUS."

[L2] Joseph is told to take (παραλαβεῖν, paralabein, "to receive to oneself") and to call the name. He is not told he is the begetter. Naming the child is the act of adopting him as heir.

[L1] Luke 2:48-49: "Thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." The child answered: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"

[L2] Mary's πατήρ σου (patēr sou, "thy father") is the household usage. The child's answer redirects the word πατρός μου (patros mou, "of my Father") to God.

[L1] John 6:42: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" This is the voice of unbelieving hearers, not narrator testimony.

[L1] Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh." [L3] Luke 1:32 promises "the throne of his father David" in the same announcement that rules out a man (Luke 1:34-35). Descent from David runs through Mary, with Joseph's legal standing giving the right. [L3] Matthew 1:12 places Joseph in the line of Jechonias, and Jeremiah 22:30 says "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." This is consistent with a legal, not a biological, passing of the title through Joseph.

Footing: [L1] Galatians 4:4 and Hebrews 10:5, with [L2] ἐκ γυναικός (ek gynaikos, "out of a woman") and κατηρτίσω (katērtisō, "thou hast prepared"), and [L3] Genesis 3:15, "the seed of the woman."

Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:20-25, Luke 2:48-49 and Luke 3:23, with [L2] παραλαβεῖν (paralabein, "to receive to oneself") and ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "was thought"), and [L3] Romans 1:3 and Luke 1:32 for the Davidic line through the woman.

**The single point both chains turn on:** whether Matthew 1:18-25's order closes the window. The challenger has the marriage, the means, and the registers; the defender has the sequence. Both were exhibited at every layer required. Neither was withdrawn. I do not resolve it.
