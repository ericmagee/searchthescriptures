CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED**

[L1] Matthew 1:18: “Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost.”

[L1] Matthew 1:19–20: “Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily. But while he thought on these things, behold, the angel of the Lord appeared unto him in a dream, saying, Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost.”

[L1] Matthew 1:24–25: “Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife: And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS.”

[L3] Deuteronomy 22:24 calls an espoused woman “his neighbour’s wife.” Genesis 4:1 says, “And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD.”

[L4] The chain is that Joseph and Mary are called husband and wife during the espousal; Matthew dates the finding before their coming together and states the abstinence after Joseph took her. Together with the named Davidic father and seed in link 3, I infer that Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal. That timing is an inference, not a separately narrated act.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED**

[L1] Luke 2:48–49: “And when they saw him, they were amazed: and his mother said unto him, Son, why hast thou thus dealt with us? behold, thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing. And he said unto them, How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father’s business?”

[L3] 1 Chronicles 28:6: “And he said unto me, Solomon thy son, he shall build my house and my courts: for I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father.”

[L4] The parallel supports the limited premise: God calls Solomon his son while also calling him David’s son. It shows that divine and human father-language can stand together; it does not by itself establish Joseph’s biological fatherhood.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED**

[L1] Romans 1:3: “Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh;”

[L1] Luke 2:4: “And Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth, into Judaea, unto the city of David, which is called Bethlehem; (because he was of the house and lineage of David:)”

[L3] Acts 2:30: “Therefore being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him, that of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne;” Luke 2:48 calls Joseph “thy father.”

[L1] Matthew 1:16: “And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ.”

[L1] Galatians 4:4: “But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,”

[L3] Matthew 11:11 speaks of John among those “born of women”; Luke 1:13 says, “thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son.”

[L4] The inference joins the fleshly seed of David to Joseph, the named father of David’s lineage. Matthew’s birth wording and Galatians’ “made of a woman” state birth from Mary; the John comparison shows that the latter wording does not itself decide the father question.

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED**

[L1] Luke 1:31: “And, behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name JESUS.”

[L1] Luke 1:34–35: “Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man? And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God.”

[L3] Acts 1:8: “But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.”

[L3] Luke 9:34: “While he thus spake, there came a cloud, and overshadowed them: and they feared as they entered into the cloud.”

[L4] The premise says the parallels show that “come upon” and “overshadow” are **also** used of power and presence. They support that claim; they do not remove Luke’s promise of conception or assert that Luke’s verbs mean power and presence *only*.

**LINK 5: SUPPORTED**

[L1] Jeremiah 22:30: “Thus saith the LORD, Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper in his days: for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah.”

[L3] Haggai 2:23: “In that day, saith the LORD of hosts, will I take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant, the son of Shealtiel, saith the LORD, and will make thee as a signet: for I have chosen thee, saith the LORD of hosts.”

[L3] Luke 1:32–33: “He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David: And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.”

[L4] The premise is supported at its stated scope: Jeremiah includes “in his days” concerning the man and “in Judah” concerning the rule. I do not infer that “in his days” grammatically limits the seed clause. Haggai’s signet language and Luke’s throne promise are additional witnesses; neither explicitly says Jeremiah’s sentence was revoked.
