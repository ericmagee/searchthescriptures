CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

## Challenge

**The disputed step is not whether Jesus was born of Mary or whether Matthew says the conception was “of the Holy Ghost.” Both are written. The dispute is whether those words exclude Joseph as the biological father.** [L4]

### LINK 1 — The genealogy’s change of wording

[L1] Matthew 1:16 says: “And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ.”

[L2] “Was born” is passive, and “of whom” refers grammatically to Mary. The verse names her as the one from whom Jesus was born.

[L3] But Matthew 1:20 also names Joseph: “Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost.”

[L4] The passive in verse 16 does not itself say Joseph did not beget Jesus. Naming Mary in the birth statement is not the same as excluding Joseph from the begetting.

### LINK 2 — “Of the Holy Ghost” and the timing

[L1] Matthew 1:18: “Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost.”

[L1] Matthew 1:25: “And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS.”

[L3] Matthew 1:19 calls him “Joseph her husband”; verse 20 calls her “Mary thy wife.” Deuteronomy 22:24 likewise calls an espoused woman “his neighbour’s wife.”

[L3] Genesis 4:1 states the means of begetting: “And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD.”

[L4] Read together, Matthew dates the **finding** before they came together and reports that Joseph did not know Mary through the birth after taking her as his wife. The inference pressed here is that Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal. That inference is not a quotation from Matthew; it rests on the named husband and wife, the timing words, and Genesis’s account of knowing and conception.

[L3] The words “of the Holy Ghost” remain in Matthew 1:18 and 20. Compare 1 John 5:1: “Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is born of God: and every one that loveth him that begat loveth him also that is begotten of him.”

[L4] Being “of” God does not by itself establish biological divine paternity. The defender must show that Matthew’s words exclude the named husband, not merely repeat that the conception is “of the Holy Ghost.”

### LINK 3 — Mary’s question and the angel’s answer

[L1] Luke 1:34: “Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?”

[L1] Luke 1:35: “And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God.”

[L2] “I know not” is present tense: Mary states her condition when she speaks.

[L3] Acts 1:8 says: “But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.”

[L3] Luke 9:34 says: “While he thus spake, there came a cloud, and overshadowed them: and they feared as they entered into the cloud.”

[L4] The parallel language of coming upon and overshadowing speaks of power and presence. Luke 1:35 names both the divine action and the title; those verbs do not themselves state that Joseph was excluded.

### LINK 4 — Joseph called father

[L1] Luke 2:48: “And when they saw him, they were amazed: and his mother said unto him, Son, why hast thou thus dealt with us? behold, thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing.”

[L1] Luke 2:49: “And he said unto them, How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father’s business?”

[L3] 1 Chronicles 28:6: “And he said unto me, Solomon thy son, he shall build my house and my courts: for I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father.”

[L4] God calls Solomon his son in the same sentence that calls Solomon David’s son. Therefore Jesus’ words about his Father’s business do not, by themselves, correct Mary’s “thy father.”

[L1] Luke 3:23 says: “And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli.”

[L3] Acts 16:13 uses the same verb form for what was customary: “And on the sabbath we went out of the city by a river side, where prayer was wont to be made; and we sat down, and spake unto the women which resorted thither.”

[L4] “As was supposed” reports how Joseph’s sonship was regarded; the word alone does not pronounce the supposition false.

### LINKS 5–6 — David’s seed and the named line

[L1] Romans 1:3: “Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh;”

[L3] Acts 2:30: “Therefore being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him, that of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne;”

[L3] Luke 2:4: “And Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth, into Judaea, unto the city of David, which is called Bethlehem; (because he was of the house and lineage of David:)”

[L4] The stated fleshly seed and the named Davidic father point through Joseph. The proposed Davidic line through Mary is not established by Luke 3:31: that register names David and traces to Joseph in verse 23; it does not name Mary in the genealogy.

## Unsupported conclusions pressed against the defender

1. **LINK 1:** “The genealogy therefore withholds the begetting from Joseph.” The passive states Mary’s bearing; it does not state that exclusion. [L1–L2]
2. **LINK 2:** “It places the pregnancy before any union.” Matthew 1:18 explicitly dates when she **was found** with child; the broader claim is an inference that must answer the espousal and husband-and-wife texts. [L1, L3, L4]
3. **LINK 3:** “Mary was a virgin at conception, and the angel names divine overshadowing as the cause.” Mary states her condition when she speaks; the parallel verbs name power and presence. The asserted exclusion requires another link. [L1–L3]
4. **LINK 4:** “Calling Joseph ‘father’ is household or legal usage and supposition, and the text corrects it.” Mary’s words and Jesus’ words stand together; 1 Chronicles 28:6 shows that divine and human father language can stand together. [L1, L3, L4]
5. **LINK 5:** “Jesus had real human descent through the woman alone.” Romans names David’s fleshly seed, and Luke names Joseph’s Davidic lineage; neither establishes the defender’s “alone.” [L1, L3, L4]
6. **LINK 6:** “Jesus had no biological human father.” This conclusion depends on the exclusions asserted in links 1–5. Those exclusions have not been established by the quoted words. [L1, L3, L4]
