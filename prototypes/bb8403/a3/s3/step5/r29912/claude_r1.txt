DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

# Opening: Jesus had no biological human father

**Position.** The KJV teaches that Jesus had a biological mother, Mary, and no biological human father. His conception was of the Holy Ghost. Joseph stood in the place of a father legally and in the household, and was not the begetter. [L4, resting on the chain below]

By "biological father" I mean the man who supplies the seed of begetting (Greek *gennaō*, to beget; Hebrew *yālad*, to bear or beget).

---

## I. Matthew 1: the genealogy breaks its own pattern

**1.** [L1] Matthew 1:2-15 runs "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob..." down to "Jacob begat Joseph." Verse 16 then reads: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom was born Jesus**, who is called Christ."

**2.** [L2] The verb "begat" throughout is *egennēsen* (he begat), active voice, with a man as subject. In verse 16 the verb changes to *egennēthē* (was born), passive voice. The relative pronoun is *hēs* (of whom), **feminine singular**, so the birth is "out of her" alone. Had Joseph been the begetter, the established formula "Joseph begat Jesus" was ready to hand. Matthew abandons it at the one place it would matter.

**3.** [L2] Joseph is called *andra* (husband, or man) of Mary. The word names his marital standing and not his fatherhood.

## II. Matthew 1:18-25: the source of the conception is stated

**4.** [L1] "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was found with child **of the Holy Ghost**" (Matthew 1:18).

**5.** [L2] *Prin ē sunelthein autous* means "before they came together". It places the pregnancy before any union. *Ek pneumatos hagiou* means "out of spirit holy": the source (*ek*, out of) of the conception is the Holy Ghost.

**6.** [L1] The angel tells Joseph: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20). Joseph had been "minded to put her away privily" (1:19). That reaction makes no sense if he were the father. [L4, resting on 1:19 and 1:20]

**7.** [L2] "That which is conceived" is *to gennēthen* (that which has been begotten or conceived), **neuter**, and the same *ek* (out of) is used. The angel assigns the begetting to the Holy Ghost and not to Joseph.

**8.** [L1] "[Joseph] knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25). [L2] *Ouk eginōsken* (was not knowing) is imperfect tense, a continued state. *Heōs hou* (until which) marks the time up to the birth, and it covers the whole conception and pregnancy.

## III. Luke 1: Mary's own testimony

**9.** [L1] Luke 1:27 calls Mary "a virgin" (*parthenon*, virgin). In 1:34 she asks: "How shall this be, **seeing I know not a man**?"

**10.** [L2] *Epei andra ou ginōskō* uses a present-tense verb, "I am not knowing a man". It is not a future vow. She states her present condition, and the angel's answer takes it as true.

**11.** [L1] The angel answers: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35).

**12.** [L2] *Episkiasei* (will overshadow) is the verb used for the cloud over the tabernacle in the LXX of Exodus 40:35. It signals divine action, not human union. *Dio kai* (therefore also) makes the divine act the stated ground for the title Son of God. [L3, Exodus 40:35 and Luke 1:35]

## IV. Isaiah 7:14 and "a woman"

**13.** [L1] Matthew 1:22-23 says the conception took place "that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son."

**14.** [L2] The Hebrew in Isaiah 7:14 is *ʿalmāh* (young woman of marriageable age). I grant that the Hebrew word does not by itself say "never touched". Matthew, however, quotes the Greek *parthenos* (virgin). The Spirit-given use of *ʿalmāh* for Rebekah in Genesis 24:43 sits beside *bĕtûlāh* (virgin) and "neither had any man known her" in 24:16. [L3, Genesis 24:16, 24:43]

**15.** [L1] "God sent forth his Son, **made of a woman**, made under the law" (Galatians 4:4). [L2] *Genomenon ek gunaikos* (coming to be out of a woman) names only the woman as source. [L3] This agrees with Genesis 3:15, "her seed" (Hebrew *zeraʿ*, seed), where the seed is credited to the woman.

## V. Biological descent runs through the mother

**16.** [L1] "[Jesus Christ] was made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**" (Romans 1:3). Acts 2:30 says "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ."

**17.** [L2] *Kata sarka* (according to flesh) is bodily descent. [L3] Luke 3:31 traces a line through Nathan, son of David. Jesus therefore had real human flesh and real Davidic descent, and this is carried by Mary. [L4, resting on Romans 1:3, Luke 3:31 and Matthew 1:16]

**18.** [L1] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me." [L2] *Katērtisō* (thou hast fitted or prepared) points to a provided body and not an inherited one.

## VI. Objections answered

**Objection: Luke 2:33, 2:48 and John 6:42 call Joseph his father.**
[L1] Luke 3:23 settles the usage: "being (as was **supposed**) the son of Joseph." [L2] *Hōs enomizeto* (as was thought or reckoned) marks it as opinion. In Luke 2:48 Mary says "thy father and I have sought thee," and the next verse has Jesus correct the title: "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?" (Luke 2:49). [L3] John 6:42 reports what the crowd says ("whose father and mother we know"), and Luke 3:23 corrects that report. In Luke 2:33, the narrator's "Joseph and his mother" is household language, the same as "his parents" (2:41), and 1:34-35 and 3:23 govern it. [L4, resting on Luke 3:23 and 2:49]

**Objection: Joseph's descent from David is needed for the throne.**
[L1] Matthew 1:20 calls Joseph "son of David," and Matthew 1:16 passes through Jechonias (1:11-12). [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 says of Coniah, "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." [L4] A begotten son of Joseph would be Coniah's seed, which would put the throne claim under that word. The virgin conception, with descent from David through Mary (Luke 3:31), avoids the difficulty. This is secondary support and not a load-bearing link. [L4, resting on Matthew 1:11-12, Jeremiah 22:30 and Luke 3:31]

## DECLARED CHAIN -- the links your case stands on

1. Matthew 1:16 drops the active "begat" (*egennēsen*, he begat) of verses 2-15 for the passive "was born" (*egennēthē*, was born) with the feminine "of whom" (*hēs*, of whom). The genealogy therefore withholds the begetting from Joseph. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:2-16, [L2] *egennēsen/egennēthē* and feminine *hēs*, [L3] contrast with the unbroken pattern of verses 2-15.
2. The source of the conception is stated as the Holy Ghost, before Joseph and Mary came together. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:18-20 and 1:25, [L2] *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of spirit holy), *prin ē sunelthein* (before coming together), neuter *to gennēthen* (that which is begotten), [L3] Matthew 1:20 and Luke 1:35 agree on the same source.
3. Mary was a virgin at conception, and the angel names divine overshadowing as the cause. Footing: [L1] Luke 1:27, 1:34-35, [L2] *parthenos* (virgin), present-tense *ou ginōskō* (I am not knowing), *episkiasei* (will overshadow), [L3] Isaiah 7:14 as quoted in Matthew 1:23, and Exodus 40:35.
4. Calling Joseph "father" is household or legal usage and supposition, and the text corrects it. Footing: [L1] Luke 3:23 and 2:48-49, [L2] *hōs enomizeto* (as was supposed), [L3] John 6:42 (the crowd's report) answered by Luke 3:23.
5. Jesus had real human descent through the woman alone: "made of a woman" and "of the seed of David according to the flesh." Footing: [L1] Galatians 4:4, Romans 1:3, [L2] *ek gunaikos* (out of a woman), *kata sarka* (according to flesh), [L3] Genesis 3:15, Luke 3:31, Acts 2:30.
6. Jesus had no biological human father. His conception was of the Holy Ghost, Mary was his only biological parent, and Joseph was not the begetter. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:16-25, Luke 1:34-35, Luke 3:23, [L2] *egennēthē*/*hēs*, *ek pneumatos hagiou*, *ou ginōskō*, *hōs enomizeto*, [L3] Galatians 4:4, Romans 1:3, Isaiah 7:14 with Matthew 1:23, carried through links 1-5.
