DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

# Round 2: Defense of the chain

The challenger agrees that Jesus was "born of Mary" and that the conception is "of the Holy Ghost". The dispute is whether those words exclude Joseph as begetter. The chain answers that they do, because the links bear on each other. The challenger has taken them one at a time, and I answer in the same order. Points the challenger did not touch (Matthew 1:19, Galatians 4:4, Hebrews 10:5, Genesis 3:15, Isaiah 7:14, Jeremiah 22:30) stand as held.

---

## LINK 1: Matthew 1:16 does not stand alone

**1.** [L1] The challenger says the passive "was born" does not itself exclude Joseph. Matthew 1:16 is not read alone. Verse 18 opens, "Now the **birth** of Jesus Christ was on this wise", and the explanation that follows names the Holy Ghost as source (1:18, 1:20). [L2] "Birth" there is *genesis* (origin, coming into being), the noun of the same root as *egennēsen* (he begat) and *egennēthē* (was born). Matthew breaks the verb pattern in verse 16 and then states the origin in verse 18. [L4, resting on Matthew 1:16 with *genesis*/*egennēthē* in 1:18 and the pattern of 1:2-15]

**2.** [L1] Joseph is named in 1:20 as the person addressed: "Joseph, thou son of David, **fear not** to take unto thee Mary thy wife: **for** that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." [L2] *Gar* (for, because) makes the Holy Ghost source the reason for not fearing. A man who was the begetter would have nothing to fear. [L4, resting on 1:20 *gar* and 1:19]

**3.** [L1] Matthew 1:19: "Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was **minded to put her away privily**." [L3] Deuteronomy 22:23-24, the passage the challenger cited, treats an espoused woman as "wife" in the case where another man has lain with her. Joseph's deliberation in 1:19 fits that case and no other. [L4] If Joseph had known her, no ground for putting her away would exist, and the challenger has not answered 1:19. [L4, resting on Matthew 1:19 and Deuteronomy 22:23-24]

---

## LINK 2: "Before they came together" and the timing

**4.** [L1] The challenger says 1:18 dates only the *finding*. [L2] *Heurethē* (she was found) is passive: she was discovered to be, *en gastri echousa* (having in womb, pregnant). A thing can be found only if it already exists. [L1] If the finding was *prin ē sunelthein autous* (before they came together), then the pregnancy found was also before they came together. [L4, resting on Matthew 1:18 and the grammar of *heurethē* with *prin ē*]

**5.** [L1] The sequence in Matthew runs as follows. She is found with child (1:18). Joseph is told "fear not **to take** unto thee Mary thy wife" (1:20), so the taking was still future. Then "[Joseph] took unto him his wife" (1:24), and "knew her not till she had brought forth" (1:25). [L4] No interval is left in the text for Joseph to have known her and begotten the child. The challenger's "knew her within the espousal" is offered as an inference with no verse of its own.

**6.** [L3] The challenger's Deuteronomy 22:24 ("his neighbour's wife") is answered by its own context. Verse 23 reads: "a damsel that is a **virgin betrothed** unto an husband." [L4] "Wife" is therefore the legal title of a betrothed virgin, and the title does not imply that they had come together. Matthew 1:19-20 follows the same usage (*gunaika*, woman or wife), and 1:18 adds "before they came together". [L4, resting on Deuteronomy 22:23-24 and Matthew 1:18-20]

**7.** [L3] Genesis 4:1 is the challenger's own lexicon: "Adam **knew** Eve his wife; and she conceived." The LXX *egnō* (he knew) uses *ginōskō* (to know, as in marital union). [L1] Matthew 1:25 uses the same verb (*ouk eginōsken*, was not knowing), and Luke 1:34 uses it also (*ou ginōskō*, I am not knowing). Both texts deny the Genesis 4:1 act at the time of the conception. [L4, resting on Genesis 4:1, Matthew 1:25 and Luke 1:34]

**8.** [L3] On 1 John 5:1, the claim is not that "of God" gives the Holy Ghost physical seed. By my own definition the question is whether a human man supplied the seed. [L2] Matthew 1:20 uses *to gennēthen* (that which is begotten), the same verb Matthew reserved for the male line in 1:2-16. It joins *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of spirit holy) to that verb. The verb's usual subject, a man, is replaced by the Holy Ghost, and no human begetter is named beside him. [L4, resting on Matthew 1:20 with 1:2-16]

---

## LINK 3: Mary's question and the angel's answer

**9.** [L1] The challenger concedes that *ou ginōskō* (I am not knowing) is present tense. [L1] She is already "espoused" (Luke 1:27), and the angel has said without condition, "thou shalt conceive in thy womb" (1:31). Mary asks "How shall this be?" [L4] If she expected conception by marriage to Joseph, the question would have no point, and the angel could have answered "after the marriage". He instead names a cause that is not a man: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee." The answer replaces the thing she said was lacking. [L4, resting on Luke 1:27, 1:31 and 1:34-35]

**10.** [L1] Luke 1:35 closes, "that holy thing which shall be **born of thee**" (the received text has *ek sou*, out of thee). [L2] *Dio kai* (therefore also) makes the divine act the stated ground of the title "Son of God". [L4] If Joseph's begetting were part of the cause, the "therefore" would have a second cause behind it, and the text names none. [L4, resting on Luke 1:35 *dio kai* and 1:31]

**11.** [L3] The challenger cites Acts 1:8 and Luke 9:34, and they help the chain. Acts 1:8 has *epelthontos* (coming upon) of the Holy Ghost, and Luke 9:34 has *episkiazō* (to overshadow) of the cloud. Both name divine action as the agent. [L1] In Luke 1:35 that divine action is the stated answer to "How shall this be?", where the thing to come about is a conception "in thy womb" (1:31). [L4] Context fixes that object, so these verbs name God as the agent of the conception, and Mary's "I know not a man" removes the human agent. [L4, resting on Luke 1:31, 1:34-35 and Luke 9:34]

---

## LINK 4: "Father" and "as was supposed"

**12.** [L3] The challenger says *enomizeto* (was supposed) does not itself declare the supposition false. Luke's own use of the verb *nomizō* (to suppose, to reckon by custom) shows how he uses it:

- [L1] Luke 2:44: "But they, **supposing** him to have been in the company" (*nomisantes*). He was not.
- [L1] Acts 14:19: "**supposing** he had been dead" (*nomizontes*). He was alive.
- [L1] Acts 16:27: "**supposing** that the prisoners had been fled" (*nomizōn*). They had not.
- [L1] Acts 21:29: "whom they **supposed** that Paul had brought into the temple" (*enomizon*). He had not.
- [L1] Acts 7:25: "he **supposed** his brethren would have understood" (*enomizen*). They did not.

[L4] In Luke's own writing, *nomizō* with a person as its object reports a belief the narrative shows to be mistaken. The challenger's Acts 16:13 ("was wont") concerns the reputed character of a place and does not state a person's sonship. [L4, resting on Luke 2:44 and Acts 14:19, 16:27, 21:29 and 7:25]

**13.** [L1] Luke 2:44 is in the same chapter as Luke 2:48-49. [L4] Luke places the reader's knowledge (1:35) before the genealogy and then qualifies Joseph's sonship with "as was supposed" (3:23). Had Luke thought Joseph the begetter, the qualifier would have been pointless. [L4, resting on Luke 1:35, 3:23 and 2:44]

**14.** [L1] On 1 Chronicles 28:6, God says of Solomon, "I have **chosen** him to be my son, and I will be his father." [L2] *Bāḥartî* (I have chosen) is the language of appointment. The text never says that David did not beget Solomon, and Solomon has no "as was supposed". [L1] In Luke 1:35 the divine fatherhood rests on conception by the Holy Ghost and carries "therefore also". [L4] The two texts differ in just the points that matter. [L4, resting on 1 Chronicles 28:6 and Luke 1:35]

**15.** [L1] Luke 1:32 reads, "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his **father** David." [L2] *Patros* (father) is used of an ancestor a thousand years removed. [L4] The word "father" in Luke can therefore name lineage and standing without naming the begetter, and this narrows the force of "thy father" in 2:48. In 2:49 Jesus answers that very sentence with "my Father's business" (*tou patros mou*, of my father). [L4, resting on Luke 1:32, 2:48 and 2:49]

---

## LINKS 5-6: Descent from David, and "alone"

**16.** On Luke 3:31, Noted. Not conceding here. Goes to review. The load-bearing claim does not rest on the register naming Mary. It rests on the following.

**17.** [L1] Romans 1:3 reads "*genomenou* (come to be) of the seed of David according to the flesh". Galatians 4:4 reads "*genomenon ek gunaikos* (come to be out of a woman)". [L3] Both use the same verb and the same *ek* of origin. [L1] Luke 1:32-33 gives the throne of David to the child, and this is spoken to Mary at the moment the angel names the Holy Ghost. [L4] The text says the child comes to be out of the woman, and it says he is of David's seed in the flesh. The only human source the text names for him is the woman. [L4, resting on Romans 1:3, Galatians 4:4 and Luke 1:32-35]

**18.** [L1] Luke 2:4 says only that Joseph was "of the house and lineage of David". That is not in dispute. Joseph's descent from David was needed for the legal reckoning (Matthew 1:20, "thou son of David"). [L1] Matthew 1:12 puts Jechonias in his line, and Jeremiah 22:30 says of Coniah, "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." [L4] That word, which the challenger left unanswered, sits against a begotten son of Joseph. [L4, resting on Matthew 1:11-16 and Jeremiah 22:30]

---

## Declared chain, re-seated

1. [L1] Matthew 1:16 and 1:18 with [L2] *egennēthē* (was born), the feminine *hēs* (of whom) and *genesis* (origin) join to 1:20 *gar* (for). Because the chain explains Joseph's fear, no begetting by Joseph is stated.
2. [L1] Matthew 1:18-20, 1:24-25 with [L2] *heurethē* (was found), *prin ē* (before) and *ouk eginōsken* (was not knowing), seated by [L3] Genesis 4:1 and Deuteronomy 22:23-24. The pregnancy came before any union, and the text gives no interval in which Joseph could have known her.
3. [L1] Luke 1:27, 1:31 and 1:34-35 with [L2] *ou ginōskō* (I am not knowing) and *dio kai* (therefore also). The answer to "How shall this be?" names a cause that is not a man.
4. [L1] Luke 3:23, 2:44 and 2:48-49 with [L2] *nomizō* (to suppose) and [L3] Acts 14:19, 16:27, 21:29 and 7:25. "Father" for Joseph is the supposition of others, and Luke's own use of the verb shows such suppositions to be mistaken.
5. [L1] Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 with [L2] *genomenou*/*genomenon* (come to be) and *ek* (out of), seated by [L3] Genesis 3:15 and Jeremiah 22:30. His fleshly descent is carried through the woman.
6. [L4] Jesus had no biological human father. The conclusion rests on links 1-5 and on Matthew 1:16-25, Luke 1:34-35 and Luke 3:23.
