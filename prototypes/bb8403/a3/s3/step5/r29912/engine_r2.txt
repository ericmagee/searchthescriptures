## Closing challenge

### LINK 2: What Matthew dates

[L1] Matthew 1:18: “Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost.”

[L1] Matthew 1:19: “Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily.”

[L1] Matthew 1:20: “But while he thought on these things, behold, the angel of the Lord appeared unto him in a dream, saying, Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost.”

[L1] Matthew 1:24–25: “Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife: And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS.”

[L4] The pregnancy existed when it was found, and the finding preceded their coming together. The disputed link is what “came together” means here. Matthew follows it with Joseph’s taking his wife; the text does not say that Joseph’s intent to put her away arose from knowledge that another man was the begetter. [L1]

[L3] Genesis 4:1: “And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD.”

[L3] Deuteronomy 22:24 calls the espoused woman “his neighbour’s wife,” while Matthew calls Joseph “her husband” before the taking.

[L4] The inference I press is that **Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal**: the named husband and wife, Genesis’s knowing-and-conception account, and Matthew’s sequence provide its footing. Matthew’s “knew her not till” is held as written; on this reading it covers the time after he took her, through the birth. [L1, L3]

### LINK 3: Mary’s words and the angel’s verbs

[L1] Luke 1:34–35: “Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man? And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God.”

[L2] “I know not” is present tense: it states Mary’s condition when she asks.

[L3] Acts 1:8: “But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.”

[L3] Luke 9:34: “While he thus spake, there came a cloud, and overshadowed them: and they feared as they entered into the cloud.”

[L4] The angel’s answer is divine action; the parallels show that coming upon and overshadowing are also used of power and presence. They do not themselves establish the defender’s additional exclusion of Joseph. [L1–L3]

### LINK 4: “Father” and “supposed”

[L1] Luke 2:48–49: “And when they saw him, they were amazed: and his mother said unto him, Son, why hast thou thus dealt with us? behold, thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing. And he said unto them, How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father’s business?”

[L3] 1 Chronicles 28:6: “And he said unto me, Solomon thy son, he shall build my house and my courts: for I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father.”

[L4] God calls Solomon his son while also calling him David’s son. Jesus’ reference to his Father’s business therefore does not itself cancel Mary’s “thy father.” [L1, L3]

[L1] Luke 3:23: “And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli.”

[L3] Acts 16:13: “And on the sabbath we went out of the city by a river side, where prayer was wont to be made; and we sat down, and spake unto the women which resorted thither.”

[L2] The same verb form translated “was supposed” in Luke 3:23 is translated “was wont” in Acts 16:13.

[L4] The defender’s examples show mistaken suppositions in their contexts; they do not make falsity inherent in the word. Luke 3:23 must be read alongside Mary’s statement, not assumed to cancel it. [L1–L3]

### LINKS 5–6: The fleshly seed and the named father

[L1] Romans 1:3: “Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh;”

[L3] Acts 2:30: “Therefore being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him, that of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne;”

[L3] Luke 2:4: “And Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth, into Judaea, unto the city of David, which is called Bethlehem; (because he was of the house and lineage of David:)”

[L4] The fleshly seed is David’s; Joseph is the named father of David’s lineage. Galatians 4:4 calls Jesus “made of a woman,” but that phrase does not establish that the fleshly descent was through Mary alone. [L1, L3]

[L3] Matthew 11:11: “Verily I say unto you, Among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist: notwithstanding he that is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.”

[L4] Being born of a woman does not itself settle the father question: John is so described, and Luke names his father Zacharias (Luke 1:13). [L1, L3]

[L1] Jeremiah 22:30: “Thus saith the LORD, Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper in his days: for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah.”

[L3] Haggai 2:23: “In that day, saith the LORD of hosts, will I take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant, the son of Shealtiel, saith the LORD, and will make thee as a signet: for I have chosen thee, saith the LORD of hosts.”

[L4] Jeremiah’s words stand, including “in his days” and “in Judah.” Haggai calls Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel and God’s chosen signet; it does not call him king. Neither passage states the defender’s proposed maternal genealogy or applies Jeremiah’s sentence to exclude Joseph’s begetting. [L1, L3]

## UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES

1. LINK 2: The exclusion of an interval for Joseph’s knowing is asserted from the sequence: “No interval is left in the text for Joseph to have known her and begotten the child.”
2. LINK 3: Mary’s question is made to exclude Joseph: “If she expected conception by marriage to Joseph, the question would have no point.”
3. LINK 4: The supposition is made false by the verb’s use elsewhere: “Luke’s own use of the verb shows such suppositions to be mistaken.”
4. LINK 5: The woman is made the exclusive human source: “The only human source the text names for him is the woman.”
5. LINK 6: The exclusions are gathered into the keystone: “Jesus had no biological human father.”
