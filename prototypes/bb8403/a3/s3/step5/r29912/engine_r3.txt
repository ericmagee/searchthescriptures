DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED**

Pressed against it: Matthew 1:18, "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." Matthew 1:19, "Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." Matthew 1:20, "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." Matthew 1:24–25, "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." Genesis 4:1, "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived." Deuteronomy 22:24, "his neighbour's wife."

Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:18 says she "was found with child" before they "came together." The Greek is heurethē (was found) en gastri echousa (having in womb), and synelthein (to come together).
- [L1] Matthew 1:19 says Joseph was "minded to put her away" (apolusai, to release or send off). This is Joseph's reaction on discovering the pregnancy. Deuteronomy 22:23–24 treats the espoused woman as "his neighbour's wife," so the legal husband-language does not mean knowing had occurred. [L3]
- [L2] In Matthew 1:20, to gennēthen (the thing begotten or conceived) is "ek pneumatos hagiou" (out of the Spirit, holy). The preposition ek (out of) names the source of the conception. The angel names the source only after Joseph's intent to put her away. A man who had known her would have recognised the child as his own, and there would be no occasion for "fear not."
- [L1] Matthew 1:25 says "knew her not" (ouk eginōsken, was not knowing) "till" (heōs hou, until which time) the birth. Genesis 4:1 uses "knew" for the act that leads to conception, and Matthew 1:25 uses the same verb for the act Joseph did not perform.
- Chain: the pregnancy is found before the coming together (1:18). Joseph's reaction is to put her away (1:19). The angel assigns the source as ek pneumatos (1:20). Joseph is said not to have known her through the birth (1:25). So the interval for Joseph's knowing has no footing in any verse. It rests on the silence of Matthew 1:18, which Matthew 1:19–20 fills.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED**

Pressed against it: Luke 1:34–35, "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man? … The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee." Acts 1:8, "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you." Luke 9:34, "there came a cloud, and overshadowed them."

Footing:
- [L1] Luke 1:27 has Mary "espoused to a man whose name was Joseph." Luke 1:31 gives the future promise, "thou shalt conceive in thy womb."
- [L2] Mary's reply is ginōskō (I know), the same verb Genesis 4:1 and Matthew 1:25 use for marital knowing, with andra (a male man) as its object. Her question "how" (pōs) is asked "seeing" (epei, since). The since-clause grounds the question in her present state, and the question is about a conception still to come.
- [L1] The angel's answer, "therefore" (dio, for which reason), supplies the means in place of a man: "Holy Ghost shall come upon thee."
- Chain: the promise is of conception (1:31). Her stated condition is not knowing a man (1:34). The answer gives the Spirit as the means (1:35). Acts 1:8 and Luke 9:34 show that these verbs can denote power and presence, but they do not remove the "therefore" tying the verb to the conception in her womb. The exclusion of Joseph is the question-and-answer pair itself, not the verb alone.

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED**

Pressed against it: Luke 2:48–49, "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing … my Father's business." 1 Chronicles 28:6, "I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father." Luke 3:23, "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph." Acts 16:13, "prayer was wont to be made."

Footing:
- [L2] The verb is nomizō (to reckon by custom or opinion), here enomizeto. In Acts 16:13 it has the customary sense, and I do not claim that falsity is inherent in the word. The premise concerns Luke's use.
- [L1] Luke's other uses show opinion contrary to fact: Luke 2:44, "supposing him to have been in the company," when he was not. Acts 7:25, "he supposed his brethren would have understood," and they did not. Acts 14:19, "supposing he had been dead," when he was not.
- [L1] The "as was supposed" in Luke 3:23 stands in the same author's account as Luke 1:34–35, and the two are read together.
- Chain: Luke's pattern has the supposition contradicted by the narrative. Luke 3:23 sits beside Luke 1:34–35, which supplies the contradiction.
- The 1 Chronicles 28:6 parallel does not defeat this. Solomon's descent from David is stated elsewhere as begetting, so that verse shows a double sonship with two real fathers. Luke 2:48 records Mary's speech, and Luke 2:49 gives the reply, "my Father's business." The text places the two fathers in tension and does not equate them.

**LINK 5: SUPPORTED**

Pressed against it: Romans 1:3, "made of the seed of David according to the flesh." Acts 2:30, "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh." Luke 2:4, "he was of the house and lineage of David." Galatians 4:4, "made of a woman." Matthew 11:11, "born of women." Luke 1:13, naming Zacharias as father of John.

Footing:
- [L2] Galatians 4:4 has genomenon ek gunaikos (come to be out of a woman). The ek (out of) names source, and no man is named beside her.
- [L2] In Matthew 1:16 the formula "begat" (egennēsen) is repeated through the list, then breaks: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom (ex hēs, out of whom, feminine) was born Jesus." The verb turns passive and the relative is feminine, so the line's begetting stops at Joseph and the birth is attributed to her alone.
- [L1] Luke 1:35 says "that holy thing which shall be born of thee."
- [L3] Contrast John: Luke 1:13 names the father, "Elisabeth shall bear thee a son." For Jesus no verse names a begetter.
- Romans 1:3, Acts 2:30 and Luke 2:4 name David's seed and Joseph's lineage, not a begetter. Neither verse says the seed came through Joseph, so none names a human source other than the woman.

**LINK 6: SUPPORTED**

Pressed against it: Jeremiah 22:30, "Write ye this man childless … for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah." Haggai 2:23, "will I take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant … and will make thee as a signet."

Footing:
- [L1] Links 2–5 supply the premises: Matthew 1:18, 20, 25; Luke 1:34–35; Matthew 1:16; Galatians 4:4; Luke 3:23.
- [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 says "no man of his seed" shall sit on David's throne. Matthew 1:11–12 places Jechonias in the line leading to Joseph. Luke 1:32–33 promises this child "the throne of his father David."
- [L3] The sentence in Jeremiah 22:30 reads "any more," and "in his days" modifies the man, not the seed. Haggai 2:23 names Zerubbabel as chosen, but it does not say the sentence is revoked.
- Chain: the stated exclusions from links 2–5, together with the throne promise, give the keystone. The keystone stands as inference from those verses and not as a verse of its own.

**CHALLENGER'S UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES**

1. That Joseph's knowing of Mary occurred within the espousal. This props the objection that Matthew 1:18 leaves room for his begetting. Quote: "Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal". No verse says it, and Matthew 1:25, "knew her not," is read against it.
2. That the double sonship of Solomon shows "thy father" can stand beside "my Father." This props the objection that Luke 2:49 does not cancel Luke 2:48. Quote: "God calls Solomon his son while also calling him David’s son." Solomon's begetting by David is stated, which assumes a begetter and does not show what "thy father" means in Luke 2:48.
3. That the fleshly seed of David passes through Joseph. This props the objection to the maternal descent. Quote: "The fleshly seed is David’s; Joseph is the named father of David’s lineage." Romans 1:3 and Luke 2:4 do not name Joseph as begetter.
4. That the Spirit's verbs reduce to power and presence only. This props the objection to Luke 1:35 as excluding a man. Quote: "The angel’s answer is divine action; the parallels show that coming upon and overshadowing are also used of power and presence." Neither Acts 1:8 nor Luke 9:34 concerns conception, and Luke 1:31, "conceive in thy womb," is not addressed.
5. That Jeremiah's sentence is limited by its own words. This props the objection to the throne argument. Quote: "Jeremiah’s words stand, including “in his days” and “in Judah.”" The phrase "in his days" modifies "a man that shall not prosper," not "no man of his seed," and the premise does not exhibit why it limits the seed clause.
