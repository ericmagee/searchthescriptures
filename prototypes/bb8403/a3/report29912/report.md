## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession". It adds: "A charge is stronger than its quote only when it asserts what the quote does not: a negation, a universal (all, none, only, never), or a claim the quote's own words do not make. Dropping, reordering or paraphrasing the quote's words is not stronger. A limb the link round narrows counts in its narrowed form." and "A premise's quote is checked against every earlier round of the opponent; it is verbatim if found in any of them, and the judge names that round."
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

### Opening: Jesus had no biological human father

**Position.** The KJV teaches that Jesus had a biological mother, Mary, and no biological human father. His conception was of the Holy Ghost. Joseph stood in the place of a father legally and in the household, and was not the begetter. [L4, resting on the chain below]

By "biological father" I mean the man who supplies the seed of begetting (Greek *gennaō*, to beget; Hebrew *yālad*, to bear or beget).

---

#### I. Matthew 1: the genealogy breaks its own pattern

**1.** [L1] Matthew 1:2-15 runs "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob..." down to "Jacob begat Joseph." Verse 16 then reads: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom was born Jesus**, who is called Christ."

**2.** [L2] The verb "begat" throughout is *egennēsen* (he begat), active voice, with a man as subject. In verse 16 the verb changes to *egennēthē* (was born), passive voice. The relative pronoun is *hēs* (of whom), **feminine singular**, so the birth is "out of her" alone. Had Joseph been the begetter, the established formula "Joseph begat Jesus" was ready to hand. Matthew abandons it at the one place it would matter.

**3.** [L2] Joseph is called *andra* (husband, or man) of Mary. The word names his marital standing and not his fatherhood.

#### II. Matthew 1:18-25: the source of the conception is stated

**4.** [L1] "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was found with child **of the Holy Ghost**" (Matthew 1:18).

**5.** [L2] *Prin ē sunelthein autous* means "before they came together". It places the pregnancy before any union. *Ek pneumatos hagiou* means "out of spirit holy": the source (*ek*, out of) of the conception is the Holy Ghost.

**6.** [L1] The angel tells Joseph: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20). Joseph had been "minded to put her away privily" (1:19). That reaction makes no sense if he were the father. [L4, resting on 1:19 and 1:20]

**7.** [L2] "That which is conceived" is *to gennēthen* (that which has been begotten or conceived), **neuter**, and the same *ek* (out of) is used. The angel assigns the begetting to the Holy Ghost and not to Joseph.

**8.** [L1] "[Joseph] knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25). [L2] *Ouk eginōsken* (was not knowing) is imperfect tense, a continued state. *Heōs hou* (until which) marks the time up to the birth, and it covers the whole conception and pregnancy.

#### III. Luke 1: Mary's own testimony

**9.** [L1] Luke 1:27 calls Mary "a virgin" (*parthenon*, virgin). In 1:34 she asks: "How shall this be, **seeing I know not a man**?"

**10.** [L2] *Epei andra ou ginōskō* uses a present-tense verb, "I am not knowing a man". It is not a future vow. She states her present condition, and the angel's answer takes it as true.

**11.** [L1] The angel answers: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35).

**12.** [L2] *Episkiasei* (will overshadow) is the verb used for the cloud over the tabernacle in the LXX of Exodus 40:35. It signals divine action, not human union. *Dio kai* (therefore also) makes the divine act the stated ground for the title Son of God. [L3, Exodus 40:35 and Luke 1:35]

#### IV. Isaiah 7:14 and "a woman"

**13.** [L1] Matthew 1:22-23 says the conception took place "that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son."

**14.** [L2] The Hebrew in Isaiah 7:14 is *ʿalmāh* (young woman of marriageable age). I grant that the Hebrew word does not by itself say "never touched". Matthew, however, quotes the Greek *parthenos* (virgin). The Spirit-given use of *ʿalmāh* for Rebekah in Genesis 24:43 sits beside *bĕtûlāh* (virgin) and "neither had any man known her" in 24:16. [L3, Genesis 24:16, 24:43]

**15.** [L1] "God sent forth his Son, **made of a woman**, made under the law" (Galatians 4:4). [L2] *Genomenon ek gunaikos* (coming to be out of a woman) names only the woman as source. [L3] This agrees with Genesis 3:15, "her seed" (Hebrew *zeraʿ*, seed), where the seed is credited to the woman.

#### V. Biological descent runs through the mother

**16.** [L1] "[Jesus Christ] was made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**" (Romans 1:3). Acts 2:30 says "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ."

**17.** [L2] *Kata sarka* (according to flesh) is bodily descent. [L3] Luke 3:31 traces a line through Nathan, son of David. Jesus therefore had real human flesh and real Davidic descent, and this is carried by Mary. [L4, resting on Romans 1:3, Luke 3:31 and Matthew 1:16]

**18.** [L1] Hebrews 10:5: "a body hast thou prepared me." [L2] *Katērtisō* (thou hast fitted or prepared) points to a provided body and not an inherited one.

#### VI. Objections answered

**Objection: Luke 2:33, 2:48 and John 6:42 call Joseph his father.**
[L1] Luke 3:23 settles the usage: "being (as was **supposed**) the son of Joseph." [L2] *Hōs enomizeto* (as was thought or reckoned) marks it as opinion. In Luke 2:48 Mary says "thy father and I have sought thee," and the next verse has Jesus correct the title: "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?" (Luke 2:49). [L3] John 6:42 reports what the crowd says ("whose father and mother we know"), and Luke 3:23 corrects that report. In Luke 2:33, the narrator's "Joseph and his mother" is household language, the same as "his parents" (2:41), and 1:34-35 and 3:23 govern it. [L4, resting on Luke 3:23 and 2:49]

**Objection: Joseph's descent from David is needed for the throne.**
[L1] Matthew 1:20 calls Joseph "son of David," and Matthew 1:16 passes through Jechonias (1:11-12). [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 says of Coniah, "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." [L4] A begotten son of Joseph would be Coniah's seed, which would put the throne claim under that word. The virgin conception, with descent from David through Mary (Luke 3:31), avoids the difficulty. This is secondary support and not a load-bearing link. [L4, resting on Matthew 1:11-12, Jeremiah 22:30 and Luke 3:31]

## Round 1 - Challenger (ChatGPT)

CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

#### Challenge

**The disputed step is not whether Jesus was born of Mary or whether Matthew says the conception was “of the Holy Ghost.” Both are written. The dispute is whether those words exclude Joseph as the biological father.** [L4]

##### LINK 1 — The genealogy’s change of wording

[L1] Matthew 1:16 says: “And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ.”

[L2] “Was born” is passive, and “of whom” refers grammatically to Mary. The verse names her as the one from whom Jesus was born.

[L3] But Matthew 1:20 also names Joseph: “Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost.”

[L4] The passive in verse 16 does not itself say Joseph did not beget Jesus. Naming Mary in the birth statement is not the same as excluding Joseph from the begetting.

##### LINK 2 — “Of the Holy Ghost” and the timing

[L1] Matthew 1:18: “Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost.”

[L1] Matthew 1:25: “And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS.”

[L3] Matthew 1:19 calls him “Joseph her husband”; verse 20 calls her “Mary thy wife.” Deuteronomy 22:24 likewise calls an espoused woman “his neighbour’s wife.”

[L3] Genesis 4:1 states the means of begetting: “And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD.”

[L4] Read together, Matthew dates the **finding** before they came together and reports that Joseph did not know Mary through the birth after taking her as his wife. The inference pressed here is that Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal. That inference is not a quotation from Matthew; it rests on the named husband and wife, the timing words, and Genesis’s account of knowing and conception.

[L3] The words “of the Holy Ghost” remain in Matthew 1:18 and 20. Compare 1 John 5:1: “Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is born of God: and every one that loveth him that begat loveth him also that is begotten of him.”

[L4] Being “of” God does not by itself establish biological divine paternity. The defender must show that Matthew’s words exclude the named husband, not merely repeat that the conception is “of the Holy Ghost.”

##### LINK 3 — Mary’s question and the angel’s answer

[L1] Luke 1:34: “Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?”

[L1] Luke 1:35: “And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God.”
