## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession". It adds: "A charge is stronger than its quote only when it asserts what the quote does not: a negation, a universal (all, none, only, never), or a claim the quote's own words do not make. Dropping, reordering or paraphrasing the quote's words is not stronger. A limb the link round narrows counts in its narrowed form." and "A premise's quote is checked against every earlier round of the opponent; it is verbatim if found in any of them, and the judge names that round."
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Defender (Claude)

DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

### Opening: Christ had no human biological father

**Term defined.** By "biological father" I mean a man whose seed (Hebrew *zera'*, seed; Greek *sperma*, seed) begat the child. The standard reading is that Jesus had no such man. Mary conceived by the Holy Ghost while a virgin. Joseph was the husband of Mary and the child's father only by supposition and law. [L4, resting on the verses below]

---

#### 1. The conception is stated as "of the Holy Ghost," before any union

**[L1]** Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was found with child **of the Holy Ghost**."

**[L2]** *Prin ē sunelthein autous* means "before they came together" (before they were united as husband and wife). The text fixes the conception before any union. *Ek pneumatos hagiou* means "out of holy spirit," and *ek* (out of) marks the source of the conception.

**[L1]** Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."

**[L2]** *To gennēthen* (that which has been begotten) is the verb *gennaō* (to beget, to bring forth). It is the same verb used of fathers throughout Matthew 1:2-16. In this verse the same act of begetting is credited to the Holy Ghost, with no man named as its source.

#### 2. Matthew's genealogy breaks its own pattern at verse 16

**[L1]** Matthew 1:2-16 runs: "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob…" Then verse 16 reads: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom** was born Jesus, who is called Christ."

**[L2]** The Greek *egennēsen* (begat) is repeated about forty times. In verse 16 it is dropped. The text says *Iōsēph ton andra Marias, **ex hēs** egennēthē Iēsous*, "Joseph the husband of Mary, out of **whom** (feminine singular) was born Jesus." The relative pronoun *hēs* (whom, feminine) can refer only to Mary. The chain of begetting stops at Joseph, and Jesus is traced to Mary alone. Joseph is called *andra* (husband), not begetter.

**[L3]** Luke 3:23 matches this: "being (**as was supposed**) the son of Joseph." The Greek *enomizeto* means "was deemed, was reckoned by custom." Luke marks Joseph's fatherhood as a public opinion, not a fact.

#### 3. Mary's own testimony and the angel's answer

**[L1]** Luke 1:27: "a **virgin** espoused to a man whose name was Joseph." Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I **know not a man**?"

**[L2]** *Parthenos* means a virgin. *Andra ou ginōskō* means "I do not know a man." The verb *ginōskō* (to know) is the same euphemism for union as Hebrew *yada'* (to know) in Genesis 4:1: "Adam **knew** Eve his wife; and she conceived."

**[L1]** Luke 1:35 gives the answer: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."

**[L2]** *Dio kai* means "for which cause also." The angel gives the Holy Ghost's action, and not a man's, as the reason the child is called the Son of God.

**[L1]** Matthew 1:25: Joseph "knew her **not till** she had brought forth her firstborn son." Matthew 1:19-20 shows Joseph "minded to put her away privily" until the angel explained.

**[L4]** Joseph knew he was not the father. This rests on two footings. First, [L1] Matthew 1:19, where he plans to put her away because she was found with child. Second, [L1] Matthew 1:20, where the angel's word of the Holy Ghost resolves the matter.

#### 4. The prophetic line, from the beginning

**[L1]** Matthew 1:22-23: "that it might be fulfilled… Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son."

**[L2]** The Hebrew of Isaiah 7:14 is *'almah* (a maiden of marriageable age). Matthew's Greek is *parthenos* (virgin). The Holy Ghost's own rendering in Matthew's text, together with Luke 1:27, 34, settles the sense.

**[L3]** *'Almah* is used of Rebekah in Genesis 24:43. Genesis 24:16 calls the same woman *bethulah* (a virgin), "neither had any man known her." So the word is used for a woman who was in fact a virgin.

**[L3]** Genesis 3:15 speaks of "**her** seed" (*zar'ah*, her seed), where Scripture ordinarily reckons seed through the man. Galatians 4:4 says "God sent forth his Son, **made of a woman**," *genomenon ek gynaikos* (come into being out of a woman). Hebrews 10:5 says "a **body hast thou prepared** me," *sōma katērtisō* (you fitted a body).

#### 5. The child himself speaks of a different Father

**[L1]** Luke 2:48-49: Mary says, "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." Jesus answers, "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?"

**[L4]** At the first recorded point where Mary says "thy father," the child corrects it. This rests on two footings. [L1] Luke 2:49 gives the correction. [L1] Luke 1:35 gives the reason for it, that he is the Son of God by the Holy Ghost's work.

**[L1]** John 6:42 records the crowd's belief: "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?" The text reports their assumption and does not endorse it, as Luke 3:23 shows.

#### 6. A necessary consequence: the curse on Jechoniah

**[L1]** Matthew 1:11-12 places Jechonias in Joseph's line. **[L1]** Jeremiah 22:30: "Write ye this man childless… for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David."

**[L4]** If Joseph's seed had begotten Jesus, he would stand under this word. Yet Luke 1:32-33 says the Lord God "shall give unto him the throne of his father David." The conception by the Holy Ghost removes the conflict, with Joseph as legal father and no seed passed from Jechonias's line. This rests on [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 and [L1] Luke 1:32-33, read through [L1] Matthew 1:20.

---

#### Answering the likely objections in advance

- **"Father" in the Gospels.** Luke 2:48 and John 6:42 report what people said or assumed. Luke 3:23 labels it "as was supposed." These reports do not outweigh the narrator's own statements in Matthew 1:18, 20 and Luke 1:35.
- **"Firstborn" and "till" (Matthew 1:25).** These words speak to the time of the birth and say nothing about the manner of conception already given in 1:18, 20.
- **God as Father.** I grant that the text names God as the Father, since Luke 1:35 says "the Son of God." The question asks about a biological father, meaning a man. The text gives none, and gives the Holy Ghost as the source of conception.

[L2] *prin ē sunelthein* (before they came together) and *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of holy spirit), with *gennēthen* (begotten) as the same verb used of the fathers in the genealogy.

[L2] *'almah* (maiden) and *parthenos* (virgin), and *genomenon ek gynaikos* (come into being out of a woman). [L3] Genesis 24:16, 43; Genesis 3:15 ("her seed"); Hebrews 10:5.

## Round 1 - Challenger (MiMo)

CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

### CHALLENGE: The Seed Rows Require a Man, and the Text Names Him

The declaration asks me to break the chain. I aim at the keystone **D6** and the links it leans on — **D1**, **D3**, **D5** first, with **D2** and **D4** answered where they matter.

---

#### TARGET D6 — the keystone fails against the seed rows

The declaration concludes "Christ had no human biological father." Paul and Peter state the opposite in plain words about the same person:

**[L1]** Romans 1:3: "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of **the seed of David according to the flesh**."

**[L1]** Acts 2:30: "of the **fruit of his loins**, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne."

**[L1]** 2 Samuel 7:12: "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall **proceed out of thy bowels**."

**[L1]** Hebrews 7:14: "it is **evident** that our Lord **sprang out of Juda**."

**[L2]** *Sperma* (seed), *osphys* (loins), *kata sarka* (according to the flesh). These are anatomical words — seed proceeding out of a man's body. *Anatellō* (sprang) at Hebrews 7:14 is physical emergence.

**[L3]** Psalm 132:11: "Of the **fruit of thy body** will I set upon thy throne." Genesis 35:11: "kings shall come **out of thy loins**." 1 Kings 8:19: "thy son that shall come forth **out of thy loins**."

**[L4]** The Davidic promise is fulfilled by seed out of a man's body according to flesh. Footings: the [L1] rows above name seed, loins, bowels, body, "according to the flesh"; the [L2] words *sperma*, *osphys*, *kata sarka* are anatomical. If no man begat the child, the promised seed is absent — yet Paul says it is present and "according to the flesh."

The declaration offers "her seed" (Genesis 3:15) and "made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4) as substitutes. **[L2]** Seed (*zar'ah*) predicated of a woman in the offspring sense occurs at only four rows in the whole Bible (Genesis 3:15; 16:10; 24:60; Isaiah 54:3) — **none Davidic**. Every Davidic seed row goes through the man's body. Galatians 4:4's "made of a woman" is the mortality idiom — the same way John the Baptist is "born of women" (Matthew 11:11, *genomenos ek gynaikōn*) while his father Zacharias is named (Luke 1:5, 13, 57-64). **[L4]** "Made of a woman" states human mortality, not the absence of a father. Footings: [L1] Matthew 11:11 of John whose father is named; [L2] *ek gynaikos* matched to *ek gynaikōn*.

---

#### TARGET D1 — "of the Holy Ghost" is covenant origin, not paternity

The declaration reads *ek pneumatos hagiou* as naming the biological source. The New Testament's own pattern for *ek* + God or Spirit never means biological paternity:

**[L3]** John 1:13: "born of God" (*ek theou*). 1 John 3:9: "born of God." 1 John 5:1: "born of God." Every one is spiritual origin — none is biological begetting. When Paul marks biological flesh, he adds a qualifier: *ek spermatos Dauid **kata sarka*** (Romans 1:3, "of the seed of David **according to the flesh**"). Matthew 1:20 has **no** *kata sarka* — the biological qualifier is absent. **[L4]** "Of the Holy Ghost" marks covenant origin — God's claim on the child, not God's begetting of flesh. Footings: [L2] *ek* + God/Spirit pattern across John 1:13, 1 John 3:9, 5:1 — all covenant; [L3] Romans 1:3's *kata sarka* present where flesh is meant, absent at Matthew 1:20.

The title "Son of God" is given to Solomon — a man with a named biological father:

**[L1]** 1 Chronicles 28:6: "Solomon **thy son** … I have chosen him to be **my son**, and I will be his father." One verse: the son David begat and the son God calls his own. **[L1]** 2 Samuel 7:14: "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" — of the same Solomon. **[L4]** "Son of God" is a covenant designation that runs alongside biological fatherhood, never replacing it. Footings: [L1] 1 Chronicles 28:6 names both in one verse; [L3] 2 Samuel 7:14; Psalm 2:7 applied at the resurrection (Acts 13:33).

The declaration's argument from *gennēthen* (begotten) at Matthew 1:20 faces its own grammar: the passive *egennēthē* at 1:16 comes after 39 active *egennēsen* forms. **[L2]** A passive states "was born" and does not narrate the agent. Non-narration is not counter-narration. The rest of the canon — the seed rows quoted above — fills what this one verb leaves quiet.

---

#### TARGET D3 — Luke 1:34 is a present state; the angel's verbs are not begetting

**[L1]** Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I **know not** a man?"

**[L2]** *Andra ou ginōskō* — present active indicative ("I do not know a man" **now**, as I speak). A permanent vow would require *ou mē* + aorist subjunctive. The text gives neither.

**[L1]** Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall **come upon** thee, and the power of the Highest shall **overshadow** thee."

**[L2]** *Eperchomai* (come upon) — 10 rows in the New Testament. Acts 1:8: "after that the Holy Ghost is **come upon** you" — spoken to the apostles, none of whom was begotten by it. *Episkiazō* (overshadow) — 5 rows: Matthew 17:5, Mark 9:7, Luke 9:34 (the transfiguration cloud), Acts 5:15 (Peter's shadow), Luke 1:35. **Not one** denotes biological union or conception at any row. **[L4]** Luke 1:35 describes divine presence and empowerment, not a begetting. Footings: [L2] the full row counts of both verbs; [L3] Acts 1:8's parallel to the same crowd of men, and the transfiguration cloud.

**[L4]** And Mary's "I know not a man" is a state at the moment of speaking, and she is espoused to a man the same passage names. Footings: [L2] present indicative, not *ou mē* + aorist; [L3] Genesis 4:1: "Adam **knew** Eve his wife; and she conceived" — knowing is the canon's stated means of begetting, and Luke 1:34 uses the same verb.

---

#### TARGET D5 — four registers name Joseph as the father

The declaration says Joseph's fatherhood is "supposed." The text names him in four independent registers:

**[L1]** **Mary herself**: "thy **father** and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48).
**[L1]** **The narrator**: "the **parents** brought in the child Jesus" (Luke 2:27); "his **parents**" (2:41); "Joseph and his mother" (2:33, 43).
**[L1]** **The town**: "Is not this Jesus, the **son of Joseph**, whose **father** and mother we know?" (John 6:42; cf. Luke 4:22).

# Final Round: The Seed Rows Are Not "Stock," and the Chains Break

The defender's core move is to reduce *sperma* (seed), *osphys* (loins), *bowels*, and *kata sarka* (according to the flesh) to mere "descent" or "stock." That move cannot survive the words themselves.

---

## 1. D6 cannot be saved by re-defining "seed" as "stock"

**[L1]** Acts 2:30: "Therefore being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him, that **of the fruit of his loins**, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne."

**[L2]** *Ek karphou tēs osphyos autou, kata sarka* — "out of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh." *Osphys* (loins) is anatomical at every one of its New Testament rows (Matthew 3:4; Mark 1:6; Hebrews 7:5, 10; Ephesians 6:14). **[L3]** Hebrews 7:5 uses the identical word of the Levitical priests taking tithes "**out of the loins of Abraham**" — physical descent from Abraham's body, and Hebrews 7:9-10 makes Levi "in the loins of his father" while still unborn.

**[L1]** Romans 1:3: "made of the **seed of David according to the flesh**."

**[L2]** *Ek spermatos Dauid kata sarka*. *Sperma* (seed) paired with *kata sarka* is the New Testament's biological-descent formula; *kata sarka* is the qualifier Paul adds exactly when flesh-lineage is meant. The declaration has no row where "seed of David" is stated without this anatomical frame.

**[L4]** The Davidic seed row is anatomical, not "stock." Footings: [L2] *osphys* (loins) at Hebrews 7:5, 7:9-10 of Levi's physical descent; *kata sarka* (according to the flesh) as the biological qualifier at Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30; [L3] Psalm 132:11 "fruit of thy **body**," Genesis 35:11 "out of thy **loins**," 2 Samuel 7:12 "proceed out of thy **bowels**."

**On "raised" as the whole act.** The declaration tries to shift the raising (Acts 2:30, *anastēsein*) to God and away from the begetting. That confuses two acts: Peter says God "would **raise up** Christ to sit on his throne," and the oath he is quoting is about the *fruit of his loins*. The raising is God's; the fruit of the loins is the prerequisite.

**On Galatians 3:16 and "seed of Abraham."** Hebrews 2:16 says he "took on him the seed of Abraham." **[L1]** Hebrews 2:14: "Forasmuch then as the children are **partakers of flesh and blood**, he also himself likewise **took part of the same**." The incarnation is stated as flesh-and-blood taken — the same category as the children's. Galatians 3:16 names Christ as the covenant head; it does not cancel Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30's "according to the flesh" of David specifically.

**On Luke 1:32 "his father David."** The declaration says "father" here is a forebear, and therefore Davidic-seed language cannot require an immediate begetter. **[L1]** Luke 1:32: "the Lord God shall give unto him the **throne of his father David**." **[L1]** Luke 1:69: "hath raised up an horn of salvation for us in the **house of his servant David**." **[L2]** *Tou patros autou Dauid* — "his father David," the dynastic sense; but the seed rows (Acts 2:30 "fruit of his loins"; Romans 1:3 "seed of David according to the flesh") speak of **how Christ descends from David**, not of David as an immediate father. **[L4]** The word "father" spanning generations is irrelevant to the anatomical seed rows. Footings: [L2] *osphys* and *sperma... kata sarka*; [L3] Psalm 132:11 "fruit of thy body."

## 2. D3: the timing rows, read in their own order, place the union before the finding

The declaration says my reading of "till" rules out union before conception. That misreads the sequence.

**[L1]** Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was **espoused** to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was **found** with child of the Holy Ghost."

**[L2]** *Prin ē sunelthein autous* (before they came together) modifies "was **found**" — the text dates the **finding**, not the conception. The pregnancy became visible to Joseph before he had brought her home.

**[L1]** Matthew 1:24: "being raised from sleep... **took unto him his wife**." **[L1]** Matthew 1:25: "And **knew her not till** she had brought forth her firstborn son."

**[L2]** *Heōs hou eteken* (till she brought forth) — "till" bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy after the taking. The *synerchomai* (came together) of 1:18 is the coming into the house of 1:24.

**[L3]** Deuteronomy 22:23-24 calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's **wife**"; Matthew 1:19 calls Joseph "her **husband**," and Matthew 1:20 has the angel say "Mary **thy wife**." To part, Joseph must "put her away" — the word for divorce.

**[L4]** Joseph and Mary were husband and wife from the espousal. The finding preceded the taking. The abstinence covers the pregnancy from the taking to the birth. Footings: [L1] Matthew 1:18 ("found"), 1:24 ("took"), 1:25 ("till"); [L3] Deuteronomy 22:24 (betrothed = wife in law).

The declaration's appeal to Deuteronomy 22:23-24 (the penalty Joseph avoids) assumes that Joseph knew the child was not his — but the text gives two stated grounds for his intent ("just" and "not willing to make her a publick example," Matthew 1:19) and no verb of knowledge. **[L1]** Matthew 1:19 at the word level: *dikaios ōn, mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai, eboulēthē lathra apolysai autēn* — intent and two grounds. The angel's word resolves the intent; it does not testify to prior knowledge of paternity.

## 3. D5: "my Father's business" is not a denial of Joseph's fatherhood

The declaration presses Luke 2:48-49 as the child correcting Mary. The text does not carry that weight.

**[L1]** Luke 2:48-49: Mary: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." Jesus: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"

**[L2]** The noun "father" in 2:49 is not in the Greek of the KJV's "my Father's business" — the Greek is *en tois tou patros mou* ("in the things of my Father"). The child speaks of his **calling**, not of biology. **[L1]** Luke 2:51-52 immediately continues: "he went down with them, and came to Nazareth, and was **subject unto them**." **[L1]** Luke 2:41: "his **parents** went to Jerusalem." **[L1]** Luke 2:27: "the **parents** brought in the child Jesus."

**[L4]** The child's statement about his mission does not erase the four naming registers. Footings: [L1] Luke 2:27, 41, 51; 2:48 (Mary's "thy father"); [L2] *en tois tou patros mou*.

## 4. D1: the qualifier Paul uses when he means biological is absent at Matthew 1:20

The declaration resists the *kata sarka* test, saying Matthew 1:20 has its own setting. But the point of the test is that Paul needed a qualifier to mark biological descent. **[L1]** Romans 1:3: "seed of David **according to the flesh**" (*kata sarka*). **[L1]** Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" — **no** *kata sarka*. **[L3]** John 1:13: "born of God" (*ek theou*). 1 John 3:9: "born of God." 1 John 5:1: "born of God." **[L4]** "Of the Holy Ghost" marks covenant origin; the biological qualifier is absent. Footings: [L2] *ek* + God/Spirit pattern at John 1:13, 1 John 3:9, 5:1; [L3] *kata sarka* present at Romans 1:3 where flesh is meant.

**On 1 Chronicles 28:6.** The declaration says Solomon's sonship is "conditional," and that Luke 1:35's *dio kai* differs. **[L1]** 1 Chronicles 28:6: "Solomon **thy son** … I have chosen him to be **my son**, and I will be his father." Footings: [L1] 1 Chronicles 28:6 names both in one verse; [L3] 2 Samuel 7:14 (the covenant formula applied to Solomon).

## 5. D2: the passive and *ex hēs* do not signal the absence of a man

The declaration says the drop of *egennēsen* (begat) and the use of *ex hēs* (out of whom, feminine) is "the narrator's signal." But the passive is a grammatical feature of any clause whose subject is a woman. **[L2]** *Egennēthē* (was born) is passive, and a passive does not name the agent. Non-narration is not counter-narration. **[L1]** Matthew 1:16 names "Joseph the husband of Mary" in the same clause. **[L3]** Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" — Paul's own summary of the descent Matthew 1 traces. **[L4]** The genealogy serves the flesh-seed requirement of the Davidic promise, and Joseph is of the house of David (Luke 1:27; 2:4). Footings: [L2] passive *egennēthē*; [L3] Romans 1:3.
