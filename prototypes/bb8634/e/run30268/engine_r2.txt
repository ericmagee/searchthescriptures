CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, CLOSING AND PREMISE LIST

# Close of Challenge: the seed rows have not moved

## The keystone (D6) — "no biological father"

The seed rows are anatomical and they are plural. "I will set up thy seed after thee, **which shall proceed out of thy bowels**" (2 Samuel 7:12). "Of **the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30). "Of **the fruit of thy body** will I set upon thy throne" (Psalm 132:11). "For **it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda**" (Hebrews 7:14). "Jesus Christ **of the seed of David**" (2 Timothy 2:8). "made of **the seed of David according to the flesh**" (Romans 1:3). Bowels, loins, body, flesh, springing — five different words, one claim. [L1] at every line. [L2] ὀσφύς (osphys, "loins") is Abraham's word at Hebrews 7:5, 10 — "though they come **out of the loins of Abraham**"; κατὰ σάρκα (kata sarka, "according to flesh") marks flesh when flesh is meant.

Defender answers "made," not "begat." [L1] "made **of the seed of David according to the flesh**" — the made-flesh IS the Davidic seed; γενόμενον (genomenon, "having come to be") is the coming-to-be verb, and Romans 1:3 names what the flesh is made OF. A change of verb names no absence of source.

Defender answers "kata sarka classifies descent." [L1] Romans 9:3 "my kinsmen **according to the flesh**" classifies because Paul's kinsmen ARE of his flesh; classification and specification are the same move when the flesh is the topic. Romans 1:3 specifies what kindred the flesh came from — David's seed.

Defender answers Deuteronomy 25:6 (levirate name-succession) and Genesis 48:5 (Jacob adopting Ephraim and Manasseh) so the throne runs "not by Joseph's seed." [L1] Deuteronomy 25:6 is one institution — a dead brother's name preserved through the living brother's child; Genesis 48:5 is a grandfather adopting grandsons. Neither is about whose seed forms a child; and adoption produces no seed, while Acts 2:30 says there is seed "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh." [L3] And the reckoning in Israel is by the father's house: "after their families, **by the house of their fathers**... every male by their polls" (Numbers 1:2); "declared their pedigrees... **by the house of their fathers**" (1:18).

Defender answers Coniah's ban (Jeremiah 22:30) as a trap. [L1] "Write ye this man childless" — yet 1 Chronicles 3:17-18 names Jeconiah's sons; Luke 3:27 gives Salathiel "which was the son of **Neri**"; and the ban is never cited against Jesus' throne anywhere in the New Testament. [L3] Jeremiah 18:7-8: "If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil." The trap is sprung and does not fire.

## D5 — Joseph's fatherhood

[L1] Mary's own mouth: "behold, **thy father** and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48). The narrator: "**his parents** went to Jerusalem" (2:41); "when **the parents** brought in the child Jesus" (2:27). The town: "Is not this Jesus, **the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know**?" (John 6:42). A disciple: "Jesus of Nazareth, **the son of Joseph**" (John 1:45). The angel: "**Joseph, thou son of David**" (Matthew 1:20).

[L2] ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "was reckoned by custom") takes its verdict from its row: "where prayer **was wont** to be made" (Acts 16:13) — the reckoning right; "supposing him to have been in the company" (Luke 2:44) — the reckoning wrong. The defender says Luke 3:23's context decides; the same Gospel's narrator just wrote "his parents" at 2:27, 41, and the mother just said "thy father" at 2:48. [L3] "Solomon **thy son**, he shall build an house for my name... I have chosen him **to be my son**, and I will be his father" (1 Chronicles 28:6) — one verse, the son David begat and the covenant title God gave him. [L1] Luke 2:49's "my Father's business" names a second Father; it does not unname the first. [L1] Romans 1:4's declaration is "**by the resurrection from the dead**," not by the conception. The naming is the father's act: "thou shalt call his name JESUS" (Matthew 1:21), done at 1:25 — as Zacharias named John (Luke 1:13, 62-63, "signs to his father, how he would have him called").

## D2 — the timing rows

[L1] "Then Joseph being raised from sleep... **took unto him his wife**: And **knew her not till** she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:24-25). The abstinence runs from the **taking** forward; "till" bounds it to the birth. The finding is earlier: "before they came together, **she was found** with child" (1:18) — the finding, not the begetting, is what the verse dates. [L1] "Joseph **her husband**" (1:19); "Mary **thy wife**" (1:20); the law calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's **wife**" (Deuteronomy 22:24). [L3] "Adam **knew** Eve his wife: and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1). [L2] ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (ek pneumatos hagiou, "out of holy spirit") is the covenant-origin marker — "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14); when biological flesh is meant the text says κατὰ σάρκα (Romans 1:3). [L2] Luke 1:35's verbs are presence, not generation: ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, "shall overshadow") is the transfiguration cloud (Matthew 17:5; Mark 9:7; Luke 9:34) and Peter's healing shadow (Acts 5:15); ἐπέρχομαι (eperchomai, "come upon") is "after that the Holy Ghost is **come upon** you" (Acts 1:8) of the twelve.

## D3 — "of a woman" and "a body prepared"

[L1] "Among them that are **born of women** there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" (Matthew 11:11) — and John's father is Zacharias (Luke 1:5, 13). The idiom is the mortality idiom: "Man that is **born of a woman**" (Job 14:1; 15:14; 25:4). [L1] "her seed" is a mother's offspring: Hagar (Genesis 16:10), Rebekah (24:60), Zion (Isaiah 54:3) — every one with the father named in the narrative. [L1] "a body hast thou prepared me" (Hebrews 10:5) sits in a sentence about **sacrifice**: "Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me: in burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin thou hast had no pleasure" (10:5-6). [L2] κατηρτίσω (katērtisō, "thou hast fitted") is the mending word (Matthew 4:21). Fitted for the offering.

## D4 — Isaiah's sign

[L1] The sign is bounded in Isaiah's own book: "For **before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good**, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken of both her kings" (Isaiah 7:16). "**For before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother**, the riches of Damascus... shall be taken away" (8:3-4). "the stretching out of his wings shall fill the breadth of thy land, **O Immanuel**" (8:8). [L2] The sign-word is הָעַלְמָה (ha-almah, "the young woman") — Genesis 24:43 calls Rebekah עַלְמָה (almah, "young woman") and 24:16 calls the same woman בְּתוּלָה (bethulah, "virgin"); two words because the words differ. The 1611 margin at Isaiah 7:14: "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call." [L1] "A sign" (אוֹת, 'ôṯ) does not require a miraculous birth: "I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are **for signs and for wonders** in Israel" (Isaiah 8:18) — ordinary children.

## D1 — Matthew 1:16

[L1] The verse **states a birth**: "Jacob begat Joseph **the husband of Mary**, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." It never says "not of Joseph." A passive verb is non-narration, not denial. [L3] The same Gospel names the angel's address: "**Joseph, thou son of David**" (1:20), and the child receives "the throne of **his father David**" (Luke 1:32), reckoned "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2).

**The record's verdict.** The seed rows require seed out of a man's loins and body "according to the flesh." The father is named by Mary, the narrator, the town, a disciple, and the angel. The reckoning is by the father's house. Therefore "Christ had no biological father" is contradicted by 2 Samuel 7:12; Psalm 132:11; Acts 2:30; Romans 1:3; 2 Timothy 2:8; Hebrews 7:14, quoted above at [L1]. The record holds: Christ had a biological father, Joseph, of the house of David.
