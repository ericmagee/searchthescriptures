## 1. HELD AT L1–L2–L3 (no charge on either side's list contests these)

1. **[L1]** The seed rows state Davidic and Judahite flesh-descent: "which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Sam 7:12); "Of the fruit of thy body" (Ps 132:11); "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30); "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Rom 1:3); "Jesus Christ of the seed of David" (2 Tim 2:8); "our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Heb 7:14). Neither seat disputes what these rows say; the dispute is what they require.
2. **[L1–L2]** Matthew 1:16's grammar: the passive ἐγεννήθη (egennēthē) and the feminine ἐξ ἧς (ex hēs). Both seats parse it the same; the inference is charged.
3. **[L1–L2]** Matthew 1:25's "knew her not till" (ἕως οὗ) states a bounded denial of knowing, bounded at the birth. Both agree on the span.
4. **[L1–L2]** Luke 1:34's "I know not a man" is present indicative — her state when she spoke, not a vow. Both agree.
5. **[L1]** Joseph is "of the house of David" (Luke 1:27), "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4), and is addressed "thou son of David" (Matt 1:20). Uncontested.
6. **[L1]** Mary's own mouth says "thy father" (Luke 2:48); the narrator says "his parents" (Luke 2:27, 41); the town says "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know" (John 6:42). The rows are uncontested; their probative force is charged.
7. **[L2]** The almah/bethulah facts: Genesis 24:16 uses בְּתוּלָה of Rebekah, Genesis 24:43 uses עַלְמָה of the same woman; Isaiah 7:14 has הָעַלְמָה; the 1611 margin reads "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call." The defender acknowledged the challenger "is accurate" on the lexical point.
8. **[L1–L3]** "Born of women" (Matt 11:11; Job 14:1, 15:14, 25:4) does not by itself exclude a named father — John's father is Zacharias (Luke 1:5, 13). The defender granted this "for argument."
9. **[L1–L2]** Hebrews 10:5's sentence is about sacrifice, and κατηρτίσω (katērtisō) is a "fitted/prepared" word (cf. Matt 4:21). Context and gloss uncontested; the inference is charged.
10. **[L1–L3]** Joseph and Mary were husband and wife in law from the espousal: "Joseph her husband" (Matt 1:19); "Mary thy wife" (Matt 1:20); a betrothed damsel is "his neighbour's wife" (Deut 22:24).

### (c) Charged points, both answers, unresolved

**A. What do the seed rows require?** Challenger [L1–L2]: five converging words (bowels, loins, body, fruit, sprang) state flesh from a man's body; ὀσφύς is Abraham's word at Hebrews 7:5, 10; "a change of verb names no absence of source." Defender: γενόμενον is the coming-to-be verb shared with Galatians 4:4; κατὰ σάρκα classifies kindred (Rom 9:3, 5; 4:1) and stands opposite κατὰ πνεῦμα. Its name-reckoning support (Deut 25:6; Gen 48:5) was conceded as floating; the kept limb is the Coniah ban. *Unresolved.*

**B. Matthew 1:16 — withholding or narration?** Defender: the active "begat" drops at the sixteenth link, the pronoun is feminine, so the begetting is withheld from Joseph. Challenger: "A passive names the one born; it narrates, it does not exclude," and the same Gospel names Joseph "son of David" (1:20). *Unresolved; the defender's R3 narrowed to "alone does not exclude."*

**C. The conception rows — paternity or covenant-origin?** Defender: ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου is the stated source, γεννηθέν carries the begetting root with ἐκ, διὸ καί ties sonship to the Spirit's coming, and the angel's answer to Joseph's intent is a statement of source. Challenger: ἐκ + God/Spirit is the canon's covenant-origin idiom (John 1:13; 1 John 4:7, 5:1, 3:9); ἐπισκιάσει is the transfiguration-cloud verb, ἐπέρχομαι is Pentecost's "come upon"; "when biological flesh is meant the text says κατὰ σάρκα (Romans 1:3)" and Matthew 1:20 does not; the title and throne are covenant words (Luke 1:32; 2 Sam 7:14; 1 Chr 28:6); Romans 1:4 declares by resurrection. *Unresolved.*

**D. Timing and the union.** Challenger: "till" bounds abstinence to the pregnancy; "she was found with child" dates the finding; the espousal is marriage in law; the canon's means is knowing; therefore the union fell within the espousal [L4, footed]. Defender: through the whole pregnancy Joseph was not knowing her; and the angel's answer is not "the child is yours." *Unresolved — the live joint.*

**E. Isaiah 7:14.** Defender: almah is used of a virgin; the offer was "in the depth, or in the height above"; the "you" is plural; Isaiah 8:3's child bears a different name; 8:8 addresses the land to Immanuel; "a near fulfilment in Isaiah's day and the fulfilment Matthew states do not conflict." Challenger: the sign's clock is the child's growth against two kings (7:15–16; 8:3–4); אוֹת does not require a miraculous birth (8:18); "Matthew states the fulfilment, and I set no scripture against scripture." *Unresolved.*

**F. Joseph's naming and fatherhood.** Challenger: five registers name him; ἐνομίζετο takes its verdict from its row (Acts 16:13 right; Luke 2:44 wrong); 1 Chr 28:6 sets covenant sonship beside a begotten son. Defender: Luke 3:23 marks the household title as a reckoning; Luke 2:49 names another Father; John 7:27–29 answers a crowd's "we know"; naming is acknowledgement (Luke 1:31 gives the same command to Mary; Ruth 4:17; Exod 2:10). *Unresolved.*

**G. "Of a woman" and "her seed."** Defender: Galatians 4:4 names the woman alone; Hebrews 2:14's κεκοινώνηκεν/μετέσχεν distinction; God prepared the body. Challenger: "born of women" is the mortality idiom with fathers named; "her seed" rows have fathers in the narrative (Gen 16:10; 24:60; Isa 54:3); Hebrews 10:5's body is fitted for the offering (10:7, 10). *Unresolved.*

## 3. GUARD TEST ON THE CHALLENGER SEAT

**DG-8 (biological father, both directions) — no violation.**
- It did not assert "no human father" as its own claim; it quoted the defender's phrase only to refute it: "cannot stand with 'no human male parent.'"
- It stated the union as the guard requires, at L4 with footing: "Therefore Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal. Footing: … Matthew 1:19, 20, 24, 25 ('till'); Deuteronomy 22:24; … Genesis 4:1; 2 Samuel 7:14; 1 Chronicles 28:6."
- It did not route the flesh through Mary; when the defender did, it pressed the claim and the defender dropped it.
- It kept "of the Holy Ghost" in its row, quoted whole: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."

**DG-18 (Son of God = covenant title) — no violation.** "1 Chronicles 28:6 — one verse, the son David begat and the covenant title God gave him"; "Romans 1:4's declaration is 'by the resurrection from the dead,' not by the conception."

**DG-56 (no invented genealogy construct) — no violation.** It cited the Salathiel/Neri divergence as L1 data without resolving it ("Luke 3:27 gives Salathiel 'which was the son of Neri'"), and its line "Deuteronomy 25:6 is one institution — a dead brother's name preserved through the living brother's child… adoption produces no seed" matches the record's own holding that the text institutes name-succession, not fatherhood.

**DG-59 (scripture never corrects scripture) — no violation.** It flagged the defender's move rather than making one: "Luke 2:49 does not 'correct' 2:48 — scripture does not correct scripture; both stand."

**DG-60 (seed, not blood) — no violation.** It argued in seed throughout and exhibited the promise; no blood-verdict was asserted in either direction.

**DG-61 (chain is the form of proof) — no violation.** Its premise list demanded exhibit of layers, not a single stating verse: "asserted without any exhibited layer — one verse named, no lexical ground, no cross-reference."

**DG-63 (second construction or UNVERIFIED) — two method exposures, no substance effect.**
1. "the ban is never cited against Jesus' throne anywhere in the New Testament" — a canon-wide negative with no count or verification mark; it is a supporting point beside genuinely cited rows (1 Chr 3:17–18; Luke 3:27; Jer 18:7–8).
2. "Every row is covenant/spiritual origin; none is a begetting" — a universal over the ἐκ + God/Spirit construction with four rows listed (John 1:13; 1 John 4:7, 5:1, 3:9).
Same family, not pressed: "a form of narration that coexists with a named father at every other row where the pair stands."

**Bottom line for this section:** no content guard was breached; the two DG-63 items are scope claims that should carry a count or an UNVERIFIED tag, and neither carries the case.

## 4. HOW THE CHALLENGER ENGAGED; DID THE DEFENSE HOLD?

The challenger engaged the defender's declared chain **as a chain** — taken in reverse order (link 6 down to link 1), each row quoted whole, each layer separated, each inference labeled. It read contexts wide rather than isolating terms: Matthew 1 entire (1:16–25), Isaiah 7–8 entire, Hebrews 10:5–10, Romans 1:3–4 against Romans 9:3, 5. It then applied the same exhibit standard to the defender's added claims and produced three concessions (Mary's line dropped; the levirate/adoption application conceded as floating; the "just man" maxim dropped). In its own link round it answered all six pressed premises with footing — six of six CITED-WITH-FOOTING, zero concessions — including the one [L4] it was required to foot (the union within the espousal).

The defense **held part of its ground**: the L2 grammar of Matthew 1:16 and 1:20, the plain sense of the timing/source rows ("before they came together," "knew her not till," "I know not a man"), and the valid point that "born of a woman" and "her seed" do not by themselves exclude a father — which the challenger granted. It did **not** hold its three added L4s (conceded in the link round), its "sign means beyond ordinary generation" gloss (not renewed), or its "Luke 2:49 corrects 2:48" framing (retracted to "answers point for point" and "a form of address"). Its central inference was restated as a chain consequence rather than an exhibit: "no room is the consequence, not a free-standing claim."

## 5. MOST VULNERABLE — NO VERDICT

**Challenger's most vulnerable joints:**
- The conception rows sit closest to the question and its answer runs through inference: the covenant-origin reading of ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου, and the [L4] that the union fell within the espousal. That L4 is footed and guard-authorized, but it is inference, not a stated row, and the strongest contrary data ("conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost"; "knew her not till"; Luke 1:34–35) stand exactly there.
- The Isaiah limb is the least necessary part of its case and the most exposed: it must hold Matthew's "all this was done, that it might be fulfilled" and a sign bounded in Isaiah's own book at once. The disclaimer does the work; if the bounded premise were pressed toward denying the virgin conception it would overrun the evidence the case actually needs.
- The two canon-wide negatives (DG-63) are the only guard-level findings against it.

**Defender's most vulnerable joints:**
- The inference from non-narration: the whole weight of "withheld" rests on a verb change the verse never glosses, and the defense itself narrowed it to "Matthew 1:16 alone does not exclude Joseph."
- Three added L4s conceded in the link round leave its chain shorter than it opened: no Mary-line route, no levirate/adoption route, no "just man" maxim.
- Its answer to the seed rows is reclassification ("made," "classifies kindred") rather than an exhibited row in which a child's flesh is of a father's line without a begetting; the five-word convergence (bowels, loins, body, fruit, sprang) stands as cited against it.

No verdict is delivered here; the ledger above is the record of what each side exhibited, what each conceded, and what stands charged on both sides.
