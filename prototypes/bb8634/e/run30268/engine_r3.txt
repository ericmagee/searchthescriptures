DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

# LINK ROUND

**LINK 6 [premise 1]: NARROWED TO:** the throne is promised to the child in words spoken to Mary before any union, and it is given by God. The claim that Mary herself is of David's line is dropped, because no KJV verse states her descent.

Footing:
- [L1] Luke 1:31-32 is addressed to Mary: "thou shalt conceive in thy womb... and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David."
- [L1] Mary's reply comes before any union: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34).
- [L1] The verb is "shall give," not "shall inherit by begetting."
- [L3] Luke 1:27 puts "of the house of David" after Joseph's name. Luke 2:4 says Joseph went up "because he was of the house and lineage of David." Luke 2:5 adds, "to be taxed with Mary his espoused wife, being great with child." That is registration by "the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2), and it names the household. It does not say whose seed formed the child.
- Acts 2:30 ("of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh"), Romans 1:3 ("made of the seed of David according to the flesh") and Hebrews 7:14 ("our Lord sprang out of Juda") state a Davidic and Judahite source of the flesh. They do not name which parent carried it. This premise therefore claims no more than the Luke 1 chain.

**LINK 6 [premise 2]: SUPPORTED, narrowed to the flesh-source named in the narrative.**
- [L1] The only source of the child's body named in the narrative is the mother. "Thou shalt conceive in thy womb" (Luke 1:31). "That holy thing which shall be born of thee" (Luke 1:35). "Blessed is the fruit of thy womb" (Luke 1:42). "That which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20).
- [L2] γενόμενον (genomenon, "having come to be") is the same verb in Romans 1:3, "made of the seed of David," and in Galatians 4:4, "made of a woman." The one participle names the one coming-to-be from both sources, and Galatians 4:4 adds no man.
- [L3] Luke 1:42 "fruit of thy womb" and Acts 2:30 "fruit of his loins" use the same noun, καρπός (karpos, "fruit"). The chain is: the fruit is of her womb, the fruit is of David's loins, and the narrative names no other parent for it.
- Against Matthew 11:11 "born of women" with Luke 1:5, 13: that idiom leaves a father named in the narrative. Here the narrative names none: "seeing I know not a man" (Luke 1:34).
- The pressed texts 2 Samuel 7:12 ("which shall proceed out of thy bowels") and Psalm 132:11 ("Of the fruit of thy body") state David's descendant. Neither names a father.

**LINK 6 [premise 3]: NARROWED TO:** the throne right does not come through Joseph's seed.

I concede that Deuteronomy 25:6 and Genesis 48:5 are analogies. No verse joins them to Luke 1:32, so that application floats.

Footing for the kept limb:
- [L1] Matthew 1:11-12 runs "Josias begat Jechonias... Jechonias begat Salathiel," and Matthew 1:16 ends at "Joseph the husband of Mary."
- [L1] Jeremiah 22:30 says "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David."
- [L3] The chain: Joseph is of Jechonias's line by Matthew's own list, and the ban reaches Jechonias's seed. A begotten son of Joseph would therefore be under the ban. Luke 1:32 says "the Lord God shall give."
- Against 1 Chronicles 3:17-18, Luke 3:27, Jeremiah 18:7-8 and Numbers 1:2: Jeremiah 22:30 says "no man of his seed shall prosper." Naming Jeconiah's sons does not lift that. Jeremiah 18:7-8 turns on repentance, and the text does not say Coniah repented. Numbers 1:2 reckons households, not fleshly formation.

**LINK 2: NARROWED TO:** the angel's answer in Matthew 1:20 shows what the ground of Joseph's intent was. The general maxim as I stated it is dropped.

Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost."
- [L1] Matthew 1:19: Joseph "being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily."
- [L1] Matthew 1:20 answers that intent directly: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." The remedy given is a statement of the child's source, so the source was the matter in doubt.
- [L3] Deuteronomy 22:23-24 shows what the law did with a betrothed damsel found with another man. Matthew 1:19 stands against that statute ("not willing to make her a publick example").

**LINK 5 [premise 5]: SUPPORTED.**
- [L1] Naming is not confined to begetters. Luke 1:31 gives the same command to Mary: "thou shalt call his name JESUS."
- [L3] Ruth 4:17: "the women her neighbours gave it a name, saying, There is a son born to Naomi." The namers were not the begetters, and Naomi was not the mother. Exodus 2:10: Pharaoh's daughter "called his name Moses." Genesis 4:25: "she called his name Seth."
- [L2] ἐκάλεσεν (ekalesen, "he called") in Matthew 1:25 is the verb of Matthew 1:21, and the angel's command in 1:20-21 comes right after "fear not to take... Mary." Naming is therefore what Joseph did on taking her, which is acknowledgement, not proof of begetting.
- The pressed texts Luke 1:13, 62-63 show a father naming. They do not say only a father names.

**LINK 5 [premise 6]: SUPPORTED.**
- [L1] Luke 2:48: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." Luke 2:49 answers it point for point: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" Luke 2:50 adds, "they understood not the saying."
- [L3] Luke 1:35 gives the child's source as "the Son of God." Luke 2:33 says "Joseph and his mother marvelled," and Luke 2:48 says "they were amazed."
- Romans 1:4, "declared to be the Son of God with power... by the resurrection from the dead," uses ὁρισθέντος (horisthentos, "marked out"), a declaring. It is not a making, so it does not displace Luke 1:35.
- 1 Chronicles 28:6 names a covenant sonship laid on a son already begotten. The child in Luke 2:49 names a Father whom Mary's "thy father" did not name. Luke 2:48's "thy father" is a form of address, not a verse stating begetting.

## CHALLENGER'S UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES

1. A change of verb is read as no change of source. Quote: "A change of verb names no absence of source." PRESSED WITH: Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David according to the flesh"; Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman"; Luke 1:35 "that holy thing which shall be born of thee".

2. "Till" is read to fix the date of Joseph's abstinence at the taking forward. Quote: "till" bounds it to the birth. PRESSED WITH: Matthew 1:18 "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost"; Luke 1:34 "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?".

3. A passive verb is read as no statement about the source. Quote: A passive verb is non-narration, not denial. PRESSED WITH: Matthew 1:16 "of whom was born Jesus"; Matthew 1:20 "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost".

4. "Out of holy spirit" is read as a covenant-origin marker and not a source. Quote: ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου (ek pneumatos hagiou, "out of holy spirit") is the covenant-origin marker. PRESSED WITH: Matthew 1:18 "found with child of the Holy Ghost"; Matthew 1:20 "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost"; Luke 1:35 "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee".

5. The body "prepared" is read as fitness for the offering only. Quote: Fitted for the offering. PRESSED WITH: Hebrews 10:5 "a body hast thou prepared me"; Luke 1:35 "that holy thing which shall be born of thee".

6. The Immanuel sign is read as bounded by Isaiah's own book. Quote: The sign is bounded in Isaiah's own book. PRESSED WITH: Isaiah 7:14 "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son"; Matthew 1:22-23 "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet".
