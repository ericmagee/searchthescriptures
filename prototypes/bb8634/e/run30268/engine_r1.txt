CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

# The Challenge: the seed is David's, and the father is named

**What is actually at issue.** Nobody disputes that God was at work in this child. The question is narrow and it is answered in the words themselves: was there a man's seed? The text answers it in a phrase it uses more than once — "according to the flesh." "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of **the seed of David according to the flesh**" (Romans 1:3) [L1]. "Of **the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30) [L1]. Seed out of loins, "according to the flesh," is a begetting. Every link below has to survive that.

---

## LINK 6 — the keystone: "Christ had no biological father"

Contrary [L1], quoted whole:

> "I will set up thy seed after thee, **which shall proceed out of thy bowels**, and I will establish his kingdom." (2 Samuel 7:12)

> "Therefore being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him, that **of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne;" (Acts 2:30)

> "The LORD hath sworn in truth unto David; he will not turn from it; **Of the fruit of thy body** will I set upon thy throne." (Psalm 132:11)

> "For **it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda**; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood." (Hebrews 7:14)

> "Remember that **Jesus Christ of the seed of David** was raised from the dead;" (2 Timothy 2:8)

[L2] Acts 2:30's "fruit of his loins" is καρπὸς τῆς ὀσφύος (karpos tēs osphyos, "fruit of the loins"), ὀσφύς (osphys, "loins") — the same word of Abraham in Hebrews 7:5, 10 ("though they come **out of the loins of Abraham**"). "According to the flesh" is κατὰ σάρκα (kata sarka, "according to flesh").

[L3] And the text reckons that seed **by the father's house**: "Take ye the sum of all the congregation of the children of Israel, **after their families, by the house of their fathers**, with the number of their names, every male by their polls" (Numbers 1:2). Joseph is "of the house of David" (Luke 1:27) and "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4), and the child receives "the throne of **his father David**" (Luke 1:32).

[L4] Seed proceeding out of a man's loins and body "according to the flesh," and a tribe counted by the father's house, cannot stand with "no human male parent." Footing: [L1] 2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30; Psalm 132:11; Hebrews 7:14; [L2] osphys, kata sarka; [L3] Numbers 1:2 with Luke 1:27; 2:4.

And the father is **named**, by five different speakers: Mary — "behold, **thy father** and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48); the narrator — "**his parents** went to Jerusalem" (Luke 2:41), "when **the parents** brought in the child Jesus" (Luke 2:27), "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (Luke 2:33); the town — "Is not this Jesus, **the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know**?" (John 6:42); "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22); a disciple — "Jesus of Nazareth, **the son of Joseph**" (John 1:45); the angel — "**Joseph, thou son of David**" (Matthew 1:20). [L1] at every line.

## LINK 5 — "supposition and legal standing, not begetting"

[L2] ἐνομίζετο (enomizeto, "was reckoned / was wont") decides nothing by itself: it is the same verb form as "where prayer **was wont** to be made" (Acts 16:13), where the reckoning was right; and Luke 2:44, where "supposing him to have been in the company" was **wrong**. One word, two outcomes — the row's context decides.

Contrary [L1]: Luke 2:48 is not the crowd's supposition; it is the **mother's own mouth** to the child. John 6:42 says "we **know**," not "we suppose." Luke 2:49 does not "correct" 2:48 — scripture does not correct scripture; both stand. Two registers, exactly as when God says of Solomon in one verse: "Solomon **thy son**, he shall build my house... for I have chosen him **to be my son**, and I will be his father" (1 Chronicles 28:6) [L1] [L3]. A covenant title travels alongside a named biological father; it does not erase him. And Romans 1:4 declares the sonship "**by the resurrection from the dead**," not by the conception [L1].

## LINK 4 — Isaiah 7:14's sign

Contrary [L1]: the sign is bounded **inside Isaiah's own book**. "Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel" (7:14). Then: "Butter and honey shall he eat, that he may know to refuse the evil, and choose the good. **For before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good**, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken of both her kings" (7:15-16). And 8:3-4: "I went unto the prophetess; and she conceived, and bare a son... **For before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother**, the riches of Damascus and the spoil of Samaria shall be taken away." And 8:8: "the stretching out of his wings shall fill the breadth of thy land, **O Immanuel**." The sign is given to Ahaz in his own day and expires at a child's weaning.

[L2] Two different Hebrew words are used of the **same woman** in Genesis 24: Genesis 24:16 has בְּתוּלָה (bethulah, "virgin") — "a virgin, neither had any man known her" — and Genesis 24:43 has עַלְמָה (almah, "young woman"). Isaiah 7:14 has the **young-woman** word, הָעַלְמָה (ha-almah, "the young woman"). The 1611 margin at Isaiah 7:14 carries the alternate "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call."

[L1] "A sign" (אוֹת, 'ôṯ) does **not** mean a birth beyond ordinary generation: "Behold, I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are **for signs and for wonders** in Israel" (Isaiah 8:18) — ordinary children, named as signs.

[L4] The sign-word is the young-woman word; "conceiving without a man" is nowhere in Isaiah 7:14; the sign expires before the child can say "my father." Footing: [L1] Isaiah 7:14-16; 8:3-4, 8, 18; Genesis 24:16; [L2] bethulah / almah. Matthew 1:22-23's inspired application stands as written — but it does not rewrite the Hebrew word or lift the sign out of its own chapter.

## LINK 3 — "the human source is the woman alone"

Contrary [L1]: "**made of a woman**" is the mortality idiom, and it is said of a man whose father is named. "Among them that are **born of women** there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" (Matthew 11:11) — and John's father is Zacharias: "a certain priest named Zacharias... and his wife was of the daughters of Aaron, and her name was Elisabeth" (Luke 1:5); "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son" (Luke 1:13). So "born of a woman" excludes no father. Same idiom of all mankind: "Man that is born of a woman is of few days" (Job 14:1); "he which is born of a woman, that he should be righteous?" (15:14); "how can he be clean that is born of a woman?" (25:4) [L3]. [L2] γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός (genomenon ek gynaikos, "coming to be out of a woman") is the phrasing shared with the Job texts.

Contrary [L1]: "her seed" is a mother's offspring wherever it appears — Hagar: "I will multiply thy seed exceedingly" (Genesis 16:10); Rebekah: "be thou the mother of thousands of millions" (Genesis 24:60); Zion: "thy seed shall inherit the Gentiles" (Isaiah 54:3) [L3]. Genesis 3:15 is one of a family, and none of them is a fatherless-conception claim.

Contrary [L1]: Hebrews 10:5 says "**a body hast thou prepared me**" inside a sentence about **sacrifice** — "Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me: in burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin thou hast had no pleasure" (Hebrews 10:5-6). [L2] κατηρτίσω (katērtisō, "thou hast fitted / prepared") is the fitting word of mending nets (Matthew 4:21) and of perfecting (Hebrews 13:21). Prepared for the offering; not a statement about whose seed formed it.

[L4] These three texts give the mother and God as the human and divine source in their own idioms; none of them says a man's seed was absent. Footing: [L1] Matthew 11:11; Luke 1:5, 13; Job 14:1; 15:14; 25:4; Genesis 16:10; 24:60; Isaiah 54:3; Hebrews 10:5-6; [L2] genomenon ek gynaikos, katērtisō.

## LINK 2 — "of the Holy Ghost," before union, no man known

Contrary [L1]: "And knew her not **till** she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS" (Matthew 1:25). The word "till" states a span and bounds it to the pregnancy.

Contrary [L1]: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man..." (1:19); "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee **Mary thy wife**" (1:20); "Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and **took unto him his wife**" (1:24). Husband and wife in law from the espousal — and the law calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's **wife**" (Deuteronomy 22:24) [L3]. The canon's stated means of begetting is knowing: "Adam **knew** Eve his wife: and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1) [L3].

[L2] Matthew 1:18's συνελθεῖν (synelthein, "to come together") names married union at 1 Corinthians 7:5 ("come together again"); the sentence dates the **finding** — "she was **found** with child" — not the begetting. Luke 1:34's ἄνδρα οὐ γινώσκω (andra ou ginōskō, "I know not a man") is **present indicative**: her state when the angel spoke, not a vow.

[L2] Luke 1:35's verbs are not begetting words anywhere: ἐπισκιάσει (episkiasei, "shall overshadow") is the transfiguration cloud (Matthew 17:5; Mark 9:7; Luke 9:34) and Peter's healing shadow (Acts 5:15); "come upon" (ἐπέρχομαι, eperchomai) is "after that the Holy Ghost is **come upon** you" (Acts 1:8) of the twelve at Pentecost — anointing and presence, not generation.

[L3] "Of the Holy Ghost" is the covenant-origin marker: "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14) said of Solomon, and "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6) — one verse, the son David begat and the title God gave him. And when biological flesh **is** meant, the text says so: "according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3; Acts 2:30). Matthew 1:20 does not.

[L4] Therefore Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:19, 20, 24, 25 ("till"); Deuteronomy 22:24; [L2] ginōskō, heōs hou, synelthein; [L3] Genesis 4:1; 2 Samuel 7:14; 1 Chronicles 28:6.

## LINK 1 — Matthew 1:16

Contrary [L1]: the verse **states a birth**, not a withholding: "Jacob begat Joseph **the husband of Mary**, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." It never says "not of Joseph." A verb changing to the passive names no absence; it simply does not narrate the begetting. Meanwhile the same Gospel has the angel say "Joseph, **thou son of David**" (1:20) and gives the child that throne (Luke 1:32) [L1].

Contrary [L1]: the genealogy runs **through Joseph** — "a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, **of the house of David**" (Luke 1:27); "because he was of **the house and lineage of David**" (Luke 2:4); "the throne of **his father David**" (Luke 1:32). [L3] reckoned "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2).

[L4] Non-narration is not denial: one passive verb does not outweigh a named father in five registers and seed "out of the loins... according to the flesh." Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:16, 20; Luke 1:27; 2:4; John 6:42; [L3] Numbers 1:2.

## UNSUPPORTED [L4] PREMISES

1. *"Because the one verb 'begat' governs fifteen links and is deliberately dropped at the sixteenth, and because the pronoun is feminine (footings: Matthew 1:2-16 and the Greek grammar of ex hēs), the text withholds from Joseph the begetting it grants to every other father in the list."* — "Deliberately dropped" and "withholds" are supplied; the verse names no withholding, and Romans 1:3 / Acts 2:30 state the flesh seed.

2. *"Together they exclude Joseph, or any man, as the begetter."* — The rows state time, source and a question; none states exclusion. "Exclude" is the very claim at issue.

3. *"The text therefore gives no room for a human father's seed in the body of the child."* — Contradicted by Matthew 11:11 with Luke 1:5, 13 (John "born of women," father named) and by Acts 2:30 ("fruit of his loins, according to the flesh").

4. *"A 'sign' (Isaiah 7:14, אוֹת, 'ôṯ, 'sign') means something beyond ordinary generation."* — No lexical footing for "beyond ordinary generation"; Isaiah 8:18 names ordinary children "for signs and for wonders."

5. *"The sign is a woman conceiving without a man, and Matthew declares it fulfilled in Mary."* — "Without a man" is not in Isaiah 7:14; the sign-word is the young-woman word (Genesis 24:43), and 7:16 / 8:3-4 bound the sign to a child of Isaiah's day.

6. *"The text itself separates the opinion of the crowd from the fact, so every 'father' passage naming Joseph rests on legal reckoning and public supposition (L1, L2). It cannot be pressed as a statement of begetting."* — "Every" excludes Mary's own mouth ("thy father and I," Luke 2:48) and the narrator's "his parents" (Luke 2:27, 41); enomizeto carries no verdict (Acts 16:13).
