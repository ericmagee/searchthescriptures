CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

**LINK 1: SUPPORTED** — the change of verb narrates the event from the mother's side and states no absence of source.
- [L1] The pressed rows name a source in their own words: Romans 1:3 "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh"; Galatians 4:4 "But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law"; Luke 1:35 "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
- [L2] The verbs: Matthew 1:16's egennēthē (G1080, "was born") is passive after thirty-nine active egennēsen ("begat") tokens in the same list (the count as served at Sight 1578 item 5); genomenou (G1096, "having come to be") in Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 marks the becoming and occurs where the source *is* named — "of the seed of David," "of a woman." A passive names the one born; it narrates, it does not exclude.
- [L3] "Made of a woman" is the mortality idiom of Job 14:1 "Man that is born of a woman is of few days"; Job 15:14; Job 25:4; Matthew 11:11 "Among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" — and John's father is named in the same gospel: Luke 1:5 "a certain priest named Zacharias, of the course of Abia: and his wife was of the daughters of Aaron"; Luke 1:13 "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son."
- Chain: born of a woman / made of a woman rows are spoken of men whose fathers are named; "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" names a male source. So a shift from "begat" to "was born" or "made" names no absence of source. The premise stands.

**LINK 2: SUPPORTED** — and the union is stated as the rows carry it.
- [L1] Matthew 1:25 "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS." The row itself places the bound at the birth. The pressed rows stand as written: Matthew 1:18 "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" — that dates the *finding*; Luke 1:34 "Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"
- [L2] ouk eginōsken autēn heōs hou eteken: eginōsken (G1097, imperfect, "was knowing") denied up to heōs hou (G2193/G3739, "till when") with eteken (G5088, "brought forth") — a denial of stated duration, not a vow; and Luke 1:34's andra ou ginōskō (G1097) is present active indicative, her state when she spoke.
- [L3] The marriage rows: Matthew 1:19 "Then Joseph her husband"; Matthew 1:20 "Mary thy wife"; Deuteronomy 22:24 "the man, because he hath humbled his neighbour's wife"; Genesis 4:1 "And Adam knew Eve his wife: and she conceived."
- [L4, footed on those rows] Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal: the canon's stated means of begetting is knowing (Genesis 4:1), the law names the espoused pair husband and wife (Deuteronomy 22:24), and the "till" row bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy. This is built on the quoted rows, not on any silence.

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED** — the passive is narration from the mother's side; the father is named in the same book and town.
- [L1] Matthew 1:16 "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ"; Matthew 1:20 "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." The pressed rows are quoted whole, and the row that speaks of origin speaks covenant origin (see below).
- [L2] egennēthē (G1080, passive, "was born") with ex hēs ("of whom") states the one born and the one bearing. A passive of birth does not state a verdict on the begetter.
- [L3] The same mother-side narration stands beside a named father at other rows: Luke 2:7 "And she brought forth her firstborn son, and wrapped him in swaddling clothes" — no father named in that row — with Luke 2:48 "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing," John 6:42 "Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know," and Luke 4:22 "Is not this Joseph's son?"
- Chain: a mother-side clause is a form of narration that coexists with a named father at every other row where the pair stands; Matthew 1:16 is that form. It is non-narration of the begetting, and the affirmative witnesses name Joseph.

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED** — ek + God/Spirit is covenant-origin language throughout the New Testament.
- [L1] The pressed rows: Matthew 1:18 "she was found with child of the Holy Ghost"; Matthew 1:20 "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost"; Luke 1:35 "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee." Note the angel's "for" grounds the command to *take* her — and the same speech gives the title and throne in the covenant word: Luke 1:32 "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David"; 2 Samuel 7:14 "I will be his father, and he shall be my son."
- [L3] The canon's ek + God/Spirit rows: John 1:13 "Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God"; 1 John 4:7 "every one that loveth is born of God"; 1 John 5:1 "Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is born of God"; 1 John 3:9. Every row is covenant/spiritual origin; none is a begetting. And the flesh marker is a separate phrase: Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**" — kata sarka appears there and not at Matthew 1:20.
- [L2] ek (G1537) + the genitive names origin; the human source in the same passage is expressed of Mary ("of whom," ex hēs, Matthew 1:16).
- Chain: "of God / of the Spirit" is the origin idiom of covenant and new birth; "according to the flesh" is the idiom of bodily descent, and it is present where flesh is meant. "Of the Holy Ghost" is the covenant-origin marker, not paternity.

**LINK 5: SUPPORTED** — Hebrews 10:5 speaks of the body as the offering, fitted to do the will.
- [L1] Hebrews 10:5 "Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me"; 10:7 "Then said I, Lo, I come (in the volume of the book it is written of me,) to do thy will, O God"; 10:10 "By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all." The body is named inside the sentence about the rejected sacrifices and is laid down to do the will — the row's subject is the offering.
- [L2] katērtisō (G2675, "fitted, made ready, prepared") is a readiness word; it names fitness, and the fitness named is for the offering and the will.
- [L3] Hebrews 2:14 "Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same"; 2:17 "Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren." The flesh he took is the children's, "likewise," and in *all* things he is made like them. Luke 1:35's "that holy thing which shall be born of thee" is the mother-side of that same childlikeness.
- Chain: prepared = fitted for the offering and the will; the flesh is the brethren's flesh. The row is about fitness, and it is not evidence about the begetting.

**LINK 6: SUPPORTED** — the sign's own clock and its second child are in Isaiah's words.
- [L1] Isaiah 7:14 "Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel"; 7:15 "Butter and honey shall he eat, that he may know to refuse the evil, and choose the good"; 7:16 "For before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken of both her kings." The stated clock is the child's own growth against the two kings.
- [L1] Isaiah 8:3-4 "And I went unto the prophetess; and she conceived, and bare a son. Then said the LORD to me, Call his name Mahershalalhashbaz. For before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother, the riches of Damascus and the spoil of Samaria shall be taken away"; 8:8 "and the stretching out of his wings shall fill the breadth of thy land, O Immanuel"; 8:18 "Behold, I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are for signs and for wonders in Israel."
- [L2] ha-almah (H5959, "the young woman") harah ("with child") ve-yoledet ben ("and bearing a son") — the young-woman word; the KJV prints "a virgin" and the 1611 margin reads "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call"; bethulah (H1330) is not the word of this verse.
- [L1] The pressed row, quoted whole: Matthew 1:22-23 "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel." Matthew states the fulfilment, and I set no scripture against scripture; the premise kept is the sign's bound as Isaiah speaks it — butter and honey, the child's discernment, the two kings' land forsaken, the name in the invasion of 8:8, and the children as signs at 8:18.
- Chain: the sign's timing, its second child, its name and its sign-children are all inside Isaiah 7-8. The sign is bounded within Isaiah's own book.
