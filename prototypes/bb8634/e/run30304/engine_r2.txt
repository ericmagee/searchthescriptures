CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 2, CLOSING AND PREMISE LIST

# CLOSING — the flesh row is one row: the loins and the throne are in the same sentence

**A. The keystone fails at Acts 2:30.** The defender now answers "evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14) by splitting "two routes": a legal route through Joseph and a flesh route "through the woman." [L1] The text will not split: Acts 2:30 has **flesh and throne in one sentence** — "of the fruit of his loins, **according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ **to sit on his throne**." Romans 1:3 likewise: "made of the seed of David **according to the flesh**." [L1] And the birth chapters name the one Davidic man: "Joseph, **of the house of David**" (Luke 1:27); "he was **of the house and lineage of David**" (Luke 2:4). [L4, footing [L1] Acts 2:30, Romans 1:3, Luke 1:27, 2:4 + [L2] *kata sarka*, *osphys*]: there is one named line, and the flesh row requires a man's body down it.

**B. Loins at depth never deletes the begetter.** [L1] The defender's own examples name a father at every generation: Genesis 46:26's souls "out of his loins" are Jacob's sons and grandsons, each begetting named ("Judah... Levi," Genesis 35:23-26); Hebrews 7:9-10's Levi in Abraham's loins runs "Isaac begat Jacob; and Jacob begat Levi" — and **Levi's mother was Leah** (Genesis 35:23 "the sons of Leah; Levi"). Jochebed was not Levi's mother; she is of his line (Exodus 6:20 "Amram took him Jochebed his father's sister to wife"). [L4, footing [L1] + [L3]]: depth of the idiom is granted; at no row does it remove a father. The begetter is named at every step the defender cites.

**C. The timing rows, as written.** [L1] Matthew 1:19 states **intent and two grounds** — "being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily" — and never the ground of the intent. The angel does not say "she was unfaithful"; he says "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: **for** that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (1:20) — the "for" grounds the command to take. [L1/L3] The law calls the espoused woman "his neighbour's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:24); the canon's stated means is "Adam **knew** Eve his wife: and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1). [L1] "Knew her not **till** she had brought forth her firstborn son" (1:25) bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy. [L4, footing [L1] Matthew 1:19-20, 25 + [L3] Deuteronomy 22:24; Genesis 4:1]: **Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal**, and none of the conception rows says she conceived without a man.

**D. "How" is the barren-woman question, not the virginity question.** [L1] The angel's own answer points to the precedent: "thy cousin Elisabeth, **who was called barren**, hath also conceived a son in her old age" — "For **with God nothing shall be impossible**" (Luke 1:36-37). Elisabeth's "how" was answered by her husband: "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son" (1:13), and "he called his name John" (1:63). [L4, footing [L1] + [L3]]: a question about means is never a statement that no man is involved.

**E. Registers and *ek*.** [L1/L2] The narrator's own word is *goneis* (those who parented) — "the parents brought in the child Jesus" (Luke 2:27); "his parents went to Jerusalem" (2:41). *Enomizeto* carries no verdict of its own: it is the verb of "where prayer **was wont** to be made" (Acts 16:13). [L1] And the list runs from "the son of Joseph" through Heli, David, Adam to God (Luke 3:23-38) — whatever the article, the route is Joseph's. [L2/L3] *Ek* + God names covenant origin at rows of men with fathers — John 1:13 "born of God" of believers; when the canon marks flesh it says *kata sarka* (Romans 1:3). The angel's verbs are presence and power at every row: *eperchomai* (come upon) is Acts 1:8 "come upon you" (the apostles); *episkiazō* (overshadow) is the cloud (Luke 9:34) and Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15).

**F. The sign and the bar.** [L1] The sign is bounded in its own chapter: "before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good" (Isaiah 7:16); "before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother" (8:3-4); and "**I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are for signs** and for wonders in Israel" (8:18) — Isaiah's own children, born of the prophetess. A sign proves a sign; it does not state a mechanism. [L1] The Jeconiah bar is never applied to Jesus by any row: his sons are listed (1 Chronicles 3:17-18), the signet is restored to Zerubbabel (Haggai 2:23), and Jeremiah's own rule stands — "If that nation... turn from their evil, **I will repent of the evil**" (18:7-8). Luke 3:27's Salathiel is "of Neri," and the text never says whether it is the same man. [L4, footing [L1] + [L3]]: the "collision" is the defender's construction.
