**LINK 1: SUPPORTED.**

Footing:
[L1] The promise names the seed as out of the man's own body: 2 Samuel 7:12 "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels"; Psalm 132:11 "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne"; 1 Kings 8:19 "thy son that shall come forth out of thy loins"; Acts 2:30 "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne"; Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David according to the flesh."
[L2] The body-words: 2 Samuel 7:12 *mimme'eykha* (from thy bowels); Acts 2:30 *osphys* (loins) with *kata sarka* (according to flesh); Romans 1:3 *sperma* (seed) with *kata sarka* (according to flesh) — each a body-word of the man addressed, David.
[L3] The reckoning runs down the father's body: Hebrews 7:9-10 "Levi also, who receiveth tithes, paid tithes in Abraham. For he was yet in the loins of his father, when Melchisedec met him"; Numbers 1:2, 18 "by the house of their fathers... declared their pedigrees."
Chain [L4]: the promised seed proceeds out of David's bowels and loins, and the same seed is named of the child — "made of the seed of David according to the flesh." A seed out of a man's loins requires a man's body as its channel; the only line the text names for that house is Joseph's — "Joseph, of the house of David" (Luke 1:27), "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4). The pressed position would route the flesh through the woman alone; no verse states her Davidic line, which is now conceded.

Pressed verses: Matthew 1:16 "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus" — *hēs* (of whom), feminine singular, attributes the birth to the one who bore; every birth is borne by a mother, and the same clause names "the husband of Mary." It does not state whose seed is the channel. Luke 1:35 "that holy thing which shall be born of thee" states the mother and is answered in the same gospel by "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48). Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman" is the mortality idiom — Job 14:1 "man that is born of a woman is of few days"; Job 25:4 "how can he be clean that is born of woman?"; Matthew 11:11 "them that are born of women" (of John, whose father is named in Luke 1:13) — said of every man who has a father, and naming no Davidic channel. Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30 state the seed and the loins, quoted above. Ezra 2:61 "took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai the Gileadite, and was called after their name" is answered by Ezra 2:62: "These sought their register among those that were reckoned by genealogy, but they were not found: therefore were they, as polluted, put from the priesthood" — the wife's-house register was refused. Numbers 32:41 "Jair the son of Manasseh" has its begetter named at 1 Chronicles 2:21-22 "Segub begat Jair." Hebrews 7:14 "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" is evidenced by the law's own reckoning, Numbers 1:2 "by the house of their fathers" and Hebrews 7:5 "they that are of the sons of Levi... take tithes of the people according to the law."

**LINK 3: SUPPORTED.**

Footing:
[L1] The seed rows require seed out of the man's body (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30; Romans 1:3, quoted above); the canon's stated means of begetting is the husband knowing his wife — Genesis 4:1 "And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain"; Joseph is husband from the espousal — Matthew 1:19 "Joseph her husband"; Matthew 1:20 "Mary thy wife"; Matthew 1:19 he must "put her away" to part; Luke 2:48 "thy father and I."

Matthew 1:25 "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" — *heōs hou* (till) bounds the stated abstinence. Luke 1:34 *andra ou ginōskō* (I know not a man) is present indicative — the state when the angel spoke.
Chain [L4], footed on those rows: Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal; the child is of his loins. Read inside that chain, the timing rows state: Matthew 1:18 "before they came together, she was found with child" dates the finding, and Matthew sets what came together by what follows — 1:24 "took unto him his wife," her coming into his house (*synerchomai*, come together, names married union at 1 Corinthians 7:5 "come together again"); 1:25's abstinence is the pregnancy after that taking.
Pressed verses: Matthew 1:18 dates the finding, and "of the Holy Ghost" is the covenant word of the child God claims — "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" (2 Samuel 7:14), with "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32). Matthew 1:20 "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" — the "for" grounds the command to take the wife and keep the child, and the same verse names "thou son of David" and "Mary thy wife." Deuteronomy 22:24 "his neighbour's wife" is my footing, and the pressed case of 22:23-24 is "a damsel that is a virgin betrothed unto an husband, and a man find her in the city, and lie with her" — a man other than the husband; Matthew 1:19 at the word level reads *dikaios œn* (being a just man), *mē thelōn autēn paradeigmatisai* (not willing to make her a publick example), *eboulēthē lathra apolysai autēn* (was minded to put her away privily) — intent and two grounds, no verb of knowledge, and no stated ground that the child is not his. Matthew 1:25 "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" states abstinence and states its bound.

**LINK 4: SUPPORTED.**

Footing:
[L1] Luke 1:34 "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" — "How shall this be" asks the manner; the ground clause is her present state.
[L2] *andra ou ginōskō* (I know not a man) is present active indicative; a permanent negation would take *ou mē* (not at all) with the aorist subjunctive. The row states her state, not a vow.
[L3] The parallel question: Luke 1:18 "Whereby shall I know this? for I am an old man, and my wife well stricken in years," answered at Luke 1:13 "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son" — the question carried no verdict in either case; and the same gospel names the father afterwards, Luke 2:48 "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing."
Chain [L4]: a question of means states the asker's present state and asks the manner; it does not state that no man is involved.
Pressed verses: Luke 1:34 quoted. Luke 1:35 "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee" — *eperchomai* (shall come upon) is the empowering word (Acts 1:8 "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you" — of the apostles, each with a father; 1 Samuel 16:13 "the Spirit of the LORD came upon David"), and *episkiazō* (shall overshadow) is the presence-cloud word (Luke 9:34 the cloud; Acts 5:15 "his shadow"); neither verb denotes begetting at any row, and the sentence answers with the child's standing, "shall be called the Son of God." Luke 1:18 and 1:13 show the question-form answered with a means — "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son" — and the question itself issues no verdict.

Footing:
[L1] Isaiah 7:14 "Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel" — the row calls itself a sign and says "behold."
[L2] The word at the row is *almah* (young woman); the served count (S-1578): *almah* = 7 rows, *bethulah* (virgin) = 50 rows / 19 books, 9 in the Torah, and *bethulah* is not used at Isaiah 7:14; Matthew 1:23's received Greek is *hē parthenos* (the virgin).

Chain [L4]: a sign names what is to be looked on; it does not state the mechanism of the conceiving — the conceiving is announced ("shall conceive, and bear a son"), not explained.
Pressed verses: Isaiah 7:14 and Matthew 1:23 "Behold, a virgin shall be with child" both announce the conception as a sign; neither states a mechanism. The mechanism is stated where the text states it: seed out of the man's body (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30) and the husband knowing his wife (Genesis 4:1).

The six premises each stand on exhibited footing, and the defender's narrowing left the seed rows unanswered: the flesh row requires a man's body down the named line (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30; Romans 1:3), and no row removes the father the text names.
