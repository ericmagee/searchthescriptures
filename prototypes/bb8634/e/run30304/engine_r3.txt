DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

**LINK 5 [premise 1]: NARROWED TO:** the flesh-descent is *of the woman*, and Joseph's seed is nowhere stated as the channel. I drop any claim that a verse states Mary's Davidic lineage in so many words.

Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus." The "begat" pattern stops at Joseph. The relative "of whom" is *hēs*, feminine singular, "of her", so the birth is attributed to Mary alone.
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "that holy thing which shall be born of thee."
- [L1] Galatians 4:4: "made of a woman."
- [L2] *kata sarka* (according to flesh) in Romans 1:3 says what kind of descent it is. It does not say through whom.
- [L3] Luke 1:32: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David." This is joined to Luke 1:31, "thou shalt conceive in thy womb."
- [L4] On that footing, the flesh-row fits the woman's line. The pressed verses do not say otherwise:
  - Acts 2:30: "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh."
  - Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh."
  - 2 Samuel 7:12: "which shall proceed out of thy bowels."
  - Psalm 132:11: "Of the fruit of thy body."
  - 1 Kings 8:19: "thy son that shall come forth out of thy loins."
  - Luke 1:27 and 2:4: "Joseph, of the house of David" and "of the house and lineage of David."
  - Numbers 1:2: "by the house of their fathers."
  - Hebrews 7:14: "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda."
  - Genesis 4:1: "Adam knew Eve his wife: and she conceived."

  None of these names whose body carries the line. Genesis 4:1 describes an ordinary conception and does not say all conceptions are ordinary.

**LINK 5 [premise 2]: NARROWED TO:** a register can follow the wife's house, so Hebrews 7:14 does not require a named father-line. I concede that this does not by itself prove the case.

Footing:
- [L1] Ezra 2:61: a priest "took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai the Gileadite, and was called after their name." Here the register follows the wife's house.
- [L3] 1 Chronicles 2:22, "And Segub begat Jair," read with Numbers 32:41, "Jair the son of Manasseh." Jair is counted to Manasseh, which is his mother's line (1 Chronicles 2:21).
- [L4] Ezra 2:62 ("as polluted, put from the priesthood") shows registers are enforced. It does not show that they run only by the father. Numbers 1:2 states a general rule, and Ezra 2:61 is an instance where the rule bends.

**LINK 2 [premise 3]: SUPPORTED.**

Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." The Greek *synelthein* (to come together) places the pregnancy before any union.
- [L1] Matthew 1:19: Joseph is "minded to put her away privily." The ground of his intent is that the child is not his.
- [L1] Matthew 1:20: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." The "for" answers the very worry that she had conceived by another.
- [L3] Deuteronomy 22:23-24: the espoused woman is "his neighbour's wife," and unfaithfulness in the espousal is a capital matter. That is why Joseph, being "a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example," reasons as he does.
- [L4] The chain is 1:18 (no union before the pregnancy), then 1:19 (the pregnancy is a cause for putting her away), then 1:20 (the answer). "Knew her not till she had brought forth" (1:25) bounds the abstinence and does not assert a prior union. If Joseph had known her within the espousal, "neighbour's wife" would give him no problem.

**LINK 2 [premise 4]: SUPPORTED.**

Footing:
- [L1] Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" The Greek *ginōskō* (I know) is present tense, "I am not knowing."
- [L1] Luke 1:35 answers not with "thy husband shall," but with "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee."
- [L3] Contrast Zacharias in Luke 1:18, who asked on the ground of age: "I am an old man, and my wife well stricken in years." The angel answered him with Luke 1:13, "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son." Mary's stated ground is different: "I know not a man."
- [L4] Luke 1:36-37 ("who was called barren," "with God nothing shall be impossible") offers Elisabeth as an "also," a parallel in the impossible. It does not make the two cases identical in means. The means differ, as the two answers (1:13 and 1:35) show.

**LINK 2 [premise 5]: NARROWED TO:** Matthew 1:20 marks the begetting as "of the Holy Ghost" where the genealogy had a father. I drop John 1:13 as a load-bearing limb.

Footing:
- [L2] Matthew 1:20 uses *gennēthen* (that which is begotten/conceived) with *ek* (out of). The genealogy of 1:2-16 uses the same verb, *egennēsen* (begat), of fathers. The begetting is here ascribed *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of the Holy Spirit), where a father stood in every other row.
- [L1] Luke 1:35: "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." The *dio* (therefore) ties the sonship to the Spirit's coming.
- [L4] The pressed verses do not remove this footing:
  - John 1:13: "born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh... but of God."
  - 1 John 5:1: "born of God."
  - Romans 1:3: "according to the flesh."
  - Acts 1:8: "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you."
  - Luke 9:34: "the cloud."
  - Acts 5:15: "his shadow."

  The believers' birth is spiritual and says nothing of the conception row. The verbs *eperchomai* (come upon) and *episkiazō* (overshadow) are read in their sentence. That sentence, Luke 1:34-35, answers a question about "I know not a man."

**LINK 4: NARROWED TO:** Joseph's naming is an act done under command, in the legal role the text gives him. I concede that naming alone does not prove that role.

Footing:
- [L1] Matthew 1:20-21: Joseph is addressed as "thou son of David" and told "thou shalt call his name JESUS." Matthew 1:25 records "he called his name JESUS."
- [L1] Luke 3:23: "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph." The Greek *enomizeto* (was supposed) is the verb of mistaken supposition in Acts 14:19, "supposing he had been dead." He was not dead. Acts 16:13's "was wont" is the customary sense, but Luke 3:23 sets the word beside "as" and the genealogy's own contrast.
- [L3] The pressed verses reflect the household's own speech and the crowd's:
  - Luke 2:27: "the parents brought in the child Jesus."
  - Luke 2:41: "his parents went to Jerusalem."
  - Luke 2:48: "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing."
  - John 6:42: "whose father and mother we know."
  - John 1:45: "the son of Joseph."

  They are answered in the next breath by Luke 2:49: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"
- [L4] The legal role stands. The biological claim is carried by 1:18-20, not by the naming.

## CHALLENGER'S UNSUPPORTED-L4 PREMISES

1. The flesh-row requires a man's body down the Davidic line. It props the Acts 2:30 and Romans 1:3 objection. Quote: "there is one named line, and the flesh row requires a man's body down it." PRESSED WITH: Matthew 1:16 "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus"; Luke 1:35 "that holy thing which shall be born of thee"; Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman"; Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David according to the flesh"; Ezra 2:61 "took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai the Gileadite, and was called after their name"; Numbers 32:41 "Jair the son of Manasseh"; Hebrews 7:14 "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda".

2. The begetter is never removed from any row. It props the objection that "loins at depth" requires a father at every generation. Quote: "at no row does it remove a father." PRESSED WITH: Matthew 1:16 "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus"; Luke 3:23 "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph".

3. Joseph knew Mary within the espousal. It props the claim that the conception rows do not deny a man. Quote: "Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal". PRESSED WITH: Matthew 1:18 "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost"; Matthew 1:20 "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost"; Deuteronomy 22:24 "his neighbour's wife"; Matthew 1:25 "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son".

4. Mary's "how" is only a question of means. It props the reading that Luke 1:34 does not exclude a man. Quote: "a question about means is never a statement that no man is involved." PRESSED WITH: Luke 1:34 "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"; Luke 1:35 "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee"; Luke 1:18 "I am an old man, and my wife well stricken in years"; Luke 1:13 "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son".

5. *Ek* plus God marks only covenant origin. It props the objection to the reading of Matthew 1:20. Quote: "+ God names covenant origin at rows of men with fathers". PRESSED WITH: Matthew 1:20 "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost"; Luke 1:35 "therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God"; John 1:13 "born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh... but of God".

6. A sign never states a mechanism. It props the objection to Isaiah 7:14 and Matthew 1:23. Quote: "A sign proves a sign; it does not state a mechanism." PRESSED WITH: Isaiah 7:14 "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son"; Matthew 1:23 "Behold, a virgin shall be with child".
