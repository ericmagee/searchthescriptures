DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 2, RESPONSE

# DEFENDER'S REPLY, ROUND 2: re-seating the loins, the "till", and the registers in their chain

The challenger's points are ammunition. The case is the chain: Luke 1:34-35 and Matthew 1:18-25 fix the conception, Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 fix the flesh, and Matthew 1 and Luke 2 fix the legal name. The challenger must break the chain, not one link.

## 1. The question: "How is it evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda?" (Hebrews 7:14)

**[L1]** Hebrews 7:14: "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda." **[L2]** *Prodēlon* (plainly manifest, publicly known) states that the fact is open. It does not state the route.

**[L1]** The text gives two routes, which the chain keeps apart.

- **The legal route.** Matthew 1:1: "the son of David, the son of Abraham." Matthew 1:16: "Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary." Matthew 1:20-21: "thou son of David… thou shalt call his name JESUS." Matthew 1:24-25: Joseph "took unto him his wife… and he called his name JESUS."
- **The flesh route.** Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh." Galatians 4:4: "made of a woman."

**[L3]** The law of reckoning separates the registered name from the begetter.

- Deuteronomy 25:5-6: the firstborn "shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead."
- Genesis 48:5: "thy two sons… are mine; as Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine."
- Exodus 2:10: "he became her son. And she called his name Moses."
- Esther 2:7: Mordecai "took her for his own daughter."

In each case the register and the begetter are different persons. Naming and taking are acts of legal sonship, and Matthew 1:21 and 1:25 give those acts to Joseph.

**[L3]** Numbers 1:2 and 1:18 (the father's house) are rules of a census of males "by their polls." They do not state that tribal standing never passed through a woman.

- Numbers 32:41 calls Jair "the son of Manasseh," and 1 Chronicles 2:21-22 gives his line through Hezron and "the daughter of Machir."
- 1 Chronicles 2:34-35: Sheshan "had no sons, but daughters," and gave his daughter to his servant, and her line is reckoned.
- Ezra 2:61: the priest "took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai… and was called after their name." Here a wife's house gave the name.

**[L4]** Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:20-25 and Hebrews 7:14, and [L3] Deuteronomy 25:5-6, Exodus 2:10, Numbers 32:41 with 1 Chronicles 2:21-22. The chain: the text has a legal route (Joseph names and takes) and a flesh route (Romans 1:3 with Galatians 4:4), and its own examples show the register following the legal father or the wife's house. So Hebrews 7:14 does not require Joseph's loins. It requires only that the registered line be open, and Matthew 1 opens it.

Noted: no KJV verse names Mary's father. Not conceding here. Goes to review. The chain does not need that name, as §2 shows.

## 2. "Loins" and "bowels" (Acts 2:30; 2 Samuel 7:12): the idiom is descent at any depth

**[L1]** Genesis 46:26: "All the souls that came with Jacob into Egypt, **which came out of his loins**, besides Jacob's sons' wives." This covers grandsons. **[L1]** Hebrews 7:10: Levi "was yet in the loins of his father [Abraham], when Melchisedec met him." Levi was Abraham's great-grandson.

**[L2]** *Osphys* (loins) at Acts 2:30 is the word the challenger himself cites from Hebrews 7:9-10. The challenger's own witness therefore shows that "loins" reaches through several generations, and through mothers' wombs as well, since Levi was born of Leah and Jochebed.

**[L4]** Footing: [L1] Acts 2:30, Genesis 46:26, Hebrews 7:10, and [L2] *osphys* (loins). The chain: "fruit of David's loins" names David's body as the root. It does not name Joseph's body as the last step, because the same idiom is used of Levi in Abraham and of Jacob's whole house. Joseph's name does not appear in Acts 2:30. The challenger supplies it.

**[L1]** Luke 1:32, said to Mary: "the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David." Luke 1:31: "thou shalt conceive in thy womb." Luke 1:35: "that holy thing which shall be born **of thee** (*ek sou*, out of you)." **[L3]** Romans 1:3 and Galatians 4:4 use the same verb *ginomai* (to come to be) and the same preposition *ek* (out of) for the same event. One says "out of the seed of David," the other "out of a woman." **[L4]** Footing: Luke 1:31-35 and Galatians 4:4 with Romans 1:3, and [L2] *ek* (out of). The chain: where Paul gives the flesh-origin of Christ, one verse names David's seed and the other names a woman, and neither names a man between them. Luke's angel tells Mary that the child is "of thee" and carries David's throne.

**[L2]** *Ginomai* in Galatians 4:4 differs from *gennaō* (to beget or bear), the verb Paul uses for Ishmael at Galatians 4:23 ("was born after the flesh"). **[L4]** Footing: Galatians 4:4 against 4:23, and [L2] *ginomai* against *gennaō*. The chain: Paul has the begetting verb to hand and does not use it of Christ. This corroborates the chain and is not its keystone.

## 3. The timing rows (Matthew 1:18-25; Luke 1:34): the challenger's reading cannot stand with Joseph's own conduct

**[L1]** Matthew 1:19: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." **[L1]** Deuteronomy 22:23-24, the challenger's own verse: the betrothed damsel with another man was "his neighbour's wife," and both were to be stoned.

**[L4]** Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:19-20 and Deuteronomy 22:23-24, and [L3] Matthew 1:18 (*prin ē synelthein*, before they came together). The chain:

- If Joseph had known Mary within the espousal, as the challenger says, her pregnancy would give no ground to put her away.
- Joseph acts as a man who knows the child is not his. The angel then answers that doubt with "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (1:20).
- The angel does not say "it is yours."

**[L1]** Matthew 1:24-25 gives the order: Joseph "took unto him his wife; and **knew her not** till she had brought forth." **[L2]** *Ouk eginōsken* (was not knowing) is imperfect, continued abstention. The taking comes after the finding, and the abstention begins at the taking. The challenger's "Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal" has no verse behind it. **[L1]** Luke 2:5, days before the birth: "Mary his espoused wife, being great with child." She is still the espoused wife.

**[L4]** Footing: Matthew 1:24-25 and Luke 2:5, and [L2] *ouk eginōsken* (was not knowing). The chain: the text dates the pregnancy before the taking (1:18), the taking before any knowing (1:24-25), and the "till" after it. "Till" bounds the abstinence, not the conception. The pregnancy was already in place before the abstinence began.

**[L1]** Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" **[L2]** *Pōs* (how) asks for the means. *Ginōskō* (I know) is present tense, which the challenger affirms. **[L4]** Footing: Luke 1:27, 31, 34, and [L2] *pōs* (how). The chain: Mary is espoused (1:27) and told "thou shalt conceive" (1:31). If the means were to be marriage and Genesis 4:1, no "how" would arise. Her question presupposes that the announced conception does not come by the ordinary means. The angel's answer, "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee," supplies the means.

## 4. *Ek pneumatos hagiou*: a womb-conception, not the "born of God" idiom

**[L1]** John 1:13 sets "born of God" against its contrast: "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man." **[L1]** Matthew 1:20 speaks of "that which is conceived **in her**." **[L2]** *To gennēthen en autē* (the thing begotten in her) is neuter and refers to a conceptus in a womb.

**[L4]** Footing: John 1:13 and Matthew 1:20, and [L2] *ek* (out of) with *gennēthen* (begotten). The chain: John 1:13 says that God-origin excludes "the will of man." Matthew 1:20 applies the same *ek* to a literal conception in a womb. The challenger's covenant-idiom rows concern believers, who have human fathers. Matthew 1:20 concerns an unborn child in his mother, so the two do not match.

**[L1]** Luke 1:35 gives the cause: *dio kai* (for which reason also) "that holy thing… shall be called the Son of God." The title follows from the conception just described. The challenger notes that the title appears at 1:32, but 1:35 states the ground for it.

## 5. The registers (Luke 2:48-49; 2:27; 4:22; John 6:42)

**[L1]** The narrator's two uses: Luke 2:27, 41 say "the parents" (*goneis*, parents). Where the narrator names Joseph in relation, he writes "Joseph and his mother" (2:33, 2:43). He does not write "his father." **[L3]** Luke 4:22 ("Is not this Joseph's son?") and John 6:42 ("whose father and mother we know") are speech of the crowd, as Luke 3:23 marks.

**[L2]** *Enomizeto* (was supposed, was thought) is the verb of Luke 3:23. **[L1]** Luke's own other uses of this verb record mistaken supposition:

- Luke 2:44: "supposing him to have been in the company." He was not.
- Acts 7:25: "he supposed his brethren would have understood." They did not.
- Acts 14:19: "supposing he had been dead." He was not.
- Acts 16:27: "supposing that the prisoners had been fled." They had not.
- Acts 21:29: "whom they supposed that Paul had brought into the temple." He had not.

At Acts 16:13 (*enomizeto*, was supposed to be), the place of prayer is a fair reading of the context. That use alone does not make the verb neutral in Luke's hand.

**[L2]** At Luke 3:23 the name Joseph is the only one in the list that lacks the article *tou* (of the), while every name from "of Heli" onward carries it. **[L4]** Footing: Luke 3:23 and Luke 2:44 with Acts 14:19, and [L2] *enomizeto* (was supposed) and the anarthrous *Iōsēph* (Joseph without the article). The chain: Luke marks Joseph's place in the list as reputed, and elsewhere uses the verb for suppositions that prove false. This does not prove the point alone. It stands as one more row in the chain.

**[L1]** Naming does not prove begetting. Luke 1:60: Elisabeth said "he shall be called John." Genesis 4:25: Eve "called his name Seth." **[L1]** Exodus 2:10: Pharaoh's daughter "called his name Moses." **[L4]** Footing: Luke 1:60, Genesis 4:25, Exodus 2:10, and [L3] Matthew 1:21, 25. The chain: mothers name, and an adoptive mother names. Naming marks legal acknowledgement, which is exactly the role the text gives Joseph.

**[L1]** 1 Chronicles 28:6, which the challenger cites, has David the begetter ("thy son"), and God says "I have chosen him to be my son." There, two fathers are two registers because both are true: David begat Solomon, and God chose him. At Luke 1:35 the angel gives God as the source of the conception in the womb ("the Holy Ghost shall come upon thee"). That is the place where the challenger's two-register reading must produce a begetter, and Luke 1:34-35 names none.

## 6. Isaiah 7:14 is a sign to the house of David, and its child is not Isaiah's son

**[L1]** Isaiah 7:13: "Hear ye now, O house of David." Isaiah 7:14: "the Lord himself shall give **you** a sign." **[L2]** *Lākem* (to you) is plural, so the sign is given to the house of David. Matthew 1:20 addresses Joseph as "thou son of David." **[L1]** Isaiah 7:11: "ask it either in the depth, or in the height above," and the sign is on that scale. An ordinary conception would not be one.

**[L1]** Isaiah 8:3 names the prophetess's son "Maher-shalal-hash-baz" and not Immanuel. **[L1]** Isaiah 8:8 says "thy land, O Immanuel," which addresses Immanuel as the owner of the land. **[L1]** Isaiah 9:6-7: "unto us a child is born… upon the throne of David." **[L4]** Footing: Isaiah 7:14 with 8:3, 8:8 and 9:6-7, and [L2] *lākem* (to you, plural). The chain: the child Immanuel is not the son born at 8:3, who bears a different name. He is the owner of the land and heir of David's throne. Isaiah 7:16 dates the sign's first effect, but Matthew 1:22 states its fulfilment: "All this was done, that it might be fulfilled."

**[L2]** The challenger affirms that *ʿalmāh* (young woman) and *bĕtûlāh* (virgin) both describe Rebekah (Genesis 24:16, 43). That point is unchallenged, and Matthew's *parthenos* (virgin) fixes the sense.

## 7. The Jeconiah row

**[L1]** Jeremiah 22:30: "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah." **[L1]** Matthew 1:11-12: "Jechonias begat Salathiel." **[L1]** Luke 1:32: the child gets "the throne of his father David."

**[L4]** Footing: Jeremiah 22:30, Matthew 1:11-12, Luke 1:32. The chain: if the child were Joseph's begotten seed, the promise at Luke 1:32 and the bar at Jeremiah 22:30 would collide. The conception "of the Holy Ghost" in Mary removes the collision.

The challenger's replies do not break the link.

- Haggai 1:1 calls Zerubbabel "governor," not king, so no seed of Jeconiah sat on the throne. That fits the bar.
- Jeremiah 18:7-8 speaks of "that nation" turning from evil. Nothing in Jeremiah 22 records Jeconiah's repentance.

## Declared chain (Round 2)

1. **Matthew 1:16.** The birth is "of whom," feminine. [L1] Matthew 1:16, [L2] *hēs* (of whom, feminine), [L3] Luke 3:23 with Luke 2:44.
2. **Conception before any union.** [L1] Matthew 1:18-25, Luke 1:34-35, Luke 2:5, [L2] *prin ē synelthein* (before they came together), *ouk eginōsken* (was not knowing), *pōs* (how), [L3] Deuteronomy 22:23-24.
3. **Isaiah 7:14.** [L1] Isaiah 7:13-14, 8:3, 8:8, 9:6-7, [L2] *lākem* (to you, plural), *ʿalmāh* (young woman) with *parthenos* (virgin), [L3] Genesis 24:16, 43.
4. **Joseph as legal father.** [L1] Matthew 1:20-25, Luke 2:48-49, [L2] *enomizeto* (was supposed), [L3] Deuteronomy 25:5-6, Exodus 2:10, Genesis 48:5.
5. **Flesh through the woman; Davidic line by registered name.** [L1] Romans 1:3, Galatians 4:4, Luke 1:31-35, Genesis 46:26, [L2] *ek* (out of), *osphys* (loins), [L3] Hebrews 7:10 with Numbers 32:41 and 1 Chronicles 2:21-22.
6. **Conclusion.** Christ had no biological human father. Links 1-5 rest on Luke 1:34-35 and Matthew 1:18-25.
