DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

# Opening: Christ had no biological human father

**Position.** The KJV says Jesus was conceived in Mary by the Holy Ghost, without any man. Joseph was his husband-of-record and protector, not his begetter. The case is one interlocking chain, not a single proof-text.

## I. The genealogy breaks its own pattern (Matthew 1:16)

**[L1]** Matthew 1:2-15 runs unbroken: "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob…" Then verse 16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, **of whom was born Jesus**, who is called Christ."

**[L2]** The verb "begat" is *egennēsen* (he fathered), active, with a man as subject. It is used for every father in the list. At the last step Matthew changes the verb to *egennēthē* (was born), which is passive. He also changes the subject. The relative pronoun *hēs* (of whom) is **feminine singular**, so it can refer only to Mary. It cannot be "of whom" in the masculine, and it cannot be plural, "of whom both."

**[L4]** The footing is Matthew 1:2-16 (the fixed "begat" pattern) and the Greek feminine singular *hēs*. The chain: an author who has said "begat" forty times and then switches verb, voice and gender at the one point where the line reaches Jesus has deliberately withheld the father's role from Joseph. Joseph is "the husband of Mary," not the one who "begat."

**[L3]** Luke 3:23 matches this. Jesus began to be about thirty, "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph." *Enomizeto* means "was reckoned or thought by custom." Luke marks the sonship as reputed, not real.

## II. The conception was "of the Holy Ghost," before any union (Matthew 1:18-20; Luke 1:34-35)

**[L1]** Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, **before they came together**, she was found with child **of the Holy Ghost**." Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."

**[L2]** *Ek pneumatos hagiou* means "out of the Holy Spirit," and *ek* marks source or origin. *Gennēthen* (that which is begotten or conceived) is neuter and passive. The agent is not named as Joseph. *Prin ē synelthein autous* means "before they came together," so the pregnancy is dated before any union.

**[L1]** Luke 1:34: "How shall this be, **seeing I know not a man**?" Luke 1:35: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: **therefore also** that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."

**[L2]** *Andra ou ginōskō* (I know not a man) uses the present tense for her ongoing state. *Episkiasei* means "will overshadow." *Dio kai* means "for which reason also," so the cause of the title is the conception described just before it.

**[L4]** The footing is Matthew 1:18-20 (*ek*, source, and *prin*, timing) and Luke 1:34-35 (Mary's *ou ginōskō* and the angel's *dio kai*). The chain: Mary's question excludes any man as the means, and the angel's answer supplies a non-human source in place of one. The conception therefore has no male human contribution.

## III. The sign: a virgin (Isaiah 7:14; Matthew 1:22-23)

**[L1]** Matthew 1:22-23: "that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Behold, **a virgin** shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son."

**[L2]** The Hebrew in Isaiah 7:14 is *ʿalmāh* (a young woman of marriageable age, unmarried). The Greek Matthew uses is *parthenos* (virgin). Matthew's inspired rendering fixes the sense.

**[L3]** Genesis 24:43 uses *ʿalmāh* of Rebekah. Genesis 24:16 describes the same woman as "a virgin, neither had any man known her." The word is therefore used in the text for an unmarried virgin. Isaiah 7:14 calls the birth a "sign" (*ʾôt*), and an ordinary conception within marriage would be no sign.

**[L4]** The footing is Matthew 1:22-23 (*parthenos*) and Genesis 24:16, 43 (*ʿalmāh* applied to a virgin). The chain: the sign promised in Isaiah is the one Matthew says was fulfilled. A virgin who conceived did so without a man.

## IV. Joseph was husband and legal father, not begetter (Matthew 1:25; Luke 2:48-49)

**[L1]** Matthew 1:25: Joseph "**knew her not till** she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS."

**[L2]** *Ouk eginōsken … heōs hou* means "he was not knowing her until which time," an imperfect tense for continued abstention through the whole pregnancy.

**[L1]** Luke 2:48: "Thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." Luke 2:49: "wist ye not that I must be about **my Father's** business?"

**[L4]** The footing is Matthew 1:25 (*ouk eginōsken*, continued abstention through the pregnancy) and Luke 2:48-49. The chain: Mary's "thy father" is the household's usage, and Jesus answers in the very next verse by naming a different Father. The text sets the two usages side by side and corrects the first with the second. The same pattern appears in Luke 3:23 ("as was supposed") and John 6:42, where the crowd says "the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know." John 6:42 records the crowd's words and does not assert them as true.

## V. Davidic descent does not need Joseph's seed

**[L1]** Romans 1:3: "made of the seed of David according to the flesh." Acts 2:30: God swore "that of the fruit of his loins, **according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne." Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, **made of a woman**."

**[L2]** *Genomenou ek spermatos Dauid kata sarka* means "having come into being out of the seed of David according to flesh." Galatians 4:4 says *genomenon ek gynaikos*, "come into being out of a woman," and names no man.

**[L3]** Jeremiah 22:30 says of Jeconiah, "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David." Matthew 1:11-12 places Jechonias in Joseph's line. Genesis 3:15 speaks of "her seed" (*zeraʿ*, seed), which is unusual because seed is normally reckoned to the man.

**[L4]** The footing is Romans 1:3 and Acts 2:30 (Jesus is of David "according to the flesh") and Jeremiah 22:30 with Matthew 1:11-12 (Joseph's line carried a bar on the throne). The chain: the flesh-descent from David runs through Mary, who is the only human parent the text names for his body. Joseph's line gives legal title through adoption and naming (Matthew 1:21, 25). This avoids Jeconiah's bar, which a begotten son of Joseph would have inherited.

## DECLARED CHAIN -- the links your case stands on:

1. Matthew 1:16 changes verb, voice and gender (*egennēsen* to *egennēthē*; feminine singular *hēs*), so the line of begetting stops at Joseph and the birth is "of" Mary alone. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:2-16; [L2] *hēs* (of whom, feminine) and *egennēthē* (was born); [L3] Luke 3:23 "as was supposed."
2. The conception was "of the Holy Ghost" before any union, and Mary says she knows no man. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:18-20, Luke 1:34-35; [L2] *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of the Holy Spirit), *prin synelthein* (before coming together), *dio kai* (for which reason also).
3. The sign is a virgin conceiving, with *parthenos* fixing the sense of *ʿalmāh*. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:22-23; [L2] *ʿalmāh* (young woman) and *parthenos* (virgin); [L3] Genesis 24:43 with 24:16.
4. Joseph is husband and reputed father only, and Jesus himself names a different Father. Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:25, Luke 2:48-49, Luke 3:23; [L2] *ouk eginōsken* (was not knowing her) and *enomizeto* (was reckoned); [L3] John 6:42 as the crowd's words.
5. Davidic descent "according to the flesh" runs through Mary, and Joseph's line carried Jeconiah's bar. Footing: [L1] Romans 1:3, Acts 2:30, Galatians 4:4; [L2] *ek gynaikos* (out of a woman) and *kata sarka* (according to flesh); [L3] Jeremiah 22:30 with Matthew 1:11-12.
6. Christ had no biological human father: he was conceived in Mary by the Holy Ghost, and Joseph was husband and legal father only. Footing: links 1-5, resting on [L1] Matthew 1:16-25 and Luke 1:34-35, [L2] *hēs*, *ek pneumatos hagiou* and *ou ginōskō* (I know not), and [L3] Isaiah 7:14 with Genesis 24:16, 43.
