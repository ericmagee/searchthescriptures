CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

# CHALLENGER'S REPLY — "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh"

The keystone **LINK 6** ("Christ had no biological human father") is answered by the defense's own verses, so I start there and work down.

## 1. LINK 5 and LINK 6 — the flesh rows name a man's loins, and the only Davidic man the birth chapters name is Joseph

[L1] Acts 2:30: "Therefore being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him, that **of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh**, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne." Romans 1:3: "Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was **made of the seed of David according to the flesh**."

[L1] The oath itself: 2 Samuel 7:12 "I will set up thy seed after thee, **which shall proceed out of thy bowels**"; Psalm 132:11 "Of **the fruit of thy body** will I set upon thy throne"; Genesis 35:11 "kings shall come **out of thy loins**"; 1 Kings 8:19 "thy son that shall come forth **out of thy loins**."

[L2] "Loins" at Acts 2:30 is *osphys*, the same loins-word used of Levi standing "in the loins of his father" (Hebrews 7:9-10); "bowels" at 2 Samuel 7:12 is *mimme'eykha* (out of thy bowels). These are body-words of a man.

[L1] The man the text names: Luke 1:27 "espoused to a man whose name was **Joseph, of the house of David**; and the virgin's name was Mary." Luke 2:4 "Joseph also went up... (because **he was of the house and lineage of David**:)". The angel: "Joseph, **thou son of David**" (Matthew 1:20).

[L4] **Footing = [L1] Acts 2:30, Romans 1:3, 2 Samuel 7:12, Psalm 132:11, Luke 1:27, Luke 2:4 + [L2] *osphys*, *mimme'eykha*.** Link: "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" is a seed-row out of a man's body; the text names exactly one man of the house of David in the birth chapters, Joseph. D5's answer — "the flesh-descent from David runs through Mary" — is the move the text does not make.

## 2. Against LINK 5's "through Mary": no row states Mary's descent, and the reckoning is by the father's house

[L1] The only kin row the text gives Mary is Luke 1:36 "thy cousin Elisabeth," and Elisabeth's line is Luke 1:5 "his wife was **of the daughters of Aaron**." No Davidic name is attached to Mary anywhere in the canon.

[L1] The reckoning rule: Numbers 1:2 "after their families, **by the house of their fathers**"; 1:18 "they declared their pedigrees... **by the house of their fathers**." Ezra 2:61-62: priests who "took a wife of the daughters of Barzillai... and **was called after their name**... sought their register among those that were reckoned by genealogy, but they were not found: therefore were they, **as polluted, put from the priesthood**."

[L1] Hebrews 7:14: "For **it is evident** that our Lord sprang out of Juda..."

[L4] **Footing = [L1] Numbers 1:2, 18; Ezra 2:61-62; Hebrews 7:14; Luke 1:5, 36 + [L2] *beth 'av* (father's house).** Link: a wife's-house route is the route the register excludes in the text's own example; "evident" sprouting out of Judah therefore requires a named line — and the named line is Joseph's. **Question to the defender: how is it "evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda," given Numbers 1:2, 18, if the flesh runs through a woman whose house the text never names?**

Three more of D5's props fall at the row level:

[L1/L3] "her seed" (Genesis 3:15) never excludes a father: the same idiom is said of Hagar (Genesis 16:10 "I will multiply thy seed exceedingly") and of Rebekah (Genesis 24:60 "be thou the mother of thousands of millions"), and both bear seed with a named father (Genesis 16:15-16 "Hagar bare **Ishmael to Abram**"; Genesis 25:21 "Rebekah conceived" of Isaac). [L4, footing L1+L3]: the idiom is an offspring idiom, not a no-father idiom.

[L1/L3] Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman" is the mortality idiom: Job 25:4 "how can he be clean **that is born of a woman**?"; Matthew 11:11 "Among them that are **born of women** there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" — and John's father is named (Luke 1:13 "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son"; Luke 1:63 "His name is John"). [L4, footing L1+L3]: "of a woman" never means "without a father" at any row.

[L1] The Jeconiah bar is never applied to Jesus' throne by any row: Jeremiah 22:30 "no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David," yet 1 Chronicles 3:17-18 names his sons, Matthew 1:12 says "Jechonias begat Salathiel," and the plucked signet (22:24) is set on "Zerubbabel... the son of Shealtiel" (Haggai 2:23) who was **governor** (Haggai 1:1; Ezra 3:2), never king. Jeremiah's own rule stands: 18:7-8 "If that nation... turn from their evil, **I will repent of the evil**." Luke 3:27 gives Salathiel "of Neri," and the text never says whether it is the same man. [L4, footing L1+L3]: the bar is a bridge the defense supplies; it cannot route flesh through a line the text never states.

## 3. LINK 2 — the timing rows date a finding and bound an abstinence; "of the Holy Ghost" is the covenant-origin marker

[L1] Matthew 1:18: "before they came together, **she was found with child**" — the sentence dates what was *found*. [L2] *synelthein* (to come together) names married union ("come together again," 1 Corinthians 7:5); Matthew names the coming together at 1:24, "**took unto him his wife**." [L1] 1:25 "knew her not **till** she had brought forth her firstborn son: and **he called his name JESUS**" — "till" bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy.

[L2] Luke 1:34 *andra ou ginōskō* is **present active indicative** — "I know not a man," her state when the angel spoke, not a vow.

[L2/L3] *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of the Holy Ghost) is the covenant-origin pattern, never biological paternity: John 1:13 "born of God"; 1 John 3:9; 5:1 "born of God"; 1 John 4:7 "is of God." When the canon marks biological origin it says *kata sarka* — "according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3). [L2/L3] The angel's own verbs: "the Holy Ghost shall **come upon** thee" (*eperchomai*) is Acts 1:8 "after that the Holy Ghost is **come upon** you," said of the apostles; "shall **overshadow** thee" (*episkiazō*) is the transfiguration cloud (Luke 9:34) and Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15) — presence and power at every row, begetting at none.

[L1] The canon's stated means of begetting: Genesis 4:1 "And Adam **knew** Eve his wife: and she conceived, and bare Cain." [L1/L3] The two were husband and wife in law from the espousal: "Joseph her husband" (Matthew 1:19), "Mary thy wife" (1:20), and Deuteronomy 22:24 calls a betrothed damsel "**his neighbour's wife**."

[L4] **Footing = [L1] Matthew 1:18, 25; Genesis 4:1; Matthew 1:19-20 + [L3] Deuteronomy 22:24; 1 Corinthians 7:5.** Link: the rows hold that **Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal**, and the child is of his loins. That is the sentence the seed rows require and the timing rows permit.

## 4. LINK 4 and LINK 1 — the father is named in five registers; the two "fathers" are two registers, not a denial

[L1] Mary's mouth: Luke 2:48 "behold, **thy father and I** have sought thee sorrowing." The narrator: Luke 2:27 "**the parents** brought in the child Jesus"; 2:41 "**his parents** went to Jerusalem"; 2:33 "Joseph and his mother marvelled." The town: Luke 4:22 "Is not this **the son of Joseph**?"; John 6:42 "the son of Joseph, **whose father and mother we know**." A disciple: John 1:45 "Jesus of Nazareth, **the son of Joseph**." The angel: Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David."

[L1/L3] Luke 2:49 "my Father's business" is the covenant register, which the canon keeps beside the begetting register in one verse: 1 Chronicles 28:6 "Solomon **thy son**, he shall build my house... for **I have chosen him to be my son**, and I will be his father." Acts 13:33 applies "this day have I begotten thee" to **the resurrection**; Romans 1:4 "declared to be the Son of God... **by the resurrection**"; and Luke 1:32 gives the title with the throne **before** Mary's question at 1:34.

[L4] **Footing = [L1] Luke 2:48-49; 1 Chronicles 28:6; Acts 13:33 + [L3] 2 Samuel 7:14; Luke 1:32-34.** Link: the covenant title never deletes the father who begat — Solomon is "thy son" to David and "my son" to God in one breath. So "my Father" (Luke 2:49) does not correct "thy father" (2:48); it speaks in the other register.

[L1] Matthew 1:16 is a **birth** clause: "of whom was **born** Jesus, who is called Christ." [L2] *egennēthē* is passive — non-narration of a begetting is not a denial of one. The same chapter gives the father-act to Joseph: "thou shalt call his name JESUS" (1:21) and "he called his name JESUS" (1:25) — and the canon gives the naming to the father (Luke 1:13 to Zacharias; 1:62-63 "they made signs to **his father**, how he would have him called"). [L1/L2] Luke 3:23 immediately runs the line "which was the son of Heli" **through Joseph**, and *enomizeto* ("was supposed") is the same verb form as Acts 16:13 "where prayer **was wont** to be made" — a usage-word carrying no verdict of its own.

[L4] **Footing = [L1] Matthew 1:16, 21, 25; Luke 1:13, 62-63; 3:23 + [L2] *egennēthē*, *enomizeto*.** Link: a passive verb cannot do the work D1 assigns it ("deliberately withheld the father's role") when the same chapter has Joseph perform the father's naming, and the next chapter of Luke runs David's line through him.

## 5. LINK 3 — the sign is bounded in its own chapter

[L1] Isaiah 7:11 "Ask thee a sign"; 7:14 "the Lord himself shall give **you** a sign" (1611 margin "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call"); 7:16 "For **before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good**, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken of both her kings."; 8:3-4 the prophetess bears the child; 8:8 "O Immanuel."

[L2] The word at 7:14 is *ha-almah* (the young woman); the virgin-word *bethulah* is what the defender's own pair uses at Genesis 24:16 "a virgin, neither had any man known her" — and 24:43 calls the same woman *almah*. Both words describe Rebekah.

[L4] **Footing = [L1] Isaiah 7:11, 16; 8:3-4, 8 + [L2] *almah*, *bethulah*.** Link: the sign in its own chapter is bounded to a child in Isaiah's day and recurs by name at 8:8 there; the sign therefore cannot carry the mechanism D6 asserts. Matthew 1:23's "a virgin" stands as written, and it says nothing of seed, loins, or a father.

## UNSUPPORTED [L4] PREMISES (the defender's words, quoted verbatim)

1. **D5:** "The flesh-descent from David runs through Mary, who is the only human parent the text names for his body." — No exhibited footing: no row states Mary's Davidic descent; the one kin row names a wife "of the daughters of Aaron" (Luke 1:5, 36).
2. **D5:** "This avoids Jeconiah's bar, which a begotten son of Joseph would have inherited." — Inference with no row applying the bar to Jesus; 1 Chronicles 3:17-18, Haggai 2:23 and Jeremiah 18:7-8 are unmet.
3. **D2:** "The conception therefore has no male human contribution." — Contradicted by Acts 2:30 "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh," 2 Samuel 7:12 "out of thy bowels," and the stated means, Genesis 4:1.
4. **D3:** "A virgin who conceived did so without a man." — Rests on the sign while the sign's own bounds (Isaiah 7:16; 8:3-4, 8) go unexhibited.
5. **D1:** "has deliberately withheld the father's role from Joseph." — Intent-inference from a passive birth-verb, while Matthew 1:21, 25 has Joseph name the child.
6. **D4:** "Mary's 'thy father' is the household's usage." — Unfooted; and Luke 2:27's narrator's "the parents" is not answered at all.

**The case, in one line each:** [L1] the seed is "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh" (Acts 2:30; Romans 1:3) — a man's body; [L1] the father is named in five registers (Luke 2:48, 2:27, 4:22, John 1:45, 6:42, Matthew 1:20); [L1] the reckoning is "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18) and Joseph is "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4); [L1] the stated means is "Adam knew Eve his wife: and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1), and they were "husband" and "wife" (Matthew 1:19-20) with "till" bounding the abstinence to the pregnancy (1:25). [L4, footing L1 + L3 as above]: **Christ had a biological father — Joseph, the named Davidic father.**

**And the question stands:** By what reckoning is it "evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14), given Numbers 1:2, 18 — if the flesh runs through a house the text never names?
