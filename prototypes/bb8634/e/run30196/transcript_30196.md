## How to read this debate

**The method.** Isaiah 28:10 (KJV): "For precept *must be* upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, *and* there a little:" The case is not one verse; it is verses laid on verses until the point stands. That is how these debates are built and how they are judged.

- **[L1] The KJV verse:** the verse itself, quoted exactly.
- **[L2] Source language:** the Hebrew or Greek behind the English.
- **[L3] Cross-reference:** what the rest of scripture says.
- **[L4] Inference:** a conclusion the debater draws from at least two prior layers.

**How the case is judged.** To win, the challenger must break the defender's chain, the linked argument the defender builds for the topic. Defeating one isolated verse or term does not break the chain. A chain breaks in one of two ways: the challenger shows from the text that a link is false (a cited verse says the opposite and the defender has no answer), or the defender takes down enough of its own links.

**The link round.** The challenger ends its second round, and the defender ends its link round (round 3), by listing up to six of the other side's inferences that it says have no footing. Each side then answers every item on the list against it: SUPPORTED, showing the verses and layers it rests on, or CONCEDED, admitting it has no footing. Only these answers are counted; everything else is argument.

**Labels in the judges' texts.** [CR1], [CR2] and [CR3] are the defender's rounds 1 to 3; [DR1], [DR2] and [DR3] are the challenger's. D1, D2 and so on are the links of the defender's declared chain, in its own order; C1, C2 and so on are the claims the defender listed against the challenger, in the order the challenger answered them in [DR3].

**How each judge reaches a verdict.** The rules are applied in this order, and the first that fits decides.

- **BROKEN:** the challenger refuted at least one of the defender's declared links from the text, or the defender withdrew more than half of the links it declared, or withdrew its conclusion link (the last link of its chain).
- **PARTIAL:** the defender withdrew at least one declared link, or its defense standing is COLLAPSED.
- **HELD:** none of the above.

**One rulebook.** Every panel judgment applies these same rules.

**What else decides a link.** The judges' rulebook adds three rules, quoted here in its own words.

- **UNEXHIBITED.** A link is graded UNEXHIBITED "when the block asserted the link and offered no layer footing at all"; it counts as conceded.
- **VOID.** A premise "listed without a verbatim quote, or charged in terms stronger than its quote, is VOID -- it counts for neither side, and a CONCEDED answer to a void premise is NOT a concession". It adds: "A charge is stronger than its quote only when it asserts what the quote does not: a negation, a universal (all, none, only, never), or a claim the quote's own words do not make. Dropping, reordering or paraphrasing the quote's words is not stronger. A limb the link round narrows counts in its narrowed form." and "A premise's quote is checked against every earlier round of the opponent; it is verbatim if found in any of them, and the judge names that round."
- **KEYSTONE.** The keystone is "the LAST declared link, Dn, which is the conclusion link": "WITHDRAWN if it is withdrawn or conceded". A withdrawn keystone is a withdrawn conclusion link, so the verdict is BROKEN.

**What the standings mean.** Both sides are graded the same way, from their link-round answers.

- **SELF-SUPPORTED:** every listed claim shows the layers it stands on (each inference shows at least two prior layers).
- **PROP-DEPENDENT:** some listed claims were conceded or stood without showing their layers, but not more than half. Those claims do not count.
- **COLLAPSED:** more than half of the claims the other side listed against it were conceded or left without footing. Exactly half is not COLLAPSED.

## Round 1 - Defender (Claude)

DEFENDER (standard reading) -- ROUND 1, OPENING

### OPENING: Jesus had no biological father

**Position.** I defend the standard reading: Jesus had no human biological father. His conception in Mary was by the Holy Ghost, and Joseph was husband and legal guardian, not begetter.

**Term.** "Biological father" means the male parent who begets, the one the KJV marks with "begat." The Greek is *gennaō* (to beget, when said of a father; to bear or be born, when said of a mother). The question is whether the text assigns that act to a man.

---

#### I. Matthew 1: the begetting chain breaks at Joseph

**1. The repeated "begat" stops.** [L1] Matthew 1:2-16 runs "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob…" through to verse 16: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." [L2] Every "begat" is *egennēsen* (he begat), active voice, with a male subject. At verse 16 the pattern changes. The text says *ex hēs egennēthē Iēsous* (out of whom was born Jesus). *Hēs* (of whom) is feminine singular, so its antecedent can only be Mary, not Joseph. *Egennēthē* is passive (was born). Joseph is called *ton andra Marias* (the husband of Mary), and no active "begat" is ascribed to him. [L4] The genealogy has "begat" in every prior generation and withholds it here. Both the verb change (L2) and the feminine relative (L2) stand in the verse (L1). So the text deliberately declines to make Joseph the begetter.

**2. The source of the conception is stated.** [L1] Matthew 1:18: "When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." [L2] *Prin ē sunelthein autous* (before they came together) uses *sunerchomai* (to come together), here marital union. *Heurethē en gastri echousa* means "was found having in womb." *Ek pneumatos hagiou* means "out of Spirit holy," and *ek* (out of) marks source or origin. [L1] Matthew 1:20: "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." [L2] *To gennēthen en autē* (that which has been begotten or conceived in her) is the same *gennaō*, and *ek pneumatos estin hagiou* gives its source as the Holy Ghost. [L4] The word that elsewhere names a father's begetting is used here, and its source is *ek pneumatos* (out of Spirit). That leaves no place for a human male source.

**3. Joseph's own reaction fits no other reading.** [L1] Matthew 1:19: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." [L2] *Apolusai* (to dismiss, to release) is the word for divorce. [L1] Matthew 1:20 then has the angel say "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife." [L4] Joseph knew he was not the father (Matt 1:19), and he accepted the pregnancy only after the angel's word (Matt 1:20). The reassurance would be needless if he were the father.

**4. No union before the birth.** [L1] Matthew 1:25: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son." [L2] *Ouk eginōsken* (he was not knowing) uses *ginōskō* (to know), the verb for marital intimacy. [L3] Genesis 4:1, "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived," uses *yada'* (to know) in the same sense. This verse speaks only of the time up to the birth, and that is all my case needs. [L4] Both the verb's idiom (L2, Gen 4:1) and Matthew's statement (L1) place the conception wholly outside any union with Joseph.

#### II. Luke 1: Mary's own statement and the angel's answer

**5.** [L1] Luke 1:34: "Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" [L2] *Andra ou ginōskō* (a man I do not know) has *ginōskō* in the present tense, a current condition. [L1] Luke 1:35 answers: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." [L2] *Epeleusetai* (shall come upon) and *episkiasei* (shall overshadow) are the promised agency. *Gennōmenon ek sou* (being born out of you) names Mary as the only human source. [L4] Mary asks how it can happen without a man, and the angel answers by naming a divine agency instead of a man (Luke 1:35). So the reply confirms that no man is involved.

**6. The "father" language is accounted for.** [L1] Luke 3:23: "Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph." [L2] *Enomizeto* (was supposed, was reckoned by custom) marks a belief, not a fact. [L1] John 6:42, "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know?", is what the crowd says, not the narrator. [L1] Luke 2:48 has Mary say "thy father and I have sought thee," and Luke 2:49 corrects the word: "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" [L4] Luke 3:23 itself says "as was supposed," so every verse calling Joseph the father reports appearance. Luke 2:48-49 then shows the correction in the same passage.

#### III. The prophetic footing

**7.** [L1] Matthew 1:22-23 cites Isaiah 7:14: "Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son." [L2] The Hebrew is *'almah* (a young woman of marriageable age). Matthew's Greek is *parthenos* (virgin). [L3] *'Almah* is used of Rebekah in Genesis 24:43, and Genesis 24:16 describes the same woman with *bethulah* (virgin) and says "neither had any man known her." So *'almah* can describe a woman who has not known a man. Matthew's inspired choice of *parthenos* (Matt 1:23), together with Matthew 1:18-25, fixes the sense for this sign. [L3] Genesis 3:15 speaks of "her seed," *zera'* (seed). That word is ordinarily reckoned through the male, and here it is reckoned through the woman. [L3] Galatians 4:4: "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman" (*genomenon ek gynaikos*, coming to be out of a woman), names only a woman. [L4] A sign given to the house of David (Isa 7:13-14) concerns a conception unlike an ordinary one. Matthew's *parthenos* (L2) and the other scriptures that trace the seed to the woman alone (L3) confirm that the sign is a conception without a man.

#### IV. Anticipated objections

- **"Joseph is in the genealogy, so he is the father."** [L1] Matthew 1:16 does not say Joseph begat Jesus. It says Joseph is "husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus." [L3] Joseph's line gives the legal right to David's throne, because he took Mary as wife (Matt 1:20, 24) and named the child (Matt 1:25). [L3] Romans 1:3, "made of the seed of David according to the flesh," shows the fleshly descent also ran through Mary, since Joseph was not the begetter.
- **"Matthew 1:25 says 'till.'"** [L1] The verse limits only what happened before the birth, which is the point at issue.
- **"Spirit-conception is not 'biological.'"** [L1] Luke 1:35 says "that which shall be born of thee." Mary's flesh is the human source, as Galatians 4:4 says "of a woman," and no man's seed is named anywhere in the narrative.

---

#### DECLARED CHAIN -- the links your case stands on

1. Matthew 1:16 breaks the "begat" pattern: [L1] Matt 1:2-16 has *egennēsen* (he begat) in each prior generation; [L2] verse 16 uses feminine singular *hēs* (of whom) with passive *egennēthē* (was born) and calls Joseph only *andra* (husband), so Joseph is not named as begetter.
2. Matthew states the source of the conception as the Holy Ghost: [L1] Matt 1:18, 20 "of the Holy Ghost"; [L2] *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of Spirit holy) uses *ek* (out of, source) with *gennaō* (to beget or conceive); [L3] Luke 1:35 names the same agency.
3. Mary and Joseph both treat the pregnancy as without a man: [L1] Luke 1:34 "I know not a man" and Matt 1:19-20, 25; [L2] *ginōskō* (to know) as the marital idiom, matched at [L3] Gen 4:1 *yada'* (to know).
4. Every "father" reference to Joseph is reported supposition, not fact: [L1] Luke 3:23 "as was supposed"; [L2] *enomizeto* (was supposed, reckoned by custom); [L3] Luke 2:48-49 corrected by "my Father's business," and John 6:42 as the crowd's words.
5. The prophetic pattern places the seed in the woman alone: [L1] Matt 1:23 quoting Isa 7:14 "a virgin"; [L2] *'almah* (young woman of marriageable age) rendered *parthenos* (virgin); [L3] Gen 24:16, 43 for the *'almah*/virgin usage, Gen 3:15 "her seed," and Gal 4:4 "made of a woman."
6. Therefore Jesus had no human biological father: [L1] Matt 1:18-20, Luke 1:34-35; [L2] *ek pneumatos hagiou* (out of Spirit holy) and *ek hēs* (out of whom, feminine); [L3] Gal 4:4 and Gen 3:15. His conception came by the Holy Ghost in Mary, and Joseph was husband and legal guardian, not begetter.
