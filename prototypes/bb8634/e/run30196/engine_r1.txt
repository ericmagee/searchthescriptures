CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 1, CHALLENGE

# CHALLENGER OPENING — the seed is out of a man's body, and the man is named

The question is whether the text assigns the begetting to a man. It does — in the promise's own words, in the naming, and in the reckoning. I press the keystone **D6** first, then the links it stands on.

---

## TARGET D6 and D5 — the promised seed is out of the loins; "of a woman" names mortality, not the absence of a father

[L1] "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12) — the throne-seed comes out of a man's body. [L1] "Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne" (Psalm 132:11). [L1] "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne" (Acts 2:30). [L2] *osphys* (the loins) is the same word Hebrews 7:5, 10 uses for Levi "in the loins of his father." [L1] "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3); [L2] *kata sarka* (according to flesh) is the biological marker — and it is precisely what Matthew 1:20 does **not** carry. [L1] "it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda" (Hebrews 7:14) — [L2] *anatellō* (sprang up) — and the tribe is counted "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18). [L1] "Jesus Christ of the seed of David" (2 Timothy 2:8).

A legal guardian produces no seed: the seed must "proceed out of thy bowels" (2 Samuel 7:12). If Joseph begets nothing, D6 leaves the promise of the flesh-seed with **no row anywhere in the narrative**.

**D5's "made of a woman" is the mortality idiom, and the text itself supplies the check.** [L1] "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman" (Galatians 4:4) — the same phrase is said of John: "among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist" (Matthew 11:11), and the same gospel names his father, Zacharias (Luke 1:13, 57). [L3] Job 14:1 "man that is born of a woman is of few days"; Job 15:14 "he which is born of a woman"; Job 25:4 "how can he be clean that is born of a woman?" [L4] "Born of a woman" marks frailty and mortality; it does not exclude a father — John is the proof-case. Footing: [L1] Matthew 11:11 with Luke 1:13 + [L3] the three Job rows.

**D5's "seed in the woman alone" is one row, and it is not Davidic.** [L1] Genesis 3:15 "her seed" (*zera'*, offspring). [L3] The offspring rows that predicate seed of a woman are four — Genesis 3:15, 16:10 (Hagar), 24:60 (Rebekah), Isaiah 54:3 (Zion) — none of them Davidic, and none of them a statement of how a child is begotten.

**The flesh D5 routes through Mary is not in the text.** Mary is given no lineage. [L1] Luke 1:36 names her kinswoman (*syngenēs*, of kin) Elisabeth, who is "of the daughters of Aaron" (Luke 1:5) — a priestly house, not David's. The only Davidic line named is Joseph's: "of the house of David" (Luke 1:27), "of the house and lineage of David" (Luke 2:4), and the throne "of his father David" (Luke 1:32). "Mary's genealogy" is an import; the text never writes one.

---

## TARGET D1 — the passive verb is non-narration, and the register runs by the father's house

[L1] "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" (Matthew 1:16). [L2] *egennēthē* (was born) is passive; *hēs* (of whom) is feminine. [L4] A silent passive does not say Joseph did not beget; it does not narrate the begetting, and one un-narrating verb cannot overrule the rows that name the father and the loins. Footing: [L1] the naming rows and the seed rows + [L2] the passive's own silence.

The register is not neutral: the line is Joseph's ("Jacob begat Joseph"), and the reckoning rule is the father's house — "by the house of their fathers" (Numbers 1:2, 18); he who could not show it was put out (Ezra 2:59, margin "or, pedigree"); the priests called after the **wife's** house were "as polluted, put from the priesthood" (Ezra 2:61-62). [L1/L3] D1's "legal right through the wife" reading walks straight into the canon's own rule against it. And the father's act is narrated: "thou shalt call his name JESUS" (Matthew 1:21), "he called his name JESUS" (1:25) — the naming is the father's (Luke 1:13; 1:62-63).

---

## TARGET D2 — "of the Holy Ghost" is covenant origin; the two verbs are power and presence

[L1] "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:20). [L2] *ek* (out of) with God or the Spirit is the covenant-origin marker: "born of God" (John 1:13; 1 John 3:9; 5:1), "is of God" (1 John 4:7) — spiritual origin at every row, never biological paternity. Where the text means flesh it says so: *kata sarka*, Romans 1:3.

[L1] "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee" (Luke 1:35). [L2] *eperchomai* (come upon) is anointing and power — "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you, and ye shall be witnesses" (Acts 1:8), said of the apostles. *episkiazō* (overshadow) is the presence cloud — the transfiguration cloud (Matthew 17:5; Luke 9:34) and Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15). [L3] Neither verb denotes begetting at any row. And the title is *given*, not begotten: "shall be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35) — the covenant title God gave Solomon while Solomon had a father: "Solomon thy son... I have chosen him to be my son" (1 Chronicles 28:6). D2's "agency" is no begetting row.

---

## TARGET D3 — the timing rows bound the abstinence; the espousal was a marriage

[L1] "seeing I know not a man" (Luke 1:34). [L2] *andra ou ginōskō* (a man I do not know) is present active indicative: her state when the angel spoke, not a vow. [L1] "before they came together, she was found with child" (Matthew 1:18) dates the **finding**; [L2] *synerchomai* (come together) names married union elsewhere ("come together again," 1 Corinthians 7:5), and Matthew says what the coming together is: "took unto him his wife" (1:24). [L1] "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (1:25) — [L2] *ouk eginōsken* (he was not knowing): the abstinence covers the pregnancy and is bounded by "till." [L3] And the canon's stated means of begetting is that knowing: "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" (Genesis 4:1).

They were husband and wife in law from the espousal: "Joseph her husband" (Matthew 1:19); "Mary thy wife" (1:20); the law calls a betrothed damsel "his neighbour's wife" (Deuteronomy 22:24). [L4] **Therefore the child of Mary, wife of Joseph of the house of David, is of his loins within the espousal.** Footing: [L1] Matthew 1:19, 20, 25 + [L3] Genesis 4:1 + [L2] the till-bounded imperfect of *ginōskō*.

---

## TARGET D4 — the father is named in four registers; "as was supposed" carries no verdict

[L1] Mary's own mouth, after the angel's words (Luke 1:26-38): "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (Luke 2:48). [L1] The narrator: "the parents brought in the child Jesus" (2:27), "his parents went to Jerusalem" (2:41), "Joseph and his mother marvelled" (2:33). [L1] The town: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22). [L1] A disciple: "Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph" (John 1:45). [L1] The angel: "Joseph, thou son of David" (Matthew 1:20).

[L2] *enomizeto* (was supposed, was wont) is the verb of "where prayer was wont to be made" (Acts 16:13) and "supposing him to have been in the company" (Luke 2:44) — custom and reckoning, no verdict of its own. Luke 2:49 "my Father's business" answers the child's mission; the same narrator still says "Joseph and his mother" (2:33). The two registers stand in one passage and the text resolves neither — a father in the house and a Father in the covenant title (1 Chronicles 28:6).
