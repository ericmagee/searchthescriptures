CHALLENGER (record's reading) -- ROUND 3, LINK ROUND

LINK 1: SUPPORTED — the union stands as the record's own held sentence: Joseph knew Mary his wife within the espousal, and the child is of his loins.

Footings exhibited:
- [L1] Matthew 1:19 "Joseph her husband"; Matthew 1:20 "Mary thy wife"; Matthew 1:25 "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son"; Matthew 1:21 "thou shalt call his name JESUS"; Matthew 1:25 "he called his name JESUS".
- [L2] egennēsen (begat), active, the word of the whole line in Matthew 1; ouk eginōsken autēn heōs hou eteken (knew her not till she brought forth) — the abstinence is bounded by the birth.
- [L3] Deuteronomy 22:24 "his neighbour's wife" — the law's own word for a betrothed woman; Genesis 4:1 "Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived" — the canon's stated means of begetting; 2 Samuel 7:12 "thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels"; Acts 2:30 "of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh"; Romans 1:3 "made of the seed of David according to the flesh."

Chain: husband and wife in law from the espousal (Deuteronomy 22:24; Matthew 1:19-20); the stated means is knowing (Genesis 4:1); the promised seed must proceed out of a man's bowels and loins (2 Samuel 7:12; Acts 2:30); "till" bounds the abstinence to the pregnancy. So [L4] resting on those rows: the knowing is within the espousal.

The pressed rows, read inside that chain:
- Matthew 1:18 "before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" — the verse dates the FINDING. And synerchomai (to come together) is the married-union verb of 1 Corinthians 7:5 "come together again"; Matthew 1:24 "took unto him his wife" shows what coming together Matthew means — her coming into his house.
- Matthew 1:20 "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" — covenant origin, not the begetter: God speaks the same over Solomon, "I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father" (1 Chronicles 28:6), while Solomon is the son "that shall come forth out of thy loins" (1 Kings 8:19).
- Luke 1:34 "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" — andra ou ginōskō (I know not a man) is present indicative: her state when the angel spoke.
- Luke 1:35's two verbs are presence and power: eperchomai (to come upon) is the word of Acts 1:8 "after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you", of the apostles; episkiazō (to overshadow) is the word of the transfiguration cloud (Luke 9:34) and of Peter's shadow (Acts 5:15). Neither denotes begetting at any of their rows.

LINK 2: NARROWED TO: 2 Samuel 7:14's "I will be his father, and he shall be my son" is the covenant designation spoken over the son, and it sits beside the son's human begetter in the same record; therefore it does not state that God begat the child's flesh. The dropped limb is the phrase "a title only" taken as an exclusive claim about everything else the title carries.

Footings exhibited:
- [L1] 1 Chronicles 28:6, one verse holding both: "Solomon thy son ... I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father" — the son David begat and the son God calls his; 2 Samuel 7:12 "thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels"; 1 Kings 8:19 "thy son that shall come forth out of thy loins".
- [L3] Psalm 2:7 "this day have I begotten thee" applied at Acts 13:33 "by the resurrection of Jesus Christ" and paired with 2 Samuel 7:14 at Hebrews 1:5; Luke 1:32 gives the title and the throne before Mary's question at 1:34, and Luke 1:35 says "shall be called the Son of God" (klēthēsetai, shall be called).

Chain: the same father-son formula is spoken of a man with a named human begetter, and the begetting of Psalm 2:7 is placed at the resurrection, not at a conception. The formula therefore does not decide the begetter question. On the pressed rows: Luke 1:32 "the throne of his father David" names David as the father of the throne-line; Luke 1:35 gives a CALLING after "born of thee", and its verbs are presence and power as shown above.

LINK 3: NARROWED TO: Mary's "thy father" names Joseph as the child's father in the plain word, standing with the other registers that name him; the dropped limb is the sharper claim that the word "father" by itself narrates the act of begetting — the argument is the named father plus the seed rows, not the noun alone.

Footings exhibited:
- [L1] Luke 2:48 "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing" (the mother's own mouth); Luke 2:27 "the parents brought in the child Jesus"; 2:33 "Joseph and his mother"; 2:41 "his parents"; 2:43 "Joseph and his mother"; Luke 4:22 "Is not this Joseph's son?"; John 6:42 "Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know"; John 1:45 "the son of Joseph"; Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David".
- [L3] 1 Chronicles 28:6, where "thy son" and "my son" sit in one verse over one child.

Chain: four registers — the mother, the narrator, the town, a disciple — name Joseph as father, and 1 Chronicles 28:6 shows that a second designation over the same child does not cancel the named begetter. The pressed rows are answered inside that: Luke 2:49 "wist ye not that I must be about my father's business?" does not revise 2:48 — the same scene still calls them "his parents" and "Joseph and his mother" (2:43). John 6:38 "I came down from heaven" states his sending; John 6:42 states the parentage the town knows, and no narrator calls it false. Both rows stand as written.

LINK 4: NARROWED TO: the sentence of Jeremiah 22:30 is not shown to stand unbroken against the throne-seed; the prophet's own rule for pronounced sentences and the named signet answer it. The dropped limb is the positive claim that the sentence is narrated as lifted.

Footings exhibited:
- [L1] Jeremiah 18:7-8, in the LORD's own mouth: "If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil that I thought to do unto them"; Jeremiah 22:24 "as I live, saith the LORD, though Coniah ... were the signet upon my right hand, yet would I pluck thee thence"; Haggai 2:23 "I will take thee, O Zerubbabel, the son of Shealtiel, my servant ... and will make thee as a signet: for I have chosen thee."
- [L3] 1 Chronicles 3:17-18, the sons of Jeconiah; Matthew 1:12 "Jechonias begat Salathiel"; Haggai 1:1 "Zorubbabel ... governor of Judah".

Chain: the sentence's own book states the rule by which a pronounced sentence is repented, and the very symbol of the sentence is given back by name to Zerubbabel — whom the text never places on the throne (Jeremiah 22:30 bars the throne, and the answer to it is a governor and a signet). So the curse is not shown to bar Joseph's seed from the throne, and that limb has already left the defender's own narrowed link.

LINK 5: NARROWED TO: the sign as given in Isaiah is bounded inside its own chapter, and the verse speaks the young-woman word; therefore Isaiah 7:14 cannot be pressed alone to state a conception without a man. The dropped limb is any denial of Matthew 1:23's application.

Footings exhibited:
- [L1] Isaiah 7:16 "before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good"; 8:3-4 "the prophetess conceived, and bare a son ... Maher-shalal-hash-baz"; 8:8 "his wings shall fill the breadth of thy land, O Immanuel".
- [L2] almah (H5959, young woman) is the word at Isaiah 7:14 — 7 rows in the canon (Genesis 24:43; Exodus 2:8; Psalm 68:25; Proverbs 30:19; Song of Solomon 1:3; 6:8; Isaiah 7:14); bethulah (H1330, virgin) is 50 rows across 19 books, and is not the word used.
- [L3] Matthew 1:23 quotes the verse, "Behold, a virgin shall be with child" (hē parthenos, the virgin); the 1611 margin at Isaiah 7:14 reads "or, thou, O virgin, shalt call". Both are carried as the record gives them.

Chain: the sign's own bounds are in the text of Isaiah — 7:16's child's-age limit and 8:8's Immanuel in Isaiah's land — and the word spoken is the young-woman word. Matthew 1:23 is the apostle's application and stands as written; no scripture is set against scripture. What neither row states is "no man": Luke 1:34 is the sentence that says it, and it is present tense.

LINK 6: NARROWED TO: "made of a woman" is the mortality idiom — it names what man is, born of a woman and under the law — and states no absence of a man. The dropped limb is the word "only", as if the phrase carried no other sense; it also states his true humanity.

Footings exhibited:
- [L1] Job 14:1 "man that is born of a woman is of few days"; Matthew 11:11 "among them that are born of women there hath not risen a greater than John the Baptist"; Luke 1:13 "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son" — John's father, named; Galatians 4:4 "made of a woman, made under the law".
- [L3] Job 15:14 "what is man, that he should be clean? and he which is born of a woman"; Job 25:4 "how then can man be justified with him? or he which is born of a woman"; Hebrews 2:14 "partook of flesh and blood".

Chain: the phrase is said of every man born, including John, whose father is named in the same canon; so "made of a woman" marks mortality and law-subjection, and cannot bear "no man was involved." [L2]: gynaikos (of a woman) carries no negation of a man — the text has that negation as a sentence (Luke 1:34 "I know not a man", andra ou ginōskō), and Galatians 4:4 does not speak it.
