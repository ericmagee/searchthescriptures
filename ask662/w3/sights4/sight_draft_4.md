THE WORD AND ITS ROWS [L2]. Lexicon G1080: "to procreate (properly, of the father, but by extension of the mother); figuratively, to regenerate". kjv_word_map: 105 rows in 65 verses (exact and compound tags). original_words: 96 words in 64 verses. All 65 verses were read. The one verse of difference is 2 Peter 2:12: kjv_word_map tags "made" G1080, and original_words has gegenhmena, G1096, which is not gennao. It is left out of the groups below. In Matthew 1 original_words has three forms: egennhsen 39 times (1:2 to 1:16, "begat"), egennhqh once (1:16, "was born"), gennhqen once (1:20, "conceived").

THE 65 VERSES BY WHAT THE VERB IS SAID OF (a sorting; the verses are named so it can be checked).
(a) A man begetting a child: Matthew 1:2-16 (39 times); Acts 7:8 "and so [Abraham] begat Isaac"; Acts 7:29 "where he begat two sons."
(b) A woman bearing: Luke 1:13 "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son"; Luke 1:57 "she brought forth a son."; Luke 23:29 "the wombs that never bare"; John 16:21 "as soon as she is delivered of the child".
(c) A person "born", the begetter not the subject: Matthew 1:16, 1:20, 2:1, 2:4, 19:12, 26:24; Mark 14:21; Luke 1:35; John 3:4, 9:2, 9:19, 9:20, 9:32, 9:34, 16:21, 18:37; Acts 2:8, 7:20, 22:3, 22:28; Romans 9:11; Galatians 4:23, 4:29; Hebrews 11:12, 11:23.
(d) The new birth, twelve verses. Ten say "of God", "of the Spirit" or "of him": John 1:13; John 3:5, 3:6, 3:8; 1 John 2:29 ("of him"), 3:9, 4:7, 5:1, 5:4, 5:18. Two say "born again": John 3:3, 3:7. Psalm 2:7 quoted: Acts 13:33, Hebrews 1:5, Hebrews 5:5. Claimed by the hearers: John 8:41 "We be not born of fornication; we have one Father, [even] God."
(e) A man begetting by the gospel: 1 Corinthians 4:15 "for in Christ Jesus I have begotten you through the gospel."; Philemon 1:10 "I beseech thee for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds:".
(f) Things: Galatians 4:24 "which gendereth to bondage"; 2 Timothy 2:23 "they do gender strifes."

WHAT THE ROWS GIVE.
1. One chapter, one verb, three English words. Matthew 1 has "begat" 39 times, "was born" at 1:16 and "conceived" at 1:20, all gennao; the 1611 margin at 1:20 gives "[conceived: Gr. begotten]". "Conceived" renders gennao in that one verse only.
2. The same angel uses the verb of both women in Luke 1. Luke 1:13 "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son" (gennhsei uion soi); Luke 1:35 "that holy thing which shall be born of thee" (to gennwmenon ek sou). In 1:35 "of thee" is ek with Mary.
3. In group (d) the ones born of God or of the Spirit are those who believe. 1 John 5:1 "Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is born of God"; John 1:12-13; John 3:8 "so is every one that is born of the Spirit." John 3:4 shows such a one was first born of a mother. 1 John 3:9 adds seed: "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him" (sperma, G4690).
4. In group (e) a man begets one who has another father in the flesh. Paul writes "my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds" and tells the Corinthians "I have begotten you through the gospel."
5. In John 8 men who say "Abraham is our father" (8:39) say in the next breath "we have one Father, [even] God" (8:41). In their own mouths the two fathers stand together.
6. Psalm 2:7 "this day have I begotten thee" is applied at Acts 13:33 to God having "raised up Jesus again" (Sight 1583 holds this).

THE READING, EACH LINE MARKED.
R1 [L2, Greek on Greek]. The word at Matthew 1:20 is the chapter's own word "begat" in the passive. The clause reads gennhqen ek pneumatov estin agiou: the begotten one "is of the Holy Ghost".
R2 [L3]. Gennao with "of God", "of the Spirit" or "of him" stands in ten verses of John and 1 John, said of those who believe; John 3:4 shows such a one was first born of a mother. Gennao is also said of Paul toward Onesimus and the Corinthians. So in the New Testament's own use, to be begotten of God, of the Spirit, or of an apostle is said of persons already born.
R3 [L4, a reading]. Matthew 1:20 read with those rows names the Spirit as where the child is from, as John 3:6 does of every one born of the Spirit. This agrees with DG-8 and Sights 1583 and 1822 as they stand.

WHAT A CHALLENGER CAN SAY, STATED PLAINLY.
- In Matthew 1 ek stands with the mother four times in the line of descent (1:3 "of Thamar", 1:5 "of Rachab" and "of Ruth", 1:6 "of her [that had been the wife] of Urias") and at 1:16 "of whom was born Jesus"; then 1:18 and 1:20 have ek with the Holy Ghost. A challenger can say the chapter's own use of ek with gennao is for a parent. The answer of the rows is item 3 and R2: the same verb and ek are said of the Spirit and of God for every believer. The record's method stands over this: context and comprehension, not word counting (thread ruling of 20:00 CST 2026-11-17). Applied here [L4]: the passage is read whole, and a preposition supports a reading and does not decide it.
- Group (d) is about believers, and Matthew 1:20 is about a child in the womb. R3 is marked a reading for that cause.
- Luke 3:23 "being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph" is not a gennao row and is not read here.
