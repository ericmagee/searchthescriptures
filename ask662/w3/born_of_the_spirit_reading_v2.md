A. ONE GREEK VERB, gennao (G1080), IN EVERY ONE OF THESE VERSES [L2, Greek verses only].
Lexicon G1080: "to procreate (properly, of the father, but by extension of the mother); figuratively, to regenerate". kjv_word_map renders it begat 42, born 39, begotten 7, "conceived" once, and other words once or twice. That one "conceived" is Matthew 1:20, and the 1611 margin there reads "[conceived: Gr. begotten]".
- Matthew 1:20 "for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost. [conceived: Gr. begotten]". original_words: "gennhqen ek pneumatov estin agiou" (G1080, G1537, G4151, G40).
- John 3:6 "That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit." original_words: "gegennhmenon ek tou pneumatov" (G1080, G1537, G4151).
- John 3:8 "so is every one that is born of the Spirit." Same three words.
- John 3:5 "Except a man be born of water and [of] the Spirit". Same verb, ek, pneuma.
- 1 John 5:1 "Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is born of God: and every one that loveth him that begat loveth him also that is begotten of him. [is born: Gr. has been born]". Born, begat, begotten are all G1080; "of God" is ek.
- John 1:13 "Which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." G1080 with ek four times.
- Galatians 4:23, 4:24 ("gendereth"), 4:29: G1080.
- Matthew 1:16 "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." Begat and born are both G1080.
So the words of Matthew 1:20 (gennao + ek + pneuma) are the words of John 3:6 and 3:8, where they are said of "every one that is born of the Spirit".

MY READING, EACH LINE MARKED.
R1 [L2, Greek on Greek]. The words "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" and "that which is born of the Spirit" are one construction in Greek: gennao, ek, pneuma. The 1611 margin at Matthew 1:20 itself gives "begotten".
R2 [L3, another passage]. John 3:6 and 3:8 say those words of "every one that is born of the Spirit"; the question they answer is John 3:4 "How can a man be born when he is old? can he enter the second time into his mother's womb, and be born?", so the one born of the Spirit was first born of a mother. Galatians 4:29 says "after the Spirit" of Isaac, whose father Abraham is named in the same passage and at Genesis 21:2. So the phrase beside a birth is used in Scripture of a person with a named father in the flesh (Isaac).
R3 [L4, a reading]. Matthew 1:20 read with those passages agrees with DG-8 as the record already holds it: "of the Holy Ghost" marks the child, and Joseph is addressed in that verse as "thou son of David" and Mary as "thy wife".
R4 [L4]. Isaac is the nearest pattern in the canon for Matthew 1:20: a son promised beforehand by God's word, his birth called "after the Spirit" and "by promise", his father named.

WHAT A CHALLENGER CAN SAY, STATED PLAINLY.
- John 1:13 "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God" could be turned to say that birth "of God" excludes a man. In its sentence (John 1:12-13) it is said of "as many as received him". The same answer serves there as for John 3:6.
- Galatians 4:29 uses kata ("after"), Matthew 1:20 uses ek ("of"). R2 does not claim the prepositions are the same. John 3:6 and 3:8 carry the ek.
- Galatians 4:24 calls the passage "an allegory". Paul's allegory stands on the history of Genesis 21, which is quoted above.
