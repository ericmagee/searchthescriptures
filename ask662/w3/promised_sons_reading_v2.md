THE SONS, EACH BY ITS ROWS.
1. Isaac. Announced: Genesis 17:19 "Sarah thy wife shall bear thee a son indeed"; Genesis 18:14 "Is any thing too hard for the LORD? At the time appointed I will return unto thee, according to the time of life, and Sarah shall have a son." Father: Genesis 21:2 "For Sarah conceived, and bare Abraham a son in his old age"; 21:3 "his son that was born unto him". The act is not told; Genesis 21:1 says "And the LORD visited Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did unto Sarah as he had spoken." Of him the New Testament says "by promise" (Galatians 4:23), "after the Spirit" (Galatians 4:29), and Romans 9:8 "the children of the promise are counted for the seed"; of his father, Hebrews 11:12 "Therefore sprang there even of one, and him as good as dead".
2. Samson. Announced: Judges 13:3 "Behold now, thou [art] barren, and bearest not: but thou shalt conceive, and bear a son." Father: Judges 13:2 "Manoah; and his wife [was] barren"; Judges 16:31 "the buryingplace of Manoah his father". The act is not told: Judges 13:24 "And the woman bare a son, and called his name Samson". The Spirit: Judges 13:25 "And the Spirit of the LORD began to move him at times".
3. The Shunammite's son. Announced: 2 Kings 4:16 "thou shalt embrace a son". 2 Kings 4:14 "she hath no child, and her husband is old." The act is not told: 4:17 "And the woman conceived, and bare a son". Father: 4:18 "he went out to his father to the reapers."
4. Samuel. Asked: 1 Samuel 1:11, with the margin "[a man...: Heb. seed of men]". Granted: 1:17. The act is told: 1:19 "and Elkanah knew Hannah his wife; and the LORD remembered her."
5. Solomon. Announced to David: 1 Chronicles 22:9 "Behold, a son shall be born to thee". The act is told: 2 Samuel 12:24 "went in unto her, and lay with her: and she bare a son".
6. Josiah. Announced by name: 1 Kings 13:2 "a child shall be born unto the house of David, Josiah by name". Father: 2 Kings 21:24 "made Josiah his son king in his stead" (Amon). The word done: 2 Kings 23:16 "according to the word of the LORD which the man of God proclaimed".
7. Immanuel. Announced: Isaiah 7:14 "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." In the next chapter: Isaiah 8:3 "And I went unto the prophetess; and she conceived, and bare a son." Isaiah 7:16 "For before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good" stands beside Isaiah 8:4 "For before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother". Matthew 1:22-23 applies Isaiah 7:14 to the birth of Jesus. The two Isaiah rows are set side by side only; this reading does not say they are one child.
8. John. Announced: Luke 1:13 "thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son". Luke 1:7 "Elisabeth was barren, and they both were [now] well stricken in years." The act is not told: Luke 1:23 "he departed to his own house", 1:24 "And after those days his wife Elisabeth conceived". Father: Luke 1:59 "after the name of his father", 1:62 "they made signs to his father". The Spirit: Luke 1:15 "he shall be filled with the Holy Ghost, even from his mother's womb."
9. Jesus. Announced: Luke 1:31 "thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son"; Matthew 1:21 "And she shall bring forth a son". The act is not told. Named in the rows: Matthew 1:20 "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife"; Luke 2:48 "thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing." The Spirit: Luke 1:35; Matthew 1:18, 1:20.

THE ANGEL'S OWN PARALLEL [L1]. In the one speech to Mary, after Luke 1:35, the angel says Luke 1:36 "And, behold, thy cousin Elisabeth, she hath also conceived a son in her old age: and this is the sixth month with her, who was called barren." and Luke 1:37 "For with God nothing shall be impossible." The text itself sets Elisabeth's conceiving beside Mary's with the word "also".

MY READING, EACH LINE MARKED.
R1 [L1, a count of the rows above]. Of the nine sons, the act is told for two (Samuel, Solomon) and not told for seven (Isaac, Samson, the Shunammite's son, Josiah, Immanuel, John, Jesus). Of those seven, the rows name a father for five: Isaac (Genesis 21:2), Samson (Judges 16:31), the Shunammite's son (2 Kings 4:18), Josiah (2 Kings 21:24), John (Luke 1:59). Isaiah 7 names no father for Immanuel. For Jesus the rows are Matthew 1:20 and Luke 2:48, and DG-8 holds the record's reading.
R2 [L3]. Luke 1:37 "For with God nothing shall be impossible" stands with Genesis 18:14 "Is any thing too hard for the LORD?": the one is said by the LORD in Genesis 18:13-14 and the other by the angel Gabriel in Luke 1:26-37, each about a son promised to a woman, and in Genesis the son is Abraham's. The two verses are joined by their setting, not by a shared word (one is Hebrew, one Greek).
R3 [L3]. The Spirit is named on three of the promised sons whose fathers are named: Isaac "after the Spirit" (Galatians 4:29), Samson (Judges 13:25), John "from his mother's womb" (Luke 1:15).
R4 [L4, a reading]. The angel answers Mary's "How shall this be" (Luke 1:34) with the Spirit's coming (1:35) and then with Elisabeth (1:36-37). Read with the eight before him, the announcing of Jesus follows the pattern of the promised son: God speaks first (Genesis 18:14, Judges 13:3, 2 Kings 4:16, Luke 1:13, Luke 1:31), the woman conceives, and the angel says "also" at Luke 1:36. This agrees with DG-8 and Sight 1822 as they stand.
