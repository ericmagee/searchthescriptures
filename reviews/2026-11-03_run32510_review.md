## 3. Why the defender does not concede

### 3.1 Narrowing costs nothing in the defender's instructions

The live link-round instruction to the defender (step "D3 Build") offers three forms:

> "LINK n: SUPPORTED -- exhibit its layer footing …; or LINK n: CONCEDED -- it floats free (rests on no exhibited prior-layer footing); or LINK n: NARROWED TO: <the limb you keep> -- exhibit that limb's footing as for SUPPORTED; the limb you drop leaves the link; the link is judged in its narrowed form."

There are three problems with this:
- Conceding is defined only as having no footing at all. A link with any two layers attached is never conceded, whether or not those layers bear on the step.
- Nothing requires the kept limb to stand without the dropped limb.
- So the cheapest move is always to narrow. In this run the defender narrowed all five links and conceded none. The synthesis records: "No link on either side was CONCEDED-BY-ITS-OWN-SIDE in full."

### 3.2 The judges grade footing by count, not by whether it bears on the step

The panel prompt says: "A premise counts as CONCEDED only if the link round block itself states it as conceded, or exhibits no layer footing for it in that block." Gemini applied this word for word: "all six listed premises had their two prior layers exhibited."

### 3.3 The panel prompt contradicts itself on close-round concessions

The same prompt carries both of these as binding:
- "COUNTING RULE -- BINDING, read before grading: grade and COUNT only from the two LINK ROUND blocks above, [CR3] (defender) and [DR3] (challenger)."
- "BINDING (ruling 412): a concession made in a close round ([CR4] or [DR4]) of a declared link or of a listed premise ENTERS the counts exactly as a link-round concession does … end its line here with COUNTED."

All three judges followed the older rule. Not one close-round line carries "COUNTED."

A model told to hold its chain will therefore narrow every time. ## 5. Solutions (for the advisor and the builder; the operator decides)

1. **Close the free narrowing**, in both debaters' link-round instructions and under one standard for both sides (Commitment 4). Proposed text:
   > "NARROWED TO may keep only a limb that stands without the limb you drop. If the claim you keep still needs a step you have said the text does not state, or a footing you have withdrawn, the link is CONCEDED."

   This also answers the open question in section 3.4.
2. **Make footing bear on the step.** Add to Commitment 2:
   > "Two layers that do not supply the step the [L4] takes are not footing. Name the step and the layer that supplies it."

   Judges then grade "the chain from them," not the count of tags.
3. **Remove the contradiction in the panel prompt.** Amend the "COUNTING RULE" and the "counts as CONCEDED only if the link round block" sentences to carry ruling 412. Then add a program check: any OUT-OF-ROUND line from [CR4] or [DR4] that grants ground on a declared link or listed premise, and does not end in COUNTED, is flagged for review.
4. **Make the judges rule on each withdrawal.** The synthesis already builds a "Register of withdrawals by their own side (quoted)." Serve that register to the judges as a checklist. Each judge would answer every entry in one line:
   > "WITHDRAWAL: <quote> -- touches D<i> -- the link STANDS WITHOUT IT because <the layer that still supplies the step> | CONCEDED."

   A withdrawal could then no longer be listed and ignored, as "I do not rest D5 on Luke 3 being Mary's line" was here.
5. **Rule on the narrowing question** from run 32373 (ruling 412 item 6), with run 32510 as the specimen.
6. **Add DG-18 to the program's checks.** When the record's seat quotes Psalm 2:7 or "this day have I begotten thee," check that Acts 13:33, Hebrews 5:5 and Hebrews 1:5 all appear in its rounds, and flag the row if not. The synthesis missed this breach in this run.
7. **Make conceding cheaper than being broken** (my inference, for the advisor to weigh). Tell both debaters that a narrowed link whose kept limb still needs a withdrawn step is graded BROKEN, which costs more than CONCEDED. The honest move then becomes the cheaper one.

**Suggested order:** items 1 to 3 first. They change no verdict rule; they make the existing rules reach the case. Item 4 is the check that would have caught this run directly.
