## 2. Findings on the debaters

### 2.1 The challenger trimmed the lexicon, twice (most serious)

In its link round and again in its close round, the challenger quoted the gloss for *synerchomai* (G4905, "came together" in Matthew 1:18) as:

> "to convene, depart in company with, associate with … come (together)"

The lexicon row reads in full:

> "to convene, depart in company with, associate with, or (specially), cohabit (conjugally)"

The dropped clause, "or (specially), cohabit (conjugally)", is the sense the defender's press rests on. Only the second-opinion judge caught it. The three panel judges did not.

**Feedback:** a gloss quoted with an ellipsis that drops the clause in dispute should count as a misquotation under the citation check, the same as a misquoted verse.

### 2.2 The challenger made a false claim about where a word occurs

In LINK 1 of its link round, the challenger wrote that *huios* (G5207) "stands in every link" of Luke 3. `original_words` holds G5207 only at Luke 3:23. Luke 3:24 and Luke 3:31 carry only the article *tou* (G3588). Luke 3:31 reads: "...which was [the son] of Nathan, which was [the son] of David," and the bracket marks the supplied word.

The [L2] check passed the tag because G5207 does occur in Luke 3:23. **The check confirms that a word occurs in the cited verse; it cannot test a claim that a word occurs throughout a passage.**

### 2.3 The defender used a false [L2] tag

## 4. Findings on the judges and the pipeline

### 4.1 The cap parser counts [L3] cross-references as presses

The audit reports these press counts:
- premise 1: 9 presses, with Matthew 1:20 over the cap;
- premise 3: 12 presses, with 1 Chronicles 28:6, Acts 13:33, Romans 1:4 and Luke 1:32 over the cap.

The real count is 8 for each premise. The parser counted verses cited inside "[L3]" segments as presses. As a result, real witnesses (1 Chronicles 28:6, Acts 13:33 and Luke 1:32) were dropped as over the cap.

**Fix:** count only the reference that leads each PRESSED WITH entry, and not references inside its MEANS, [L2], [L3] or PROVEN IN segments.
