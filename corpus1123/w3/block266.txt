

THREE EVENTS, NOT ONE: THE COMING TOGETHER, THE TAKING AND THE KNOWING (operator, 11-23; rulings 359 and 500).
L1:
- "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." (Matthew 1:18)
- "Then Joseph her husband, being a just [man], and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." (Matthew 1:19)
- "But while he thought on these things, behold, the angel of the Lord appeared unto him in a dream, saying, Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." (Matthew 1:20)
- "Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife:" (Matthew 1:24)
- "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS." (Matthew 1:25)
- "And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD. [Cain: that is, Gotten, or, Acquired]" (Genesis 4:1)
- "And they rose up in the morning early, and worshipped before the LORD, and returned, and came to their house to Ramah: and Elkanah knew Hannah his wife; and the LORD remembered her." (1 Samuel 1:19)
- "For laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men, [as] the washing of pots and cups: and many other such like things ye do." (Mark 7:8)
L2:
- "came together" (Matthew 1:18) is synerchomai (G4905). The KJV renders it "accompany, assemble (with), come (together), come (company, go) with, resort". Its sense at Matthew 1:18 is set by the narrative (Matthew 1:20, 1:24) and by rulings 359 and 500, not by a lexicon gloss.
- "take unto thee" (Matthew 1:20) and "took unto him" (Matthew 1:24) are one verb, paralambano (G3880). The KJV renders it "receive, take (unto, with)".
- "knew" (Matthew 1:25) is ginosko (G1097), a different word from both, and the word of Genesis 4:1 in the same act ("Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived").
- "make her a publick example" (Matthew 1:19) is deigmatizo (G1165): "to exhibit:--make a shew"; "privily" is lathra (G2977): "privately:--privily, secretly".
- "tradition" (Mark 7:8) is paradosis (G3862): "transmission, i.e. (concretely) a precept".
L3:
- The rows name them husband and wife before the coming together: "Joseph the husband of Mary" (Matthew 1:16), "Joseph her husband" (Matthew 1:19), "Mary thy wife" (Matthew 1:20).
- The angel bids the taking (Matthew 1:20), and Joseph does what he was bidden (Matthew 1:24) with the same verb, paralambano: the taking of Matthew 1:24 is the one the angel ordered.
- The knowing has its own word and its own place in the order: "knew her not till she had brought forth" (Matthew 1:25) comes after the taking (Matthew 1:24).
L4 (each step rests on more than one row):
1. "Came together" (Matthew 1:18) is "their ceremonial or household union" (the operator, 09:07 CST 11-23; rulings 359 and 500: the ceremony, which includes living together). Matthew narrates Joseph's own act in it as the taking: the angel bids it (Matthew 1:20) and Joseph does it (Matthew 1:24). It is not the union of husband and wife, whose word is "knew" (Matthew 1:25; Genesis 4:1).
2. They were husband and wife before the ceremony (Matthew 1:16, 1:19, 1:20). The canon's way of conceiving is a husband knowing his wife (Genesis 4:1; 1 Samuel 1:19). She was found with child before they came together (Matthew 1:18). So what became visible was the private relation of husband and wife, before the public union ([L4]; Sight 32 step (4) holds the same).
3. The publick example Joseph would not make (Matthew 1:19, deigmatizo, "to exhibit") is that exposure. The putting away privily (lathra) shields her from it. The scorn rests on the tradition of men, not on the commandment of God (Mark 7:8, [L3] application: it speaks of washings, and Matthew names no tradition): the rows call her his wife (Matthew 1:19, 1:20).
4. "Fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" (Matthew 1:20) answers that fear and bids the public taking, the open course set against "put her away privily" (Matthew 1:19), and Joseph takes her (Matthew 1:24).
5. After the taking, he "knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" (Matthew 1:25 read with Matthew 1:24): the withholding runs from the taking to the birth.
So, in the operator's words, ""came together," "took unto him his wife," and "knew her" describe distinct events in the narrative". The rows name them with three words: synerchomai for the household union (Matthew 1:18), paralambano for Joseph's act of taking (Matthew 1:20, 1:24), and ginosko for the knowing (Matthew 1:25). The knowing also stands apart by its place in the order, after the taking (Matthew 1:24, 1:25). Matthew 1:18 dates the finding before the ceremony, not before any union.
