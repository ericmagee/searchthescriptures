The standard biblical teaching established across both Testaments is that Jesus Christ did not have a biological father: he was conceived in the womb of the virgin Mary by the direct spiritual agency of the Holy Ghost without human male paternity.

### 1. The Prophetic Witness to Conception by a Virgin

Scripture sets forth the promised deliverer in terms of maternal origin from the outset. [L1] Genesis 3:15 declares: "And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." [L2] The noun translated "seed" is `zera` (H2233, seed, offspring, posterity), here attached specifically to the woman as "her seed." [L3] Galatians 4:4 later affirms this singular maternal origin: "But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law," using [L2] `ginomai` (G1096, to come into being, to be made), `ek` (G1537, out of, from), and `gune` (G1135, a woman).

This promise becomes explicit regarding virgin conception in [L1] Isaiah 7:14: "Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." [L2] The word translated "virgin" is `almah` (H5959, a virgin, an unmarried maiden of marriageable age), joined to `harah` (H2029, to conceive, become pregnant). [L3] Matthew 1:22-23 directly cites this prophecy and records its fulfilment in Mary: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying," (Matthew 1:22) "Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us." (Matthew 1:23). [L2] In Matthew 1:23, the Greek term rendering `almah` (H5959) is `parthenos` (G3933, a virgin, an unmarried woman who has not known a man). [L4] Because Isaiah 7:14 [L1] presents the conception of Immanuel by an `almah` [L2] as a divine sign, and Matthew 1:22-23 [L3] identifies the mother of Jesus as the `parthenos` [L2] fulfilling that sign, Mary conceived Christ while remaining a virgin without human fatherhood.

### 2. Mary's Virginity Prior to Marital Union and Before Knowing Any Man

The Gospel accounts affirm positively that Mary became pregnant prior to marital union with Joseph and before knowing any man. [L1] Matthew 1:18 states: "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." [L2] Here `mnesteuo` (G3423, to betroth, espouse) marks Mary's legal betrothal to Joseph; `prin` (G4250, before) joined to `sunerchomai` (G4905, to come together, cohabit) establishes that conception occurred prior to any sexual union between them; and `ek` (G1537, out of, from, denoting originating source or agency) with `pneuma` (G4151, Spirit, Ghost) and `hagios` (G40, holy) identifies the Holy Ghost as the source of the child in her womb.

[L3] Luke 1:27 independently identifies Mary in the same state: "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin's name was Mary," again using [L2] `parthenos` (G3933, a virgin) and `mnesteuo` (G3423, espoused). When told in [L3] Luke 1:31, "And, behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name JESUS," Mary states in [L1] Luke 1:34: "Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" [L2] The verb `ginosko` (G1097, to know, used idiomatically for sexual intercourse) in the present tense with `aner` (G435, a man, husband, male) declares her complete absence of sexual relations with any man. [L3] Matthew 1:19 confirms Joseph's own recognition that he had not fathered the child: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily." [L4] From Matthew 1:18 [L1], `sunerchomai` after `prin` [L2], and Luke 1:34 [L3] together with Matthew 1:19 [L3], it follows that Mary conceived while knowing no man and before cohabiting with Joseph, so neither Joseph nor any other human male was the child's biological father.

### 3. The Holy Ghost as the Originating Agent of Conception

Scripture does not leave the cause of Christ's conception unspecified; it positively identifies the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest as the sole originating agent. [L1] Matthew 1:20 records the words of the angel of the Lord to Joseph: "But while he thought on these things, behold, the angel of the Lord appeared unto him in a dream, saying, Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." [L2] The passive participle from `gennao` (G1080, to beget, conceive, bring forth) paired with the preposition `ek` (G1537, out of, from, denoting originating cause) states that the conception itself proceeded from the Holy Ghost.

[L3] Luke 1:35 answers Mary's question of Luke 1:34 in parallel terms: "And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." [L2] Here `eperchomai` (G1904, to come upon) and `episkiazo` (G1982, to overshadow) describe the divine creative act, and the inferential conjunction `dio` (G1352, therefore, for this cause) directly links this spiritual act to `gennao` (G1080, "which shall be born") and the child's title "the Son of God." [L3] John 4:24 affirms the non-biological nature of God: "God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth," using [L2] `pneuma` (G4151, spirit). [L4] Because Matthew 1:20 [L1] attributes the begetting or conception (`gennao`, `ek`, [L2]) directly to the Holy Ghost, and Luke 1:35 [L3] grounds the birth of the Son of God (`dio`, [L2]) in the overshadowing of the power of the Highest, while John 4:24 [L3] states God is a Spirit, Christ's conception occurred by miraculous spiritual power rather than by a biological father.

### 4. Joseph's Abstinence and Legal Rather Than Biological Paternity

The New Testament explicitly marks the boundary between Joseph's legal fatherhood and biological descent. [L1] Matthew 1:24-25 records Joseph's obedience: "Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife:" (Matthew 1:24) "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS." (Matthew 1:25). [L2] Just as in Luke 1:34, `ginosko` (G1097, to know sexually) negated by `ou` ("knew her not") establishes that Joseph had no sexual relations with Mary prior to the birth of Jesus.

[L3] Matthew 1:2 and Matthew 1:16 confirm this distinction in the genealogy itself. Throughout the genealogy, [L3] Matthew 1:2 establishes the pattern of biological paternity: "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob; and Jacob begat Judas and his brethren;" using [L2] the active verb `gennao` (G1080, begat). At the end of that chain, [L1] Matthew 1:16 breaks the active male pattern: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ." [L2] Whereas Jacob actively `gennao` ("begat") Joseph, Jesus' birth is expressed with the passive `gennao` ("was born") and the preposition `ek` (G1537, "of") governing `hos` (G3739) in the feminine singular form `hes` ("of whom"), referring grammatically to Mary alone rather than to Joseph or to both. [L3] Luke 3:23 likewise guards Joseph's relation to Jesus: "And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli," using [L2] `nomizo` (G3543, to suppose, reckon by custom or opinion). [L4] Because Joseph "knew her not" (`ginosko`, [L2]) until after the birth [L1], and Matthew 1:16 [L3] shifts from active paternal begetting to passive birth "of whom" (`hes`, feminine singular, [L2]) while Luke 3:23 [L3] qualifies Joseph's paternity with `nomizo` ("as was supposed", [L2]), Joseph was Christ's legal and supposed father rather than his biological father.

### 5. Conclusion: Made of a Woman Without Biological Paternity

The apostolic summary of Christ's entry into the world brings all four lines of testimony together. [L1] Galatians 4:4 states: "But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law," using [L2] `ginomai` (G1096, to come into being, be made), `ek` (G1537, out of, from), and `gune` (G1135, a woman). [L3] Read with Genesis 3:15 ("her seed", `zera`, H2233), Isaiah 7:14 ("a virgin shall conceive", `almah`, H5959), Matthew 1:18 ("before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost"), Matthew 1:20 ("that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost"), Matthew 1:25 ("knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son"), and Luke 1:34-35 ("I know not a man... The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee"), [L4] Scripture uniformly testifies that Christ's human flesh was formed of the virgin Mary by the Holy Ghost without human male seed, and therefore Christ did not have a biological father.
