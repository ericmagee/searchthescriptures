The standard reading of Scripture affirms that Christ did not have a biological father: he was conceived in the virgin Mary by the direct agency of the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest, without sexual union between Mary and Joseph or any other man. The case rests on five linked proofs from the King James Version, grounded in lexical analysis and cross-reference.

### 1. Mary was a virgin who had not known a man, and she conceived before she and Joseph came together

[L1] Luke 1:27 states: "To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin's name was Mary." [L1] Luke 1:34 records Mary's direct response to the angel's announcement that she would conceive: "Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"

[L2] In Luke 1:27, *parthenos* (G3933, a virgin, an unmarried woman who has not had sexual intercourse) is applied twice to Mary ("a virgin... the virgin's name was Mary"). In Luke 1:34, Mary's words "I know not a man" render *andra* from *aner* (G435, a man) with *ou* (G3756, not) and *ginosko* (G1097, to know, used idiomatically of sexual intercourse).

[L3] Genesis 4:1 establishes this biblical idiom of "knowing" for marital union resulting in conception: "And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD." [L2] Here *yada* (H3045, to know) denotes the sexual union that produces conception. [L3] Matthew 1:18 independently confirms Mary's status at the time she became pregnant: "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost." [L2] The clause "before they came together" translates *prin e* with *synerchomai* (G4905, to come together, cohabit).

[L4] Standing on [L1] Luke 1:27 and Luke 1:34, [L2] *parthenos* (G3933, a virgin), *ou* (G3756, not) with *ginosko* (G1097, to know) and *aner* (G435, a man), *yada* (H3045, to know), and *synerchomai* (G4905, to come together), and [L3] Genesis 4:1 and Matthew 1:18: because Mary was a virgin who testified in the present tense that she did not know a man in the procreative sense of Genesis 4:1, and was found with child before she and Joseph came together, her conception of Jesus did not result from sexual union with Joseph or with any other man.

### 2. The generating cause of Christ's conception was the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest

[L1] Luke 1:35 gives the angel's direct answer to Mary's question of how conception could occur without her knowing a man: "And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."

[L2] In Luke 1:35, the angel specifies the active agency as *Pneuma Hagion* (G4151 *pneuma*, spirit or ghost; G40 *hagios*, holy) with *eperchomai* (G1904, to come upon), and *dynamis* (G1411, power) of *Hypsistos* (G5310, the Highest) with *episkiazo* (G1982, to overshadow). The inferential conjunction *dio* (G1352, therefore, wherefore) connects that divine action as the direct cause to *to gennomenon* from *gennao* (G1080, to beget or bear; here passive, that which shall be born) being called the Son of God.

[L3] Luke 1:37 confirms the supernatural character of this act: "For with God nothing shall be impossible." [L3] Matthew 1:20 independently states the exact same origin to Joseph: "But while he thought on these things, behold, the angel of the Lord appeared unto him in a dream, saying, Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." [L2] In Matthew 1:20, "that which is conceived in her" renders *gennethen* from *gennao* (G1080, to beget or conceive, passive participle), and "of the Holy Ghost" uses the preposition *ek* (G1537, out of or from, denoting source and origin) with *Pneumatos Hagiou* (G4151 *pneuma*, spirit or ghost; G40 *hagios*, holy). [L3] Matthew 1:22–23 declares that this event fulfills prophetic Scripture: "Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying," (Matthew 1:22) "Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us." (Matthew 1:23) [L2] Matthew 1:23 again uses *parthenos* (G3933, a virgin). [L3] This cites Isaiah 7:14: "Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." [L2] Isaiah 7:14 designates this conception an *oth* (H226, a sign) involving an *almah* (H5959, a virgin).

[L4] Standing on [L1] Luke 1:35, [L2] *eperchomai* (G1904, to come upon), *dynamis* (G1411, power), *episkiazo* (G1982, to overshadow), *dio* (G1352, therefore), *gennao* (G1080, to beget or conceive), *ek* (G1537, out of or from), *parthenos* (G3933, a virgin), and *oth* (H226, a sign), and [L3] Luke 1:37, Matthew 1:20, Matthew 1:22–23, and Isaiah 7:14: because both angels assign the begetting (*gennao*) directly to the Holy Ghost (*ek*, out of or from) and the overshadowing power of the Highest as the causal ground (*dio*, therefore) of the child's birth and title "the Son of God," fulfilling the prophetic sign (*oth*) that a virgin (*parthenos*) would conceive, the generating cause of Christ's conception was divine power rather than human paternity.

### 3. Joseph abstained from marital union with Mary until after Christ was born

[L1] Matthew 1:25 states regarding Joseph's conduct after taking Mary as his wife: "And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS."

[L2] In Matthew 1:25, "knew her not" translates *ou* (G3756, not) with the imperfect active *eginosken* from *ginosko* (G1097, to know, of sexual union), bounded by *heos* (G2193, until or till) and *eteken* from *tikto* (G5088, to bring forth or bear).

[L3] Matthew 1:19 confirms Joseph's own awareness before the angel spoke that he had not fathered the child: "Then Joseph her husband, being a just man" ... "was minded to put her away privily." [L2] Here *apoluo* (G630, to put away or divorce) with *lathra* (G2977, privily or secretly) expresses Joseph's decision to terminate the espousal upon finding Mary pregnant. [L3] Matthew 1:24 records Joseph's obedience to the angel's explanation in Matthew 1:20: "Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife:" [L3] Genesis 4:1 ("And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD.") confirms that "knew" (*yada*, H3045, to know; equivalent to *ginosko*, G1097, to know) is the marital act by which a husband begets a child by his wife.

[L4] Standing on [L1] Matthew 1:25, [L2] *ou* (G3756, not), *ginosko* (G1097, to know), *heos* (G2193, until), *tikto* (G5088, to bring forth), and *apoluo* (G630, to put away), and [L3] Matthew 1:19, Matthew 1:20, Matthew 1:24, and Genesis 4:1: Joseph's initial resolve to put Mary away privily upon her pregnancy, followed by his complete abstinence from knowing her sexually until after she had brought forth her firstborn son, excludes Joseph as the biological father of Jesus.

### 4. Both Gospel genealogies distinguish Joseph's legal or supposed standing from biological begetting

[L1] Matthew 1:16 concludes the genealogy of Jesus Christ with a deliberate grammatical break: "And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."

[L2] Throughout the preceding genealogy, Matthew uses the active verb *egennesen* from *gennao* (G1080, to beget) to trace male-to-male biological descent, ending with "Jacob begat Joseph." At Jesus, the active male begetting stops: Joseph is named "the husband of Mary," and Jesus's birth is traced via *ek* (G1537, out of or from) plus the feminine singular relative pronoun *hes* from *hos* (G3739, whom, agreeing strictly with Mary alone) and the passive verb *egennethe* from *gennao* (G1080, to beget; passive, to be born).

[L3] Matthew 1:2 exemplifies the uniform paternal pattern that Matthew 1:16 breaks: "Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob; and Jacob begat Judas and his brethren;" [L3] Luke 3:23 independently qualifies Joseph's relation to Jesus at the head of Luke's genealogy: "And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli," [L2] In Luke 3:23, "as was supposed" translates *hos* (G5613, as) with the imperfect passive *enomizeto* from *nomizo* (G3543, to suppose, reckon by custom or common opinion).

[L4] Standing on [L1] Matthew 1:16, [L2] *gennao* (G1080, to beget or be born), *ek* (G1537, out of or from), *hes* (G3739, whom, feminine singular), and *nomizo* (G3543, to suppose), and [L3] Matthew 1:2 and Luke 3:23: because Matthew breaks his active male-begetting chain at Joseph and links Jesus's birth solely to Mary (*ex hes*, of whom, feminine singular), and Luke explicitly qualifies Jesus's filiation to Joseph as *hos enomizeto* ("as was supposed"), both genealogies distinguish Joseph's legal or supposed paternity from actual biological fatherhood.

### 5. Conclusion: Christ did not have a biological father

[L1] Matthew 1:18 ("When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost.") and Luke 1:34–35 ("Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" and "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God.") directly locate Jesus's conception in Mary's virginity and in the creative power of the Holy Ghost. [L2] The lexical chain unites *parthenos* (G3933, a virgin) in Luke 1:27 and Matthew 1:23, *ou* (G3756, not) with *ginosko* (G1097, to know) in Luke 1:34 and Matthew 1:25, *gennao* (G1080, to beget or conceive) with *ek* (G1537, out of or from) the Holy Ghost in Matthew 1:20, the feminine singular *hes* (G3739, whom) in Matthew 1:16, and *nomizo* (G3543, to suppose) in Luke 3:23. [L3] Matthew 1:16, Matthew 1:20, Matthew 1:25, and Luke 3:23 converge from two independent Gospel witnesses to confirm that Joseph did not know Mary until after Christ's birth and stood as his father only as was supposed. [L4] Taken together, these four links establish that Christ had no human biological father.

DECLARED CHAIN
1. Mary was a virgin who had not known any man sexually and was already with child before she and Joseph came together: [L1] Luke 1:27 ("To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin's name was Mary.") and Luke 1:34 ("Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"); [L2] *parthenos* (G3933, a virgin), *ou* (G3756, not), *ginosko* (G1097, to know), *aner* (G435, a man), and *synerchomai* (G4905, to come together); [L3] Matthew 1:18 ("When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost.") and Genesis 4:1 ("And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD."); [L4] therefore Mary's conception did not arise from prior sexual union with Joseph or any other man.
2. The direct generating cause of Christ's conception was the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest, fulfilling the prophetic sign of a virgin conceiving: [L1] Luke 1:35 ("And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."); [L2] *eperchomai* (G1904, to come upon), *dynamis* (G1411, power), *episkiazo* (G1982, to overshadow), *dio* (G1352, therefore), *gennao* (G1080, to beget or conceive), *ek* (G1537, out of or from), and *parthenos* (G3933, a virgin); [L3] Matthew 1:20 ("for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."), Matthew 1:23 ("Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us."), and Isaiah 7:14 ("Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel."); [L4] therefore Christ's conception was effected directly by divine power rather than human paternity.
3. Joseph had no sexual union with Mary until after she brought forth Jesus and therefore did not beget him biologically: [L1] Matthew 1:25 ("And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS."); [L2] *ou* (G3756, not), *ginosko* (G1097, to know), *heos* (G2193, until), *tikto* (G5088, to bring forth), and *apoluo* (G630, to put away); [L3] Matthew 1:19 ("Then Joseph her husband, being a just man" and "was minded to put her away privily."), Matthew 1:20 ("fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."), and Genesis 4:1 ("And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD."); [L4] because Joseph knew Mary not until after the birth and had resolved to put her away upon learning she was with child, Joseph was excluded from biological paternity.
4. Both Gospel genealogies sever biological begetting between Joseph and Jesus, tracing Christ's physical birth to Mary alone and marking Joseph's paternity as supposed: [L1] Matthew 1:16 ("And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."); [L2] *gennao* (G1080, to beget or be born), *ek* (G1537, out of or from), *hes* (G3739, whom, feminine singular referring to Mary alone), and *nomizo* (G3543, to suppose); [L3] Matthew 1:2 ("Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob; and Jacob begat Judas and his brethren;") and Luke 3:23 ("And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli,"); [L4] therefore both genealogies distinguish Joseph's legal or supposed status from actual biological fatherhood.
5. Christ did not have a biological father: [L1] Matthew 1:18 ("When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost.") and Luke 1:35 ("The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."); [L2] *ek* (G1537, out of or from) and *gennao* (G1080, to beget or conceive) in Matthew 1:20, *ou* (G3756, not) and *ginosko* (G1097, to know) in Luke 1:34 and Matthew 1:25, and *nomizo* (G3543, to suppose) in Luke 3:23; [L3] Matthew 1:20 ("for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost."), Luke 1:34 ("Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?"), Matthew 1:25 ("And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS."), Matthew 1:16 ("And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ."), and Luke 3:23 ("being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph"); [L4] combining Links 1 through 4, Mary conceived as a virgin without knowing a man, Joseph abstained until after the birth and was father only as was supposed, and the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest directly effected the conception, establishing that Christ had no biological father.
